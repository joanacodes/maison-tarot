## Why we don't learn the 78 cards {#why}

A Rider-Waite-Smith card doesn't have *one* meaning. It has a number, a house, colours, a posture, sometimes an astrological sign — and the combination is what speaks. Learning "the Five of Cups means grief" is learning an answer without the question. Learning that *five* is conflict and *Water* is emotion means you can read the Five of Cups, the Five of Swords, the Five of Wands, the Five of Pentacles — and any five you've never seen.

That's the principle of the whole [Library](/library/): lenses, not meanings.

## The five lenses, in order {#the-five-lenses}

1. **[Colours](/library/tarot-colours/)** — the fastest lens. A colour sets the mood of a card before you've even found its name.
2. **[The four elements](/library/four-elements/)** — the card's house says what it's about: matter ([Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/)), emotion ([Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/)), drive ([Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/)), the mind ([Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/)).
3. **[Numerology](/library/numerology/)** — the number says where the situation is in its cycle, from the [1](/library/numerology/number-1/) that begins to the 10 that turns the page.
4. **[Feminine and masculine energies](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/)** — is the card showing itself or hiding? Acting or thinking?
5. **[Astrological modes](/library/astrological-modes/)** — cardinal, fixed, mutable: to start, to hold, to change. The lens that says the tempo.

With the first three you already read half the deck. The last two refine. [Astrology](/library/astrology/) and [symbols](/library/tarot-symbols/) come after: extra depth if you want it, never a requirement.

## Your first reading, today {#first-reading}

Draw a card at random. Before looking for its name, say what you see: its dominant colour, its number, its house. Then say, in one sentence, what it's about. You've just read a card.

The rest — the sentence to say first, the silence rule, the notebook for counting — is in [How to read tarot cards for beginners](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/). That's the article to start with.

## What I've checked {#checked}

Three hundred strangers got a free reading from me, none of them saying a word about themselves first, and I kept count. The result — about nine times in ten by my count, misses included — and the way of counting are set out in [the method](/method/). You don't have to believe it: the method is there so you can test it.
