When someone tells me I have a gift, I say thank you. Then I hand it back, as gently as I can: you have it too. Everyone does. The only difference between us is that I stopped trying to be right.

## Why it's a kind thing to say {#kind}

"You have a gift" is said in surprise. Something landed that the person didn't expect, and "gift" is the nearest word to hand for that feeling. It's a generous thing to say, and I take it as it's meant.

So when I hand it back, it isn't because the person said something foolish. It's because the word is too small for what happened. **A gift is something one person has and another doesn't, and that isn't what happens in a reading.**

## What people mean by a gift {#what-it-means}

In tarot and all around it, a gift means an ability you're born with — to see, to know, to sense — that most people don't have. Say a reader mentions a sister you never spoke about. "Gift" is the word that explains it: she's built differently from you.

I don't think she is. We all receive impressions: images, sounds, words, smells, sensations, emotions. Some people are sharper in one channel than another — they see more readily, or hear, or feel — but nobody is missing the whole set.

Reading is closer to drawing than to a sixth sense. Some people have more talent, and anyone who practises can do it. When I've made friends and family try, every single one managed to read on the first, second or third try. If you're wondering whether you need a gift to start at all, I've answered that separately, [with a ten-minute test](/blog/do-you-need-psychic-abilities/).

## What I actually stopped doing {#trying-to-be-right}

I didn't acquire anything. I stopped something: trying to be right.

Trying to be right is what makes a reading go flat. Say you draw the Six of Cups, a card of childhood and taking time for yourself, and the word "sister" arrives. The part of you that wants to be right gets nervous. Too specific. What if the person in front of you doesn't have one? So you round it off into "family", which is safe, true of everyone, and useless.

The reader people call gifted is often the one who said "sister" anyway. That's the whole of it: trust what comes, don't take yourself too seriously, and let the messages arrive without checking them for safety first.

**Saying the odd detail means you'll sometimes be wrong.** About one reading in ten misses, by my count, and I say so when it does. If you're worried that what comes to you is invented, there's a way to find out: [how to tell whether you're making it up](/blog/am-i-making-it-up-tarot/).

My own first reading surprised me the same way. I'd expected to need training first; it's one of the things [I believed in 2020 and see differently now](/blog/what-i-believed-about-tarot/).

## Why I'd rather you didn't believe in my gift {#why-hand-it-back}

Because of what the word does next. If I have something you don't, my words start to weigh more than yours, about your own life. A reading is something to think with, not an instruction. You act on what you feel, not on what a special person told you; that's why I hold that [tarot is not an authority](/blog/tarot-is-not-an-authority/).

The word also makes a reader hard to leave. If the gift is hers alone, you need her every time a question comes up, and tarot should never turn into a circle of dependency, for you or for the person reading. I sell lessons, never readings. A gift couldn't be taught; this can.

And the word closes a door. If reading takes a gift, most of the people who'd enjoy it never try. There are no Chosen Ones at Maison Tarot; it's the premise of everything I teach.

If you've ever told a reader they have a gift, you weren't wrong to feel what you felt. Something real happened. I'd only say that what it came from isn't rare, and some of it was sitting on your side of the table too.

## How to find out you have it too {#try-it}

1. Find someone who'll tell you nothing about their question.
2. Draw one card.
3. Say what you see in plain words, odd details included, without trying to be right.
4. Let them tell you whether it held, and write it down either way.

The moment that made me want to teach is the one where someone does this, says something true, and is shocked by what they've just said. I can describe that moment, but it won't help much. You can't be told what it's like. You can only try.
