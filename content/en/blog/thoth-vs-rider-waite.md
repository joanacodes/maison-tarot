The Thoth tarot is the deck Aleister Crowley designed and Frieda Harris painted between 1938 and 1943; it was first printed as a pack in 1969, after both had died. Switching from the Rider-Waite-Smith changes three things: the minor cards lose their scenes, several majors and all the court cards change names, and astrology is printed on the cards. In my own notes, the Thoth sits under “to explore later”: very developed, powerful, with a strongly occult side. So this is a comparison from a Rider-Waite-Smith reader, not a Thoth teacher.

## What the Thoth tarot is {#what-it-is}

The Thoth is a 78-card tarot, like the Rider-Waite-Smith, built on the same skeleton: 22 major arcana, four suits, ten numbered cards and four court cards per suit. It's named after Thoth, the Egyptian god of writing and wisdom.

[Aleister Crowley](/library/tarot-figures/aleister-crowley/), an English occultist who had been through the Golden Dawn before founding his own system, Thelema, wrote the symbolism. [Frieda Harris](/library/tarot-figures/frieda-harris/), a painter, made the paintings, redoing many of them as the project grew. It was meant to take six months; it took about five years. Crowley's book on the deck, *The Book of Thoth*, came out in 1944 in a small edition; the cards themselves had to wait another quarter of a century.

Harris's original paintings are kept at the Warburg Institute in London, which showed them in 2025, for the first time in England since her death ([the Warburg's exhibition page](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/news-events/news/tarot-origins-afterlives-opening-soon-warburg-institute)).

## What changes in the major arcana {#major-arcana}

Eight of the 22 majors are renamed, and two of them change number.

| Rider-Waite-Smith | Thoth |
|---|---|
| I The Magician | I The Magus |
| II The High Priestess | II The Priestess |
| VIII Strength | XI Lust |
| X Wheel of Fortune | X Fortune |
| XI Justice | VIII Adjustment |
| XIV Temperance | XIV Art |
| XX Judgement | XX The Aeon |
| XXI The World | XXI The Universe |

Lust at XI and Adjustment at VIII is the older order, the one of the Tarot de Marseille; it's the Rider-Waite-Smith that had swapped them in 1909, following the Golden Dawn. The new names already carry an interpretation. “Adjustment” sounds less like a courtroom than “Justice”: balance being found, rather than a verdict handed down. “Art” for Temperance puts the accent on mixing and making.

## What changes in the minor arcana and court cards {#minors-and-courts}

Pentacles become Disks. Wands, Cups and Swords keep their names.

The numbered cards from Two to Ten have no scenes. Each is an arrangement of the suit's emblems on a coloured ground, with a one-word title and the sign of a planet in a zodiac sign. The titles say a lot: the Two of Cups is “Love”, the Three of Swords “Sorrow”, the Five of Cups “Disappointment”, the Seven of Swords “Futility”. The planet-in-sign symbols are the Golden Dawn's decans, which I explain in [the decans, the astrology hidden in the minor arcana](/blog/decans-tarot/).

The court cards become Knight, Queen, Prince and Princess. Many readers map them roughly onto the Rider-Waite-Smith this way: the Thoth Knight sits closest to the King, the Prince to the Knight, the Princess to the Page. The Queen stays the Queen.

## What it changes for you as a reader {#for-you}

First, the scene shortcut disappears. With a Rider-Waite-Smith Five of Cups, you read the bowed figure and the two cups still standing. With the Thoth, you read the number, the element, the colours and the title. **The lenses still work on the Thoth; the picture stops doing half the work for you.** Colour carries much more of the meaning, so [the colour lens](/library/tarot-colours/) becomes your first door rather than your third.

Second, the titles steer you. “Sorrow” tells you what to feel before you've looked. That's clear and quick, and it also leaves less room for your own first impression — which, in my way of reading, is where most of the useful material comes from.

Third, the imagery is dense with Crowley's own system: Thelema, astrology, alchemy and the Kabbalah's [Tree of Life](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/). For some readers that depth is the whole point, and the deck feels like a library. For others, it stands between them and the person in front of them. Both reactions are fair. Mine, for now, is to give it the time it asks for rather than pick it up in a hurry.

## Should you switch? {#should-you-switch}

You don't need to. One deck can last a whole reading life, and the [Rider-Waite-Smith](/blog/rider-waite-smith-tarot/) is a fine one to stay with. If you're curious, though, curiosity is a good reason.

A gentle way in: borrow or buy a Thoth deck and, for a week, lay one card next to its Rider-Waite-Smith twin each day. Describe both, out loud, through number, element and colour, before you open any book. Write down where they agree and where they pull apart. You can't be told what it's like. You can only try.

Then give the new deck its own first week, the way I describe in [how to get to know a new tarot deck](/blog/new-tarot-deck/): no ritual, a few cards a day, and a notebook.
