Yes, in my experience. Most of my readings for strangers were done online, often with nothing more than a username and a voice, and the ones that held didn’t need us to be in the same room. A screen changes the practical side of a reading. It doesn’t seem to change what the reading can see.

## Does tarot work over video call? {#short-answer}

When I counted my [300 blind readings](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/), most of them happened online: on Discord servers, in group calls, and during a live. Some were face to face. About nine in ten held, by my own count, and the readings behind that number were mostly done through a screen.

[My very first reading for a stranger](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/) was one of them: it happened on a Discord group, and the confirmation came days later, by text. Both reached me through a screen.

One honest limit: that nine in ten covers the online and the face-to-face readings together. It can’t tell you whether one kind held better than the other. What it does show is that a screen didn’t stop readings from holding.

## What changes when you read through a screen {#what-changes}

Less information reaches me, and that helps. In a room, a reader sees shoes, a wedding ring, a tired face, the way someone sits down. On a call I often get a voice and a first name, sometimes only a username. **The less I know before I start, the more a confirmation afterwards is worth.** That’s the whole reason for [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/).

Video gives back one thing a voice call doesn’t: a face. Faces nod, smile and sigh at the right moment, and that’s information too. If you read over video, it helps to ask the person, kindly, to keep a poker face and save their reactions for the end. Some can’t, and that’s fine; it’s worth noting next to the reading.

The person doesn’t touch the cards. People often ask whether that matters. I don’t think so. The cards aren’t where the reading comes from; they help me focus, or unfocus, so my own thoughts step aside. More and more, the first impressions come before I’ve shuffled; I go on laying out cards mainly so the person has the pictures in front of them. So over video, the useful thing is to hold each card up to the camera, so the person sees what you see.

What I can’t explain is why distance doesn’t seem to matter. I don’t know how tarot works at all, and I lay out [the four explanations people give](/blog/how-does-tarot-work/) without picking one I need.

## What can go wrong online {#what-goes-wrong}

Not the reading itself, usually. The setting.

- The connection drops in the middle of a sentence, and the thread goes with it. Say where you were when it comes back, and don’t start again from the top.
- Someone else is in the room, listening. A person who isn’t alone isn’t really open, and you get half the reading.
- Someone walks past the camera — a partner, a flatmate — and it’s tempting to read them too. They haven’t asked. I always ask permission before reading anyone, and a screen doesn’t change that.
- Screens make it tempting to do one reading after another. Out of caution, I sometimes end a session at the two-hour mark, and online is where that limit is easiest to forget.
- Recording. Ask before you record anything. A reading is personal, even a free one.

## Group calls and lives {#group-calls}

Some of my readings were done in front of others: in group calls, and during a live. Someone I read during a live later left me a review on Facebook. The format is still the same: they tell me nothing, I say what comes, they say whether it landed. That’s how my free online events work today.

At the far end of distance there’s the pick-a-card video, recorded by someone who has never met you. One of those made me sob, because it put words on my situation for the first time. I wrote about [whether pick-a-card readings work](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/), and where they fall short.

## How to get a good reading over video {#how-to}

1. Find a quiet room where you’re alone, with headphones if you can.
2. Keep your question and your circumstances to yourself until the end.
3. Ask the reader to show you every card, so you see what they see.
4. Keep a straight face if you can; react at the end.
5. Write down what was said. Confirmations sometimes come days later.

My one-to-one [sessions](/sessions/) are by video, sixty minutes. They’re lessons rather than readings: you read the card first, I read after you, and we compare. If you want to see what a screen does to a reading, that’s a good way to find out from both sides.

And if you’re learning and need people to practise on, online is where I found mine, through a Discord group, Facebook groups and a pen-pal app. I’ve written up [where to find people to read for](/blog/where-to-find-people-to-read-for/), people who will happily tell you nothing.
