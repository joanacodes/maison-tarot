A tarot certificate shows that someone followed a course, and sometimes that they practised for a set number of hours. A lineage shows who taught them. Neither can show whether a reader is accurate or ethical; those you check another way, and it doesn’t take long.

## What a tarot certificate can show {#what-it-shows}

A certificate is a document a school or a teacher issues to say you completed their course. Some courses issue one after a weekend, others after a year of supervised practice, and both are called certificates. What it’s worth depends on what it states.

Depending on the course, it can show that someone attended a number of sessions, practised a number of readings with a teacher watching, or passed the school’s own test. It also shows something less official: that a person gave time and money to learning, and stuck with it to the end.

That counts for something. Say a certificate reads “40 hours, 30 supervised readings”. It tells you this person has sat across from other people before, with someone checking. That’s more than many readers can show.

## What a certificate can’t show {#what-it-cant}

It can’t show accuracy. No certificate counts the misses after the course ends. The only record of that is a notebook: on one side what the reader said, on the other what the person confirmed. That’s why I keep [a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/), and why I’d ask any reader whether they keep one.

It can’t show ethics either. A certificate won’t tell you whether a reader asks before reading someone, steps back from health or court cases, or will one day “discover” a curse and offer to lift it for a fee. For that, the [nine red flags of a fake reader](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/) are more useful than any frame on a wall.

And it can’t show whether someone still practises. A certificate from ten years ago describes the person who sat that course, not the person reading for you today.

**A certificate records a course; it can’t record what happens at the table afterwards.**

## Is there an official tarot qualification in France? {#france}

No. In France, tarot reading isn’t a regulated profession: there’s no state diploma for it and no licence to obtain. Fortune-telling has been a free activity there since the old penal code was replaced in 1994, and the general rules on fraud and consumer protection apply to it like to any trade. I go through the details in [is tarot reading legal?](/blog/is-tarot-reading-legal/)

So a certificate from a French school is the school’s own document. That doesn’t make it worthless. It makes it a private document, to be read for exactly what it says: how many hours, what was practised, who signed it.

## What a lineage means, and what it carries {#lineage}

A lineage is a chain of teachers: I learned from this person, who learned from that one, going back to a founder or a tradition. In some traditions it matters a great deal, because the teaching is passed on in person and the chain is how it stays recognisable.

Tarot has its own family trees. Many of the correspondences readers use today, between cards, planets and letters, go back to [the Golden Dawn and its tarot](/library/tarot-history/golden-dawn-tarot/). A reader who says they trained in a particular school is telling you which vocabulary and which way of working they inherited.

That’s what a lineage carries: a language, a set of correspondences, a method, and often a sense of belonging and gratitude that has value of its own. What it can’t carry is the reader’s own track record. A lineage is a family tree, not a notebook.

Sometimes a lineage is offered as a guarantee: my teacher was taught by someone who was taught by a famous name. It can be a true and lovely story. It still answers a different question from yours, which is whether this reader will be careful with you.

## Five questions to ask a teacher instead {#questions}

1. How do you check your readings? Do you count your misses, and what’s your rate?
2. What don’t you read on, and why?
3. What will I be able to do without you at the end of the course?
4. Can I watch you read for a stranger who tells you nothing?
5. If there’s a certificate, what exactly does it state — hours, readings, a test?

The first question matters most. A miss is a miss, and a teacher who can say so calmly, with numbers, is showing you something no certificate can.
