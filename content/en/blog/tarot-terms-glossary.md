Tarot has its own vocabulary, and most of it is plainer than it sounds. Here are 25 terms you'll meet in books, videos and readings, each defined in a sentence and shown with an example. Where I use a word differently, or not at all, I say so after the definition.

## The deck: cards and suits {#the-deck}

1. **Tarot deck** — a set of 78 cards: 22 major arcana and 56 minor arcana. Example: the Rider-Waite-Smith follows exactly that structure, like most tarot decks sold today.
2. **Arcana** — from the Latin *arcanum*, "a secret"; in tarot, the word for the cards themselves. Example: "the major arcana" are the 22 big cards; one of them is "an arcanum".
3. **Major arcana** — the 22 named cards, numbered 0 to 21, from the Fool to the World. They speak about the big chapters of a life. Example: Death is major arcanum number 13.
4. **Minor arcana** — the other 56 cards, in four suits of fourteen. They speak about the days inside those chapters. Example: the Three of Cups is a minor. [The difference between the two groups](/blog/major-vs-minor-arcana/) has its own page.
5. **Suit** — one of the four families of the minor arcana, each tied to an element: Wands (Fire), Cups (Water), Swords (Air), Pentacles (Earth). Example: a spread full of Cups is mostly about feelings and relationships.
6. **Pip cards** — the numbered minors, Ace to Ten, forty in all. Example: the Five of Pentacles is a pip card.
7. **Court cards** — the sixteen "people" cards: Page, Knight, Queen and King of each suit. Example: the King of Cups, often read as a person, or as a way of behaving.
8. **Rider-Waite-Smith** — the deck published in London in 1909, conceived by Arthur Edward Waite and drawn by Pamela Colman Smith, in which every card has a scene, pips included. Example: every card shown on this site comes from it.
9. **Tarot de Marseille** — the older French pattern, whose pips show the suit symbols arranged on the card, without a scene. Example: its Three of Cups shows three cups, not three women raising them.
10. **Oracle deck** — a deck with no fixed structure: any number of cards, with meanings set by its author. Example: an oracle might hold 44 cards with a word printed on each. It isn't a tarot, and [here's how the two differ](/blog/tarot-vs-oracle-cards/).

## The reading: spreads and draws {#the-reading}

11. **Reading** — the whole act of drawing cards and interpreting them, for someone else or for yourself. Example: "she gave me a reading" covers everything from the shuffle to the last sentence.
12. **Spread** — a layout: how many cards, where they go, what each place stands for. Example: a three-card spread, or the ten-card Celtic Cross.
13. **Position** — one place in a spread, with its own meaning. Example: in a past–present–future spread, the first card sits in the "past" position.
14. **Pull** (or **draw**) — to take a card from the deck; also the card itself. Example: "my pull today was the Star". A daily pull is [the one-card practice](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/) I recommend to every beginner.
15. **Shuffle** — to mix the cards before drawing. There's no wrong way. Example: riffle them like a card player, or spread them face down on the table and stir.
16. **Cut** — to split the deck into piles and restack them before drawing, sometimes done by the person being read. Example: three piles, put back in a different order.
17. **Reversed card** — a card that comes out upside down. Some readers give it its own meaning, others turn it back. Example: an upside-down Sun. On this site, a reversed card is read as the same card, slowed down or in excess.
18. **Significator** — a card chosen to represent the person before the reading starts, often a court card. Example: a Queen of Wands set in the middle for someone fiery. I read without one, and [here's why](/blog/significator-card/).
19. **Clarifier** — an extra card drawn to explain one that confuses you. Example: a puzzling Moon followed by a "clarifying" Two of Swords. I don't use them. If a reading doesn't land, a miss is a miss, and a fourth card only muddies it.
20. **Jumper** — a card that falls out of the deck while you shuffle. Some readers treat it as a message; others put it back. Example: the Ace of Cups slipping to the floor mid-shuffle. Neither choice is a rule.

## The way of reading {#the-practice}

21. **Lens** — the word I use for one way of looking at any card: its colour, its element, its number, its feminine or masculine energy, its astrological mode. Example: through the number lens, every Five is a moment of conflict or trial, whatever the suit.
22. **Element** — Fire, Water, Air or Earth, the four qualities tied to the suits and to many major arcana. Example: the Fool is an Air card, light and headed somewhere new.
23. **Intuitive reading** — reading from the impressions a card gives you rather than from a memorised meaning. Example: looking at the Eight of Swords and saying "someone waiting for a permission they don't need". [How to do it, and check it](/blog/read-tarot-intuitively/).
24. **Blind reading** — a reading where the person tells you nothing first: no question, no context. Example: at my events, people are drawn at random and say nothing until the end. It's [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/), and it's how I read.
25. **Cold reading** — gathering clues from someone's appearance, reactions and answers, then handing them back as insight. Example: "I sense a loss around you", said to almost anyone, then adjusted to their face. A blind reading is how a reader keeps it out.

## Where to go from here {#next}

You don't need all 25 to start. Three are enough for a first evening: pull, element, lens. Draw one card, name its element, look at it through one lens, and say what you see.

The rest will come as you read. When you want the lenses laid out in order, they're on [the method page](/method/).
