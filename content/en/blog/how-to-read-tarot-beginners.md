The most important thing to know about reading tarot is that you do **not** need a PhD in occultism to understand how it works. You don't need a gift either, because there isn't one — or rather, everyone has the same one. What you need is to let go, and I can give you the exact sentence that does it.

## The one tip that works almost every time {#the-tip}

Before you read, tell yourself: *I'm about to talk nonsense.*

It's funny, and I'm not joking. This one sentence lets you stop thinking and stop making up stories. You will say all kinds of things, you won't really understand why you're saying them, but you're just saying them. Your ego and your ideas about how life works go to the back of the room, where they belong.

Do you know when the magic happens? When the person in front of you says: *"What? How did you know that?"*

And sometimes they'll be doubtful during the reading — which, in my opinion, is **the best state a person can be in** while they're read. A few days later they write: *"How did you know?"* That's the moment. Not the reading; the message three days later.

## What happened the first time I tried it {#first-time}

I wasn't afraid of being ridiculous. I was curious and I went for it.

It was in a Discord group I'd found on Tumblr. I asked who wanted a reading and read for a stranger. It came out as something about a relationship, and at the end she told me it didn't resonate: she wasn't looking for a relationship at all. Fine. I'd said what came, and I'll admit I'm more at ease with careers and self-development than with love — but you don't pick what comes, you say it.

Three or four days later she texts me, a little shocked. Everything I'd said had happened. She didn't understand it, because again, she hadn't been looking for anything. And the other part — the one the cards had shown and neither of us had liked — was that she hadn't been especially kind to the other person.

I still find that funny. It also taught me the rule I've kept since: **don't try to say something right. Say what comes up.**

## What you actually need {#what-you-need}

Four things, none of them magic.

1. **A Rider-Waite-Smith deck.** The 1909 one, drawn by Pamela Colman Smith. Every card has a scene, and every scene is readable without a book. Other decks come later, if ever. ([Which deck, and why it matters less than you think →](/library/tarot-cards/))
2. **One lens.** Not seventy-eight meanings — one way of looking. The fastest are the [colours](/library/tarot-colours/): yellow says joy before you know which card you're holding. Then the [number](/library/numerology/), then the [element](/library/four-elements/). Hold those three and you already read half the deck without having learned it.
3. **Someone who tells you nothing.** No question, no context. It's the only way to find out, afterwards, whether you were reading them or reading what they'd told you.
4. **A notebook.** Write down what you said and what they confirmed. Count. I did it three hundred times; the numbers are on the [method page](/method/). Counting is what turns "I think this works" into "I know when it works".

## Your first reading, step by step {#first-reading}

1. Shuffle. Any way you like; there is no wrong way and no ritual to learn.
2. Draw one card. One. Not a ten-card spread you found online.
3. Say the sentence. *I'm about to talk nonsense.*
4. Look before you think. What colour is everywhere? Is the figure showing itself or hiding? What number is at the top, and what does that number do in a cycle?
5. Say it out loud, in plain words, to the person. Not "the Three of Cups means celebration" — "three people, cups raised, a lot of yellow: something got finished and it's being celebrated. Does that land?"
6. Let them answer. If it doesn't land, say so, and don't rescue the reading. Write it down as a miss.

That's it. The first time is strange, and the strangeness is the point. What you feel while you're doing it can't be described; it has to be experienced. It's why every [workshop](/workshops/) at the House starts with a card in the first ten minutes and the explanation after.

## What not to do {#what-not-to-do}

- **Don't memorise the 78.** You'll end up reciting instead of reading, and you'll freeze on the first card you've forgotten. The lenses exist so you never need to.
- **Don't cleanse your deck with sage.** Nothing is wrong with your deck. If something feels off, it's you who needs a break, not the cards. ([Take a break from your cards →](/disclaimers/#readings))
- **Don't read for someone in distress**, and don't read about health, death, pregnancy, court cases or money. Not because a card can't fall, but because a wrong word there does damage.
- **Don't read for yourself twenty times a day.** If you catch yourself pulling cards every hour, that isn't about tarot; it's a search for outside validation, and a reading is the wrong tool for it.

## What happens when it works — and when it doesn't {#what-happens}

When it works, the person's face changes before they've decided what to say. That look is worth more than any confirmation.

When it doesn't — and by my count that's about one time in ten — you say "that one's a miss", you note it, and you move on. You don't reshuffle, you don't reframe, you don't turn a miss into "the cards mean something you haven't realised yet". A miss is a miss. Readers who can't say that word are the ones to avoid.

## The short version {#short-version}

One deck. One lens. Someone who tells you nothing. A notebook. And, before every card, the sentence that keeps you honest: *I'm about to talk nonsense.* You'll find out soon enough whether you were.
