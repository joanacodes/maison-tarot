Usually, not straight away. A reading done in the middle of a storm reads the storm: you see your fear in every card, and you keep drawing until one agrees with it. Give it a night, and come back to the deck when you can look at a picture without needing it to rescue you.

## Why reading while upset goes wrong {#why-it-goes-wrong}

When you're upset, your mind is already telling a story, and telling it fast. The cards don't interrupt that story. They feed it.

Take the Five of Cups. A figure in a black cloak looks down at three spilled cups. Behind them, two cups are still standing, and a bridge leads away in the distance. In my notes it's the heaviest card in the deck: loss, dark emotion, being alone with your problems. Draw it on a calm day and you notice the two cups and the bridge as well. Draw it while you're upset and you see three spilled cups, full stop.

The Nine of Swords works the same way. Someone sits up in bed with their face in their hands, nine swords lined up in the dark above them. My notes read it as coming out of a nightmare, the end of a long troubled stretch, with protection above you. Upset, you only see the nightmare.

**Cards aren't magic, you are — which is exactly why they read differently when you're shaken.** The picture is the same as yesterday. The person looking at it isn't.

## Upset, or in a state? {#upset-or-in-a-state}

There's a difference, and it matters.

Upset is sad, hurt, rattled after a hard conversation. You can still sit still. A card later that evening might help you name what you feel.

In a state is something else. You're pulling card after card, reshuffling, asking the same question in new words. I see the same thing in some people who come to me for a reading, a high dose of doubt more than a question, and I usually suggest we don't read at all. Nobody has done anything wrong; another card won't settle the doubt, and asking [the same question twice](/blog/ask-tarot-the-same-question-twice/) only makes it louder.

If you recognise the second picture in yourself, the kindest move is to put the deck in a drawer for the evening. There's more on [why stepping away from the cards helps](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), and it isn't a punishment.

## What to do first {#what-to-do-first}

Before any card, in this order:

1. Breathe, slowly, for a couple of minutes. It sounds too small to matter. It isn't.
2. Write down what happened, in plain sentences, with no cards: what was said, what you felt, what you're afraid of.
3. Talk to a person — a friend, a sister, someone who knows you. A deck can't put a hand on your shoulder.
4. Sleep on it. Most of what feels urgent at midnight looks different over breakfast.

## If you still want to pull a card {#one-card}

Then pull one. One card, and a different question.

The question keeping you awake — "will he come back?", "is it over?" — is the one to leave aside tonight. It asks the cards for a verdict, and you're in no state to hear one. A gentler question: what do I need to look after right now?

Draw, and describe the card out loud before you interpret anything. What colour fills it? Is the figure facing you or turned away? What's in their hands? In [a one-card pull](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/), describing comes first and meaning comes after. Write it down, and reread your note in two days. Often the card was kinder than you were that night.

## Reading for someone else when you're upset {#reading-for-others}

The same answer, with one more reason. Reading for someone means feeling what they feel while keeping your own feelings. When you're already full of yours, there's no room left: your mood leaks into what you say, and the person ends up hearing your evening rather than their cards.

Postpone. "I'm not in the right place to read for you tonight" is a complete sentence. Even on good days, I sometimes stop after two hours of readings, out of caution. On a bad day, I'd rather not start.

## When it's more than upset {#more-than-upset}

If the upset has lasted weeks, if it doesn't lift with sleep or with talking, it isn't a tarot question any more. It's a door to someone trained: a doctor, a therapist, a helpline. I've written about [where that door is and why it isn't mine](/blog/tarot-and-mental-health/), and [the disclaimers page](/disclaimers/#therapy) says the same thing in fewer words.

For the ordinary hard days, the deck will still be there tomorrow. If you'd like to learn to read it calmly, [start with the learn path](/learn-tarot/), one card at a time.
