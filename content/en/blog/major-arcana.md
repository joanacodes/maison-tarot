The 22 major arcana are the big scenes of the deck: the Fool setting off, the Tower falling, the World dancing at the end. You don’t need to learn 22 meanings by heart. Each card already carries a number, an element, a sign or planet and an energy, and with those four you can read it before you’ve opened a book — below, every major gets one paragraph and a link to its full page.

## How to read a major arcana card without memorising it {#how-to-read}

I read every major through the same lenses I use for the rest of the deck. The order matters: the first two do most of the work.

1. The number. Cards 0 to 10 sit directly on the [cycle of numbers](/library/numerology/), from the start (1) to the corridor between two rooms (10). From 11 to 21, add the digits: 13 becomes 1 + 3 = 4, 18 becomes 9. The card then plays its number on a bigger stage.
2. The element. Each major has Fire, Water, Air or Earth — the same [four elements](/library/four-elements/) as the minor suits. Fire acts, Water feels, Air thinks and decides, Earth builds and keeps.
3. The astrology. At the end of the nineteenth century the Golden Dawn gave each major a sign, a planet or an element; Uranus, Neptune and Pluto were added later by modern astrologers. [Astrology](/library/astrology/) is an extra lens: keep it if it speaks to you.
4. The energy. [Feminine and masculine](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/feminine-masculine/) here aren’t about women and men. Feminine holds, waits, imagines, works behind the scenes; masculine acts, shows itself, builds, decides fast. We all carry both.

Then look at the drawing. Pamela Colman Smith gave every card a scene in 1909, and the scene is readable without a book. **Every card has a plus and a minus, and the lenses show you both.**

These correspondences are a system people built over time, not facts carved into the cards. They’re handles. Use the ones that help you see.

## From 0 to 6: the Fool to the Lovers {#the-fool-to-the-lovers}

The first seven cards lay out the tools: a beginning, a skill, an inner knowing, a mother, a father, a teacher, a choice.

{{< cards "ar00 ar01 ar02 ar03 ar04 ar05 ar06" >}}

### 0 · The Fool {#the-fool}

*Zero · Air · Uranus · masculine*

A young man walks towards a cliff edge, nose in the air, a bundle on a stick over his shoulder and a white rose in his hand, while a small white dog jumps at his heels. Zero is the before: the breath before the first word. The bundle says he can set up anywhere, free in his decisions. The minus is naivety, and the dog is the one trying to remind him. [The Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/) is a departure nothing justifies yet — a lovely card when he knows he’s at the edge.

### 1 · The Magician {#the-magician}

*One · Air · Mercury · masculine*

On his table lie a cup, a sword, a pentacle and a wand: the four elements, the four suits, everything he needs. He raises one hand to the sky and points the other at the ground, an infinity sign over his head. [The Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/) knows how to do everything and stays anchored while he does it; his wand receives and sends. One is the initiator, the raw diamond at the start of a cycle. The minus: he can pretend, and a person trusted blindly can turn out to be a liar.

### 2 · The High Priestess {#the-high-priestess}

*Two · Water · the Moon · feminine*

She sits between a black pillar and a white one, a veil of pomegranates behind her, a crescent moon at her feet and a half-hidden scroll in her lap. Her blue robe falls like a waterfall: this is a Water card, and the Moon pulls water. [The High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/) knows a lot and says little. Two is the bond and the pair — here, between what is shown and what is kept. Her minus is still water: a façade, the hidden face of the moon.

### 3 · The Empress {#the-empress}

*Three · Earth · Venus · feminine*

She sits on cushions in a field of ripe wheat, trees and a waterfall behind her, a crown of twelve stars on her head and a heart-shaped shield marked with the sign of Venus at her side. [The Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/) is the mother and the earth: she feeds, gives, makes things grow. Three is the machine running well, the project that works. Her minus is the other side of care: a protection that smothers, or a care that keeps someone dependent.

### 4 · The Emperor {#the-emperor}

*Four · Fire · Aries · masculine*

An old king sits on a stone throne carved with rams’ heads, barren mountains behind him. Look at his legs: under the red robe he wears armour, ready for war. [The Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/) is the father and the structure — he gives orders, he doesn’t receive them. Four is stability, the fruit of work, the house standing. His minus is Fire’s: when he loses control, he can destroy what he built, and fast.

### 5 · The Hierophant {#the-hierophant}

*Five · Earth · Taurus · masculine*

A religious figure sits between two grey pillars, a triple crown on his head and two fingers raised; two crossed keys lie at his feet and two tonsured men kneel before him. [The Hierophant](/library/tarot-cards/the-hierophant/) is the institution: school, tradition, the teacher, the rule handed down. Five is the trial, and here it’s the trial of the rule — do you follow it, question it, outgrow it? The minus is a rule kept for its own sake, and a teacher who can’t bear a question.

### 6 · The Lovers {#the-lovers}

*Six · Air · Gemini · feminine*

A naked man and woman stand under a great angel in the clouds; behind her, a tree with fruit and a snake; behind him, a tree of flames. He looks at her; she looks up at the angel. [The Lovers](/library/tarot-cards/the-lovers/) is a choice more than a romance: Air and Gemini, the card of talking and choosing. Six is harmony, success, the machine running fast. Its minus is the choice put off, or a harmony that only lasts while nobody decides anything.

## From 7 to 13: the Chariot to Death {#the-chariot-to-death}

The middle of the deck tests what the first seven built: will, courage, solitude, luck, fairness, waiting, endings.

{{< cards "ar07 ar08 ar09 ar10 ar11 ar12 ar13" >}}

### 7 · The Chariot {#the-chariot}

*Seven · Water · Cancer · masculine*

A crowned warrior stands in a stone chariot under a canopy of stars, a wand in his hand, two sphinxes — one black, one white — lying in front. There are no reins, and nothing is moving yet. In my notes [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/) is a warrior with both a feminine and a masculine spirit, who receives his orders: action called from above. Seven fits — reflection and analysis before action. Water and Cancer give the armour a soft inside. The minus is charging before the order has come.

### 8 · Strength {#strength}

*Eight · Fire · Leo · feminine*

A woman in white, flowers in her hair and around her waist, closes a lion’s jaws with her bare hands, gently; over her head floats the same infinity sign as the Magician’s. [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/) is courage that doesn’t shout: Fire and Leo, held by a feminine energy. Eight is mastery and control — and an 8 lying down is the infinity sign. The minus is control turned inwards, holding back what should roar. In the Marseille deck Strength is 11 and Justice 8; Waite swapped them.

### 9 · The Hermit {#the-hermit}

*Nine · Earth · Virgo · feminine*

An old man in a grey cloak stands on a snowy summit, head bowed, holding up a lantern with a six-pointed star inside. [The Hermit](/library/tarot-cards/the-hermit/) has climbed alone to see clearly; Virgo is the sign that analyses, the counsellor at the king’s side. Nine is the finish line of a stage: introspection, patience, last efforts — but keep moving. His minus is a retreat that lasts too long, a lantern held up for nobody.

### 10 · Wheel of Fortune {#wheel-of-fortune}

*Ten · Fire · Jupiter · masculine*

A great wheel turns in the sky, marked with letters and symbols; a sphinx with a sword sits on top, a snake slides down one side, a red jackal-headed figure rises on the other, and four winged creatures read in the corners. In my notes [the Wheel of Fortune](/library/tarot-cards/wheel-of-fortune/) says one thing: it’s up to you to turn it — create your own luck. Ten is the corridor between two rooms, a transition. The minus is waiting for the wheel to turn by itself.

### 11 · Justice {#justice}

*11 → 1 + 1 = 2 · Air · Libra · feminine*

A figure in red sits between two pillars, a sword held upright in one hand and scales in the other, a purple veil behind. Eleven reduces to two: the contract, the signature, weighing pros and cons. [Justice](/library/tarot-cards/justice/) is Air for the decision and Libra for the balance. It asks you to read the contract — the one on paper, or the one you made with yourself. Its minus is fairness without mercy, or a weighing that goes on so long nothing gets decided.

### 12 · The Hanged Man {#the-hanged-man}

*12 → 1 + 2 = 3 · Water · Neptune · feminine*

A man hangs upside down by one foot from a living tree, his other leg folded behind his knee, hands behind his back, a halo of light round a calm face. [The Hanged Man](/library/tarot-cards/the-hanged-man/) isn’t being punished; he’s looking at the world from another angle. Three is the rhythm of a project that works — here, the rhythm pauses so it can start again. Water and Neptune say let go. The minus is a pause that turns into a sacrifice nobody asked for.

### 13 · Death {#death}

*13 → 1 + 3 = 4 · Water · Scorpio · feminine*

A skeleton in black armour rides a white horse, carrying a black banner with a white rose; a king lies fallen, a bishop faces him, a child looks up, and on the horizon a sun shines between two towers. [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/) isn’t about dying. In my notes it’s the harvest and starting again — someone who wants to make their life fertile again. Thirteen gives four: a structure is cleared so another can be built. The minus is clinging to what is already over.

## From 14 to 21: Temperance to the World {#temperance-to-the-world}

The last eight cards go from the deepest shadow to the widest light, and end where everything comes together.

{{< cards "ar14 ar15 ar16 ar17 ar18 ar19 ar20 ar21" >}}

### 14 · Temperance {#temperance}

*14 → 1 + 4 = 5 · Fire · Sagittarius · feminine*

A winged angel in white, one foot on land and one in the water, pours liquid from one cup into another; a path leads off to the mountains. White, in my colour grid, is peace. [Temperance](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/) is the trial of five handled by dosage: mixing what you have, making do, finding the right measure. Fire and Sagittarius give the mixture a direction. The minus is a moderation so careful that nothing ever gets poured.

### 15 · The Devil {#the-devil}

*15 → 1 + 5 = 6 · Earth · Capricorn · masculine*

A horned figure with bat wings crouches on a black block; a naked man and woman stand chained to it, with small horns and tails of their own. Look at the chains: they’re loose enough to lift off. Fifteen gives six, the number of the Lovers, and the drawing echoes theirs. [The Devil](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/) is a bond turned into attachment — to a habit, money, a person, even the cards. Its plus is that it shows you the chain, so you can take it off.

### 16 · The Tower {#the-tower}

*16 → 1 + 6 = 7 · Fire · Mars · masculine*

Lightning strikes a tower on a rock and knocks off its crown-shaped top; flames burst from the windows and two people fall. [The Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/) is the sudden change: what was built on a crack comes down. Fire and Mars give the speed. Sixteen gives seven, and seven’s advice is the one to hold on to here: everything is temporary, and your situation will change. Its plus is the clearing — you finally see what was wrong with the building.

### 17 · The Star {#the-star}

*17 → 1 + 7 = 8 · Air · Aquarius · feminine*

A naked woman kneels by a pool, one foot on the water and one knee on the land, pouring from two jugs — one into the pool, one onto the earth — under a great star and seven smaller ones. [The Star](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/) comes after the Tower: hope, repair, breathing again. Eight is a gate between the tangible and the intangible, and she pours into both. The minus is hope without action, waiting under the star instead of walking.

### 18 · The Moon {#the-moon}

*18 → 1 + 8 = 9 · Water · Pisces · feminine*

A moon with a face shines between two towers; a dog and a wolf howl at it, a crayfish crawls out of a pool, and a path winds off into the hills. [The Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/) is uncertainty: what you sense without seeing, dreams, fears, what moves under the surface. Nine is the end of a stage, and its advice is patience — but keep moving, and the path is right there. The minus is getting lost in the fog and taking a fear for a fact.

### 19 · The Sun {#the-sun}

*19 → 1 + 9 = 10 → 1 · Fire · the Sun · masculine*

A child rides a white horse bareback, a red banner in hand, under a big sun with a face; sunflowers lean over a grey wall behind. [The Sun](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/) is joy, clarity, success you can see. Nineteen gives ten, then one: a cycle closes and the next opens in full light. Its minus is Fire’s: too much sun burns — overexposure, or a success that forgets everyone else.

### 20 · Judgement {#judgement}

*20 → 2 + 0 = 2 · Fire · Pluto · masculine*

An angel blows a trumpet hung with a flag bearing a cross; below, a man, a woman and a child rise from coffins floating on water, arms open. [Judgement](/library/tarot-cards/judgement/) is a call, a review, a second chance — and in my notes it’s neither good nor bad. Twenty gives two: a new agreement with your own past. The minus is a judgement that stays a verdict, usually on yourself, instead of becoming a step.

### 21 · The World {#the-world}

*21 → 2 + 1 = 3 · Earth · Saturn · feminine*

A figure wrapped in a purple sash dances inside an oval wreath, a wand in each hand, while the four creatures of the Wheel watch from the corners. [The World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/) is completion — in my notes, action led by the whole: two wands, both hands working. Twenty-one gives three, the machine running well, and three’s advice suits the last card: finish one project before you start another. The minus is a cycle you won’t let close.

## Reading the major arcana in a spread {#in-a-spread}

The majors carry the big themes; the 56 [minor arcana](/blog/minor-arcana/) carry the everyday detail — this week, this conversation, this email. **A major in a spread names the theme; the minors around it show how it plays out.**

Several majors in one spread usually mean a period that matters, something larger than the day-to-day. None at all isn’t a bad sign: it often means the situation is in your hands, made of ordinary choices — I explain how I read that in [no major arcana in your spread](/blog/no-major-arcana-in-spread/). Some readers work with the 22 alone; it’s a good way to start, and I go through what you gain and lose in [reading with only the major arcana](/blog/major-arcana-only/).

Reversed, a major is the same card slowed down, blocked or in excess: the Hermit who can’t come down from the mountain, the Sun that burns. And the cards that frighten people most — Death, the Tower, the Devil — are gentler than their drawings, which is why I gave them [a page of their own](/blog/scariest-tarot-cards/).

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Take the 22 out of your deck and draw one a day. Before you read its paragraph here, say its four lenses out loud — number, element, sign, energy — and one thing you see in the drawing. Then check. Don’t take my words as ultimate truth: test them and experience them for yourself.

When you’re ready to do the same with the whole deck, the [learn tarot path](/learn-tarot/) puts the lenses in order, one at a time.
