There's a genre of tarot content that consists of experienced readers being disappointed by TikTok. I've written some of it in my head. Then I remember that [a recorded pick-a-card video is the reason I read at all](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/), and I put the sneer away.

## What the criticism gets right {#criticism-right}

A lot of the format is bad, and bad in predictable ways.

**Every pile is special.** "Whoever chose this pile, you are so loved, someone is thinking of you right now." Four piles, four versions of the same flattery. That's not reading; it's a lottery where everyone wins the same prize.

**It sells.** The video ends with "for your personal reading, link in bio", or with an extended version behind a paywall, or — in the French market — with a phone number at twelve euros a question. The free reading was the top of a funnel, and [funnels shape readings](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/).

**It promises people.** "He's coming back." "They regret it." "Your person is manifesting you." Specific outcomes about a specific human who isn't in the room. [I refuse these questions](/disclaimers/#refusals) one-to-one; at scale, to thousands, they're worse.

**It never misses.** Fluent, confident, every card a positive omen. A reader who's never wrong isn't reading.

## What it gets wrong {#criticism-wrong}

The critics usually stop there, and in stopping they miss two things.

**The format reaches people.** Someone alone at midnight, in a bedroom, who has never opened a tarot book and never will, watches a video and feels seen for the first time. That's not nothing. It's the opposite of nothing. It's the thing every reader claims to want and most never achieve, and it happened to me through a phone screen.

**The format is honest about what it is**, when it's done well. "Pick a pile, and if this isn't you, skip it." That's a reader admitting, up front, that three-quarters of the audience will get nothing — which is more honesty about hit rates than most one-to-one readers manage in a career.

## The three habits that separate the two {#three-habits}

If you make this content, or you're deciding whose to watch:

1. **Commit to something specific per pile**, and accept that most viewers won't relate. "You turned down something good because you didn't feel ready" beats "abundance is coming" every time, and it costs you views.
2. **Sell nothing at the end.** Or if you must earn — everyone must — sell a lesson, a deck review, a workshop. Never "a personal reading to see what this means for you". That sentence is the hook.
3. **Say the miss.** "This pile is a bit muddy, I'm not sure it's saying much." Viewers trust the reader who says it far more than the one who never does.

## Judge the reader {#judge-the-reader}

The medium is a mirror on a stick, held up to a crowd. A good reader angles it carefully and tells you when the reflection's off. A bad one paints a smile on the glass. Neither is TikTok's fault.

If you want to see the honest version of a public reading, that's the [free event](/events/) format at the House — drawn at random, told nothing, misses said out loud, in front of everyone. It's pick-a-card with the funnel removed. It turns out that's most of what needed removing.
