"Delulu is the solulu" — being delusional is the solution — started as a joke about celebrity crushes and became, within a year, an actual life philosophy for a lot of people. It's the law of attraction with the sincerity removed and the algorithm added. I want to take it seriously, because underneath the joke there's a real idea and a real harm.

## The real idea {#real-idea}

Your expectations shape your behaviour. Someone who believes they'll get the job prepares differently, walks in differently, recovers from the first rejection differently. Confidence is partly self-fulfilling. That's true, it's well documented, and it's the kernel of everything from "manifestation" to sports psychology.

Tarot can serve it, honestly. A card can show you where you're standing in relation to a goal — the [Three of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-pentacles/) building with others, the [Seven of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-pentacles/) waiting for the harvest, the [Eight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-swords/) tied up by nothing. Seeing that clearly changes what you do next. That's the whole legitimate use.

## Where it turns {#where-it-turns}

The turn happens in two steps.

First: from "belief helps" to "belief is enough". Manifestation content skips the part where you prepare, apply, ask, work, and gets straight to *vibrating at the frequency of your desire*. The vision board replaces the application. The cards get pulled to "confirm it's coming" rather than to see where you are.

Second, and this is the cruel one: from "belief is enough" to "if it didn't happen, you didn't believe enough". That's the law of attraction's dark side, and it's structural, not accidental. If thoughts create reality, then the person whose diagnosis came back bad, whose business failed, whose partner left, *attracted* it. I've heard this said to grieving people by well-meaning friends. It's [spiritual bypassing](/blog/spiritual-bypassing-tarot/) with a smile, and it blames the victim by definition.

## Where tarot gets dragged in {#tarot-dragged-in}

"Manifestation spreads." "Pull a card to align with your desire." "Ask the deck to confirm it's already yours." This is tarot used as a mood board — the cards chosen or reread until they say yes. It's the opposite of reading. A reading is told nothing and allowed to miss; a manifestation pull is told everything and required to agree.

The tell is what happens when the card is the Tower. A reader says "something you've built is about to go, look at what". A manifester says "the Tower means clearing space for what's coming" — and it might, but notice that no card was allowed to say no.

## What the cards can do for a goal {#what-cards-can-do}

Three honest things.

**Show you where you actually are.** Not where you're telling yourself you are. Draw one card, told nothing, and describe it plainly before you interpret it.

**Show you what you're avoiding.** The goal you say you want and the thing you keep not doing about it are often two cards apart on the table.

**Give you a picture to work under.** If the Chariot helps you go to the gym, use the Chariot. That's not manifestation; that's a poster. Posters are fine.

## What they can't do {#what-they-cant}

Make anything happen. Confirm that it's coming. Tell you the universe agrees. And — the one that matters — tell you it's your fault when it doesn't.

If you're in the delulu phase about something, the honest reading is a friend: it'll show you the gap between the story and the table, and it'll do it before the gap costs you a year. If you'd rather keep the story, that's allowed; just don't pull cards for it. They'll only say yes if you make them.
