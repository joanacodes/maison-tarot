Many readers believe the phase of the moon colours a reading. I can’t tell you whether it does: I never noted the moon beside my readings, so I have nothing to count, and I won’t give you a number I don’t have. What I can do is explain what people believe each phase does, and show you how to test it with your own notebook.

## What people believe each phase does {#what-people-believe}

The moon goes through its phases in about 29.5 days: new (invisible), waxing (growing towards full), full, then waning (shrinking back to dark). In a lot of modern tarot and witchcraft practice, each phase has a job.

| Phase | What it’s traditionally for | What people read for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New moon | Beginnings, planting, intentions | What to start; a [new moon spread](/blog/new-moon-tarot-spread/) |
| Waxing moon | Growth, building, adding | A project, what to develop next |
| Full moon | Culmination, clarity, emotions running high | What has come to light; a [full moon spread](/blog/full-moon-tarot-spread/) |
| Waning moon | Letting go, clearing, rest | What to end, what to put down |
| Dark moon (the last days before new) | Rest, introspection | Some readers rest their deck; others keep it for the deepest questions |

Some people also leave their deck on a windowsill under the full moon to “charge” it, or read only on certain nights. None of this is wrong to do. It gives a practice a rhythm, and a rhythm helps people sit down and pay attention.

## Why the idea is so appealing {#why-appealing}

The moon really does move the oceans: the tides are its pull. From there, it’s a short step to thinking it moves everything made of water — feelings included. My own notes on the High Priestess start there: her element is Water, the moon influences water, and her robe falls like a waterfall. The flip side is the moon’s hidden face — the façade she shows, and everything she knows and doesn’t say.

The deck itself is full of moons. In the Golden Dawn tradition the astrological Moon belongs to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), with a crescent at her feet. The Moon card, number 18, carries Pisces — the dog and the wolf howling, the crayfish climbing out of the pool. It’s a frequent confusion, and a good reminder that a symbol on a card and a body in the sky aren’t the same thing.

**A belief can be meaningful to you without being something the cards depend on.**

## What I can and can’t tell you {#what-i-can-say}

What I can’t tell you is whether my readings held better under a full moon. My notebook tracks my statements and the person’s answers, and there’s no column for the sky.

What I can tell you is what has changed a reading, in my experience. Tiredness: interpreting takes energy, and when mine runs low, so does the reading. Pressure: when I want too much to be right, I see nothing. And the person in front of me: someone afraid of hearing something can block a reading as surely as I can. All three are things you can notice and act on tonight, whatever the moon is doing.

That’s also why I keep [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/): the person tells me nothing first. If you want to test anything about readings — the moon included — you need confirmations that weren’t fed by what you already knew.

## How to test it yourself {#test-it}

Here is how I would set it up.

1. Add a column to your [reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/): date, cards, what you said, what was confirmed, tick or cross. Leave one column blank for the phase.
2. Don’t check the phase when you read. Fill that column afterwards, all at once, from a calendar. If you know it’s a full moon, you’ll read differently — and judge differently.
3. Read for other people where you can, with the silence rule. Readings for yourself are much harder to tick or cross honestly.
4. Keep going for at least three full cycles, about three months.
5. Count. For each phase: how many readings, how many ticks. Compare the shares, not the totals.
6. Be hard on your result. A difference of two or three readings is noise. Watch for what hides behind the moon too: maybe you read more at weekends, or later at night, or when you’re tired.

If one phase still comes out ahead after six months, you’ve learned something true about you. If nothing stands out, you’ve learned that too — and you can read whenever you like.

## So should you read by the moon? {#should-you}

If a phase helps you sit down — the new moon as a monthly appointment with yourself, say — use it. A rhythm is a fine reason.

If a phase makes you anxious (“I can’t read this week, the moon is waning”), that worry is worth looking at more than the sky is. No phase makes a reading dangerous, and none makes it more true. The same goes for eclipses, which raise the most questions; I answer them in [is it OK to read during an eclipse?](/blog/eclipse-season-tarot/)

The next step is small: draw a column in your notebook tonight. In three months, you’ll have your own answer, and it will be worth more than mine.
