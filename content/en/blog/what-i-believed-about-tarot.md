I started reading tarot on 26 December 2020, and my notes from 2021, my first full year, are a fair record of what I believed then. Some of it still holds. A few things I’d put differently today, and one I’ve stopped doing altogether. Here they are, beside what I think now.

## I thought I’d need training before I got anything right {#training}

My friends gave me my first deck that Christmas, from my wishlist. I’d been expecting an astrology book. When I started reading, I assumed I’d need training before anything I said was accurate.

My first reading was accurate. I hadn’t trained for it.

What I’d add now is what that meant, and what it didn’t. It wasn’t proof of anything about me. Since then I’ve made friends and family try, and all of them said something true within three attempts. Some arrive with more flair, as in any art; everyone else gets there by practising.

**That first reading told me something about reading, not about me.** It’s why I hand back [the compliment “you have a gift”](/blog/you-have-a-gift/), and why the [learn path](/learn-tarot/) has you read a card on day one instead of testing your talent.

## ‘I don’t need the cards anymore’ {#the-cards}

That’s a line from my 2021 notes. The more I read, the more messages came before I’d even shuffled, and I was using the cards mostly because the person in front of me wanted to see them.

The idea underneath it is one I teach. The cards are a medium, something that helps you let go long enough to notice what surrounds you or another person. **What comes, comes from you, not from the cardboard.**

But the same notes give the cards a job, even when the message arrives first. They help you focus, or unfocus, so your own thoughts and biases step aside for a moment. And the person gets something to look at: a picture they can hold my words up against, instead of taking my word for it.

If you’re starting out, please don’t read that 2021 sentence as a goal. It describes where a year of reading had taken me, not a level you have to reach before you count as a reader. Most of what you need in your first year is the deck in front of you, and something to look at while you let go.

## Sage, candles and ‘channelled’ cards {#props}

In 2021 I also sold sage, candles and “channelled” cards. I stopped. Not one of those products made my readings any better.

It’s worth seeing why that kind of thing sells so well. A prop promises to do the hard part for you: clear the deck, set the mood, bring the message. But the hard part is the letting go, and nobody can sell you that.

None of this is a judgement on people who like a candle on the table. Light one if you enjoy it. It’s only that, when a reading feels off, the thing that needs a rest is usually the reader, not the deck. I’ve written more about [why I stopped selling sage](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/).

## Astrology: I thought it was a joke {#astrology}

This one comes before the deck. I put on an astrology video to mock it, read my own chart, and it was too precise.

“Too precise” was a feeling, not a result. So I tested my chart box by box, a tick or a cross for each placement, then the charts of family, friends and strangers. Overall, 30–40% held. Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75–90%. The [whole test](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) has its own page.

So I moved twice: from mocking astrology to being struck by it, and from being struck by it to counting. **Both first reactions were stronger than the numbers.**

What I hold now is narrower than either. A chart can be worth reading, and it is never an excuse. “It’s because I’m a Taurus” doesn’t settle anything: you’re not one sign, you’re a mix of everything, and if something keeps hurting the people around you, it’s worth checking yourself rather than your Moon. More on that in [when astrology becomes an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).

## What I hold most firmly now: misses count {#misses}

My notebook counts a miss in roughly every ten readings, and I say so out loud. No reframing, no extra card to rescue it.

[My first reading for a stranger](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/) shows what makes that hard to hold: on the day it was a miss, and three or four days later she texted me that everything had happened.

A story like that makes it tempting to treat every miss as a hit running late. I don’t. She came back on her own; a reading nobody comes back about stays a miss in the notebook. **A reader who can’t say “that one was wrong” can’t learn anything from being right.**

## What hasn’t changed {#unchanged}

The surprise. The moment that made me want to teach is still the reason I teach: someone I’m teaching says something true about another person, and is shocked by what they’ve just said.

My own first reading, the one I thought I’d need training for, gave me that same surprise. If you’d like to find it for yourself, the [workshops](/workshops/) are three hours, twelve people, and one lens each.
