My first notes about tarot date from 2021, a few months after I started reading on 26 December 2020. Some of what's in them still holds. A few things I'd put differently today, and one or two I've stopped doing altogether. Here they are, beside what I think now.

## I thought I'd need training before I got anything right {#training}

My friends gave me my first deck that Christmas, from my wishlist. I'd been expecting an astrology book. When I started reading, I assumed I'd need training before anything I said was accurate.

My first reading was accurate. I hadn't trained for it.

What I see differently now is what that meant. It wasn't proof of anything about me. Since then I've made friends and family try, and every single person I've helped to read managed it on the first, second or third try. Reading is like drawing: some people have more talent, and anyone who practises can do it. The only practice is letting go.

**That first reading told me something about reading, not about me.** It's why I hand back [the compliment people pay me most often](/blog/you-have-a-gift/), and why the [learn path](/learn-tarot/) has you read a card on day one instead of testing your talent.

## ‘I don’t need the cards anymore’ {#the-cards}

That's a line from my 2021 notes. The more I read, the more messages came before I'd even shuffled, and I was using the cards mostly because the person in front of me wanted to see them.

I'd keep half of that sentence. **Cards aren't magic, you are.** They're a medium that helps you let go and read what's around you, or around someone. What comes, comes from you, not from the cardboard.

The half I'd drop is "don't need". The cards do a job even when the message arrives first. They help you focus, or unfocus, so your own thoughts and biases step aside for a moment. And they give the person something to look at: a picture they can hold what I say up against, instead of taking my word for it.

If you're starting out, please don't read my 2021 sentence as a goal. Outgrowing the deck isn't a level to reach. It described a phase, written in my first year of reading.

## Sage, candles and ‘channelled’ cards {#props}

In 2021 I also sold sage, candles and "channelled" cards. I stopped. Nothing in any of it improved a reading.

What I understand now is why that kind of thing sells so well. A prop promises to do the hard part for you: clear the deck, set the mood, bring the message. But the hard part is the letting go, and nobody can sell you that.

None of this is a judgement on people who like a candle on the table. Light one if you enjoy it. It's only that, when a reading feels off, the thing that needs a rest is usually the reader, not the deck. I've written more about [why I stopped selling sage](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/).

## Astrology: I came to laugh at it {#astrology}

This one comes before the deck. I put on an astrology video to mock it, read my own chart, and it was too precise.

"Too precise" was a feeling, not a result. So I tested my chart box by box, a tick or a cross for each statement, then the charts of family, friends and strangers. Overall, 30–40% held. Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75–90%. The [whole test](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) has its own page.

So I moved twice: from mocking astrology to being struck by it, and from being struck by it to counting. **Both first reactions were stronger than the numbers.**

What I hold now is narrower than either. A chart can be worth reading, and it is never an excuse. "It's because I'm a Taurus" doesn't settle anything: you're not one sign, you're a mix of everything, and if something keeps hurting the people around you, it's worth checking yourself rather than your Moon. More on that in [when astrology becomes an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).

## What I hold most firmly now: misses count {#misses}

About one reading in ten misses, by my own count, and I say so. No reframing, no extra card to rescue it.

My first reading for a stranger shows why I'm strict about that. It sounded like a miss: she said it didn't resonate, she wasn't looking for a relationship. Three or four days later she texted me, shocked. Everything had happened.

A story like that makes it tempting to treat every miss as a hit running late. I don't. She came back on her own; a reading nobody comes back about stays a miss in the notebook. **A reader who can't say "that one was wrong" can't learn anything from being right.** Here's [what I do when a reading is wrong](/blog/tarot-reading-wrong/).

## What hasn't changed {#unchanged}

The surprise. The moment that made me want to teach is still the one I wait for: someone I'm teaching says something true about another person, and is shocked by what they've just said.

My own first reading gave me the same surprise, and that part of my 2021 notes I wouldn't change. If you'd like to find it for yourself, the [workshops](/workshops/) are three hours, twelve people, and one lens each.
