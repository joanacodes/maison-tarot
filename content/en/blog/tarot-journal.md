A tarot journal that makes you better has two columns: what I said, and what was confirmed. That's the whole method. Sketches, colour codes and pretty layouts are welcome, but they don't teach you anything. The two columns do, because they let you count.

## What to write in a tarot journal {#two-columns}

Rule a line down the middle of the page. On the left, what you said, in the words you used. On the right, what the person confirmed, in their words. Above both, the date and the cards.

Leave out the person's question — under [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/) you shouldn't know it anyway — and leave out their full name. A first name, or "stranger, group call", is enough to find the reading again. It's their life on your page.

Say you read for a friend and draw the Three of Cups, then the Eight of Pentacles. Your left column might say: "Three cups raised, a lot of red against a blue sky: people celebrating together, warm feelings. Then eight, Earth: steady work, a skill being polished." The right column, in her words: "My sister's wedding last weekend. And yes, I've just started an evening course." Two lines each, and you can already judge.

**Write the left column before you hear the right one.** Once the person has spoken, memory quietly edits what you said to fit what they told you. Mine does, and so will yours.

## How to count your readings {#counting}

Under each reading, one mark: held, or missed.

Mark it held when the person recognises their situation, with a visible reaction while you're talking or a confirmation afterwards. Mark it missed when nothing lands, or when the person says so. There's no third box. The moment you add "partly" or "we'll see", every miss starts drifting into it and the count stops meaning anything.

A missed entry looks just as plain as a held one. Left: "a move, boxes, a new city". Right: "No, nothing like that." Mark: missed. It's the most useful line on the page.

Write the mark the same evening, while a miss still stings. Then leave a little room in the right column, because confirmation sometimes arrives days later. If it does, add it with its date.

Every ten readings, count. Not to grade yourself, but to see what your misses have in common: a subject, a time of day, a long evening. A notebook is how you find your own limit.

## What my notebook says {#what-it-says}

I kept this notebook through about three hundred free blind readings for strangers, and by my count about nine in ten held and one in ten missed. [The full count, with what it doesn't prove](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/), has its own page.

The percentage isn't what changed me. Three habits did:

1. I go in ready to say nonsense. The lower I set the bar, the better the left column reads.
2. I stop when a reading misses. No second card to rescue it, no reframing: it goes in the notebook as it is.
3. I wait for the right column. Some readings only close days later.

## Logging your daily card {#daily-log}

The same two columns work for a card you draw for yourself each morning. Left, in the morning: the card and what you see — the colour that fills it, the number, the element. Right, at night: what the day held, and whether the morning note fits.

Be honest with this log in a particular way. You're writing both columns, so it's tempting to make the day fit the card. Write the evening line before rereading the morning one. There's more on drawing in [the one-card pull](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/), and on [letting impressions come before you check them](/blog/read-tarot-intuitively/).

The log also shows something no single reading can: how often you reach for the deck. If the left column holds the same worry every morning, the cards have stopped teaching you and started reassuring you. That's usually doubt, and it deserves kindness, not a fifth card. It's a good moment to [put the deck away for a while](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).

## What doesn't need to go in {#leave-out}

- Book meanings. A journal isn't a dictionary; copying meanings into it trains reciting, not reading.
- Private details the person shared after the reading. Note what confirms or contradicts, nothing more.
- A perfect layout. If colours and stickers make you open the notebook every day, keep them. If they slow you down, drop them.

## Start with ten readings {#start}

Take any notebook, the cheap kind is fine, and rule the line tonight. Find ten people who’ll sit down without explaining anything, read for each, fill both columns, mark each one, and count. Ten is enough to see what you do when you miss.
