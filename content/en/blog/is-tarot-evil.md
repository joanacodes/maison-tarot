I'm not going to talk about the Devil card today — that's a card, it has [its own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/). I'm going to answer the question people actually mean: *is tarot evil?* I can only speak from what I've experienced, so that's what you'll get.

## What tarot has actually done for me {#what-it-did}

Tarot reading, and divination readings in general, have brought me more than any practice, belief or religion I've tried. They brought clarity, and a kind of compassion I hadn't experienced before — and all of this without flattery.

That last part is the point. When I needed to hear something I didn't want to see, I got it shown to my face. It was never based on hope and faith and whatever else. It was based on: *hey, you're being an ass and you know it. You're acting this way because of this and this.* Sometimes that's exactly what you need, and it's the opposite of what people expect from something "evil". Evil flatters. Tarot doesn't.

## Then why do people think it's the devil's work {#why-people-think}

Three reasons, in my experience.

**The pictures.** Death, the Devil, the Tower, the Hanged Man. A deck drawn in 1909 with the imagery of its time looks, to someone who's never opened it, like a catalogue of bad news. Read the [Death card page](/library/tarot-cards/death/) and you'll find a white horse and a sunrise.

**The readers.** Some people who read cards also sell curses, spirits and fear, and charge to remove them. That's not tarot; that's a scam wearing tarot's coat. [I refuse all of it](/disclaimers/#refusals), and I'll write about how to spot it.

**The projection.** Tarot is a screen. What you see on it is largely what you bring. Tarot is only evil for people who see evil everywhere — and for them, so is everything else.

## The one time something came home with me {#the-grandfather}

I'll tell you the strangest thing that ever happened after a reading, because I'd rather you hear it from me than imagine worse.

I did a reading for a friend. It was fun, and something about it was more open than usual. When I got home, I had the distinct feeling that someone was there with me. I'll describe it the only way I can: it felt like my friend's grandfather. He was kind — a strict man with a lot of love in him; anyone could have told. I said, roughly, "I said I don't want anyone in my house… sir." He was persistent. Fine.

He wanted to tell me something, and the impression was of someone who spoke Japanese, so I said: we're going to have to draw. And I drew one thing, then another, then the same thing again, until the impression was that we were done. When it started to fade I said "but… we're not finished, are we?" and got, unmistakably, an irritated *"Sorewa!"* — which I looked up. It means *that's it.*

What I'd drawn was the number five, a few times, and "under the house". I sent it to my friend. She went to the underground level of her building, storage cell number five — not hers, so she wasn't thrilled — and found it already open, full of pots of paint.

That was the whole message. *There's a lot of paint in cell five.* A grandfather telling his granddaughter about paint. I'd expected something long and complicated, because it was "in Japanese", and I'd been annoying him by not accepting that it was simple.

## What I take from it {#what-i-take}

I don't know what that was. I've put it on the shelf I keep for such things — the one labelled *I'm sceptical, but if it happens, it happens, and I was warned.* What I notice is how ordinary it was. No threat, no warning, no darkness. Paint. If that's the devil's work, the devil has a very small business.

What tarot does, day to day, is far less spectacular and far more useful: it shows a person their own present in pictures they can't argue with, and a good reader says it out loud without decorating it. I've done that [three hundred times and counted](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/). The only thing I've ever seen it summon is honesty.

## What I'd say to someone who's afraid of it {#if-afraid}

Don't do it. Truly — if the pictures frighten you, there's no prize for pushing through. Fear reads badly. And if you do it anyway and it frightens you *more*, [put the deck away](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/). Not everything is about spirits; sometimes what's frightening is inside, and the right door is a therapist's, not a reader's.

But if what you're afraid of is being told something true — that's the only thing tarot has ever done to me. I recommend it.
