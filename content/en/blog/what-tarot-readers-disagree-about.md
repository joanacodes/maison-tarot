Tarot readers agree on less than you’d think. Six debates come up again and again: reversed cards, clarifier cards, cleansing, money, AI and spreads. Good readers sit on both sides of every one. Here is each debate as fairly as I can put it, then where I stand, as one reader’s position rather than a ruling.

## Reversed cards and clarifier cards {#reversals-and-clarifiers}

*Reversals.* When a card lands upside down, some readers turn it upright and read it as usual, every time. Others read the direction as part of the message, and many booklets give each card a second, reversed meaning, which turns 78 meanings into 156. The upright camp says the picture is the picture. The reversed camp says the direction adds a layer it would be a shame to throw away. Both read well.

Where I stand: not in your first weeks, then yes, but without a second list. A reversed card is the same card, slowed down or in excess: the same number, element and colours, with the energy held back or spilling over. Say the Chariot lands reversed.

Upright, it’s moving forward with the reins in hand. Reversed, it’s either stalled, the two sphinxes pulling apart, or charging through when it should slow down. And decide before you shuffle, not after you’ve seen the card. The full shortcut is in [reversed tarot cards](/blog/reversed-tarot-cards/).

*Clarifiers.* A clarifier is an extra card drawn to explain one that’s unclear. Plenty of readers use them, and use them carefully, and the case for them is real: a reading is a conversation, and sometimes you want one more word.

Where I stand: I don’t draw them. One more card doesn’t clarify the first; it adds a second picture and a second story. And the urge for one tends to arrive when a reading hasn’t landed, which is the moment I’d rather say so. I explain why in [the clarifier card](/blog/the-clarifier-card/).

**On both questions, the rule that matters most is deciding before you see the cards, not after.**

## Cleansing your deck {#cleansing}

Some readers cleanse a new deck, or one that feels heavy, with sage smoke, moonlight, salt or a crystal laid on top. For many it marks a beginning and an end, a small ritual that helps them settle before reading for someone. I understand that. Others see cleansing as unnecessary, or as something mostly being sold.

Where I stand: I don’t cleanse. In 2021 I sold sage and candles myself, and I stopped, because nothing in them ever improved a reading. When something feels off, it’s usually the reader who needs a rest, not the cards. If a ritual helps you settle, keep it, as a habit for you rather than a repair for the deck. And if your readings start to feel muddy, try stopping for a few days before reaching for smoke; more often than not, the deck is fine and the reader is tired. The whole story is in [do you need to cleanse your tarot deck?](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/)

## Charging for readings {#money}

Some readers charge for readings and see it as skilled work like any other, which it is: it takes time, attention and practice, and it’s tiring. Others feel a reading should never be sold, or only for a donation. Those who charge reply, fairly, that a free reading isn’t free for the reader: the time comes from somewhere, and so does the rent.

Where I stand: in the middle, in a specific way. I charge for lessons, one-hour sessions and three-hour workshops, and I give readings away at events. I’ve never sold a reading. That isn’t a judgement on readers who do. It’s about incentives: a practice that lives on readings needs you to come back, and one that lives on teaching needs you to need it less.

**What I’d watch isn’t whether a reader charges, but whether the price rewards keeping you uncertain.** Pay-per-question and self-renewing subscriptions do; a stated price for a stated hour doesn’t. I set out my side in [what I charge for and what I give away](/blog/what-i-charge-for/).

## AI and tarot {#ai}

This is the newest argument, and the loudest. Some readers use AI to learn symbols, draft posts or even generate readings, and some people like an AI reading because it’s there at any hour and never judges. Others feel any use of AI empties the practice. The case for it deserves a hearing: for someone learning alone, a patient explainer at midnight is a real help, and I’d rather people asked a machine what a symbol means than gave up.

Where I stand: AI is a good dictionary and a poor reader. Ask it why the High Priestess sits between two pillars and it will answer patiently and clearly. Ask it for a reading and it takes your context, commits to nothing, misses nothing because it says nothing that could miss, and refuses nothing, including the questions I step back from. I use it to look things up, not to read. More in [AI tarot reading vs a human reader](/blog/ai-tarot-vs-human/).

## Set spreads or free spreads {#spreads}

Some readers follow set patterns: the Celtic Cross, or a career reading always laid out the same way, with every position fixed in advance. Others follow their intuition and build a new spread for each person. The first camp values structure and the chance to compare readings over time. The second values fit.

Say someone is torn between two jobs. A set spread might be the Celtic Cross, ten positions decided long before they sat down. A free one might be two columns of three cards, one column per job, invented on the spot. Both can work. The risk on one side is squeezing a person into boxes that don’t fit them; on the other, deciding what a position meant after you’ve seen what landed in it.

Where I stand: both are crafts, and I’ve come to see a spread as closer to art than to a form you fill in. One reader sees one story, another sees a different one, and both can explain why. That’s how two readers can get [different answers from the same cards](/blog/two-readers-different-answers/) without either being wrong. Whichever you choose, decide what each position means before you turn the cards over.

## What’s under the debates, and your turn {#your-turn}

Under all six, the readers I respect tend to share a few things. A reading isn’t an order. Nobody is right every time, and saying so is part of the job. The person in front of you matters more than the method. Readers who agree on that can disagree about everything above and still both be worth sitting down with.

As for my positions: don’t take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself. Pick one debate, try the side you don’t hold for a week, and write down what changes in [your reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/). You may end up where I am, or somewhere better.
