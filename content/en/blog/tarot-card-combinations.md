Reading two cards together isn’t adding two meanings. It’s reading the movement from the first card to the second: what the number does, what the element does, what the colours do. Get that movement into one sentence and you have a combination — here’s how, then twenty pairs worked through.

## How to read two tarot cards together {#method}

I use the same lenses as for a single card, and then I compare. Five steps, in this order:

1. Read each card in one word through its number and element. The Tower: 16 → 7, Fire. The Star: 17 → 8, Air.
2. Watch the [numbers](/library/numerology/). Going up the cycle is moving forward. Going down is returning to an earlier stage — not always backwards, sometimes repair. The same number twice is the same stage, lived on two planes.
3. Compare the [elements](/library/four-elements/). The same element reinforces. Fire with Air and Water with Earth help each other. Fire with Water and Air with Earth pull against each other. The other mixes are neutral. Many readers call this the elemental dignities.
4. Look at the [colours](/library/tarot-colours/) side by side. Black next to yellow is absence moving towards joy before you’ve thought about any meaning.
5. Say one sentence that holds both cards.

**Two cards are one sentence, not two definitions glued together.** “Death means an ending and the Star means hope” is two definitions. “It’s over, and you’re allowed to look forward” is a reading.

## The movement between two cards {#movement}

When two cards sit side by side, I read them left to right: the first is where things are, the second is where they’re heading. Swap them and the sentence changes. The Lovers then the Devil is a choice hardening into a habit; the Devil then the Lovers is a habit loosening back into a choice. Same two cards, opposite stories.

Then I look at the drawings as if they were one picture. Where do the figures look — towards the other card, or away from it? Does one card pour, fall, walk, hold? A card full of movement next to a still one often reads as an action meeting a pause.

The energy moves too. Feminine to masculine is holding turning into acting; masculine to feminine is action settling down into care or waiting.

When I’m not sure what a pair means, I describe the movement instead: “from something that falls to something that pours”. Said out loud, the meaning usually follows within a sentence.

## Tarot combinations after a hard card {#after-a-hard-card}

These twenty pairs use twelve cards on purpose: watching the same card change with its neighbour is the fastest way to learn combinations. The heaviest of them have [their own page](/blog/scariest-tarot-cards/), if they worry you.

### The Tower, then the Star {#tower-star}

*7 → 8 · Fire → Air.* The classic after-the-storm pair. Seven’s reflection opens onto eight’s gate, and Fire and Air feed each other: the shock gives the hope its oxygen. “Something came down, and what you’re doing now is repair, not damage control.”

### Death, then the Star {#death-star}

*4 → 8 · Water → Air.* An ending, then hope. Four doubles into eight: the ground that was cleared becomes a wider foundation. Water moving into Air is a grief starting to be thought about and talked about. “It’s over, and you’re allowed to look forward.”

### Death, then the Tower {#death-tower}

*4 → 7 · Water → Fire.* Two changes at two speeds, the slow one and the sudden one, in elements that pull against each other. I’d ask whether the person is trying to end something gently that is ending loudly anyway.

### The Ten of Swords, then the Sun {#ten-of-swords-sun}

*10 → 1 · Air → Fire.* The yellow strip at the Ten’s horizon becomes the whole sky. Ten is the corridor; nineteen reduces to one, the next room. Air feeds Fire. “The worst is behind you, and you’ll see it clearly soon.”

### The Ten of Swords, then the Ace of Wands {#ten-of-swords-ace-of-wands}

*10 → 1 · Air → Fire.* The same movement on a smaller scale: the mind lies down, the urge stands up. A new project born from exhaustion — worth starting, slowly.

### Death, then the Ace of Wands {#death-ace-of-wands}

*4 → 1 · Water → Fire.* In my notes Death is the harvest and the start again, and the Ace of Wands is a staff in leaf. The elements pull apart, so grief and appetite sit together awkwardly. “You’ve cleared the field. Something wants to grow, and it’s fine if that feels too early.”

## Tarot combinations for love and attachment {#love}

These are the pairs people ask about most, and the ones where the order matters most. For the cards that really speak about relationships, one by one, see [tarot cards for love](/blog/tarot-cards-for-love/).

### The Lovers, then the Devil {#lovers-devil}

*6 → 6 · Air → Earth.* Same number, almost the same drawing: two figures under a larger one. A choice that hardened into attachment; Air and Earth pull apart, a decision turned into a habit. Look at the chains, though — they’re loose.

### The Devil, then the Lovers {#devil-lovers}

*6 → 6 · Earth → Air.* The same two cards in the other order: the chains come off and become a choice again. Someone stepping out of a pattern and deciding, this time, with their eyes open.

### The Lovers, then Death {#lovers-death}

*6 → 4 · Air → Water.* The number goes down, back to building. A choice that closes a chapter. I don’t read it as a break-up announced; I read a decision that ends one way of being together, or one of the options.

### The Lovers, then the Two of Cups {#lovers-two-of-cups}

*6 → 2 · Air → Water.* Two bond cards side by side say the same thing twice, louder. What was discussed (Air) becomes felt (Water): the choice lands in the body.

### The Two of Cups, then the Three of Swords {#two-of-cups-three-of-swords}

*2 → 3 · Water → Air.* The obvious reading is a bond, then a heartbreak. The one in my notes is different: the Three of Swords is also a blood pact — a commitment sealed, sometimes painfully. Ask before you assume.

### The Three of Swords, then the Two of Cups {#three-of-swords-two-of-cups}

*3 → 2 · Air → Water.* The number steps back from the cut to the bond. A reconciliation, or a hurt talked through until it becomes a feeling again rather than a thought on a loop.

### The Two of Cups, then the Devil {#two-of-cups-devil}

*2 → 6 · Water → Earth.* Water and Earth help each other, so the bond gets solid — perhaps too solid. A connection sliding into dependence. The question to ask: “Is this still a choice?”

### The Five of Cups, then the Two of Cups {#five-of-cups-two-of-cups}

*5 → 2 · Water → Water.* Same element, so the feeling carries straight over; the number goes down from trial to bond. After the spilled cups, someone holds out a full one: a loss that makes room for a new connection, or for repairing the old one.

## Tarot combinations for work, hope and light {#work-and-light}

### The Ace of Wands, then the Eight of Pentacles {#ace-of-wands-eight-of-pentacles}

*1 → 8 · Fire → Earth.* An idea that gets hands. One climbs to eight: the seed becomes a trade. “What started as an urge becomes a craft, if you put the hours in.”

### The Eight of Pentacles, then the Devil {#eight-of-pentacles-devil}

*8 → 6 · Earth → Earth.* Same element: everything here is matter. Mastery that turns into a chain — overwork, a job that holds you. The plus is the Devil’s: the chain is visible, so it can come off.

### The Tower, then the Eight of Pentacles {#tower-eight-of-pentacles}

*7 → 8 · Fire → Earth.* After the collapse, you rebuild by hand, stone by stone. Fire and Earth are neutral to each other: the energy of the shock goes into the work.

### The Star, then the Sun {#star-sun}

*8 → 1 · Air → Fire.* Air feeds Fire, and hope becomes light; eight’s gate opens onto a new cycle. One of the gentlest pairs in the deck. Its minus is the Sun’s: in full light, don’t forget the others.

### The Three of Swords, then the Star {#three-of-swords-star}

*3 → 8 · Air → Air.* Same element: the mind that was hurt starts to hope. Not “everything’s fine” — the hurt is still in the picture, and so is the star.

### The Sun, then the Five of Cups {#sun-five-of-cups}

*1 → 5 · Fire → Water.* Opposed elements: joy, then a loss inside it. I read what the bright moment cost, or who is missing from it.

## When two cards seem to contradict each other {#contradictions}

The Sun next to the Five of Cups looks like a mistake. It isn’t: life holds both at once, and a pair that pulls apart often describes exactly that. Read the tension itself — “a success with a grief in it” — before trying to decide which card wins.

What I don’t do is draw a third card to settle it. One more card muddies the reading more than it clears it, as I explain in [the clarifier card](/blog/the-clarifier-card/). If the pair means nothing to the person, it’s a miss, and a miss is a miss.

## Practise with your own pairs {#practise}

For a week, draw two cards each morning instead of one. Write the five steps in a line — number, element, colours, energy, sentence — and the sentence you’d say. In the evening, see whether the day agreed with it.

If you’d rather practise with other people, the [workshops](/workshops/) do exactly this with strangers who tell you nothing.
