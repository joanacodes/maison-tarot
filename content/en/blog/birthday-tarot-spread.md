A birthday is a good day to take stock, and this spread does it in four cards: the year you’re closing, what you’re taking with you, the theme of the year ahead, and a gift to give yourself. It borrows its shape from astrology’s solar return. It doesn’t predict your year; it gives you something to live with until the next candles.

## What is a solar return? {#solar-return}

A solar return is the moment each year when the Sun comes back to the exact position it held in the zodiac at the minute you were born. Because the calendar and the Sun’s cycle don’t line up perfectly, that moment falls on your birthday or close to it, sometimes the day before or after.

Astrologers cast a chart for that moment, usually for the place where you are, and read it as the tone of the year until your next birthday. Say you were born with the Sun at 14° Scorpio: each year, around your birthday, the Sun reaches 14° Scorpio again, and that’s your solar return. Chart sites such as Astro-Seek, the one I use, can calculate one from your date, time and place of birth.

In astrology, [the Sun](/library/astrology/sun/) is the centre of the chart: in my notes it’s the inner house, the part of you that you live in. Its return is a yearly “you are here”. If birth charts are new to you, [what a birth chart is](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/) covers the basics.

**A solar return is a birthday measured by the sky: the Sun back in its place, and a new year of your life starting from there.**

The other return people talk about is Saturn’s, which comes round once in about twenty-nine years and has [its own reputation](/blog/saturn-return/). The solar return is gentler: it comes every year, like the cake.

## The four positions {#positions}

This spread doesn’t calculate anything. It keeps the solar return’s idea — one cycle closing, the next one opening — and turns it into four questions.

1. *The year I’m closing.* What the last twelve months were about, looking back.
2. *What I’m taking with me.* A strength you built, a lesson, something worth keeping.
3. *The theme of the year ahead.* A question to live with, not an event to wait for.
4. *A gift to give myself.* How you’ll look after that theme: something to do, allow or stop.

Lay the first two cards on the left and the last two on the right, with a little gap between: the gap is your birthday. Read the pairs, then the four together.

## A birthday spread, card by card {#example}

Say someone turns thirty-five, tells the cards nothing, and draws the Ten of Wands, the Eight of Pentacles, the Ace of Wands and the Nine of Pentacles.

*The year I’m closing:* the Ten of Wands, a man bent under ten heavy wands, walking towards a town. Ten is the corridor between two rooms, [the end of one cycle and the door to the next](/library/numerology/number-10/). In my notes this card says: keep the creative energy, without overloading. A year of carrying too much, perhaps, and getting there anyway.

*What I’m taking with me:* the Eight of Pentacles, a craftsman at his bench, carving one coin after another. Eight is mastery, the necessary effort that turns into skill. Whatever the heavy year cost, it taught you how to do something well.

*The theme of the year ahead:* the Ace of Wands, a hand coming out of a cloud, holding a wand with fresh leaves on it, a castle on a hill in the distance. One is the start of a cycle, the raw diamond. Wands are Fire: creation, impulse, the energy that gets you going. And the advice of 1, in my notes, is humility: get ready to learn.

Look at the two numbers on either side of the gap: 10, then 1. The cycle turns over, exactly as a birthday does.

*A gift to give myself:* the Nine of Pentacles, a woman alone in a garden heavy with grapes, a bird resting on her gloved hand. Enjoying what comes, gratitude for what you have. The gift is to stop and enjoy what you’ve built before you start building the next thing.

The sentence out loud might be: “You carried a lot this year and learned your craft doing it. Something new wants to start — go in as a beginner, and take time to enjoy what you already have.” Then ask whether it lands, and write down the answer, hit or miss.

## What a birthday reading can’t tell you {#limits}

It can’t tell you what will happen this year, and I wouldn’t want it to. The Ace of Wands doesn’t promise a new job or a new love; it describes an energy you can bring to whatever comes. A card is a theme, not an appointment.

It also isn’t a reading of your solar return chart. If you want the astrology, read the chart itself, and treat it the same way: a description of tendencies, never a script. I read; you decide.

And if the year that’s closing was a very hard one, the first position may hurt to look at. You can leave it face down and start from the second card. The spread is yours.

## On the day itself {#on-the-day}

Draw the four cards on your birthday morning, or on the evening before, and write one line for each. Then close the notebook and keep the page for next year: reading last year’s spread on your next birthday is the best part, because you’ll see what the theme really turned out to mean.
