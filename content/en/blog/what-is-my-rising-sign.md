Your rising sign — also called the ascendant — is the zodiac sign that was coming up over the eastern horizon at the minute and place you were born. It describes the outside of you: how you come across the first time, how you walk into a room or a new job. Does it matter? Yes, as a door. Not as the house behind it.

## What a rising sign is {#what-it-is}

Picture the zodiac as a ring of twelve signs around the Earth. As the Earth turns, the whole ring passes over the eastern horizon once a day, one sign after another. The sign crossing the horizon at the moment you were born is your rising sign.

That's why it depends on your birth time. The rising sign changes roughly every two hours, a little faster or slower depending on the sign and on where you are. Two babies born in the same hospital on the same day share a Sun sign, and very likely a Moon sign. If one arrives at seven in the morning and the other at seven at night, they almost certainly don't share a rising sign.

In most charts, the ascendant is also the starting line of the first house: the point from which the other eleven houses are counted. Move it by an hour and the whole chart turns.

## How to find your rising sign {#find-yours}

You need three things, and the third is the one people don't have.

1. Your date of birth.
2. Your place of birth (the town is enough).
3. Your time of birth, as precisely as you can get it.

Enter them on a chart site — I use Astro-Seek — and look for “Ascendant” or the letters “AC” on the wheel. That's your rising sign.

If you don't know your time, [my beginner's explainer on the birth chart](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/#get-yours) says where to look for it, and what a chart still shows without one. If nobody knows, don't pick a time at random: a guessed time only gives you a guessed rising sign.

If your time is approximate — “around lunchtime” — look at the degree. An ascendant in the middle of a sign probably holds if you're a quarter of an hour off. One at 28° or 1° could belong to the next sign or the previous one, so hold it loosely.

## The door, not the house {#door-not-house}

In my notes, the rising sign is the outside of the house: the front door, the façade, the path people walk up before they meet you. The Sun and the Moon live inside.

**Your rising sign is how people come in, not who lives there.**

Say someone has the Sun in Virgo and Aries rising. At the door, Aries: first to say hello, quick, direct, already halfway through the task. Inside, Virgo: the one who analyses, checks, quietly advises. People who've met them once describe the Aries; people who've lived with them describe the Virgo. Neither is wrong. They've seen different rooms.

In tarot terms, that's the Emperor at the door — Aries, Fire, the one who starts and gives orders — and the Hermit inside, Virgo's card, his lantern turned towards the detail.

Another one: a Scorpio rising is often the person who says nothing at a party and draws every eye. Behind that door there may be a chatty, sunny Sun sign. The door doesn't tell you who lives there; it tells you how you'll be let in.

And nobody is one sign anyway. You're a mix of everything. [Sun, Moon and rising together](/blog/sun-moon-rising-big-three/) give you a rough floor plan; the rest of the chart is the furniture.

## Does it matter? What my test said {#does-it-matter}

When I [tested my birth chart box by box](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), tick or cross for every placement, the rising sign held about a third of the time — roughly the same as the Sun. That isn't nothing, and it isn't proof of much: a flattering description can score a third on almost anyone.

One thing makes the rising sign harder to check than the rest: you never walk past your own house. The façade is the part of you that other people see better than you do. So for a fairer test, read the description of your rising sign to someone who met you recently and let them give the tick or the cross.

What it isn't: an excuse. “I'm Aries rising, I'm blunt, that's how I am” explains a habit; it doesn't apologise for it. [Astrology is not an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/), on the doorstep or anywhere else.

The placements that did hold, at 75 to 90%, were three others: [Saturn, Lilith and Chiron](/blog/saturn-lilith-chiron/). None of them is about first impressions. All three are about what's behind the door.

## Try it with one card {#try-it}

Find your rising sign's card in [the table of the twelve signs](/library/astrology/) and take it out of the deck. Show it to someone who met you in the last year and ask one question: “Is this how I came across, the first time?”

Write down the card, their answer, and your own tick or cross. Then do the same with your Sun sign's card and compare the two pages. Don't take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself.
