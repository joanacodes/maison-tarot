A Maison Tarot workshop starts with a card in your hands, not with a talk. Within the first ten minutes you draw one, you look at it, and you say something about it — before I’ve explained a single thing. The explanation comes after, and it lands better for it.

## Why the card comes before the explanation {#why-first}

Because an explanation given first becomes something to recite. If I open with “yellow means joy”, you’ll spend the next card hunting for yellow and saying “joy”. That’s reciting, and it isn’t reading.

A card with nothing around it leaves you with the only thing that matters: the image, and what comes up when you look at it. That is the skill. Everything I teach afterwards is there to support it, never to replace it.

There’s a second reason, and it’s the reason I teach at all. When I first made friends and family try reading, there was always a moment when one of them said something true and looked shocked by what had come out of their own mouth. I can describe that moment; I can’t hand it to you.

## What the first ten minutes ask of you {#what-you-do}

Very little, on purpose. You draw a card. You look at it. You say, out loud, what you notice: a colour that jumps out, a posture, a word, a feeling, an image that isn’t on the card at all.

The exercise comes from my own notes, and it has three steps: describe what’s on the card, then what you see in it, then what you receive. The first is a description anyone can give. The third is the one people don’t expect to manage, and usually do.

You don’t need to know the card’s name. You don’t need to have read anything. If you already know a meaning by heart, put it aside for ten minutes; it will still be there afterwards.

## “But I don’t know anything yet” {#nothing-yet}

That’s the point. So far, every beginner I’ve guided has got there by the third attempt or sooner. Not because they were special — there are no Chosen Ones — but because reading is mostly letting go, and letting go is easier before you’ve filled your head with rules.

Two things can happen, and both are fine.

Something comes. You say it, a little surprised that you said it, without quite knowing where it came from. Good: that’s what you came for.

Nothing comes. That happens too, and it’s usually pressure — you want to get it right, so the door stays shut. Describe what’s on the card instead: the colours, the figure, the background. That already counts, and we talk it through. I’ve written more about [what to do when you don’t feel anything](/blog/dont-feel-anything-reading-tarot/).

Nobody is graded. You’ll get things wrong over the three hours, and we’ll look at why together, which is where most of the learning happens. When someone else reads, you’ll practise saying what landed for you — [feedback, given kindly](/blog/feedback-to-tarot-reader/), is part of the work.

## Then the lens arrives {#then-the-lens}

Once everyone has spoken, the explanation starts. Each workshop holds one lens for three hours: [colours](/library/tarot-colours/), numbers, or elements and energies. Three hours on one thing rather than thirty minutes on ten, turned over until it becomes a reflex.

This is where starting with a card pays off. If you noticed the blue on your card ten minutes earlier, the explanation of blue doesn’t arrive in an empty space. It lands on something you’ve already seen, said and felt. You don’t learn “blue is emotion” as a rule; you recognise it.

The workshop ends where it began, with a card. At the end, each of you reads an unfamiliar card blind, with the room watching. The first ten minutes and the last ones mirror each other. The difference is that by then you have a lens in your hands, and you know you can use it.

## The format, in practice {#format}

A workshop lasts three hours, online or in person, with twelve people at most — small enough that everyone reads. Each one stands alone: no levels, no certificate, no compulsory order. Colours is the usual place to start, because it’s the fastest lens to see.

You’ll need a Rider-Waite-Smith deck — no deck yet? Mention it when you book. What you won’t do: memorise 78 meanings, cleanse your deck with sage, or learn to sell readings.

## Try the ten minutes tonight {#try-tonight}

You can feel it at home before any workshop: draw one card tonight and take it through the three steps above, aloud.
