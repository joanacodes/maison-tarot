After a breakup, the kindest reading is one about you. This spread has five cards and none of them is about your ex: where you are, what you’re holding, what you need, what you learned, and one small step. It won’t tell you how the story ends. It helps you get through the week you’re in.

## What a breakup tarot spread is for {#what-its-for}

A breakup spread is a set of card positions you lay out after a relationship ends, to look at how you’re doing and what would help. It’s a check-up, not a verdict.

Say it’s been ten days. You’ve cried, slept badly and reread old messages. The spread doesn’t ask whether the phone will ring. It asks what you need on Saturday morning, and what you’re gripping that you could put down.

**A breakup spread reads the person at the table, and that person is you.**

One thing before you shuffle: the first raw night is rarely the night for cards. A walk, a friend or a long sleep comes first. The short answer to [whether to read when you’re upset](/blog/reading-tarot-when-upset/) is: wait until you can look at a card without needing it to save you.

## Why ‘will they come back?’ stays out of this spread {#will-they-come-back}

Because it’s the question that hurts most to carry. People ask it out of love, out of missing someone, and out of the fear of being left alone with what happened. There’s nothing silly about it.

But it hands your next weeks to someone who isn’t in the room. They haven’t agreed to be read, and their choices belong to them. And whatever a card says, the question keeps you waiting: a yes keeps you watching the door, a no hurts twice, and a maybe brings you back to ask again next week. More in [‘will they come back?’ and a kinder question to ask](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/).

The kinder question is the one underneath: what do I need while I don’t know? This whole spread is built on it.

## The five positions {#positions}

Draw five cards and place them side by side: today on the left, your small step on the right.

1. *Where I am today.* Not where you should be. A portrait of this week.
2. *What I’m holding on to.* A routine, a hope, a version of the future you’d planned, a grudge. Holding on isn’t wrong; the card asks whether it still serves you.
3. *What I need this week.* Rest, company, movement, quiet. Something you can actually give yourself.
4. *What this relationship showed me about what I want.* Not who was right. What you’d like next time, and what you won’t accept again.
5. *One small step back towards myself.* Small enough to do tomorrow.

Read the five together first, from today to that small step, before you take them one by one.

## A breakup spread, card by card {#example}

Say someone, three weeks after a breakup, tells the cards nothing and draws the Three of Swords, the Four of Pentacles, the Four of Swords, the Six of Pentacles and the Star.

*Where I am:* [the Three of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-swords/), a red heart pierced by three blades under grey rain. No people — it’s the feeling itself. Three is a bond that works, and its minus is betrayal; in my notes it’s also a blood pact, something that bound two people. Swords are the mind, so this is the hurt that replays in your head at night.

*What I’m holding:* the Four of Pentacles, a man sitting outside a town, one coin clutched to his chest, one on his crown, one under each foot. Four is stability, the structure you built, and its minus is [carrying on when you should stop](/library/numerology/number-4/). Perhaps it’s the flat, the shared plans, the life that made sense.

*What I need:* the Four of Swords, a knight lying still on a tomb, hands joined, three swords on the wall above him and one beneath. Rest. Look at the two Fours side by side: the same number, opposite gestures. One grips; the other lets go and lies down.

*What it showed me:* the Six of Pentacles, a man holding scales and giving coins to two people kneeling at his feet. Six is harmony and exchange, and in my notes it’s knowing your value. Maybe you gave more than you weighed, and next time you’d like the scales to work both ways.

*The small step:* the Star, a woman kneeling by a pool, pouring water from two jugs, one onto the land and one back into the pool. Pour some back into yourself: a glass of water, a night of sleep, a walk in daylight. Its love side is in [the Star in love](/blog/star-tarot-love/).

The sentence out loud might be: “This hurts, and you’re still holding on to something solid. Rest before you decide anything. Then look at what you gave, and pour a little back into you.” Then ask whether it lands. If it doesn’t, it’s a miss, and you write it down as one.

## What the cards can’t do after a breakup {#limits}

They don’t mend a heart, and they don’t tell you who was right, whether the other person is suffering too, or when it stops hurting. By my own count about one reading in ten misses; on something this tender, hold every card lightly.

If the pain stays heavy for weeks, or it reaches your sleep, your appetite or your work, a person is a better door than a deck. A friend first, a doctor, a therapist: [not everything is a matter for the cards](/disclaimers/#therapy), and knocking on that door isn’t your tarot failing. It’s the right tool for the job.

## Your first evening with this spread {#first-evening}

Put your phone in another room. Lay the five cards, write one line for each, then close the notebook and do whatever the fifth card asked, that same evening if you can.

Come back to the page in a month and read it again. You’ll see how far you’ve walked, which is the one thing a breakup hides from you. The walking was all yours, and [a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/) is how you get to see it.
