The Devil in love is about attachment: a desire, a habit or a person that holds you more than you hold them. It isn’t a judgement and it isn’t evil. And if you look closely at the drawing, the chains are loose — that’s the most useful thing the card has to tell you.

## A 6 gone stale, and Earth that holds {#stale-six}

The number: 15 becomes 1 + 5 = 6, the number of the Lovers — which is why the two chained figures look like the couple from that card. Six is harmony and exchange, the machine running fast; its advice in my notes is to stay calm and humble while things go well. The Devil is the 6 that forgot the advice. The exchange still works, so nobody checks its terms any more: the arrangement that suits you both well enough that neither of you asks whether it still suits you.

The element is Earth, what holds. What chains you in love is almost always something you value — comfort, a shared flat, shared friends, the feeling of being wanted — which is exactly why it’s hard to see as a chain. The energy is masculine: the body, desire, acting before thinking.

One detail of the drawing matters more than all the rest: the chains hang in wide loops. **The two figures could lift them off over their heads, and nothing in the drawing stops them.** The other lenses, Capricorn included, are on [the Devil’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/).

## Chemistry, a loop, or a tie that won’t cut {#what-holds}

Every card has a plus and a minus, and the Devil’s plus is real. Desire, chemistry, appetite, the energy to want someone and say so. Plenty of happy couples would recognise themselves in the fire on this card. The minus begins when the wanting starts to run you.

When you’re single, the Devil often points at an attachment that keeps the door closed. An ex you still measure everyone against. A picture of the person you’re supposed to end up with. The hunt itself: the apps opened before you’re fully awake, the little rush of a new match, the boredom once someone actually replies. None of that is wicked. It’s a loop, and the card shows it to you.

When you’re together, it can be strong physical chemistry, and nothing more worrying than that. It can also be the side of attachment that tightens: jealousy, checking, needing to know where the other person is. Or staying for reasons that have stopped being love — the flat, the routine, the fear of being alone at forty. Habits can be chains too. The same argument, word for word, every few weeks, as if you were both reading from a script.

After a break-up, the Devil is often the tie that hasn’t been cut. Messages at midnight. Sleeping together “one last time” for the fifth time. Watching their stories while telling friends you’ve moved on. If this is you, there’s no shame in it. It’s Earth doing what Earth does: holding on to what had value. The card only adds one thing — the loop around your neck is wider than it feels.

One thing to say gently and clearly. If the attachment is to someone who frightens you, hurts you, or controls your money, your phone or who you see, that isn’t a question for a card. Talk to someone you trust, and reach out to a professional or a specialist support line where you live. The cards can hold what you feel; they can’t keep you safe, and they shouldn’t be asked to. I’ve written about [where tarot stops and other doors open](/blog/tarot-and-mental-health/).

## “Does he still want me?” — and the question under it {#let-go}

People bring the Devil with two questions, often in the same breath: does he still want me, and why can’t I let go? The first is about someone who isn’t in the room, so I don’t read on it — their desire is theirs to say. The second is the one worth staying with, and the card is generous with it.

Asking a deck again and again about the same person can become a chain of its own. Some people arrive at a reading almost fidgety, as if they needed it, and what’s running through them is a high dose of doubt. When I see that, I usually don’t read. What they need is to breathe, to make a decision, or to go through the thing they’re afraid of, and one more card would only feed the doubt ([why stepping away from readings can help](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/)). If you pull a card every morning about the same person, that’s the Devil showing up in your practice rather than in your spread. Take a break from your cards now and then. They’ll still be there, and so will you.

The kinder question is: “What am I getting from this that I’m afraid to lose?” Not “what’s wrong with me”, but what this attachment gives you — reassurance, excitement, the feeling of mattering to someone, a reason not to face something else. Once you can name it, you can look for it somewhere that doesn’t hold you by the neck.

## The chain starting to slip {#reversed}

Reversed, the Devil is often the chain starting to slip. You notice the loop and lift a hand to it. You leave the phone in another room for an evening. You say “no” to the message at midnight, once, then twice. It’s rarely dramatic; it looks like small decisions repeated.

In a couple, the slipping chain can be a good sign. The two of you notice the script — the same argument, the same sulk, the same making-up — and one evening one of you laughs halfway through it. The pattern loses its grip once it’s been seen together. Nothing has to end for the Devil to loosen; sometimes it’s the habit that leaves, and the people stay.

It can also be the same attachment in excess: doubling down, hiding it better, calling it a choice. “I could stop whenever I want” is the reversed Devil’s favourite sentence. The question for this position is honest and kind at once: what would you do this week if you believed the chain was loose?

## Three cards that change the chain {#with-other-cards}

{{< cards "ar08 sw08 cu02" >}}

Next to Strength — a woman with her hands calmly on a lion’s jaws — it’s desire held rather than obeyed. Not killed, not denied: guided, with a gentle hand. A very good card to see beside this one.

Next to the Eight of Swords, the same message comes twice. In my notes, the Eight of Swords is being a prisoner of yourself while you hold every tool — you can take the blindfold off. With the Devil, the chains and the blindfold are both looser than they look; [the Eight of Swords in love](/blog/eight-of-swords-tarot-love/) reads well beside this page.

Next to the Two of Cups, it’s a strong bond where the wanting has started to make the decisions. Lovely when both people choose it; worth a second look when one of them can no longer say why they stay.

## One question, written before you can tidy it {#one-question}

Try one card, one question: “What would I do if I knew the chain was loose?” Write the first answer that comes, before you’ve had time to make it sensible. That answer is usually the one.

If you want to go further with what you keep pushing away, my [introduction to shadow work](/blog/shadow-work-beginners/) is a good next step. And if the Devil came up next to the Lovers, read [the Lovers in love](/blog/lovers-tarot-love/) too: the same couple, nine cards apart, one choosing and one held.
