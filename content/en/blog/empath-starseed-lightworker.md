An empath is someone who feels other people’s emotions more strongly than most. A starseed is someone believed to have lived on other planets or planes before being born on Earth. A lightworker works with the light around us to heal, and your higher self is your eternal self, the one that knows everything you’ve lived.

Here are those words and a few neighbours, defined plainly, each with an example. Where I have a remark of my own, it comes after, clearly as mine.

## Empath, starseed, lightworker: words for people {#people}

- *Empath.* Someone with a higher capacity than most to feel other people’s feelings. It can be trained, and trauma can make you more sensitive. Say you walk into a kitchen where two people have just argued: nobody says a word, and within a minute your own shoulders are up and your throat is tight. Someone who calls themselves an empath would say they picked up the room. The downside is absorbing an environment that isn’t good for you, if you don’t know yourself well enough to tell which feelings are yours. In my teaching notes I set it beside empathy, which is feeling and understanding someone else’s emotions *while keeping your own*. My remark: I find the word overused.
- *Starseed.* Someone believed to have lived on other planes or planets before being born on Earth, and who remembers it at some point in their life. Picture a person who has always felt slightly foreign in their own family, and who one day reads a description of starseeds and recognises themselves in it. Online you’ll see starseed types named after star systems, such as Pleiadian or Arcturian. My remark: you don’t pick your starseed type from a video. In the definition itself, it’s something you remember, not something you choose.
- *Lightworker.* Someone who works with the light around us to heal: themselves, other people, places. For example, someone who, after a tense meeting, imagines the room filling with light until it feels clear again, and calls that lightwork. My remark: I know little about it, and I’ve found it can be risky if you don’t know what you’re doing. If it draws you, learn from someone who can explain what they do and why, and go slowly.

## Higher self and spirit guides: words for what’s with you {#higher-self}

- *Higher self.* Your eternal self: the one that knows everything you’ve lived and accepts you more than anyone does. Your ultimate you. I think of it as a library of everything about you, one you can draw from — more on some days than others. Say you’re stuck before a decision and ask yourself what the version of you who has already lived through it would say, and the answer comes back calmer than your usual voice. People who use the word would call that listening to your higher self.
- *Spirit guides.* In many spiritual practices, beings believed to accompany a person through life and help them: an ancestor, an animal, an angel, or a presence with no name. A reader who feels nudged towards one card rather than another might say a guide was involved. My remark: not everything is about spirits. Sometimes it’s psychological, sometimes it’s more, and sometimes the right door is [a therapist’s](/disclaimers/#therapy). And if you try to call spirits with tarot cards, remember that you don’t know who you’re calling.

## Dimensions and vortex: words for places {#dimensions-vortex}

- *Dimensions.* The different planes we live in. We perceive a range of things and not all of it, the way our eyes see a range of colours with three kinds of cones, and our ears miss sounds that are too high or too low. A dog hears a whistle you can’t; people who speak of dimensions mean that there’s more around us than our range picks up.
- *Vortex.* Something like a door. From my own experience, it looks like being in the middle of a torus — the ring shape of a doughnut. Someone who says a spot in a forest feels like a vortex usually means it feels like a passage, a place where things seem to flow through.

## How to hold these words {#start}

Use them the way you’d use any glossary: to understand what someone means when they say them. **A word can describe what you feel; it can’t tell you who you are.**

If one of them describes something you live, the quietest first step I know is sitting still for two minutes and noticing what’s yours. I’ve written it up for people who are sure they can’t: [meditation for people who think they can’t meditate](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/).
