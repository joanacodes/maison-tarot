No, you don't have to be given your first tarot deck, and buying your own brings no bad luck. My first deck happened to be a present, and it would have read exactly the same if I'd paid for it myself. The reading never came from who bought the box.

## Where the gifted-deck rule comes from {#the-myth}

The belief goes like this: your first tarot deck should be a gift. Buy it yourself and it will bring bad luck, or it won't "work", or it won't really be yours.

It's a belief, not a rule. No tarot authority issues it, and there's no book of law it comes from. It passes from reader to reader the way "don't open an umbrella indoors" does, and it carries the same weight.

The reasons people give for it are kind ones. A gift carries the goodwill of the person who chose it. A deck should come to you when you're ready, rather than be grabbed. I understand the appeal: a present feels like a sign, and starting something new feels better with someone behind you.

None of that is harmful in itself. It only becomes a problem when it stops someone from starting. **A rule that keeps you waiting for permission to learn isn't protecting you; it's only keeping you waiting.**

## How I got my first deck {#my-first-deck}

Mine was a Christmas present from friends, picked off my wishlist. I'd thought they were giving me an astrology book.

I started reading on 26 December 2020, the day after. I'd assumed I would need some training before anything landed. My first reading landed anyway.

So yes, on paper I'm an example of the myth. But that deck was on my wishlist because I'd chosen it. My friends did the paying; I'd done the wanting. Had they gone for the astrology book, a deck bought in the shops would have read the same way, because what happened in those first readings came from looking, letting go and saying what came, not from the wrapping paper.

## Is it bad luck to buy your own tarot deck? {#bad-luck}

No. For buying a deck to bring bad luck, the deck itself would have to be doing the work, and it isn't. The reading comes from you, not from the cardboard, and certainly not from the receipt.

People who ask this are rarely superstitious so much as careful. They want to start well, and that's a good instinct. Starting well, though, means starting.

You don't have to take my word for it, either. Keep a notebook with your bought deck: what you said in each reading, what the person confirmed, held or missed. After twenty readings, [your own count](/blog/tarot-journal/) will tell you whether the receipt made any difference.

If the belief still sits uneasily with you, there are gentle ways round it. Put the deck on a wishlist, as I did, and let a birthday take care of it. Or ask a friend to come and choose it with you. Either way, don't wait months to begin; a borrowed deck will do in the meantime.

There's another "gifted" myth, by the way: that you have to be born with something to read at all. [You don't need psychic abilities](/blog/do-you-need-psychic-abilities/), and anyone who practises can learn.

## Second-hand decks, cleansing, other people's hands {#neighbouring-rules}

The gifted-deck rule has neighbours, and they get the same answer.

A second-hand deck has nothing to clear. If it's in good condition and the scenes are all there, it reads like a new one.

Some people also feel a new deck has to be "bonded" with before it reads: slept with under the pillow, carried everywhere for a week. If that ritual comforts you, enjoy it. A week of drawing one card each morning does the useful part of the job, though: you learn the pictures.

Cleansing gets the same answer; [why I stopped selling sage, and what I do instead](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/) goes through it.

Letting other people touch your cards is a matter of comfort or hygiene, not luck, and [whether to let others handle your deck](/blog/let-others-touch-tarot-cards/) has its own page. And if a deck ever feels "off", it's usually you who needs a break, not the cards.

## Buying a deck: what to look for {#buying}

If you're buying for yourself, start with a Rider-Waite-Smith: Pamela Colman Smith gave all 78 cards a full picture, minors included, so there's something to read on night one; and [here's why I start everyone on it](/blog/best-tarot-deck-for-beginners/). Any edition with the original drawings will do.

If you're buying for someone else, a deck is a lovely present, and their wishlist is the kindest guide. Give it to someone who has asked for one, or hinted. A deck nobody wanted is an object on a shelf; a deck someone wanted is a beginning. The [tarot gift guide](/blog/tarot-gift-guide/) has the rest.

Then, once the deck is in your hands, skip the ritual and spend a first week getting to know it. [This is how I'd spend that week](/blog/new-tarot-deck/).
