The full moon spread I suggest is three cards in a row, small enough to fit on a café napkin: what you can now see, what you can put down, what you keep. No candle, no crystal, no windowsill. A deck, ten minutes and a pen.

## What the full moon is, and why it suits a reading {#full-moon}

A full moon is when the Moon sits opposite the Sun, as seen from Earth, so the whole face we see is lit. It comes round about every 29 and a half days, and it always falls in the sign opposite the Sun's: while the Sun is in Scorpio, the full moon is in Taurus.

In astrology, the full moon is read as a high point. What started at the new moon reaches its peak, comes into the open or comes to an end. Plenty of people use it as a monthly moment to let something go.

That's the only reason I tie this spread to it. Once a month, it's a good night to ask what you can see now that you couldn't a month ago. Whether the Moon changes the cards themselves is another question, and I go through it in [moon phases and tarot](/blog/moon-phases-and-tarot/).

## The three positions, explained {#positions}

Lay the cards left to right.

1. *What can I see now?* Full moon, full light. This card shows something that has become clear: finished, revealed, or obvious at last. It's a picture of now, not a prediction.
2. *What can I put down?* The weight you've been carrying that has done its time. Not “what must I cut out of my life”, which is a harsh question to ask on a Tuesday night. A gentler one: what can rest?
3. *What do I keep?* What has been worth it this month, and what you'd like to carry into the next new moon.

Three cards, because three tell a small story and still fit on a napkin. A fourth would start to invite “one more, to clarify”, and [that extra card usually muddies the first three](/blog/the-clarifier-card/).

**The spread has no future position, on purpose.** Now and then I do read ahead, but I'd rather look at the present: a future worth having grows out of a present you've got both feet in.

## How to lay it out {#how-to}

Six steps, about ten minutes.

1. Pick a night within a day or so of the full moon. Indoors is fine. Clouds are fine.
2. Shuffle any way you like. There's no wrong way.
3. Draw three cards and lay them in a row on whatever's in front of you: a napkin, a placemat, the cover of a book.
4. Before you look anything up, read each card through one lens: its number, its element or its colours.
5. Say one sentence per card out loud.
6. Write the date, the three cards and the three sentences in your notebook.

You don't need candles. If you like one, light it because it's nice, not because the spread needs it. You don't need to sit under the Moon or leave your deck in its light either.

## An example, step by step {#example}

*What I can see now:* [the Eight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-swords/). A figure blindfolded and bound, eight swords planted around her, under a grey sky. Eight is control; grey is the colour of no particular will. My note on it: you are your own jailer, and you already have what you need to get free. What's now in plain view: the trap was partly self-made, and the knots are loose.

*What can be put down:* the Ten of Wands. Someone bent under ten wands, walking towards a house. Ten is a threshold, one stage handing over to the next; Fire is energy and drive. My note for this card asks you to keep your creative fire going without piling on more. What can rest: a few of those wands.

*What to keep:* [the Nine of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-pentacles/). A woman in her garden among heavy vines, a bird on her gloved hand. Nine is the finish line; Earth is anything you value. My note: take pleasure in what arrives, and be grateful for what’s already yours. What to keep: the thing already built that nobody, including you, has stopped to enjoy.

On the napkin, that becomes three short lines: “You've seen that the knot was looser than it felt. You can set down some of the jobs you took on. And what you've already made deserves an evening of you enjoying it.” Then score it, with the person or on your own: tick or cross.

## Once a month is plenty {#once-a-month}

One full moon reading a month is plenty, and between two full moons the deck can stay in the drawer.

For the other half of the cycle, the [new moon spread](/blog/new-moon-tarot-spread/) asks the opposite question: what to start. And when a lunar eclipse falls on the full moon, the same three cards work; [reading in eclipse season](/blog/eclipse-season-tarot/) explains why I'd keep it to one reading.

Next full moon, before you draw, reread last month's page in [your reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/). Mark each sentence: held, missed, or too early to say. That page is where the spread starts teaching you something.
