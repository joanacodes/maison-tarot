A chart can describe how two people tend to need, love and argue. It can’t tell you whether they’ll work. And Sun-sign compatibility — Leo with Libra, never with Scorpio — uses a small part of the picture: you’re a mix of everything, not one sign, and so is the other person.

## What zodiac compatibility is {#definition}

Zodiac compatibility is the idea that some signs get on more easily than others. In its simplest form, you compare two Sun signs by their element. Signs of the same element are said to flow together; Fire and Air feed each other, Earth and Water hold each other; Fire with Water, or Earth with Air, are said to rub.

| Element | Signs | Classic match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Aries, Leo, Sagittarius | Air |
| Earth | Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn | Water |
| Air | Gemini, Libra, Aquarius | Fire |
| Water | Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces | Earth |

Take a Leo and a Libra: Fire and Air. In my [element notes](/library/four-elements/), Fire is impulse and creation, Air is thinking and deciding. The air feeds the fire — and, unmastered, fans it.

The fuller version is called synastry: you lay two complete birth charts side by side and compare them placement by placement. Where does one person’s Venus fall in the other’s chart? How does one Moon sit with the other’s Saturn? Some astrologers also build a composite chart, a single chart made from the midpoints of the two.

## What a chart can say about two people {#what-it-can-say}

If you compare two charts, start with the placements that describe how someone lives with another person, not how they look from outside.

In my image of the chart as a house, the Sun is the inside, the rising sign the outside, and the Moon your own room. It says what each of you needs to feel safe. Venus says how each of you shows love. Mars says how each of you wants, and how you fight.

Then Saturn, Lilith and Chiron. When I tested charts box by box, these three held 75 to 90% of the time, far above the 30 to 40% of everything else. They describe each person’s hardest lesson, the wound they keep circling and what they were told to hide — which is where I’d look first for what two people might bump into. My test was done one chart at a time, though: it says nothing about pairs, and I have no compatibility numbers. The [full test, and how I ran it](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), is in its own article.

Say one person has the Moon in Capricorn and the other the Moon in Leo. One may need quiet and a plan when things go wrong; the other may need to be seen and fussed over. **A chart can’t tell two people whether they’ll last; it can give them a better question to ask each other.** Here: “When you’re low, what helps — being left alone, or being made a fuss of?”

## “I don’t date Scorpios” {#i-dont-date-scorpios}

This sentence is in my notes on the ways astrology gets misused, and I understand where it usually comes from. Someone got hurt, and the sign became a shorthand for the hurt. That’s human.

But it leaves out most of the person. You’d be ruling out roughly one person in twelve for the month they were born in. And the Scorpio you’re avoiding also has a Moon, a rising sign and a Venus, perhaps in Taurus, perhaps in Libra, that you’ve never looked at. You’re a mix of everything; so are they.

In tarot, Scorpio’s card is Death — the card everyone fears and which, read calmly, is about transformation, not about dying. The sign gets the same treatment. In my notes, [Scorpio](/library/astrology/scorpio/) knows exactly where to sting, and is also a very good observer and a very good healer.

The same goes in the other direction. “It’s because I’m a Taurus” is the twin of “I don’t date Scorpios”. If something keeps hurting the people around you, look at yourself rather than at your sign; I come back to it in [when astrology becomes an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).

## Look up their chart? Ask first {#ask-first}

Reading someone’s chart without their consent is a bit like going through their bedroom while they’re out. And if you have a crush, don’t look up their chart: let the magic happen for the two of you. I explain why in [looking up someone’s birth chart without asking](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/).

Comparing charts works best done together, both charts open on the table, as a conversation rather than a verdict.

## A gentler way to use it {#gentler-way}

1. Each of you draws up your chart — date, place and, for the Moon and rising sign, the time of birth. I use Astro-Seek.
2. Read your partner’s Moon and Venus aloud to them. They tick or cross: does it sound like them?
3. Keep the ticks as conversation starters. Drop the crosses without arguing for them.
4. Ask one question from each tick, and listen to the answer more than to the chart.

If you’d rather put cards on the table, the [love spread I use](/blog/love-tarot-spread/) looks at the relationship you have, not at whether the stars approve of it.
