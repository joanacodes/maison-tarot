You don’t need crystals to read tarot. A deck, a person who tells you nothing and your own attention are the whole kit. Crystals are lovely objects, and if you like them, keep them on the table — they aren’t what makes a reading land.

## What people use crystals for in tarot {#what-people-do}

In tarot practice, crystals are polished stones or mineral points that readers keep with their deck or set on the table while they read. The idea behind it is that each stone carries a quality and passes it on to the cards, or to the reader.

You’ll see the same pairings again and again in shops and on social media: amethyst for intuition, clear quartz to amplify a reading, black tourmaline for protection, selenite to clear a deck between readings.

In practice it looks like this. Say someone lays out three cards about a relationship and places a piece of rose quartz above the spread, because the question is about love. Another reader leaves a stone on top of the deck overnight. Another holds one in her left hand while she shuffles.

People who love crystals often describe a feeling of calm, of being held, of the reading properly starting once the stone is down. I believe the feeling. What follows is what I’ve seen change a reading, and what I haven’t.

## Do crystals make a reading more accurate? {#accuracy}

Not in anything I’ve been able to see. In 2021 I sold sage, candles and “channelled” cards, the kind of things people buy to go around a deck. I stopped, because nothing in any of it improved a reading. I see crystals the same way: lovely to have, not what makes a reading land.

What I have seen change a reading is already at the table. Whether the person told me anything before I started. Whether I’d been reading for two hours and was tired. Whether I was trying to be right instead of saying what came. None of those can be fixed with an object, and none of them need one.

Cards aren’t magic, you are — and the same goes for whatever sits next to them. **A reading lands because of the person reading and the person being read, not because of what’s on the table.**

I can’t prove a stone does nothing for you. I can tell you that what I teach doesn’t need one.

## What crystals are nice for {#nice-for}

Plenty, as long as you know which job you’re giving them.

A paperweight. At an outdoor table or by an open window, cards fly. A heavy stone on the deck is a perfectly good job for a crystal, and an honest one.

A signal to start. Some people need a small gesture that says *now I’m reading*: clearing the table, putting a stone down, making a cup of tea. The gesture helps you let go, and letting go is the only real practice in tarot. A stone does that well. So does the tea.

Beauty. I see collecting tarot decks as collecting art: each card is a picture you can read messages through. Stones can live in the same place in your life, as things you love looking at. That’s reason enough to own something.

A colour on the table. If you choose a stone for its colour, keep that eye: colour is the first lens I teach, because yellow says joy before you know which card you’re holding. The [colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/) puts it to work on the cards themselves.

## When “you need this stone” becomes a sales line {#sales-line}

The only place I’d be careful is when a crystal is sold as a condition. You need this one to read safely. This one to be protected. This one, or the reading won’t work.

A beginner who hears that spends money before drawing a single card, and starts to believe the ability lives in an object. And when a reader tells you you’re in danger and the protection happens to be for sale, that’s a hook, not a reading. It’s one of the [red flags I’d watch for in any reader](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/).

Most people who ask about protection stones are asking for something gentler underneath: to feel safe. I wrote about [what protection spells are really asking for](/blog/protection-spells/), and what helps.

## What to get instead if you’re starting {#starting}

One deck. I start everyone on the 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith, because every card has a scene you can read without a book; [here’s why that deck](/blog/best-tarot-deck-for-beginners/). Then a notebook, to write what you said and what was confirmed. That’s the whole shopping list.

No ritual is needed to begin. If you want a first week with a new deck, I’d rather you [look at every card than cleanse it](/blog/new-tarot-deck/) — and the same reasoning is behind [why I stopped selling sage to cleanse decks](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/).

If you love your crystals, keep them where they are. Then turn the first card and notice that the reading starts with you. The [learn tarot page](/learn-tarot/) is where to go next, one card at a time.
