Lenormand is a deck of 36 simple, everyday pictures — a rider, a ship, a house, a letter — read in pairs and lines, like words in a sentence. Tarot is 78 cards, each a scene with several layers, read one image at a time. Put briefly: Lenormand is at its best on what's happening around you, tarot on what's happening in you. I read and teach tarot, so this is an honest comparison from one side of the table.

## What is a Lenormand deck? {#what-is-lenormand}

A Lenormand deck has 36 numbered cards, each showing one object, animal or figure with a fixed name. In order: Rider, Clover, Ship, House, Tree, Clouds, Snake, Coffin, Bouquet, Scythe, Whip, Birds, Child, Fox, Bear, Stars, Stork, Dog, Tower, Garden, Mountain, Crossroads, Mice, Heart, Ring, Book, Letter, Man, Woman, Lilies, Sun, Moon, Key, Fish, Anchor, Cross. Many decks also print a small playing card in one corner.

The name is a little misleading. Marie-Anne Lenormand (1772–1843) was a Paris fortune-teller, famous in Napoleon's time, but there's no evidence she designed or even used this deck. Its model is a German parlour game from around 1799, *Das Spiel der Hoffnung* (“The Game of Hope”), designed by Johann Kaspar Hechtel of Nuremberg, as the [Horniman Museum's catalogue](https://www.horniman.ac.uk/object/1970.18/) explains. Publishers put her name on it after her death, because her name sold cards. Her own story is on [Marie-Anne Lenormand's page](/library/tarot-figures/marie-anne-lenormand/).

Don't confuse it with the *Grand Jeu de Mlle Lenormand*, a different 54-card deck first published in Paris in the 1840s. When people say “Lenormand” today, they usually mean the 36 cards, sometimes called the Petit Lenormand.

## How Lenormand is read {#how-lenormand-is-read}

Lenormand cards are read together, like a sentence. A common convention is that the first card is the subject and the second describes it. Many readers would read Ship next to House as a move or a trip home; Letter next to Ring as a written agreement; Clouds next to Sun as confusion that clears.

The meanings are short and mostly concrete. Dog is a friend. Mice are something nibbling away: stress, a slow loss. Key is a solution. Heart is love. Most Lenormand readers don't use reversals.

Spreads are lines of three or five cards, or the Grand Tableau: all 36 cards laid out at once, often in four rows of nine, where each card's distance from the others counts. **Lenormand is a vocabulary: you learn the words, then you learn to make sentences.**

## How tarot is read differently {#how-tarot-is-read}

A tarot deck has 22 major arcana and 56 minor arcana, and in the Rider-Waite-Smith each card is a full scene: figures, colours, a number, an element, a posture. A single card can hold a whole reading, which is why I teach with five lenses instead of a list of meanings.

Take the [Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/). At first glance it's all loss: a mourner in black facing three tipped-over cups. Yet behind that bowed back, a second pair has stayed upright. Lenormand's Mice would tell you, quickly, that something is being lost. The tarot card shows you how it feels, and what's still there.

## Two kinds of question {#two-kinds-of-question}

The difference shows in the questions each deck answers well. “What's going on with the flat?” is a Lenormand question: it wants events, people, a sequence. “Why does moving scare me so much?” is a tarot question: it wants the inside of the situation.

Neither deck is an oracle deck in the modern sense, by the way: both have a fixed structure that every reader shares, which most oracle decks don't. I explain that difference in [tarot vs oracle cards](/blog/tarot-vs-oracle-cards/).

One gentle word about the concrete cards. Lenormand has a Coffin, a Scythe and a Cross, and because the deck feels so literal, it's tempting to ask it whether someone will get better, or whether a court case will go your way. I understand why: it's usually worry, or love. But whichever deck you hold, questions of health, dying, pregnancy, legal disputes or financial choices stay outside my readings, because one careless sentence on those subjects can hurt; a doctor or a lawyer is the person to ask. A reading can still look at the worry itself, and at how you're bearing it. My [refusals](/disclaimers/#refusals) apply to both decks.

## Which one to learn first? {#which-first}

If your questions are mostly about events and you like a fixed vocabulary, Lenormand may suit you. If you want to read feelings, people and the choices in front of you, start with tarot. Learning one doesn't spoil the other; plenty of readers keep both on the table, and some add [runes or a pendulum](/blog/runes-pendulum-i-ching/) too.

I can only speak for tarot, which I read and teach. Here's a check you can run yourself: draw [one tarot card a day](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/) for a week, describe what you see, and you'll know quickly whether scenes speak to you. If they do, [the learning path](/learn-tarot/) is the next step.
