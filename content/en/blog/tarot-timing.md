Sometimes, roughly: a stage, an order, “not yet”. Rarely a date I’d stand behind. Timing is where I miss most, and I’d rather say that before you ask than hand you a date that feels precise and isn’t.

## Can tarot predict when something will happen? {#short-answer}

Not reliably, in my experience. A card shows a situation and the state of the person in it. It doesn’t show a calendar.

What I read is mostly the present: what’s moving in someone’s life at the moment we sit down. How fast that turns into events depends on decisions, on other people, on chance. The future rarely arrives in the shape anyone imagined, and it rarely arrives on the day anyone predicted.

My very first reading for a stranger, on a Discord server, came true three or four days later. I found that out from her text message, not from the cards.

## How tarot timing systems work {#timing-systems}

A tarot timing system is a convention that turns a card into a length of time or a point in the year. Readers have built several.

The best known give each suit a season. Others read the number on a card as days, weeks or months, depending on the suit. Others use the astrology printed on the minor arcana: each card from 2 to 10 carries a planet in a sign, so the card points to a stretch of the year. Say the Three of Wands falls. It carries the Sun in Aries, so a reader using that system would place it in early spring.

The systems don’t agree with each other. Depending on whom you read, Wands are spring or summer. They make a reading sound precise, and **precise is not the same as right — a date is the part of a reading people hold on to longest, so it’s the part that has to be most solid.**

## The voice that taught me about timing {#the-voice}

This wasn’t a tarot reading, and I tell the whole story in [why a reading can be wrong](/blog/why-a-reading-can-be-wrong/). The short version: after my closest friend died, I was enraged, and I asked silently, “Who’s next?” A man’s voice gave another friend’s name and said this friend “is going to have an accident”. Two weeks later, that friend’s car was hit by a truck. My friend is alive and well today.

The event came, but I had asked who would die next, and on that the information was wrong in what mattered. The voice hadn’t said when, or how it would end. What I kept is a shelf in my head labelled *sceptical, but if it happens, I was warned*. Messages go on that shelf. Dates especially.

## Why timing is where I miss most {#why-i-miss}

Because a reading shows readiness, not scheduling. A card can show that something is ripe, or blocked, or ending. It can’t see the other person’s decisions, the job that opens a month later than planned, the train that’s cancelled.

And because of what a date does to the person who receives it. Say someone is told “by spring” about a new relationship. They spend the winter waiting, and don’t notice the person who turns up in November. When spring passes, the reading becomes a disappointment, or a reason to come back and ask again — the same loop I describe in [what the question ‘will they come back?’ does to you](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/).

The “what” and the “when” of a reading are two separate claims, and one can hold while the other slips. If you keep a notebook, give the “when” its own line. Otherwise a reading that got the event right and the month wrong gets written down as a hit, and you never see where your misses gather.

When a date passes, I don’t say the cards meant a different spring. A miss is a miss, it goes in the notebook, and I’ve written about [what to do when a reading is wrong](/blog/tarot-reading-wrong/).

## What I read instead of a date {#instead-of-a-date}

The stage. The numbers on the cards tell you where you are in a cycle, which is often what “when?” was really asking: is it starting, is it in the middle of the fight, is it nearly done?

| Number | Stage | What it says about “when” |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start of a cycle | Early days; get ready to learn |
| 5 | Conflict, trying, mistakes | In the thick of it; avoid useless fights |
| 7 | Reflection, patience, harvesting | Not yet; everything is temporary |
| 9 | Finish line, last efforts | Close; keep moving |
| 10 | Transition, the corridor between two rooms | One thing ends, the next one starts |

The Eight of Wands is the card many readers treat as “fast”. I see wands that are either flying or falling — glass half full or half empty — and the picture doesn’t say which. The Wheel of Fortune, to me, says it’s up to you to turn the wheel: you create part of your own luck, and part of your own timing.

So the kinder question is rarely “when?”. It’s “what needs to happen first?” or “what can I do while I wait?”. Those have answers you can act on today.
