I want to be careful with this one, because the wrong version of it is everywhere. "Tarot is therapy." "Tarot heals." "Use the cards for your mental health." Some of that is marketing, some of it is sincere, and all of it puts a deck of pictures where a trained person should be. Here is what I've actually seen at the table, and the lines I keep.

## What a reading can do {#what-it-can-do}

It can give someone a picture for a feeling they haven't named. That's genuinely useful. A person who has been "off" for months looks at the Eight of Swords — a figure bound and blindfolded, with the ropes loose and the way out open behind her — and says "that's it, that's exactly it". Nothing has been diagnosed. Something has been seen. Sometimes seeing it is enough to move.

It can also give someone permission to say a thing out loud, because the card said it first. "You've been angrier than you've let on" is easier to hear from a picture than from a friend.

That's the whole of the therapeutic effect, in my experience: naming and permission. Both matter. Neither is treatment.

## What a reading cannot do {#what-it-cannot-do}

It cannot tell you why you feel what you feel. When a reader says "the cards show this comes from your childhood" or "this is about your mother", they have stepped from reading into interpretation of a life they don't know, and they are usually wrong in ways that stick. I don't supply causes. I reflect what's on the table and I ask what the person sees.

It cannot hold a crisis. Someone in acute distress — not sad, not stuck, but in the place where the ground has gone — does not need a picture. They need a person trained to be there, and possibly a doctor. A reading in that state does one of two things: it lands and makes everything heavier, or it misses and confirms that nothing helps. I've seen both. I don't do it any more.

It cannot replace a diagnosis, a medication, or a professional. Not everything is about spirits; sometimes it's psychological, sometimes it's more, and the honest reader knows where their competence ends. Mine ends at the picture.

## The people I don't read for {#who-i-dont-read}

Someone who arrives fidgety, on their fourth reading this month, asking the same question. That isn't a tarot situation; [it's a dependency](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), and another reading feeds it.

Someone who tells me — or shows me — that they're in danger. I stop, I say why, and I give them the number for where they are. I don't read "to see what the cards say about it". The cards are cardboard. This is a person.

Someone who is clearly not sleeping, not eating, or not in the room. A reading requires two people present; if one of them isn't, it's a performance for an audience of one, and the wrong one.

## How I say it {#how-i-say-it}

Plainly, and without drama. "I'm not going to read for you tonight. Not because of anything you did — because I think what you need isn't a reading, and I'd rather say that than pretend." Then the door: a therapist, a GP, a crisis line, depending on what I've seen. I don't insist. I say it once, kindly, and I mean it.

Most people are relieved. Some are angry. The angry ones are usually the ones who most needed to hear it, and I'd rather be the reader who refused than the one who read.

## For readers: three habits {#for-readers}

1. **Have the number.** Whatever country you read in, know the crisis line before you need it. Saying "I'll look it up" in that moment is a failure.
2. **Read less when it's heavy.** After a difficult reading, don't take the next one. Two hours of readings is a lot; four is too many for anyone to do honestly.
3. **Refuse the word "healing".** Not because nothing heals, but because promising it is the beginning of every harmful practice in this field. You can say "this might help you see something". You cannot say "this will heal you". [The disclaimers page says so](/disclaimers/#therapy) because I've watched what happens when readers don't.

## The sentence {#the-sentence}

Not everything is about spirits. I say it to sitters, to students, and to myself, because the pull in this work is always toward making every difficulty mystical — a block, an energy, a past life, a lesson from the universe. Sometimes a person is depressed. Sometimes they're grieving. Sometimes they need a doctor. The cards won't say so; a reader who's paying attention will.

If you're reading this because you're the one who's struggling: a reading can wait. The door I'd point you to is a professional's, and there is no failure in walking through it. The cards will be there afterwards, and you'll read them better.
