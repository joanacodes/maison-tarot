Tarot reading etiquette is mostly ordinary good manners, plus two habits of its own: keep your question to yourself before the reading, and say honestly afterwards what landed. Everything else follows from consent and kindness, on both sides of the table.

Here’s how I’d put it, from the chair I sit in most often and from the one opposite.

## Before the reading {#before}

Come with a question in mind, and keep it there: no context, no “it’s about my ex”, no first name if we’re online. It feels strange, I know. It’s also the only way you’ll know afterwards whether the reading came from the cards or from what you told the reader; I explain why in [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/).

Leave other people out of it. A reading is for the person in the chair, not for a partner, a boss or a mother who isn’t in the room. Reading someone’s energy without their say-so is close to opening their drawers uninvited, and nobody would want that done to them.

A few more things worth checking:

- Ask the reader what they don’t read on. At Maison Tarot the list runs to seven: health and death, pregnancy, cases still before a court, money choices, curses, and anyone not in the room. Knowing it beforehand saves you a hard stop halfway through.
- Give it time. A reading squeezed between two meetings is rushed on both sides, and you’ll spend it with one eye on the clock.
- Come as you are. No sage, no candles, no special clothes. Nothing of that kind has ever improved a reading I’ve given.
- Notice how you’re arriving. If this would be your fourth reading this month on the same question, the kindest thing may be not to have it.

## During the reading {#during}

Keep a calm face, as far as you can. Every nod, sigh and laugh is information, and a reader can steer by it without meaning to.

Say the reader turns over the Three of Swords and you gasp.

You’ve just told them the card hit something painful, and probably what kind of pain. That’s the raw material of [cold reading](/blog/cold-reading-vs-tarot/), even with a perfectly sincere reader. **Your reactions can do the reading for the reader, so give them as few as you can.** Not because it’s a test, but because you want to know afterwards what came from where.

Ask before you touch someone else’s deck. There’s nothing mystical in it; it’s their tool, the way you’d ask before picking up someone’s guitar.

If something feels too heavy, say so. A good reader will slow down, change their words, or stop. Some things aren’t ready to be heard on a given day, and that’s allowed.

Put the phone face down. And if you’d like to remember what was said, ask whether you can take notes.

## After the reading {#after}

This is where most of the value is, and where most people go quiet.

Say plainly what landed and what didn’t. “That part was right, that part wasn’t me at all.” A reader who counts needs both, and a miss named honestly is worth more than a polite nod. If a reader turns your “no” into “you’ll understand later”, take note of that too.

Let it sit. Some readings make sense on the spot; some take days. [My very first reading for a stranger](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/) “missed” on the day and came true a few days later. If something clicks later, telling your reader is a lovely thing to do.

Write it down the same day, before your memory tidies it up. Within a week most people remember the parts that landed and forget the rest; a note keeps both, which is the only way to judge the reading fairly later.

Don’t ask the same question again straight away. A second draw on the same worry usually feeds the worry, not the answer; I go into it in [asking tarot the same question twice](/blog/ask-tarot-the-same-question-twice/).

And then decide for yourself. A reading isn’t an instruction.

## If you’re the one reading {#for-readers}

The reader’s manners matter as much, and they’re a little stricter.

1. Ask permission before you read anyone, every time.
2. Say what you don’t read on before you start, gently and early.
3. Tell the person they can stop you whenever they like.
4. Say “that’s a miss” when it’s a miss. No reshuffle, no extra card to rescue it.
5. Watch your own tiredness: interpreting takes energy. If you leave a session [feeling drained](/blog/energy-after-tarot-reading/), rest before the next one.
6. End the reading on purpose rather than letting it trail off — there’s a whole piece on [how to end a tarot reading well](/blog/how-to-end-a-tarot-reading/).

## The short version {#short-version}

Before: consent, silence, no absent people. During: a calm face, ask before touching, say stop if you need to. After: honest feedback, no repeat question, your own decision.
