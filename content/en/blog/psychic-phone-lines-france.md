On 30 October 2025, *Complément d'enquête*, France 2's long-running investigative programme, gave its evening to fortune-telling. The story that travelled furthest was a retired teacher's: 72 years old, €44,000 spent over five years on psychic readings by phone, all her savings. I won't write about her or name the platform. I'll take the mechanism apart, because the mechanism is what repeats, and it's the part you can learn to recognise.

## What the investigation showed {#what-it-showed}

Here are the facts as [franceinfo reported them](https://www.franceinfo.fr/economie/44-000-euros-depenses-dans-la-voyance-en-ligne-le-temoignage-d-une-victime-accusant-une-plateforme-de-lui-avoir-soutire-de-l-argent_7585364.html) from the programme. She called a phone platform described as one of the leaders of the French market. She never met any of the psychics. The model: ten minutes free, then €3.50 a minute, rising to €9.50 for the most expensive mediums. She says everything was done to push her to spend more.

Two former employees said call length was no accident: readers, they said, were trained to say very little in the first ten minutes. The team also filmed undercover in one of the platform's call centres in Tunisia. The platform, for its part, points to prices that are clearly displayed.

Let's do the sum nobody does on the phone. €44,000 over five years is about €730 a month. At €3.50 a minute, that's roughly three and a half hours of paid calls a month; with a €9.50 medium, about an hour and a quarter. In the first case, less than an hour a week. Nothing dramatic.

**€44,000 isn't one call too many; it's hundreds of calls that each, on their own, seemed reasonable.**

A word on what this doesn't prove. It's one testimony and two former employees, in a programme built to investigate abuses, and the platform defends itself. Plenty of people call a psychic line a few times, hang up and are fine. There are honest readers at the end of those lines too, doing their best inside a model they didn't design. The point isn't that every call is a con. It's that the model rewards length, and length is what hurt her.

## Premium-rate or card-billed: two models people confuse {#two-models}

In everyday French, people say *voyance surtaxée* — "surcharged psychic lines" — for all of it. There are actually two models, and they don't protect you in the same way.

The first is the premium-rate number, the French 08 numbers you see in small ads. You pay for the call plus a service charge set by the provider. For numbers starting 089, that service charge is capped at €0.80 a minute or €3 a call, as [France's National Consumer Institute explains](https://www.inc-conso.fr/content/telephone-la-tarification-des-numeros-sva-services-valeur-ajoutee). Half an hour still costs €24 in service charges, but there is a ceiling.

The second is the card-billed platform. You dial an ordinary number, give your card details, and the platform sets its own prices. The €3.50 a minute in the programme is more than four times the 089 cap, so this is clearly the second model, where the premium-rate ceiling doesn't apply.

**The difference matters when you act.** For an 08 number, France has a reverse directory (surmafacture.fr) that tells you who runs it, and since March 2018 operators have offered a free option to block some premium-rate numbers. For a card-billed platform, your bank and the saved card are where you act. If you're reading from elsewhere, the agencies change; the mechanism travels.

## The mechanisms that keep you on the line {#mechanisms}

What follows is my reading of the model, not the programme's conclusions. I've read cards since 2020; I know the other side of the table.

1. The free minutes. Ten free minutes look generous. If, as the former employees say, little is said in them, they become a waiting room: the reading starts when the meter does. My own rule is the reverse: [the person tells me nothing](/blog/the-silence-rule/), and I start talking at the first card.
2. Price as proof. When one psychic costs €3.50 and another €9.50, the dearer one passes for the better one. The price becomes a sign of quality, when all it measures is what the platform chose to charge.
3. The question left open. A reading that ends on "call me next week, things are about to move" invites a second call. A reading that ends, full stop, invites none.
4. Always open. Doubt keeps no office hours, and neither does a platform. At two in the morning, the voice on the line is often the only one answering.
5. The invisible payment. A saved card doesn't hurt. No cash leaves your hand, nothing is signed; you hang up, and the amount arrives later, mixed in with everything else on the statement.

**None of these mechanisms needs a psychic to lie about the cards.** That's what makes them so effective: they live in the billing model, not in the talent of whoever picks up. When someone comes to me with the same doubt again and again, I usually suggest a pause rather than another reading. A platform paid by the minute has every reason to do the opposite. The cruder tricks — curses, urgency, paid rituals — are in [the nine red flags of a fake reader](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/).

## What the case says about the French market {#the-market}

First, that it's big. In 2023 a French MP quoted estimates from a trade body, the Institut national des arts divinatoires: around 100,000 people practising, for €3–4 billion a year. That's the profession's own figure, not an official statistic, so treat it as an order of magnitude. The same text cited an Ifop poll from December 2020: one French adult in four says they have consulted a psychic at least once.

Second, that it's unregulated as such. Since 1 March 1994, fortune-telling in France has been a free commercial activity with no rules of its own. The government said so again in [its answer to that written question](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/q16/16-6608QE.htm), pointing abuses towards general law: fraud, exploitation of vulnerability, consumer protection. I've set out that framework, with the UK alongside, in [is tarot reading legal?](/blog/is-tarot-reading-legal/).

Third, and most important: when you sell by the minute, the commercial interest is the length of the call, not the quality of the answer. **When the product is the minute, a good reading is a long one.** That holds even for honest readers, who then work inside a frame that doesn't pay them to finish. [Pay-per-question and subscription readings](/blog/tarot-subscriptions/) carry the same problem in a different shape.

That's why I don't sell readings. I sell lessons; readings I give for free, in public. It isn't virtue. It's a way of never having a reason to keep you on the phone.

## If you, or someone you love, are caught in these calls {#what-to-do}

Nobody rings a psychic at two in the morning out of foolishness. People call out of worry, love or loneliness, because a voice is reassuring. There's no shame in it, and shame is exactly what keeps it going.

What helps, in practice:

1. Add it up. Take twelve months of bank statements and total the payments. The number often does more than any warning.
2. Remove the card saved on the platform, and ask your bank how to block payments to that merchant.
3. For a French 08 number, look the provider up on surmafacture.fr and ask your operator to block it.
4. Keep everything — invoices, messages, payments — and report the platform. In France that's SignalConso, run by the consumer protection authority.
5. If you think someone took advantage of a vulnerable person, that can be a criminal offence: it's a matter for a complaint, and for a lawyer or a consumer association.
6. Talk to someone. With a parent, avoid putting them on trial: ask what the calls give them — company, a voice, an answer — and look together for where else to find it.

If the question that makes you pick up the phone is about money, health or a court case, those are subjects [I leave out of my readings](/disclaimers/#refusals), because a misunderstood sentence costs too much there. A gentler and more useful question is: "What do I need to make this decision myself?" The cards can sit with that one, and the decision stays yours.

Sometimes the most useful thing is to put the phone, or the deck, down for a while. I've written about that in [why you should quit tarot reading, at least for a while](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).
