A spiritual awakening, in the way most people use the phrase, is a shift in how you see yourself and the world: things you took for granted stop holding, and you start paying attention to your inner life and to what you used to dismiss. I’ve used the word about myself. I also find it misused, and it makes me cringe a little. Both are true.

## What is a spiritual awakening? {#what-is-it}

The word is old. The title Buddha means “the awakened one”, and in that tradition awakening names a long, demanding path. Today the phrase also has a looser life, mostly online, where it describes a personal turning point rather than a religious one.

In that everyday sense, people describe a spiritual awakening as a period — sometimes a single moment — after which they see differently. They question beliefs they’d inherited. They feel more sensitive to people and places. They want time alone. They get curious about meditation, tarot, astrology or dreams. It often follows a shock: a loss, a break-up, a move.

Say someone who has always been practical goes through a break-up, starts meditating, picks up a deck, and feels as if the world has changed colour. They’d probably call it an awakening, and they’d mean: I can’t go back to seeing things the way I did.

Lists of “signs of a spiritual awakening” are everywhere. Read them as descriptions of what people report, not as a test you pass or fail. Feeling tired, wanting to be alone or sleeping badly can have many causes, and not all of them are spiritual.

## Where my own story starts {#my-story}

Mine started with a pick-a-card video by Kloee Taylor. I watched it and sobbed. For the first time, someone had put words on my situation. The reader cried too.

I call that the beginning of my awakening. I tell more of that story, and what I think pick-a-card videos can and can’t do, in [the video that made me cry](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/).

What strikes me now is how ordinary the ingredients were: a recorded video, a stranger, a moment of being understood. Nothing in it made me special.

## Why I also find the word misused {#misused}

Because it often gets used in ways that don’t help the person using it, or the people around them.

- *As a rank.* Some people who call themselves awakened start to feel above everyone else, as if there were a list and they’d made it. There are no Chosen Ones. I wrote about the compliment that tends to come with this in [‘you have a gift’](/blog/you-have-a-gift/).
- *As an excuse.* “I’ve outgrown them” can be true. It can also be a way of dropping people, duties or feelings you’d rather not face — which is [spiritual bypassing](/blog/spiritual-bypassing/) under a nicer name.
- *As a finish line.* As if you wake up once and you’re done. Nobody is done.
- *As something to sell.* When a turning point becomes a product, the person going through it becomes a customer.

**Seeing differently doesn’t make you better than anyone; it makes you responsible for what you do with it.**

It’s also why I keep a little distance from some spiritual circles: they can be ungrounded, too extreme, or carry more ego than they need, and that can make them risky for the people they practise on. I explain it in [spiritual, but not into ‘spiritual’ circles](/blog/spiritual-but-not-in-spiritual-circles/).

## The word I use instead {#the-word}

When I talk about what happened to me, I say: a beginning. It’s plain, it doesn’t rank anyone, and it doesn’t pretend anything is finished.

For what came after, I talk about developing your senses. We were all born with them. You develop them by doing things that ask you to let go a little: meditating, reading, painting, any meditative art. Reading tarot, or coffee cups, or even a painting — you can read in anything.

And one piece of advice from my own notes, for anyone who feels something opening: don’t go too high too fast, or you might not find your way back home. Start crescendo.

## If you think it’s happening to you {#if-its-you}

1. Keep your feet on the ground: sleep, eat, keep a few routines.
2. Stay close to people who knew you before. They’re your best reality check.
3. Go slowly with big decisions while everything feels new.
4. If it becomes frightening, or you stop sleeping, that’s a door for a doctor or a therapist rather than a deck. [Not everything is about spirits](/disclaimers/#therapy), and there’s no shame in the other door.
5. If you want a practice to hold it, start small. [Meditation for people who think they can’t meditate](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/) is where I’d begin.

And if the beginning has made you curious about the cards themselves, [learning to read them](/learn-tarot/) is a grounded way to follow it: one card, one lens, and someone who tells you nothing.
