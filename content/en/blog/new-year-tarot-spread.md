A New Year tarot spread lays one card for each month of 2027, plus one for the year as a whole. Each card is a theme to watch, not an event that will happen. It gives you twelve questions to carry through the year, and no promises — which is exactly why it’s worth doing.

## What is a New Year tarot spread? {#what-it-is}

A New Year spread is a layout you draw once, around the first of January, to give each month ahead its own card. Some readers lay twelve cards for the twelve houses of a birth chart; this one is plainer: twelve months, in order, and one card on top for the year.

Say the Hermit comes out for April. That doesn’t mean you’ll spend April alone. It means April is a good month to step back, think before acting, and ask yourself what you already know but haven’t said.

## The layout: thirteen cards for 2027 {#layout}

1. Shuffle while you think of the year as a whole, not of one question.
2. Draw the first card and place it on top: the year card, the overall theme.
3. Draw twelve more and lay them in four rows of three, one row per season: January to March, April to June, July to September, October to December.
4. Look at the whole table for a minute before you read any single card.
5. Photograph it or write the thirteen cards down. You’ll need the list all year.

Reading by quarters helps. One card on its own can feel like a verdict; three together read like a chapter, and a season is long enough for a theme to unfold.

## 2027’s year card, by numbers {#year-card}

Some readers add a collective year card from the digits of the year. For 2027: 2 + 0 + 2 + 7 = 11. In the Rider-Waite-Smith order, 11 is Justice (in the Marseille order, it’s Strength). And 1 + 1 = 2, the High Priestess.

[Justice](/library/tarot-cards/justice/) sits between two pillars, a sword raised in one hand and scales in the other: weighing, deciding, living with the consequences. Two, in [my numerology table](/library/numerology/number-2/), is cooperation between two parties, a contract, a bond; its advice is to read the contract and weigh the pros and cons, and its minus is stubbornness. The High Priestess adds what isn’t said: still water, the side of the moon we don’t see. Put together: a year for agreements made with your eyes open.

That card is the same for everyone, so treat it as a conversation starter, not your forecast. For something personal, do the same sum with your own birthday: born on 14 March, 1 + 4 + 0 + 3 + 2 + 0 + 2 + 7 = 19, the Sun. [How birth cards are worked out](/blog/tarot-birth-card/) goes further.

## How to read a month card without turning it into a promise {#no-promises}

Here’s what I read in a spread like this, and what I don’t promise. I read themes: where your attention is likely to be pulled, what kind of effort a month asks for, what to keep an eye on. I don’t promise events, dates or outcomes. Timing is where I miss most, and [why tarot is weakest on dates](/blog/tarot-timing/) explains what I’ve learned there.

So the Ten of Swords in March isn’t “March will be a disaster”. It’s “in March, notice what is finishing, even painfully” — and the drawing has a dawn on its horizon. The Sun in August isn’t a guaranteed holiday; it’s an invitation to be seen.

**A month card is a question you carry into the month, not an appointment the month has to keep.**

Some months shouldn’t be asked about at all. “Will I be pregnant by June?” or “Which month will the money come?” come from hope and worry, and I understand both. The cards aren’t the right tool for either; a gentler question for that month is “what helps me through the waiting?”

## An example: the first quarter of 2027 {#example}

Say January, February and March come out as the Ace of Wands, the Seven of Cups and the Eight of Pentacles.

{{< cards "waac cu07 pe08" >}}

January is the Ace of Wands, a hand from a cloud holding out a wand that is already sprouting. One is the initiator, the raw diamond; Fire is impulse. Start something — with the humility of a beginner. February is the Seven of Cups: seven cups floating in clouds, each holding a different dream. Too many choices, and seven asks for patience and analysis before action. March is the Eight of Pentacles: a craftsman at his bench, carving the same coin again and again. Mastery, and the effort it takes.

As a quarter, it reads: a spark in January, a February full of options that all look good, and a March where you pick one and get to work. In the notebook: “January — what am I starting? February — which option am I choosing? March — what am I practising?”

## Keep it, then check it {#keep-it}

Put the list somewhere you’ll find it: the back of your diary, a note on your phone. At the start of each month, look at that month’s card for two minutes before you look at your calendar. At the end of the month, put a tick or a cross. A miss is a miss: don’t stretch the card until it fits.
