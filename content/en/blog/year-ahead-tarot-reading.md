A year-ahead tarot reading is twelve cards, one for each month, drawn once and then reopened every month. The day you draw it matters less than the twelve times you come back to it. That’s how a reading that promises nothing can still help.

## What is a year-ahead tarot reading? {#what-it-is}

It’s a spread that gives each month a theme: a card that says where your attention is likely to go, and what kind of effort the month asks for. You can draw it on New Year’s Day, on your birthday, or at Samhain when the harvest is in. [My New Year layout for 2027](/blog/new-year-tarot-spread/) shows the table itself: one card for the year, then the twelve months in four rows of three.

What it isn’t: a calendar of events. If June shows the Three of Swords, June isn’t booked for heartbreak. It’s a month to notice what hurts, and to say it out loud rather than carry it.

## Step one: read the twelve cards together {#read-together}

Before I read any single card, I read the whole table through the lenses. It takes five minutes and tells you more than any one card.

1. Count the suits. A year heavy in Cups is an emotional year; in Swords, a year of decisions and conversations; in Pentacles, work, money and whatever has value for you; in Wands, projects and drive. [The four elements](/library/four-elements/) explain each one.
2. Count the majors. Many majors: big themes you don’t fully control. Few: a year mostly in your hands.
3. Look for repeated numbers. Three fives in one year is a lot of trying, failing and trying again.
4. Look at the colours. A table that is mostly yellow reads differently from one in grey and blue, before you’ve named a single card.

Say five of your twelve cards are Swords and there are only two majors. Before reading any month, I’d say: “This is a year where you decide things, mostly by yourself. Expect a lot of thinking out loud.”

## Step two: one month at a time {#month-by-month}

This is the part most people skip, and it’s the part that does the work.

1. On the first day of the month, look at that month’s card for two minutes, as if you’d never seen it.
2. Describe it in your notebook: colours, figures, number, element.
3. Write one sentence: “This month, I’ll pay attention to…”
4. Live the month. Don’t reread the card every morning.
5. On the last day, write what happened, then a tick, a cross or a question mark.

Say April is the Two of Cups: two people facing each other, raising their cups.

Two is cooperation, a bond, an agreement; Cups are feelings. On 1 April you might write: “This month, I’ll pay attention to the people I’ve drifted away from.” On 30 April: “My sister and I are talking again. Tick.”

By December you have twelve sentences and twelve marks — a record of your year in your own handwriting. **The reading helps because you check it, not because it was right.** [The reading notebook I keep](/blog/tarot-journal/) follows the same two columns: what I said, what happened.

## What a year-ahead reading can’t do {#limits}

It can’t give dates. A card for May speaks about a theme, never about the 12th. [Why timing is tarot’s weak point](/blog/tarot-timing/) goes into what I’ve learned there.

It can’t answer questions about health, pregnancy or money either — “Will I be well this year?”, “Which month will the money come?” Those questions come from worry, and they deserve better tools: a doctor, an adviser, a plan. What a month card can hold is the gentler version: “What helps me look after myself in May?”

And some months it will miss, as any reading can. Mark the cross and move on.

## Why I don’t publish predictions for 2027 {#no-predictions}

Every December, feeds fill with forecasts: the year for each sign, the collective card, what 2027 “holds”. I don’t publish any, for three reasons.

First, I read people, one at a time, knowing nothing about them beforehand. A forecast for everyone is a reading for no one. Second, nobody can check it. When it misses, the miss is never counted — and [counting my hits and misses](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/) is the only reason I trust what I do. Third, a prediction quietly becomes an authority. People wait for their “good month” instead of acting in this one, and [tarot is not an authority](/disclaimers/#readings).

As I see it, the way to look after the year ahead is to stand squarely in today. A reading of your own year, checked month by month, keeps the deciding with you.

## Start your own year-ahead reading {#start}

Draw your twelve cards this winter, read them together once, then open the notebook on the first of each month. If you’d like to learn the lenses properly before you begin, a [workshop](/workshops/) takes one lens and spends three hours on it, with twelve people around the table.
