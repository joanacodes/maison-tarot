Judgement in a career reading is a call: a vocation that keeps coming back, a review of where your work has gone, a second chance at something you left. In my notes, Judgement is neither good nor bad. It makes sure you hear the call; what you answer is up to you.

## Judgement at work, through its lenses {#lenses}

An angel leans out of the clouds and blows a golden trumpet hung with a white banner and a red cross. Below, people rise from open coffins floating on water — a man, a woman, a child, more behind them — arms open, faces turned up towards the sound. White mountains in the distance. No judge, no scales, nobody sent away: everyone is answering the same call.

The number: 20 becomes 2 + 0 = [2](/library/numerology/number-2/), the contract, a bond, a weighing. Its advice in my notes: read the contract, weigh the pros and cons. Its minus: stubbornness, solitude, the ego going round on its own. At work, the two parties are the call and you. In the Instagram story I use for the numbers, 2 is the moment the founders sign with investors: answering commits you.

The element is Fire: the spark, the energy that motivates, the moment you move. The energy is masculine: acting, showing yourself. The colours: grey bodies for a long wait with no will of its own, white for a clean slate, red for action, gold for the voice that carries.

On astrology: the Golden Dawn gave Judgement to the element of Fire, not to a planet. [Pluto](/library/astrology/pluto/) — what you bury and what comes back up — is a modern link, added by later readers. Keep it if it helps. Every lens is on [Judgement’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/judgement/).

## What Judgement says about work: a calling, a review, a second chance {#work}

*A calling.* Something you wanted to do long before you had a job keeps coming back: the training you didn’t take, the trade you admired, the subject you read about on Sundays. Judgement is the moment you can no longer pretend you didn’t hear it.

*A review.* An annual appraisal, a skills assessment, the birthday when you look at your CV and wonder how you got here. The card describes the looking, not the grade. It asks whether you’ll read your own contract honestly: what you agreed to, what you got, what you still want.

*A second chance.* Going back to studies at forty, applying again where you were turned down, picking up a project you shelved. The figures on the card aren’t new people; they’re the same people, standing up.

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws Judgement. Lenses before any meaning: 2, a weighing; Fire, something wanting to move; open arms. The sentence you might say: “Something you set aside in your work is calling you back, and this time you’re listening.” Then let them say whether it lands, and what it is.

**Judgement doesn’t grade your career; it asks whether you’ll answer what you hear.**

## Judgement reversed at work {#reversed}

Reversed, the call goes unanswered, or it’s answered too often. Unanswered: you hear it every January and stay lying down — “when the children are older”, “when things calm down”. Too often: you review your career so many times that you never decide anything, or you judge yourself for every year spent elsewhere. That’s the card’s minus, the verdict that never ends. The first needs a small step; the second needs a full stop.

## “Is this my true calling?” — and a kinder question {#question}

With Judgement, people ask: is this my real calling? Should I go back to what I left? Is it too late? Those questions carry a lot — hope, regret, the fear of losing more years. I understand wanting a card to settle it.

A card can’t tell you what you’re meant to do, and I’d be wary of any reading that tried. A calling isn’t a verdict handed down; it’s something you test. You can’t be told what it’s like. You can only try. And whether to resign or pay for a course is a money decision, which I leave to you and to a careers adviser.

A kinder question: “What keeps calling me back, and what would a small test of it look like?” A weekend course, a coffee with someone who does the job, a month of trying it on the side. That answer you can act on.

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Write the call down in one sentence. Underneath, two columns, the way 2 likes it: what answering would give, what it would cost. Sit with it for a week before deciding anything.
