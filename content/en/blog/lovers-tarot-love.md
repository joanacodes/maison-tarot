The Lovers is the card everyone hopes for in a love reading, and it’s less romantic than its name. It’s a card of Air — the mind, the decision — and of Gemini, the sign of conversation. When it comes up, the question is rarely “is this love?” and much more often “what am I choosing, and am I choosing it freely?”

## Why the Lovers decides before it swoons {#decides}

Six, in my notes, is “3 exalted”: the machine is running, and running fast. Harmony, exchange, the moment you can rest. In love, that’s the relationship that works — and its risk comes from the same place. When everything runs smoothly, you stop checking whether you’d still choose it. Six’s advice is to stay calm and humble while things flow.

The element is Air, the mind. Air takes in everything at once, which makes it the best decision-maker of the four: Fire is too impulsive, Water too emotional, Earth too stubborn. A Water card would talk about feelings. This one talks about choosing, with your eyes open. Its feminine energy says how: thinking first, and approaching delicate subjects by a detour rather than head-on.

One detail of the drawing matters here. The man looks at the woman; the woman looks up at the angel; they don’t touch. **This isn’t a picture of two people in love; it’s a picture of two people deciding.** Gemini and the other lenses are on [the Lovers’ own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-lovers/).

## The talking phase, and what you actually want {#talking-phase}

When you’re single, the Lovers often asks what you actually want, as opposed to what you’ve been told to want. Two people you’re drawn to for different reasons. Two ways of living — settling down, or staying free a while longer. Sometimes the choice is to stay single, chosen rather than endured, and the card is as happy with that as with any couple.

Gemini adds its own flavour: this card often turns up in the talking phase. Messages, long voice notes, the first conversations that go on until two in the morning. Air loves that stage, and it’s a real pleasure. It’s also where you can fall for a version of someone made mostly of words. In my notes, Gemini at its best is a great reporter, and at its worst a game of Chinese whispers: the story that grows a little with every message until it’s no longer quite true. So the card asks you to meet the person as well as the conversation.

If there’s a crush, one piece of advice from my own notes: don’t go and look up their birth chart. Let the magic happen for the two of you. I’ve explained why in a piece about [looking up someone’s chart without asking](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/).

## Choosing each other again, years in {#choosing-again}

In a couple, the Lovers is often a recommitment. A decision to take — moving in, a ring, a conversation you’ve been circling — or a decision made years ago that asks to be made again. A choice made once isn’t a choice forever.

Say a couple has lived together for six years and draws the Lovers. The card isn’t congratulating them. It’s asking whether, if they met today, they’d choose this, this way. Sometimes the answer is a heartfelt yes and the evening ends well. Sometimes it opens the conversation they needed.

After a break-up, the Lovers can show a choice already made, and the effort to own it rather than replay it. Or a choice still open: trying again, or not. The card won’t make it for you. It shows the crossroads and the angel above, and leaves the walking to you.

Say someone sits down, tells me nothing, and draws the Lovers. Harmony that works, a decision, a conversation, the head as much as the heart. What I’d say out loud is something like: “There’s a choice in front of you that you already know the answer to, and you’re waiting for someone else to make it official.” Then I’d ask whether that lands, and listen. If it doesn’t, it’s a miss, and I’d note it as one.

## “Is this the one?” — the question that skips the choice {#the-one}

It’s the question this card attracts more than any other, and I understand it. You’ve met someone, it feels important, and you’d love a guarantee before you open your heart further.

I don’t read on that question, and here’s why. “The one” turns a choice into something written in advance, and the Lovers is precisely the card that says love is chosen. The other person isn’t in the room either; what they feel and what they’ll decide are theirs. A card that told you “yes, it’s them” would be taking a decision away from both of you.

A gentler question: “What am I choosing when I choose this person — and would I choose it again today?” Or: “What do I want a relationship to feel like on an ordinary day?” Those have answers inside you, and they make you better company for the other person, whoever they turn out to be.

People also ask whether the Lovers is a “yes” card. It’s tempting to say so, and it would be a shame: a yes handed down by a deck skips the decision entirely. I’ve written about [why I answer yes-or-no questions differently](/blog/yes-or-no-tarot/). I read; you decide.

## Standing in the middle so you lose nothing {#the-middle}

Reversed, the Lovers is the choice avoided. You stay in the middle because deciding means losing something, and you’d rather lose nothing. Meanwhile time decides for you: the relationship carries on by default, or fades by default, and nobody can say when the choice was made.

It can also be values out of line: two people who want different lives and haven’t said so yet — one dreams of the countryside, the other of the next promotion. Gemini reversed is the conversation that doesn’t happen, or happens only in your head, in two versions.

In excess, it’s choosing over and over without ever settling. Every new person looks like the answer for three weeks. The question for the reversed Lovers is plain to ask and hard to answer: what are you not choosing, and what is that costing you?

## Beside the Two of Swords, the Devil and the Two of Cups {#lovers-pairs}

{{< cards "sw02 ar15 cu02" >}}

Next to the Two of Swords — a blindfolded woman holding two blades crossed over her chest — the choice is being refused. Both options are held level so neither has to be picked. Taking off the blindfold comes before any decision.

Next to the Devil, the question becomes whether a choice is still a choice. The Devil’s two chained figures echo the Lovers nine cards later, and 15 comes back to 6 as well. **The Devil is the 6 that stopped choosing; the Lovers is the 6 that keeps choosing.** I’ve written about [the Devil in love](/blog/devil-tarot-love/) and its loose chains.

Next to the Two of Cups, it’s the exchange made concrete: two people facing each other, cups raised. The Lovers decides; [the Two of Cups](/blog/two-of-cups-tarot-love/) is what the decision looks like on a Tuesday evening.

## Two columns on a sheet of paper {#two-columns}

The next time the Lovers comes up, write the choice at the top of a page in one sentence. Then two columns: what you choose if you say yes, and what you choose if you say no — because both are choices. Read it again two days later, once the Air has settled.
