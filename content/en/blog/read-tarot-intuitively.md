Reading tarot intuitively means saying what a card sets off in you — an image, a word, a feeling — before you know whether it's right. Then you check. Letting go is the practice; checking is what stops it from turning into wishful thinking.

## What intuitive tarot reading is {#what-it-is}

Intuitive reading is reading from the impressions a card gives you rather than from a meaning you learned. The card is a starting point, not a dictionary entry.

Those impressions come in different shapes. Some people see images, some hear a word or a sentence, some feel a sensation in the body or an emotion, now and then even a smell. Most of us are sharper in one channel than the others, and no channel is better.

Say you draw the Eight of Swords. A book will tell you something like restriction. You look, and notice the blindfold, the feet left free, the swords that don't quite close the circle. What comes might be: "Someone waiting for a permission they don't need." That sentence is yours, and it's what a reading is made of. My own note on this card is close: a prisoner of yourself while you hold every tool — you can take the blindfold off.

The cards help, but they aren't the source. Cards aren't magic; you are. They're a medium that lets your usual thoughts and assumptions step aside long enough for you to notice what you'd otherwise talk over.

## How to let go when you read {#letting-go}

Letting go is the only real technique, and it's harder than it sounds, because you're trying to stop trying.

1. Say a sentence that lowers the stakes before you start. Mine is in [the beginners' guide](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/).
2. Describe before you interpret: what's on the card, what you see, what you receive, in that order.
3. Say it out loud, in plain words, without correcting yourself halfway.
4. Don't take yourself too seriously. A reading isn't an exam.

If nothing comes, start from what's visible. The [colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/) is the fastest way in: yellow says joy and self-expression before you've remembered which card you're holding. Often the first plain observation unlocks the rest.

Expect the same card to read differently from one person to another, and from one day to the next. That isn't inconsistency. It's the point.

## When nothing comes {#nothing-comes}

Sometimes you look at the cards and see nothing at all. In my experience it comes from one of two places: your own block, usually too much pressure to get it right, or the other person's, who may be afraid of what they'll hear.

In both cases, talk. Put the cards down for a moment and reassure them — about the reading, not about their question, which you still don't need to know. Reassure yourself too. A reading that starts after two minutes of ordinary warmth often goes better than one forced in a tense silence.

Other times something comes and you feel you shouldn't say it, or not like that. The person isn't ready, or they're scared, or wary. Adapt your attitude and your words. You don't have to say everything the moment it arrives.

## How to check yourself {#check-yourself}

**Intuition you never check is a guess you never correct.** Three habits keep it honest.

- Read for someone who tells you nothing first. If they've given you their question, you can't tell whether you read them or rephrased them.
- Keep two columns: what you said, what they confirmed. The [tarot journal](/blog/tarot-journal/) shows how.
- Count your misses. By my own count, about one reading in ten misses. Those go in the notebook too.

The honest limit: a confirmation is still the person's judgement, and people are generous. Checking doesn't prove how intuition works. It shows you when yours is working, which is what a reader needs. If you keep wondering whether you're inventing it all, I've written about [how to tell whether you're making it up](/blog/am-i-making-it-up-tarot/).

## Go slowly {#go-slowly}

The advice I give most often here: don't go too high too fast, or you might not find your way back home. Start crescendo.

In practice, that means one card a day for a while, then three cards, then a person. The [one-card pull](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/) is built for that first stage. Alongside it, anything meditative trains the same attention: painting, reading, meditation, looking at a picture in a museum and asking what it says. You can read in anything.

Reading takes energy. After a session you may feel very tired, because interpreting is work. Listen to your body and rest. I sometimes stop after two hours myself.

Empathy helps — feeling and understanding the other person's emotions — as long as you keep your own while you do it. Listen to their emotion without letting it take over. And if a reading starts carrying more than cards should, tarot doesn't replace therapy, for you or for them. [That's where I point to someone else](/disclaimers/#therapy).

## Try it this week {#try-it}

Seven mornings, one card each. Write three lines: what's on it, what you see, what you receive. On the eighth day, find one person who'll tell you nothing, read for them, and fill both columns.

If you'd like company for the first try, every [workshop](/workshops/) starts with a card in the first ten minutes and the explanation after.
