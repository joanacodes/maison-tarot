People ask me whether tarot works the way they'd ask whether a diet works: they want a yes. I can give them something better than a yes — a number, and the way I got it.

## What I actually did {#what-i-did}

Between 2020 and 2023 I read for three hundred people who told me nothing. Not their question, not their situation, not their name if I could avoid it. Most of it was online: Discord servers, group calls, a live where people were drawn at random. Some of it was face to face. All of it was free, because I wanted to know, not to earn.

After each reading I wrote two things in a notebook: what I'd said, and what the person confirmed. Then a mark — held, or missed.

## The number {#the-number}

By that count, about **nine readings in ten held**: the person recognised their situation, often in specific detail — a job they hadn't mentioned, a person, a decision they were sitting on. About **one in ten missed**: nothing landed, or the person said so afterwards, and I wrote it down.

That's the whole result. It's on the [method page](/method/) too, where it will stay, misses included.

## How I counted, and why it matters {#how-i-counted}

"Held" meant one of two things: a visible reaction during the reading — the face that changes before the person has decided what to say — or a confirmation afterwards, sometimes days later ("how did you know?"). "Missed" meant neither, or an explicit "no, that's not it".

I want to be exact about what this is: **a manual tally by the person doing the readings**, not a study. I'm the one judging what counts as a hit, and the person's confirmation is subjective. Two things keep it honest. First, the silence rule — since they told me nothing, I couldn't be reading back what they'd said. Second, I wrote the misses down the same evening, when they still stung.

## What it doesn't prove {#what-it-doesnt-prove}

It doesn't prove *how* tarot works. I don't know. It doesn't prove that the cards themselves do anything — I suspect the cards are a screen and the person reading is the instrument, and that's exactly why I [teach reading](/learn-tarot/) rather than sell readings. It doesn't prove the future is visible: what I read is the person's present, their emotional world at the moment we sit down, and a reading about "what comes" tends to arrive in a shape nobody imagined.

And it doesn't prove that *I* am special. Three hundred readings taught me the opposite: the same result comes for the people I've taught, once they stop trying to be right.

## What changed in how I read {#what-changed}

Three things.

1. **I expect to talk nonsense.** Every time. The lower I set the expectation, the more accurate it gets. [It's the first thing I teach.](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/)
2. **I stop when it misses.** No reshuffling, no reframing, no "the cards mean something you haven't realised yet". A miss is a miss, it goes in the notebook, we move on.
3. **I refuse some subjects.** Health, death, pregnancy, court cases, money, "curses". Not because a card can't fall on them, but because at a one-in-ten miss rate, a wrong word there does real damage. [The full list is on the disclaimers page.](/disclaimers/#refusals)

## Try it yourself {#try-it}

You don't have to believe any of this. Read for ten people who tell you nothing, write down what you said and what they confirmed, count. If your notebook looks like mine, you'll understand why I don't need to argue about whether it works. If it doesn't, write to me — a second notebook is worth more than a second opinion.
