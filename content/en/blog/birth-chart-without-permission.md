I don’t look up someone’s birth chart without asking them first. And if you have a crush, I’d gently suggest you don’t look up theirs at all: let the magic happen for the two of you.

That isn’t a rule handed down from above. It comes from what I saw when I tested charts, and from how I treat tarot.

## Why it feels harmless {#why-people-do-it}

A birthday on a profile, a free calculator, ten seconds. Nobody gets hurt, it seems; it’s only maths about where the planets were.

And the reasons are usually kind ones. A new partner you want to understand. A boss who baffles you. A sister you worry about. Someone you can’t stop thinking about. Wanting to understand a person is nothing to be ashamed of.

The question is what a chart does to that understanding when the person isn’t part of it.

## A chart is a private room, not a public profile {#private-room}

When I explain a chart, I use a house. The rising sign is the outside, what anyone sees from the street. The Sun is the inner house. The Moon is your own room, the most private place of all. I go through the three in [Sun, Moon and rising](/blog/sun-moon-rising-big-three/).

Now the rest. When I tested charts box by box, the placements that held best — 75 to 90% of the time — were Saturn, Lilith and Chiron: the hardest lesson, what the person learned to hide, the wound they keep circling. **The most accurate parts of a chart are also the most intimate ones.**

You wouldn’t want someone snooping in your bedroom. Opening someone’s chart behind their back is close to that. It’s also why, in tarot, I always ask before reading someone, and why I don’t read about [people who aren’t in the room](/disclaimers/#refusals).

There’s a practical sign, too. A full chart needs a birth time, and you rarely find that on a profile. If you catch yourself asking someone’s mother for it without telling them, notice it.

## What you get when the person isn’t there {#half-a-sentence}

When I tested charts — family, friends, then strangers — it was always with their permission, and they were the ones who said tick or cross. That’s what made the count worth anything. [How I tested my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) has the details.

Without the person, nobody can say “that’s not me”. And overall, only 30 to 40% of what I checked held. Read someone’s chart alone and two lines in three may be wrong, and you’ll never know which.

There’s a second effect, which I’ve noticed in readings as well. Someone who doesn’t know they’re being read isn’t open: you get half a sentence and build a whole statement on it. Then you start reading their behaviour through that statement. A late reply becomes “typical of their Moon”. This is only my experience and the hypothesis I make from it; nothing has been proven scientifically.

## Your crush included (especially your crush) {#your-crush}

With a crush, the urge is strongest and the cost is highest.

You’d meet the chart before you meet the person. Say you find their Venus in Scorpio. From then on, every silence reads as intensity and every joke as a test. You’re no longer listening to them; you’re checking them against a page.

You’d also decide in advance. It’s the “I don’t date Scorpios” problem, which I write about in [when astrology becomes an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/): one line ends up deciding about a whole person.

And you’d skip the best part. Finding out who someone is, slowly, from what they say and do, is the thing a chart can’t give you. It’s also lopsided: you’d know things about them that they never chose to tell you.

If you’re both curious later, put both charts on the table, together. That’s what [zodiac compatibility](/blog/zodiac-compatibility/) is for.

## What to do instead {#instead}

1. Ask. “Do you know your rising sign?” is a good opener. Whether they laugh, shrug or have opinions already tells you something real.
2. If they say yes, read it together, out loud, and let them tick or cross.
3. If they say no, that’s the answer. Leave it there.
4. If you’ve already looked, no drama. Put it away, don’t quote it at them, and don’t let it decide anything. You can also tell them; it might make a good story.

And if the curiosity is really about astrology, there’s one chart you can read as much as you like without asking anyone: yours. Start with [what a birth chart is and how to get yours](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/), then test it, box by box.
