A birth chart is a map of the sky at the minute and place you were born: where the Sun, the Moon and the planets were, against which signs, and in which part of the sky. To draw one you need three things, your date, time and place of birth, and a free site does the maths in seconds.

## What a birth chart is {#definition}

It’s also called a natal chart. It’s drawn as a wheel: the twelve signs around the outer ring, twelve slices inside for the houses, symbols for the planets, and lines across the middle for the aspects.

Four ingredients, then. **The planet says what, the sign says how, the house says where, and the aspects say how the planets get along.**

An example. Mars in Taurus in the tenth house: your drive (Mars) works steadily, even stubbornly (Taurus), and shows most in your career and public life (the tenth house).

## The planets: what {#planets}

In astrology, “planets” includes the Sun and the Moon, which astronomers would rightly object to. Each one describes a part of you.

| Planet | What it describes |
| --- | --- |
| Sun | Your centre, what you organise your life around |
| Moon | What you feel when nobody’s watching |
| Mercury | How you think and speak |
| Venus | What you love, and how |
| Mars | How you act and how you fight |
| Jupiter | Expansion, confidence, excess |
| Saturn | Your hardest lesson, structure |
| Uranus | Where you break free |
| Neptune | Dream, intuition, illusion |
| Pluto | Power, transformation |

Most charts also show two points I watch closely: Lilith, a calculated point (what you learned to hide), and Chiron, a small body between Saturn and Uranus (the wound you keep circling).

Speed matters. The Moon changes sign every two and a half days, the Sun every month, Jupiter every year, Saturn every two and a half years; Uranus, Neptune and Pluto stay seven years or more. Everyone in your school year shares those slow planets, so they say more about a generation than about you.

## The signs: how {#signs}

The zodiac is a band of sky that the Sun appears to travel through over a year, cut into twelve signs of 30 degrees each. A sign colours the way a planet works.

Each sign has an element (Fire, Earth, Air or Water) and a mode: cardinal signs start things, fixed signs hold, mutable signs adapt. The [three astrological modes](/library/astrological-modes/) are one of the lenses I teach. In my notes, each sign also has a verb: Aries “I am”, Taurus “I have”, Gemini “I think”, Cancer “I feel”, Leo “I will”, Virgo “I analyse”, Libra “I balance”, Scorpio “I desire”, Sagittarius “I see, I try”, Capricorn “I use”, Aquarius “I know”, Pisces “I believe”.

So Mercury in Gemini thinks out loud and fast; Mercury in Virgo analyses before it speaks. When people say “my sign”, they mean their Sun sign: one placement among many. The Library has [the twelve signs in one table](/library/astrology/).

## The houses: where {#houses}

The houses are twelve slices of the sky, calculated from your birth time and place. Each one is an area of life. The first begins at the rising sign, the sign coming up over the eastern horizon at the minute you were born; I explain it more in [what is my rising sign](/blog/what-is-my-rising-sign/).

| House | Area of life |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Yourself, how you come across |
| 2 | Money, possessions, what you value |
| 3 | Communication, siblings, short trips |
| 4 | Home, family, roots |
| 5 | Pleasure, creativity, romance, children |
| 6 | Daily work, routines, health habits |
| 7 | Partners and one-to-one relationships |
| 8 | Shared resources, intimacy, transformation |
| 9 | Travel, studies, beliefs |
| 10 | Career, public image |
| 11 | Friends, groups, hopes |
| 12 | What’s hidden, solitude, rest |

There are several ways of drawing the houses (Placidus, Whole Sign and others), which is why two sites can put the same planet in different houses. Pick one and stick to it.

## The aspects: how the planets get along {#aspects}

An aspect is the angle between two planets on the wheel, with a few degrees of leeway. Five main ones:

| Aspect | Angle | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conjunction | 0° | The two act as one |
| Sextile | 60° | An opening, if you use it |
| Square | 90° | Friction that pushes you to act |
| Trine | 120° | Flows without effort |
| Opposition | 180° | A pull between two sides |

Say someone has the Moon square Saturn. The need for comfort (Moon) and the sense of duty (Saturn) rub against each other; a common reading is that the person learned early to put their feelings off until later.

## How to get yours, and where to start {#get-yours}

1. Find your birth time, as exact as you can: the rising sign changes about every two hours, and the houses with it. Ask your family, or look at your birth certificate. In France and in Scotland the time is on it; in England and Wales it usually isn’t, except for twins and other multiple births.
2. Go to a free chart site. I use Astro-Seek. Enter your date, time and town of birth.
3. Print the chart, or save it somewhere you’ll find it again.

No birth time? You still get the Sun and the planets in their signs, and usually the Moon. The rising sign and the houses won’t be reliable.

Then don’t read everything at once. Start with three placements: [your Sun, Moon and rising](/blog/sun-moon-rising-big-three/). After that, test it. When I [tested my own chart box by box](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), 30 to 40% held overall, and [Saturn, Lilith and Chiron](/blog/saturn-lilith-chiron/) held 75 to 90%. A chart is a lens to look through, not a verdict, so print yours, take a pencil, and tick or cross.
