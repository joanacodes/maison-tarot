When I tested my birth chart box by box, most placements held 30 to 40% of the time. Three held far more often — 75 to 90%, first in my own chart and then in everyone else's I checked: Saturn, Lilith and Chiron. All three describe a difficulty: the hardest lesson, the thing you learned to hide, the wound you keep circling.

The Library gives each of them a page. This one puts them side by side: what each is, plainly, what “held” means, and how to check yours. In tarot I read them with three cards: the World for Saturn, the Devil for Lilith, the Hermit for Chiron.

## What “held” means in my test {#held}

I printed my chart and went down it like an invoice. Next to each placement — the planet, its sign, its house — I read a plain description and put a tick or a cross.

A tick meant: this could only have been written about me. A cross meant: vague, wrong, or true of my neighbour too. “You like to feel appreciated” gets a cross on principle, because everybody does. A flattering line that fits everyone counts as a miss.

**A tick had to be specific enough that it couldn't belong to someone else.**

Then I did the same for family, friends and strangers, with their permission and without them telling me anything first. “Held 75 to 90%” means that for these three placements, between three times in four and nine times in ten, the description earned a tick. The whole story is in [how I tested my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/); I won't retell it here.

## Saturn: the lesson that takes years {#saturn}

Saturn is structure, responsibility and limits: in a chart, the hardest lesson, and the place where you slowly become solid because it was hard. [Saturn's Library page](/library/astrology/saturn/) has the full definition and the cards that carry it.

It's slow: about 29 years to go round the zodiac, two and a half in each sign. So people born within a couple of years of each other share a Saturn sign; the house it sits in, which depends on your birth time, is what makes it yours. Say someone has Saturn in the tenth house, the house of career and public life. An astrologer would read a working life that started slowly and became the most solid thing they have.

Why does Saturn tell the truth when the Sun doesn't? In my test the Sun scored about a third, like the other placements everyone reads first. The Sun is the shop window: what you're happy to show, and the easiest placement to flatter. Saturn describes what cost you something, and people remember what cost them something in precise words. That's my hypothesis, not an explanation. Saturn's best-known moment, around 29, has [an article of its own](/blog/saturn-return/).

## Lilith: what you learned to hide {#lilith}

Lilith, the Black Moon of modern astrology, isn't a planet but a calculated point on the Moon's orbit, the spot where the Moon is farthest from the Earth. In a chart it shows what you learned to hide in order to be accepted. [Lilith's Library page](/library/astrology/lilith/) goes through it, and through why I read it with the Devil, whose chains are loose enough to lift off.

Say someone's Lilith sits in the seventh house, the house of partners. An astrologer would read a part of themselves they learned to leave outside their relationships: an anger, say, or wanting more than they ask for. Of the three, it's the placement people recognise and never say out loud.

**Because it touches what someone chose to hide, I read Lilith with care: you knock first.** Reading it in another person's chart behind their back is opening a door that was closed on purpose, a point I take further in [looking up a chart without permission](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/).

## Chiron: the wound you circle {#chiron}

Chiron is a small body between Saturn and Uranus, named after the centaur of Greek myth, a healer who could cure every wound but his own. In a chart it shows the wound you keep coming back to, and what you become able to help others with because of it. [Chiron's Library page](/library/astrology/chiron/) has the rest, including why I read it with the Hermit, who makes a lantern of his own night.

Say someone's Chiron sits in the third house, the house of speech and siblings. An astrologer would read a wound around being heard — and, often, a person who grows into the friend everyone calls when they need to talk something through. The wound and the skill sit on the same spot.

## Why these three, and what I don't claim {#what-i-dont-claim}

All three describe a difficulty, and people may know their difficulties better than their qualities. That's a hypothesis too. It's also a warning: a hard sentence can feel true because it's hard. These three got more checking than the others, precisely because I was hoping the result was wrong.

The usual caveat applies: I counted by hand, mostly on people I knew, and the tally sits in a notebook — it was never research.

And these placements touch tender places. If reading your Chiron opens something heavier than a page can hold, that isn't a sign to read more. [Sometimes it's a therapist's door](/disclaimers/#therapy), and walking through it is the brave option, not the weak one.

## Check your own three {#try-it}

Five steps and one printed page.

1. Print your chart, with your birth date, time and place.
2. Find Saturn, Lilith and Chiron: the sign, and the house if you know your birth time.
3. Read one plain description of each. The three Library pages are a start.
4. Tick or cross, judging your own ticks as strictly as you'd judge a stranger's.
5. Do the same for your Sun, and compare.

If yours hold the way mine did, you'll see why these three got their own pages. If they don't, I'd rather hear about your crosses than repeat my ticks.
