No. Nothing gets stuck in your cards, and they don’t need clearing before they’ll read well. If a ritual helps you settle, keep it; I sold sage in 2021, and I’m not here to laugh at anyone who still uses it.

## What cleansing a deck means {#what-it-means}

Cleansing a tarot deck is a short ritual meant to clear the cards of energy they’re believed to have absorbed — from readings, from heavy questions, from other people’s hands — or to make a new deck feel like your own.

Readers do it at different moments: when a deck is new, after a hard reading, after someone else has handled the cards, or when readings start to feel flat. Say a friend borrows your deck for a week. When it comes back, plenty of readers will cleanse it before they use it again, the way you might air a room after guests.

The belief behind it is that a deck works a little like a sponge. Whatever it soaks up, it can give back in the next reading, so you wring it out from time to time.

## Five ways people cleanse a deck {#five-ways}

Here are the most common, described the way the people who use them would describe them.

- *Sage.* A bundle of dried sage is lit, the flame put out, and the cards are passed through the smoke, sometimes one by one, often with a sentence said out loud about what’s being cleared. Smoke has been used to clear spaces in many traditions, and this is its tarot form.
- *Moonlight.* The deck is left on a windowsill overnight, usually at the full moon, to be cleared and “recharged” by its light. Some readers lay the cards out face up so each one gets its share.
- *Salt.* The deck rests next to a dish of salt, or sits in a sealed bag on a bed of salt, because salt is a traditional purifier. If you do this, keep the cards dry: cardboard and damp don’t mix.
- *Knocking.* You knock on the deck, once or three times, to wake it up or knock out the last reading. It takes a second, so readers often do it between two people.
- *Shuffling.* A long, thorough shuffle, or putting all seventy-eight cards back in their original order, one by one, to start again from zero.

Each of these does one thing for certain: it makes you stop for a moment before you read again.

## What I sold in 2021, and why I stopped {#what-i-sold}

In 2021, I sold sage, candles and “channelled” cards. I stopped. Nothing in any of it improved a reading.

That last sentence is the one that matters to me, so let me say what I mean by it. When I talk about a reading improving, I mean the only thing I measure: whether what I say holds when the person checks it. A reading is the person in front of me, what I see, and what I say. None of those three lives in a bundle of sage, and none of them comes in a box.

## Why a ritual can still help {#why-it-helps}

That doesn’t make cleansing useless, and it doesn’t make the people who do it foolish. A ritual marks a beginning. It separates one reading from the next. It slows your breathing and tells your attention that something different is about to happen. If lighting something and sitting quietly for a minute helps you read better, the difference is real. I think it happens in you, not in the cardboard.

**A ritual can change the reader; I’ve never seen it change the cards.**

Some people cleanse out of faith or family tradition, and that is theirs to keep. What I’d gently question is the fear underneath, when there is one: the idea that a deck can be dirty, or that someone else’s hands have spoiled it. They haven’t. [Letting someone else touch your cards](/blog/let-others-touch-tarot-cards/) is a matter of preference, not of safety.

And when readings start to feel flat, it’s usually the reader who is tired, not the deck. I went into that at length in [do tarot cards get tired?](/blog/do-tarot-cards-get-tired/), so I won’t repeat it here.

## What I do instead: a break {#a-break}

A break is my whole alternative to cleansing. Put the deck in a drawer for a few days, or a week, or until you miss it. If you find you’re reaching for it several times a day, or asking the same question in new words, the cards aren’t the problem; the habit is, and a break is the kindest way to see it. I never want tarot to become a circle of dependency, for me or for the people I read for, and I wish the same for you. I wrote more on [why quitting readings for a while helps](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).

Between two readings, a shuffle is enough, and there’s [no wrong way to do it](/blog/how-to-shuffle-tarot-cards/).

## If you’ve just bought a deck {#new-deck}

You don’t need to cleanse it before it will work. Get to know it instead: one card a day for a week, described out loud — what’s on it, what you see, what comes to you. By the end of the week the deck will feel like yours, which is what most cleansing rituals are trying to give you. The whole routine is in [how to get to know a new deck](/blog/new-tarot-deck/).

If you love your ritual, keep it, for the pause it gives you. Don’t spend much on it, and don’t let anyone sell you the idea that your cards need protecting from you.
