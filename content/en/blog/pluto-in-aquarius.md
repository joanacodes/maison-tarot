Pluto has been in Aquarius for good since 19 November 2024, and it stays there until 2043, with one last step back before it leaves in January 2044. That's about twenty years — long enough that nothing about it needs bracing for this week. Here are the dates, what astrology says the transit means, and what it can and can't tell you about your own life.

## When Pluto entered Aquarius {#dates}

Pluto moved into Aquarius in three goes, because from Earth it appears to back up for about five months every year. When it sits right on the border between two signs, that back-and-forth carries it across the line more than once.

| Date | Movement |
|---|---|
| 23 March 2023 | Pluto enters Aquarius for the first time |
| 11 June 2023 | it backs into Capricorn |
| 21 January 2024 | it enters Aquarius again (20 January in the Americas) |
| 1–2 September 2024 | it backs into Capricorn one last time, depending on time zone |
| 19 November 2024 | it enters Aquarius for good |
| March 2043 | it reaches Pisces, then backs into Aquarius for the autumn |
| 19 January 2044 | it leaves Aquarius for good |

[Cafe Astrology's page on the transit](https://cafeastrology.com/pluto-aquarius-transit.html) lists the full set, and the UK astrologer [Sally Kirkman](https://sallykirkman.com/pluto-enters-aquarius-power-to-the-people/) gives the 2024 dates in British time.

Why so long? Pluto takes about 248 years to go round the Sun, on an orbit so stretched that it spends anything from about twelve to more than thirty years in a sign. Aquarius gets twenty. The last time Pluto was there was from the late 1770s to 1798. In 2006 astronomers reclassified it as a dwarf planet; astrologers kept it, and so does the [Library page on Pluto](/library/astrology/pluto/), because it's in every chart you'll ever print.

## What astrologers say it means {#meaning}

In astrology, Pluto describes power and transformation: what gets buried, and what comes back up. [Aquarius](/library/astrology/aquarius/) is fixed Air, the sign whose verb is "I know": ideas, groups, networks, inventions, the future. My own archetypes for it are the mad scientist and the start-up founder — people who stay loyal to an idea long after everyone else has moved on.

Put the two together and you get the usual reading of this transit: power moving through technology, networks and collectives. Who owns the tools, who belongs to which group, which ideas organise people. The previous twenty years, Pluto in Capricorn (2008–2024), were read through institutions, governments and debt.

Astrologers also point back to the last pass, which covered the American Revolution and the French Revolution of 1789. **A twenty-year window in any century holds enormous events, so matching them afterwards proves very little.** What would count is a forecast written before, specific enough to be wrong. I keep that in mind every time I read "Pluto in Aquarius means…".

## What it means for you, personally {#for-you}

Pluto is a generational placement: everyone born between 2024 and 2043 will have it in Aquarius, so it says nothing about any one of them. The personal part is the house — the area of your chart where Aquarius falls. That needs your birth time, and if you've never looked, here's [what a birth chart is and how to read one](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/).

Say Aquarius falls in your tenth house, the one for work and public life. An astrologer would read the next twenty years as a slow change in how you work and how you're seen at work. Slow is the key word: two decades, not a crisis this month.

If you want to test it rather than believe it, find that house in your own chart, write one line about how that part of your life looks today, date it, and put it somewhere you'll find it again in five years. That's the only kind of check a transit this long allows.

I'll be honest about the limit. Pluto is hard to test, and it wasn't among the placements that held well when I checked charts box by box; I suspect it's too big for one life. And whatever it describes, it doesn't decide for you. If someone tells you Pluto is why everything is falling apart this month, they're squeezing twenty years into a headline — and if you catch yourself saying "it's my Pluto", have a look at [when astrology becomes an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).
