The reader you follow is chosen by a feed: a beautiful deck, good light, a pick-a-card that lands on the right day. The reader you need is chosen differently: by how they work, what they say when they miss, and what they won’t read on. Sometimes they’re the same person. Often they aren’t, and that’s fine.

## The reader you follow {#the-reader-you-follow}

Instagram rewards what can be seen and shared. Decks laid out in morning light. A card of the day with three lines underneath. Carousels where you pick one of four piles and swipe to yours. Reels of a shuffle, a draw, and a sentence that sounds made for you.

That’s content: made once, for many people at the same time. There’s nothing wrong with it. It’s how a lot of people meet tarot now, and some of it is generous, careful and well made. The formats are close cousins of the ones on TikTok, and [the same strengths and pitfalls apply](/blog/tarottok/). Content can even move you; I’ve written separately about [what a pick-a-card can and can’t do](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/). What it can’t do is read you: it was made before you scrolled past.

What the feed rewards, though, is the post people stop on: the most beautiful, the most reassuring, the most frequently published. None of that tells you whether the person behind it can read.

Say two accounts post the Star on the same morning.

The first writes: “Hope is coming. Everything is about to get better.” It’s warm, it’s shareable, and it would fit any card with a bright sky. The second writes: “A woman kneeling at the water’s edge, one knee on land, one foot in the pool, pouring from two jugs at once. What are you giving back to, and what are you letting go of?” The first post will probably travel further. The second one shows you someone who looks at the picture, and that’s the habit you want in a reader.

## The platform changes; a method doesn’t {#the-platform-changes}

I know Instagram’s story better than most apps’, because it’s the example I use to teach the numbers one to ten. It starts as a 1: a small location-sharing app called Burbn, cut down to the one feature people used, sharing a photo. It reaches its 9 when the whole design turns to Reels, and its 10 when its first users no longer recognise it. [The numerology pages](/library/numerology/) tell the whole cycle.

For tarot accounts, that cycle has a practical side. What the feed rewards keeps moving: square photos, then Stories, then Reels. An account built to please the feed has to change its format every time the feed does, and its readings tend to change with it: shorter, brighter, easier to save.

**A reader with a method reads the same way in a caption, a Reel or a live.**

That gives you a test you can run without booking anything. Scroll back a year or two on an account you like. If the way they talk about a card has stayed the same while the formats changed around it, the method is theirs. If every new format brought a new way of talking about the cards, you were following the feed rather than the reader.

## The reader you need {#the-reader-you-need}

When you want to be read, the criteria change. Three things matter, and none of them shows up in a grid.

Method. Can they say how they read? Not “I have a gift”, because there isn’t one and anyone can learn, but what they look at: the colours, the numbers, the elements, the scene. A reader with a method can explain why they said what they said.

Honesty. Do they ever say “that one missed”? Nobody gets every reading right — I get about nine in ten — and a reader whose posts never mention a miss is either not counting or not telling.

Limits. What won’t they read on? I keep seven subjects out of my readings — health, death, pregnancy, pending lawsuits, choices about money, curses, people who aren’t present — and [I say so publicly](/disclaimers/#refusals). A reader with no limits hasn’t thought much about being wrong.

**Follow for the pictures; choose a reader for the method.**

What to look for on a profile before you book:

- A post that explains how they work, in their own words.
- A list of subjects they don’t read on.
- The price in their bio or a pinned post, and no extras sold once the reading is over.
- Misses mentioned somewhere, even once.
- No message out of the blue saying they “picked something up” about you. That’s a sales opening, not a reading.

And you can ask. A reader with a method is usually glad to explain it, misses included. If the answer to “how do you read?” is a discount code, you have your answer.

## When following turns into checking {#following-into-checking}

A feed has a rhythm, and it can become yours. You check the card of the day before deciding what to wear, then before sending a message, then before answering one. You swipe through piles until one says what you hoped to hear.

That isn’t foolishness; it’s worry looking for a hand to hold. But what it’s hunting for is approval from outside, and a feed will supply that for ever. If you notice it, mute a few accounts for a week and see what you decide without them. Your own deck can have that week off too. I’ve written more on [why stepping back helps](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).
