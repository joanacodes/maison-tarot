The reader you follow is chosen by a feed: a beautiful deck, good light, a pick-a-card that lands on the right day. The reader you need is chosen differently: by how they work, what they say when they miss, and what they won’t read on. Sometimes they’re the same person. Often they aren’t, and that’s fine.

## The reader you follow {#the-reader-you-follow}

Instagram rewards what can be seen and shared. Decks laid out in morning light. A card of the day with three lines underneath. Carousels where you pick one of four piles and swipe to yours. Reels of a shuffle, a draw, and a sentence that sounds made for you.

That’s content: made once, for many people at the same time. There’s nothing wrong with it. It’s how a lot of people meet tarot now, and some of it is generous, careful and well made. The formats are close cousins of the ones on TikTok, and [the same strengths and pitfalls apply](/blog/tarottok/).

What the feed rewards, though, is the post people stop on: the most beautiful, the most reassuring, the most frequently published. None of that tells you whether the person behind it can read.

Say two accounts post the Star on the same morning.

The first writes: “Hope is coming. Everything is about to get better.” It’s warm, it’s shareable, and it would fit any card with a bright sky. The second writes: “A woman kneeling at the water’s edge, one foot on land, one in the pool, pouring from two jugs at once. What are you giving back to, and what are you letting go of?” The first post will probably travel further. The second one shows you someone who looks at the picture, and that’s the habit you want in a reader.

## What a pick-a-card can do {#pick-a-card}

I don’t say that to dismiss it. A pick-a-card video by Kloee Taylor made me sob. It put words on my situation for the first time, and partway through, the reader cried too.

So yes, content can move you, and the recognition is real. I’ve written about [why pick-a-card readings work](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/) the way a song does: made for everyone, and sometimes exactly about you. What a post can’t do is read you. It didn’t know you existed. It held up a mirror to a crowd, and you were in the crowd.

## The reader you need {#the-reader-you-need}

When you want to be read, the criteria change. Three things matter, and none of them shows up in a grid.

Method. Can they say how they read? Not “I have a gift”, because there isn’t one and anyone can learn, but what they look at: the colours, the numbers, the elements, the scene. A reader with a method can explain why they said what they said.

Honesty. Do they ever say “that one missed”? Nobody reads at a hundred per cent; by my count, about one reading in ten misses. A reader whose posts never mention a miss is either not counting or not telling.

Limits. What won’t they read on? I leave out health, death, pregnancy, court cases, money decisions, curses and people who aren’t in the room, and [I say so publicly](/disclaimers/#refusals). A reader with no limits hasn’t thought much about being wrong.

**Follow for the pictures; choose a reader for the method.**

What to look for on a profile before you book:

- A post that explains how they work, in their own words.
- A list of subjects they don’t read on.
- A stated price, with nothing added at the end.
- Misses mentioned somewhere, even once.
- No message out of the blue saying they “picked something up” about you. That’s a sales opening, not a reading.

## When following turns into checking {#following-into-checking}

A feed has a rhythm, and it can become yours. You check the card of the day before deciding what to wear, then before sending a message, then before answering one. You swipe through piles until one says what you hoped to hear.

That isn’t foolishness; it’s worry looking for a hand to hold. But it’s a search for outside validation, and a feed will supply it for ever. If you notice it, mute a few accounts for a week and see what you decide without them. Take a break from your cards now and then, and from other people’s. I’ve written more on [why stepping back helps](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).
