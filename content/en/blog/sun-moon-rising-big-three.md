Your “big three” are the signs of your Sun, your Moon and your rising sign. I call them the primal triad, and I explain them with a house: the Sun is the inner house, the Moon is your own room, and the rising sign is the outside of the house. Put together, they show why nobody is only their sign.

## What the big three are {#definition}

Three placements from your birth chart:

- Sun sign: the sign the Sun was in on your birthday. It’s the one horoscopes use.
- Moon sign: the sign the Moon was in when you were born. The Moon moves into a new sign roughly every 60 hours, so your birth date usually settles it, and your birth time makes sure.
- Rising sign, or Ascendant: the sign that was rising in the east at the moment of your birth. A new sign rises roughly every two hours, so it needs your exact time and place.

If you don’t have your chart yet, [my beginner’s page on birth charts](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/) walks you through getting one.

## The Sun: the inner house {#sun}

The Sun describes your centre: what you organise your life around, the light you want to give off. In the house, it’s the structure itself, the walls and the layout you live inside.

Say your Sun is in Capricorn, “I use”: a house built to last, with a plan and load-bearing walls.

It’s what people mean when they ask “what sign are you?”. It’s also the placement that’s easiest to flatter. When I tested charts box by box, the Sun held about a third of the time, roughly like the rest of the chart.

## The Moon: your own room {#moon}

The Moon is the one I insist on. It says what you feel when nobody’s watching: your need for safety, your emotional reflexes, how you comfort yourself. In the house, it’s your own room, the place where you close the door.

Two people can share a Sun sign and need very different rooms. A Moon in Cancer wants people close, food on the table, a door left open to family. A Moon in Aquarius wants space, ideas, and a door that shuts when it needs to.

**If you learn one placement beyond your Sun, make it your Moon.** It’s often the part people know least about themselves, or the part they recognise at once. The [Moon page of the Library](/library/astrology/moon/) goes further.

## The rising sign: the outside of the house {#rising}

The rising sign is the façade: what people see from the street before they know you. First impressions, your style, the way you walk into a room.

Say someone has Leo rising. People notice when they come in, whatever is going on in the rooms behind. With Virgo rising, the first impression is neat and observant instead.

How to find yours, and whether it deserves the attention it gets, is a question of its own: I answer it in [what is my rising sign, and does it matter](/blog/what-is-my-rising-sign/).

## You’re not only your sign {#together}

Now put the three together. Sun in Capricorn, Moon in Cancer, Leo rising: a solid house, a warm room, a bright façade. People meet the Leo, live alongside the Capricorn, and only the ones let into the room meet the Cancer.

One way to hold all three at once is to give each its verb. In my notes every sign has one: Capricorn “I use”, Cancer “I feel”, Leo “I will”. So the same person reads: I come across as “I will”, I’m built around “I use”, and when the door shuts, “I feel”. Try the sentence with your own three. If one part sounds like someone else, that’s a cross, not a failure.

That’s why “I’m a Scorpio” says so little, and why “I don’t date Scorpios” judges a whole house by its address. You’re a mix of everything, and the chart goes well beyond three placements. It’s also why [astrology is not an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/): a description isn’t a permission.

If you read tarot, two of the three have a card. The Sun has the Sun, arcana XIX. The Moon has the High Priestess, following the astrology Waite inherited; to me, [she guards the part of the house no visitor gets to see](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/). The Moon card, XVIII, is in fact a Pisces card, which confuses a lot of people.

## A fourth one I like to add: Jupiter {#jupiter}

Once you have your three, I like adding Jupiter: its sign and its house.

Most astrology books call Jupiter the planet of expansion and luck: where you grow, and where you risk overdoing it. In the seventh house, partners and one-to-one relationships, they would say you grow through other people, and can promise them more than you can give.

Someone’s biggest struggles show elsewhere. For those I look at Saturn first, then Chiron and Lilith; [Saturn, Lilith and Chiron](/blog/saturn-lilith-chiron/) explains why.

I want to be clear on this one. Jupiter wasn’t part of my tested numbers: the 30 to 40% overall, and the 75 to 90% for Saturn, Lilith and Chiron, don’t cover it. It’s a habit of mine, not a result.

So here’s the next step. Write your three signs on a piece of paper, then Jupiter’s sign and house. Tick or cross each one, as honestly as you would for a stranger, the way I did when I [tested my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). Then show it to someone who knows you well and let them tick too.
