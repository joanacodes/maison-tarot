Every reader knows this scam, most refuse to name it, and the people who fall for it are not stupid — they're grieving, or frightened, or exhausted, and someone offers them a reason. I'm going to describe it exactly, because knowing the script is the whole protection.

## The script {#the-script}

**Act one: the cheap reading.** Free, or ten euros, or "pay what you feel". Low enough that you don't think about it. The reading is fine — vague, warm, a few hits from things you've said.

**Act two: the discovery.** Near the end, the reader's face changes. There's something. A block. A dark energy around you. A curse, sometimes "generational", sometimes placed by a jealous person, sometimes by an ex. It explains everything: the breakup, the illness, the money, the bad luck. Of course it does. That's what it's for.

**Act three: the removal.** It can be lifted. It takes work — candles, a ritual, prayers, an object that must be bought or blessed. There's a price. It's higher than the reading, but reasonable-sounding. You pay because for the first time in months someone has offered you a cause and a cure in the same sentence.

**Act four: the complication.** The ritual worked, mostly, but the curse was stronger than expected, or it's come back, or there's a second one underneath. Another session. Another price. This continues for as long as you have money. Some victims lose tens of thousands; the retired teacher in the French documentary lost her entire savings.

## Why it works {#why-it-works}

Not because people believe in curses. Because people in pain want an explanation that isn't "life is sometimes like this" — and the scam supplies one, with a solution attached. It also works because the removal ritual is unfalsifiable: if things improve, the ritual did it; if they don't, the curse is stronger. There's no outcome that ends the relationship except running out of money.

And it works because of shame. Victims rarely report it, because reporting means saying out loud that you paid for a curse removal. The scammers count on that silence.

## The tells, before act two {#tells}

You can catch it early. A reader who asks for a lot of personal detail before starting. A "free" reading that exists to lead somewhere. Urgency of any kind. A reader who never says "that one didn't land". Flattery about your energy. [The full list of red flags is here](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/); the curse scam is where most of them lead.

## What real tarot has to say about curses {#real-tarot}

Nothing. There is no curse card. The frightening cards — [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), [the Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/), [the Devil](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/) — describe transformation, sudden change and self-made chains, and every one of them has a way out drawn into the picture. A reader who sees a curse in a spread is not reading the spread.

The House refuses the subject entirely: [no curses, no blocks, no attachments, no protection rituals](/disclaimers/#refusals). Not as a policy against superstition, but because the moment a reader accepts the premise, the con is one step away.

## What to do if it's happening to you {#what-to-do}

1. Stop paying. Today. There is no unfinished ritual that will harm you if left incomplete.
2. Stop talking to them. They are better at this conversation than you are; don't have it.
3. Keep every message and receipt.
4. Report it — to the platform if there is one, to your bank if you paid by card, and to your consumer protection authority. In most countries this is straightforward fraud.
5. Tell one person. The silence is part of how the scam works.

And if you're the one who's been carrying the fear — that something is wrong with you, that it's on you — the thing that's wrong is that someone lied to you for money. That's all. There is nothing on you. There never was.
