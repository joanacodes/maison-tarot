The Magician in a career reading says you have what you need to start. Every tool is on the table — the skill, the idea, the words, the practical sense — and the next move is yours. It’s a beginning, not a result, and its one trap is a good show standing in for the work.

## The Magician at work, through its lenses {#lenses}

The drawing: a young man stands behind a table, one hand raising a white wand to the sky, the other pointing at the ground. On the table lie the four suits of the deck: a wand, a cup, a sword, a pentacle. An infinity sign floats over his head; roses and lilies grow around him against a flat yellow background. No audience, no road, nothing made yet.

In my notes, the Magician is the card of the four elements — one tool each for Fire, Water, Air and Earth — plus the ether that links them. He knows how to do everything, and he stays anchored: one hand up, one hand down. The wand receives, the pointing hand passes it on. At work, that’s someone who can catch an idea and land it on the ground.

The number is [1](/library/numerology/number-1/): the initiator, the raw diamond, the start of a cycle. Its advice is humility — get ready to learn — and its minus is the lack of it, when we are all students our whole life.

The element is Air: the mind, the decision, the word. The energy is masculine: acting, building, showing yourself. The colours say the same before any book — yellow for will and self-expression, red for action, white for an intention that’s still clean. The astrology, if it speaks to you, is Mercury, the messenger: how you think, speak and learn. Every lens is on [the Magician’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/).

## What the Magician says about work: skills and initiative {#work}

At work, the Magician is the moment your skills meet a starting point. A new job where you’ll use everything you know. A project you’ve been asked to lead. A pitch, an interview, a first client. Sometimes it’s something of your own: the side project, the freelance plan, the shop you keep sketching in a notebook.

The four tools make a good check-up. The wand: do you still want this? The cup: do you care about the people it’s for? The sword: have you thought it through? The pentacle: does it hold up in practice — time, materials, the plain logistics? Most people are strong on two and quietly avoid the other two.

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Magician next to the Eight of Pentacles. Lenses first: 1 then 8, a start then mastery; Air then Earth, an idea then the work of the hands. What you might say out loud: “You know how to do this — now it’s about doing it every day until it shows.” Then ask whether that lands, and let them correct you.

**The Magician doesn’t promise a result; it says the tools are in your hands.** Cards aren’t magic, you are — and the same goes for the man at the table.

## The Magician reversed in a career reading {#reversed}

Reversed, the tools stay on the table. Either nobody picks them up — the project polished in your head for two years, the CV rewritten instead of sent — or they’re used for show. That’s the minus I note for this card: trusted blindly, the Magician can lie, or pretend. At work it’s the pitch better than the product, the confident meeting with nothing behind it.

It can point at you or at someone you deal with. If it’s you, the question is gentle: what would you need to learn to stop bluffing? If it’s a new partner or a client who dazzles, check what has been made, not what has been said. And sometimes reversed is the opposite worry: real skill you don’t dare show because you’re sure someone will find you out. A raw diamond is still a diamond.

## The Magician next to other cards at work {#combinations}

{{< cards "pe08 ar04 pepa" >}}

Next to the Eight of Pentacles — a craftsman at his bench, carving one pentacle after another — the skill becomes a craft. In my notes, that eight is mastery and necessary effort: the start has to be repeated until it holds.

Next to the Emperor, the idea gets a structure: rules, a budget, a timetable. That’s often the next step for a Magician’s project, and I’ve written about [the Emperor at work](/blog/emperor-tarot-career/).

Next to the Page of Pentacles — a young man in a field, holding a coin up to study it — it’s the advice of 1 in a picture: learn first. A course, an apprenticeship, the months in a new trade when you’re a student again.

## “Will my business work?” — and a kinder question {#question}

With the Magician, the question is often: will it work if I go for it? Should I quit and start my own thing? I understand why. Starting something costs money, security, sometimes pride, and you’d like a card to carry part of the risk.

I don’t read on that. Whether to resign, invest or borrow is a money decision, and those stay out of my readings ([what I don’t read on, and why](/disclaimers/#refusals)). Whether a business works depends on clients and markets no card can see. And a card that said yes might make you skip the checks the Magician himself would make. For the figures, an accountant or a business adviser will help more than any spread.

A kinder question: “Which of my four tools am I not using yet?” Or: “What could I start, small, this month, to find out?”

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Take a sheet of paper and draw four columns: wand, cup, sword, pentacle. Under each, write what you already have for the thing you want to start. The emptiest column is your first task.

If you want a layout for a work decision, the [career tarot spread](/blog/career-tarot-spread/) reads it without letting the cards decide. And if the first move on your mind isn’t at work, there’s [the Magician in love](/blog/magician-tarot-love/).
