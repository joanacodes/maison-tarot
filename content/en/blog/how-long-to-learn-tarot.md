You can do your first tarot reading today, in minutes. Of all the people I've helped, none needed more than three attempts. Reading fluently — moving through a spread without stopping, and knowing when you're wrong — takes months of regular practice.

Here's the timeline as I've seen it, stage by stage.

## The timeline at a glance {#at-a-glance}

| When | What you can do | What to practise |
|---|---|---|
| The first hour | Read one card from what you see | One card, said out loud |
| The first month | Read three cards as a sentence, for yourself and a friend | One card a day, then three |
| Months two to six | Read for people who tell you nothing, and know your misses | Counting, in a notebook |
| After that | Read with ease, and keep learning | The extra lenses, new people |

None of these stages needs a gift, a certificate or 78 memorised meanings. They need cards on a table and someone to read for.

## The first hour: your first reading {#first-hour}

Draw one card and ask it for three things: the strongest colour, the number, the element. Then say one sentence that joins the three. That's a reading. The [three-question cheat sheet](/blog/tarot-cheat-sheet/) gives you the grid.

Say the card is the Nine of Pentacles: a yellow sky over green vines, a nine, Earth. Joy resting on something that grows, the home stretch, material things. “You're near the end of a long effort, and starting to enjoy what it's brought you.” You didn't need to know the card's name to say that.

It works fast because you aren't learning information; you're learning to let go.

My own start went that way. The deck arrived from friends at Christmas 2020, my first, and I read with it the very next day, 26 December. That reading was accurate, which surprised me, as I'd thought some training would have to come first.

## The first month: one card, then three {#first-month}

For the first weeks, draw [one card a day](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/). Write a line about it. You'll meet a good part of the deck this way, one card at a time, without having memorised any of it.

When one card starts to feel small, move to three, read as one sentence. Then find a friend who agrees to tell you nothing and read for them. That's where you'll make most of the [classic first-month mistakes](/blog/tarot-beginner-mistakes/) — reshuffling, rescuing a miss, asking them to explain first — and that's fine. Everyone does.

**By the end of the first month, you should be reading, not studying.**

## Months two to six: fluency {#fluency}

Fluency is when you stop translating. Think of learning to drive: you can start the car in the first lesson, but you stop thinking about the gears only after months on the road.

In tarot, it looks like this. You read five cards as one story instead of five separate meanings. You see the Queen of Cups and think of a kind of person rather than a definition. You notice that a spread is all Swords before you've read a single card. Within a few months of my first reading, I was reading for strangers in Facebook groups.

The other half of fluency is knowing when you're wrong. That only comes from counting, with two columns side by side: your words, and what the person confirmed. A few dozen counted readings show you your own pattern — which subjects you read well, where you invent. [How I count](/method/) is set out in full.

After that come the extra lenses — feminine and masculine energies, the astrological modes, astrology itself. They refine what you already see rather than replacing it, they're optional, and they're where the slow, pleasant part of learning lives. Take one at a time, for a few weeks each.

## What doesn't speed it up {#what-doesnt-help}

Memorising the booklet. It feels like progress and turns into reciting.

Buying more decks. A second deck teaches you a second art style, not a second skill.

Reading for yourself many times a day. You learn more from one reading for a stranger than from twenty for your own worries.

Books help with the lenses; for the numbers, I recommend Corrine Kenner's tarot book. But [what a book can't give you](/blog/learn-tarot-from-a-book/) is the jolt of hearing yourself say something true about the person in the other chair.

## When is tarot “learned”? {#learned}

Honestly, never quite. I still miss now and then — about once every ten readings, on my tally — and I still learn from those.

A more useful finish line: you read a card without reaching for the book, you can say “that one's a miss” without flinching, and you enjoy it. In my experience, people who practise a few times a week get there within months, not years.

## Start today {#start-today}

Draw one card now. Say its colour, its number and its element, then one sentence. That's day one of the timeline.

If you'd prefer company for those first steps, every [workshop](/workshops/) gets a card into your hands before ten minutes have passed, and [the learning path](/learn-tarot/) sets out the lenses in order.
