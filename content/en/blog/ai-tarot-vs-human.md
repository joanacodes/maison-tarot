The question "AI or human?" assumes they're doing the same job. They aren't. Here is what each actually does, and what that means for the person sitting down.

## What the human does that the machine can't {#human-only}

**Takes nothing.** I read for people who tell me nothing. That single constraint is what makes a hit mean something: it couldn't have come from them. A model can't work this way; you have to type something, and what you type becomes the reading.

**Misses.** A real reader says "that's not it" and moves on. I do it roughly one time in ten, out loud, and write it down. A model hedges every sentence into something that can't be wrong, which is the same as never being right.

**Refuses.** Health, death, pregnancy, court cases, curses — [I won't read them](/disclaimers/#refusals), because a wrong word there does harm. A model will read anything you ask, at any hour, in any state you're in. It has no concept of the night you shouldn't be read.

**Stops.** When someone in front of me is in distress, I stop and say why. A model has no face to watch, no voice to hear, and no capacity to decide that the right thing is not to answer.

**Reads the spread.** The relation between cards — a 1 beside a 10, three Cups and one Sword, one reversal in five — carries most of a reading. Models explain cards; readers read layouts. This isn't a subtle difference; it's the difference between a dictionary and a sentence.

**Breathes.** I mean this literally. A reading is two nervous systems in a room. The moment I say the sentence and the person's face changes before they've decided what to say — that moment is the reading. There's no equivalent on a screen, and the people who say there is haven't sat at a table.

## What the machine does that most humans don't {#machine-only}

**Explains the picture, patiently, forever.** Ask why the Empress has a shield with Venus on it, why the Hanged Man looks calm, what the pomegranates on the High Priestess's veil are — and you get a clear answer, instantly, without judgement, at any hour. As a way to learn [what's drawn on the cards](/library/tarot-symbols/), it's better than most books and nearly all readers.

That's real value, and I use it that way. It's just not reading.

## The dangerous one isn't the bad one {#dangerous}

If AI readings were obviously poor, nobody would worry. The problem is that they're smooth. Confident tone, no hesitation, an answer for every question, a warm sign-off. Those are the exact properties I'd list to describe [a reader to avoid](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/): never wrong, always available, never refuses, flatters. The machine has them by design, without malice, and that's arguably worse — there's nobody to be dishonest, so nothing feels off.

## Which to use, and when {#which-to-use}

- To learn what a card shows: the machine, freely.
- To practise describing cards out loud: either, but a human will tell you when you're reciting.
- To be read: a human who takes no context and names their misses.
- At 3 a.m., in a bad state, wanting an answer: neither. [That's the night for a break](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), not a reading.
