In love, the Fool is a beginning that nothing justifies yet: a crush, a first date, a relationship started on enthusiasm alone. It’s a lovely card — free, light, open — with one condition: look where you put your feet. The little dog on the card is there to remind him, and you.

## Zero, Air, and a dog at his heels {#zero-and-air}

The Fool is zero: the breath before the cycle, the page before the first word. In love, that’s the moment before anything has a name — before “seeing someone”, before “together”. Zero has no fixed place, either. You can put the Fool at the very start, innocence setting out, or at the very end, someone who has been through everything and comes back light. A first relationship and a new one after many can both draw this card; the second kind knows where the cliff is and walks there on purpose.

His element is Air, the mind: a relationship that lives mostly in your head for now, all ideas and no weight. His energy is masculine — going, showing yourself, acting first.

My note on him has two halves. The plus: the stick and bundle — he’s free in his decisions and can make a life anywhere. The minus: he’s naive, and [the dog at his feet](/library/tarot-symbols/dog/) is trying to remind him. The colours and Uranus are on [the Fool’s card page](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/).

## The crush, the leap, the clean slate {#crush-leap-slate}

If you’re single, the Fool is the crush, the dating app downloaded on a whim, the trip where you meet someone. It says go — and keep your eyes open. One piece of advice from my own notes: if you have a crush, don’t look up their birth chart. Let the magic happen for the two of you. A chart read in secret gives you a story about someone before they’ve had a chance to tell you theirs; I explain why in [the article on reading someone’s chart without asking](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/).

If you’re together, the Fool brings fresh air into something settled: a spontaneous trip, a new project together, a decision taken on the spot — moving in, moving country. It can be wonderful. The dog’s question is whether both of you are jumping, or one is jumping and the other being pulled along. Say a couple draws it the week one of them gets an offer abroad. The card doesn’t say go or stay. It asks whether the leap has been talked about by two people, or announced by one.

After a break-up, the Fool is the urge to start again from zero. A new city, a new haircut, a new person. Sometimes that’s freedom. Sometimes it’s a jump made to get away rather than to go somewhere, and the card can’t tell those two apart for you. You can, if you ask yourself honestly what you’re walking towards.

**The Fool isn’t asking whether it’s reasonable; he’s asking whether you know you’re at the edge.**

## Who walks beside him {#beside-him}

Next to the Two of Cups, the crush finds an answer: two people meeting, cups at the same height. I go into that card in [the Two of Cups in love](/blog/two-of-cups-tarot-love/).

Next to the Moon, the edge of the cliff is in fog: you’re stepping into something you can’t fully see yet, and patience is your best friend.

## “Should I jump?” — and what the dog would ask {#should-i-jump}

The question I hear with this card is “Should I jump in?” or “Is this new person the one?” It’s a hopeful question, and a vulnerable one: starting something means you could lose it, and you’d like to know before you risk it.

The cards can’t tell you who someone else is, or whether it will last. That person isn’t in the room, and the future of two people is made by two people, day after day. What the Fool can do is show you how you’re walking.

A kinder question: “What do I already know about where I’m stepping — and what is my dog trying to tell me?” Everyone has one: the friend who raises an eyebrow, the feeling in your stomach on the second date, the detail you noticed and decided not to see. Listening to it doesn’t mean not jumping. It means jumping awake, with your eyes on the ground for a second before they go back to the sky.

## Reversed: frozen at the edge, or leaping blind {#reversed}

Reversed, the Fool is the beginning that doesn’t start, or starts without looking at all. Held back: you’d like to meet someone, but every first step feels like a cliff, so you stay on the path and nothing happens. In excess: you leap into every new thing, and the dog has stopped barking because nobody listens any more. Same card, two speeds. It’s worth asking which one is yours this month, without judging yourself for either.

## Your first step, with other people around {#first-step}

New beginnings are a good moment to learn, in love and in tarot. Every person I’ve helped to read managed it on the first to third try; nobody needed a gift, only the willingness to say what came. You can’t be told what it’s like. You can only try.
