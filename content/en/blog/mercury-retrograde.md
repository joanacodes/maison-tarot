Mercury retrograde is the stretch of about three weeks, roughly three times a year, when Mercury seems to move backwards across the sky as seen from Earth. It doesn't really reverse; it only looks that way from where we stand. In astrology it's read as a time to review, revise and reread. What it isn't is the reason your train was late.

## What Mercury retrograde is, astronomically {#astronomy}

Seen from Earth, the planets drift through the zodiac in the same direction, week after week. Retrograde means that for a while a planet appears to go the other way against the background of the stars. Astronomers call it apparent retrograde motion, and the word that matters is “apparent”.

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. It goes round in 88 days, against our 365, so about every four months it catches up with the Earth and passes between us and the Sun. Around that pass, the angle from which we see it changes so fast that it seems to slide backwards for about three weeks, then picks up its usual direction again. The days when it seems to stand still before turning are called stations.

**Mercury never turns around; we're watching it from a planet that's moving too.**

Every planet does it, seen from Earth. With Mars, Jupiter or Saturn it's the mirror image: we're the ones overtaking. Pass a slower car on the motorway and, against the hills behind it, it seems to drift backwards for a moment. Nobody in that car put it in reverse.

## What astrology says Mercury rules {#what-astrology-says}

In astrology, Mercury governs thought and speech: how quickly your mind moves, how much you enjoy words, how you take things in ([Mercury's Library page](/library/astrology/mercury/) goes further). By extension it rules messages, contracts, trade and short journeys, and the two signs of the mind, Gemini and Virgo.

So when Mercury appears to walk backwards, astrologers read three weeks of words starting with “re-”: review, revise, reread, reply, return. The usual advice follows from that. Read a contract twice before signing. Back up your phone. Check the train times. Expect old subjects, and sometimes old contacts, to come back up.

There's something in that advice I like. It gives three weeks a theme, and the theme is going back over things before rushing forward. If people use a retrograde to slow down and reread, it does them good, whatever the planet is doing.

## What it isn't responsible for {#not-responsible}

The list of things blamed on Mercury retrograde is long: the late train, the frozen phone, the email sent to the wrong person, the ex who texts at midnight, the argument that started over nothing. All of these happen in every three weeks of the year.

What changes during a retrograde is that you've been told to watch for them. So you notice them, and each one becomes evidence. That's not a criticism of anyone; it's how attention works.

In my own chart test, Mercury held about a third of the time — useful as a lens on how someone thinks and talks, nowhere near strong enough to plan a life around ([how I tested my chart, box by box](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/)). That was Mercury in birth charts. Retrograde periods were never part of the test: I have no count for them, and I haven't measured train delays against retrograde calendars, so I won't claim more than that. What I can say is that I don't move a signature, a trip or a difficult conversation because of these dates.

And it can't carry the blame for what we say. A retrograde can be a good reason to reread; it can't be the reason for the message you sent in anger. Blaming the planet leaves the other person with no apology at all, and [astrology is not an excuse](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).

## Using a retrograde with your cards {#with-your-cards}

Mercury's card is [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), and through its two signs it also carries the Lovers (Gemini) and the Hermit (Virgo). Three ways of thinking: the one who lays every tool on the table, the one who has to choose, and the one who checks by lantern-light. My notes make the Magician the messenger, his wand raised to receive and send — and, trusted blindly, a skilled pretender. That's worth keeping in mind for three weeks given over to words.

If you like the “re-” theme, give it a small spread about communication. Three cards, once per retrograde:

1. *What did I say too fast?*
2. *What didn't I hear?*
3. *What would I say now, if I said it again?*

Before looking anything up, let each card's colours, element and number speak. You don't have to send anything afterwards. Put the three cards in your notebook with a line about each, and go back to that page the day Mercury turns direct.
