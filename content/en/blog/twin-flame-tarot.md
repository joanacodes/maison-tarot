I refuse a handful of subjects, and most of them are obvious — health, death, court cases. This one isn't obvious, and it's the one I get asked about most by people in the worst shape. So it gets its own explanation.

## What the idea is {#the-idea}

The twin-flame story goes like this. There exists one person who is your literal other half, a soul split in two. Meeting them is overwhelming. The relationship is intense, then unbearable, then one of you runs. The "runner" flees; the "chaser" waits. Separation is a stage. Union is inevitable, eventually, once both have done their "work". Every twin-flame reading is a location report: where are we in the stages?

You can see what this does to a person. Any relationship — including a cruel one, an abandoned one, a one-sided one — becomes a cosmic process with a guaranteed ending. Leaving isn't rejection; it's *running*. Waiting isn't stuckness; it's *the chaser's work*. The story cannot lose.

## Why I refuse it {#why-refuse}

Three reasons, and the third is the real one.

**It reads another person.** Where is *he* in his journey, what does *she* need to heal before union. The other person isn't in the room and hasn't consented. [Reading absent third parties is a line I don't cross](/blog/tarot-ethics-short-version/) for anyone, and it's the entire content of a twin-flame reading.

**It can't miss.** Together? Union. Apart? Separation stage. Apart for years? Deep separation stage. Blocked you? Runner behaviour. There is no outcome the story doesn't absorb, which means the reading is never a reading — it's a translation of whatever happened into the vocabulary of destiny.

**It keeps people in harm.** This is the one. A large share of the people who ask me for this reading are describing relationships that were, by any plain account, unequal or unkind. The twin-flame frame tells them that the pain is proof of the bond and the departure is a stage. I've watched it keep someone waiting for three years. A reading that does that isn't neutral, and I won't give it a deck to hide behind.

## What tarot actually has for this {#what-tarot-has}

The cards have a lot to say about a person who is waiting. The Eight of Cups walking away from what was built. The Two of Swords, blindfolded, holding two blades level because deciding is unbearable. The Devil with the chains loose. The Star, finally, pouring water out with nothing held back.

None of these is about the other person. All of them are about the one sitting in front of me. That's the reading I'll do: told nothing, one card or three, about *you* — what you're holding, what you're avoiding, what "waiting" is standing in for. It doesn't have stages, and it doesn't promise a reunion. It sometimes ends with a person putting the deck down and making a phone call they should have made months ago. That's a better outcome than "union".

## If this is you {#if-this-is-you}

I'm not going to tell you the person who left wasn't important. I don't know. I'll tell you that no reader can know either, and that the ones who say they can have found a way to be paid monthly.

If you want a card, ask it about you. If you don't like what it says, that's allowed — [the cards aren't an authority](/disclaimers/#readings). And if the waiting has become the whole of your life, [that's the moment for a break](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), not another reading. The person you're waiting for is not in the deck. You are.
