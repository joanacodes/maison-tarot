A tarot reading is not an instruction. It can show you something you hadn’t put into words; what you do next is yours. So my position fits in one line: act on what you feel, not on what a card said.

## What ‘tarot is not an authority’ means {#what-it-means}

An authority is something whose word you follow even when you don’t agree with it: a law, a doctor’s prescription, a parent when you’re seven. You don’t have to be convinced; you comply.

A card isn’t that. It’s a picture that helps you look. It can surprise you, move you, name something you’d been avoiding. But the decision stays on your side of the table, and so does the responsibility for it. It’s written on [my disclaimers page](/disclaimers/#readings) in almost those words.

Say someone has a job offer they quietly want, draws the Tower, and turns the offer down. A month later they’d say “the cards told me no”. They didn’t. Fear borrowed a card’s voice, and a tower struck by lightning was the right shape for it.

**A card can show you something; it can’t give you permission, and it can’t take it away.**

## Why a card can’t carry a decision {#why-not}

Three reasons, all of them ordinary.

Readings miss. By my own count, about one in ten of mine didn’t hold, and I’ve published [how I counted, misses included](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/). One in ten is a good rate for a conversation. It’s a poor rate for handing over your life.

Images move. The same card can read differently from one person to the next, and from one day to the next for the same person. A Tower on a calm Tuesday and a Tower after a sleepless night won’t say the same thing, and neither is a ruling.

Readers are human. In my notes on the Magician, his minus side is that he can pretend: trust him blindly and he can lie to you. That’s true of any reader, me included. Not always from bad intent; sometimes we’re tired, sometimes we want to please. A good reading leaves room for you to say “no, that’s not it”.

## What to do with a reading instead {#what-to-do}

Treat it as a question to check, not an answer to obey. Here’s the routine I suggest:

1. Write down what the reading said, in your own words, the same day.
2. On a separate line, write what you feel about the situation — without looking at the cards again.
3. Where the two agree, ask yourself what you’re going to do about it, concretely.
4. Where they disagree, act on what you feel, and note what happens.
5. Reread both lines a month later.

That last step is where you learn something. You’ll see where your gut was right, where the card caught something you’d missed, and where both were wrong. Either way, you chose.

And if you can’t tell what you feel, that’s information too. Give yourself a day without the cards, then finish this sentence out loud: “If nobody could see what I chose, I would…”. Whatever comes after “I would” is usually closer to your gut than any card. For bigger choices, I wrote a whole piece on [using the cards without letting them decide](/blog/tarot-for-decision-making/).

## The Wheel is turned by you {#the-wheel}

There’s a card I like for this. In my notes, the Wheel of Fortune isn’t luck landing on you: it’s up to you to turn the wheel, to create your own luck. Next to it I’d put the Emperor, who sits armoured under his robe and gives orders without ever taking them.

A reading should never become your Emperor. And when [the Wheel of Fortune](/library/tarot-cards/wheel-of-fortune/) comes up while you’re waiting for the cards to decide, I read it as a nudge back to you: the wheel has a handle, and it’s on your side. Cards aren’t magic, you are.

## When you start asking before every choice {#dependency}

If you catch yourself pulling a card before every decision — what to wear, whether to text, which train to take — that isn’t about tarot any more. It’s a search for outside validation, and the deck has become a crutch.

Put it in a drawer for a few weeks. Not as a punishment: as training. You need to be confident in yourself, to make decisions and mistakes, to accept being imperfect. That’s a muscle, and a reading can’t lift the weight for you. If you’re lucky enough to have a stable life, you can play with the cards a little more. More on that in [why you should quit tarot reading, for a while at least](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).

## Where to go from here {#from-here}

If you want to understand how a reading works rather than receive one, [learn to read the cards yourself](/learn-tarot/). It’s the surest way to stop treating them as an authority: once you’ve watched a picture become a sentence in your own mouth, you know who wrote the sentence. And if you want to see how a reading goes wrong — through timing, through interpretation, or as a plain miss — I take it apart in [why a reading can be wrong](/blog/why-a-reading-can-be-wrong/).
