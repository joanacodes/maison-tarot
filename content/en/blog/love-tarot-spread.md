An honest love spread reads two things: you, and the relationship as it is between you. It doesn’t read the other person’s mind. Five cards, a question you can act on, and room for everything you feel about it.

## What this spread reads, and what it leaves aside {#what-it-reads}

It reads your side: where you are, what you give, what you need. And it reads the space between you and the other person — the relationship as it lives day to day, not their private thoughts.

It works whether you’re in a relationship, between two, or not looking at all. If you’re single, the fourth position reads the kind of bond you’re making room for rather than a particular person.

The cards don’t know what’s in someone else’s head. They don’t know what’s in yours either, until you look. **What a love reading can see is what you bring to the table — and that’s the part you can change.**

## The five positions {#positions}

Draw five cards, one per position.

1. Where I am in love right now. Your state today: open, tired, hopeful, guarded.
2. What I bring. What you give in a relationship — what people feel when they’re with you.
3. What I need. Often something you haven’t said, sometimes not even to yourself.
4. The relationship as it is. The space between you: its mood, its rhythm, what’s stuck in it. If you’re single, the kind of bond you’re making room for.
5. One thing I can do. Small, concrete, within reach this week.

Every position starts with “I” or “the relationship”. None starts with “he” or “she”.

## A love spread, worked through {#example}

Take a reading laid for a relationship, with these five: the Four of Cups, the Queen of Pentacles, the Ace of Cups, the Two of Swords and the Page of Cups.

Where I am: the Four of Cups. A young man sits under a tree, arms crossed, looking at three cups on the grass while a hand from a cloud offers him a fourth. Water, number 4: stable, settled — and on its minus side, carrying on out of habit. Comfortable, a little closed, not seeing what’s offered.

What I bring: the Queen of Pentacles, seated in a garden with a coin in her lap and a rabbit at her feet. [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): steadiness, practical care, the person who makes a home feel like one.

What I need: the Ace of Cups, a cup overflowing in a hand that comes out of a cloud. Water, number 1: a new start in feeling. After all that steady giving, what’s needed is to receive.

The relationship as it is: the Two of Swords. A woman sits blindfolded, two swords crossed over her chest, the sea behind her, nearly everything grey. Air, number 2 — cooperation, with stubbornness as its minus — and grey, in my colour grid, is neutrality, no particular will. Between the two of you, a conversation that nobody starts.

One thing I can do: the Page of Cups, a young man in a blue tunic looking at a fish that pops out of his cup. The Page is the learner and the messenger; blue is emotion. Say something light and a little surprising first — a question, not a verdict.

Three Cups out of five: this is about feeling that wants to move. As one message for this couple: “You’ve settled in and stopped noticing what’s offered; you give steadily, and what you need is to receive; between you there’s a conversation nobody is starting — start it, lightly, this week.”

If you lay this for a friend, ask them to tell you nothing first — that’s [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/) — and let them say afterwards how much of it was true. What wasn’t is recorded as a miss — no sixth card to patch it up.

## Why “does he love me?” is left aside {#does-he-love-me}

It’s the question most people arrive with, in one form or another: does he love me, is she thinking of me, will they come back. It comes from love, and often from worry, and there’s nothing silly about it. Pulling the petals off a daisy — he loves me, he loves me not — is the same question, far older than any deck.

Here’s why I’d rather not put it to the cards. The other person has no seat at this table and hasn’t said yes to a reading; nobody wants a stranger poking around their bedroom, and reading someone’s heart without asking feels close to that. Whatever card comes out, you’ll read it through your hope or your fear. And even a “yes” wouldn’t tell you what to do on Monday. [Reading about someone who isn’t there](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/) goes into this at more length.

What to ask instead:

- What do I need from this relationship, and have I said it?
- What am I bringing that I’d like to be met?
- What would help me feel steadier while I wait for an answer?

And sometimes the kindest step is outside the cards altogether: ask the person. If the question in your chest is [whether someone will come back](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/), there’s a whole article on what that question does to you, and a gentler one to put in its place.

## Try it tonight {#try-tonight}

1. Write the five positions on a slip of paper.
2. Shuffle, and lay five cards in a row.
3. Before any meaning, name each card’s element, number and main colour.
4. Sum up the row in one sentence that starts with “I”, and say it aloud.
5. Write it down, and reread it in a week.
