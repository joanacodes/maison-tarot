Most people never do their first reading because they think they have to know 78 meanings first. You don't. You need one card, three things you can see on it, and the nerve to say a sentence out loud before you've checked whether it's right.

## What you need, and what you don't {#what-you-need}

A deck. I use the 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith, drawn by Pamela Colman Smith, because the images do most of the work. Any deck with figures, colours and numbered suits will do for today.

A pen and something to write on. Not for the meaning of the card; for what you said about it. A quiet ten minutes. That's the list.

What you don't need: a candle, a cloth, a cleansed space, a crystal on the corner of the table. I sold sage and candles in 2021 and stopped, because none of it ever changed what happened in a reading.

You also don't need the little white book. Put it in a drawer. **Every meaning you read before you look at the card is a sentence you won't say yourself.**

## Step one: ask a question you'd ask a friend {#question}

The quality of the reading is set before you touch the deck. "Will he text me" gives a card nothing to work with. "What am I not seeing about this conversation" gives it a whole room.

Avoid yes-or-no questions on your first pull. They turn the card into a coin, and a coin doesn't need 78 pictures.

Some subjects stay off the table whatever the phrasing: health, death, pregnancy, an ongoing court case, a money decision, curses, and other people who haven't asked to be read. The reasons are on [the refusals page](/disclaimers/#refusals), and they hold at your kitchen table as much as at mine.

Say the question out loud once. It stops you quietly changing it after you've seen the card.

## Step two: shuffle and draw one card {#draw}

Shuffle however your hands like. Overhand, riffle, swirl them face down on the table. There's no correct way. Stop when you feel like stopping, take the top card or slide one from the middle, and turn it face up.

One card. Not three, not a Celtic Cross. Three cards give a beginner three places to hide, and the eye jumps between them looking for the friendly one. [A single card](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/) has to be looked at.

If it lands upside down, turn it the right way up. Reversals are a second lesson, not a first.

Now look at it for a full ten seconds before you do anything else. Longer than feels comfortable.

## Step three: read the colours first {#colours}

Ask yourself one question: what colour is everywhere? Not the detail, the wash. Say it.

A card drowning in yellow is about clarity, exposure, things brought into the light whether you want them there or not. A grey sky with a small figure in red is a different story from a green field with the same figure. Blue water at the bottom of the frame says the matter lives in feeling, not in plans.

You don't have to know the codes yet. Notice what the colour does to you and say that. "It's cold." "There's one warm thing in the middle of a lot of grey." Those are readings. The full [colour lens](/library/tarot-colours/) is in the Library; on your first pull the point is to prove to yourself that you already see something.

## Step four: number and element {#number-element}

Two facts are printed on almost every card, and together they give you a structure.

The suit gives you the element. Cups are water: feelings, relationships. Swords are air: thoughts, words, decisions. Wands are fire: drive, what you want. Pentacles are earth: body, money, work. Ask which of these your question belongs to, and whether the card agrees. When it disagrees, that's information. The [four elements](/library/four-elements/) are a quick read if you want more than a line each.

The number gives you the stage. One is the spark. Two is a choice or a pairing. Five is the wobble, when the thing you started stops going to plan. Ten is the end of a cycle, full or overloaded. I teach the [1-to-10 cycle](/library/numerology/) through the story of how an app grows, and it sticks because you've lived it.

Put them together in one phrase. "Feelings, at the wobble stage." "Work, right at the start." That's most of your reading already.

## Step five: say it out loud {#say-it}

Look at the card with the question in your head, and say one sentence. Not the meaning of the card. What the card says about the question.

Use your own words. If the sentence is "I've been pretending this is fine and it isn't," say that.

This is the part everyone wants to skip, and it's the whole method. When I sit friends down to try, the moment I teach for is the one where they say something true and are shocked by what came out of their own mouth. It can't be described, only reached, and one card is enough to reach it.

If nothing comes, look at the figure on the card. What are they doing? Would you want to be them? Answer that out loud and you'll find you've answered the question.

## What to do with what you said {#after}

Write the sentence down, dated, next to the question. That's your first [reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/) entry. Reread it in a week; the card won't have changed, but you'll know whether what you said held.

Don't draw a second card to soften the first. If it landed too hard, it landed. If it made no sense, it's a miss, and a miss is a miss.

And don't do anything because of the card. Tarot is not an authority. Act on what you feel, not on what a card said. You now have a sentence in your own voice about your own situation; that was yours before the card arrived.

When you want the other lenses, the ones that turn a single pull into a reading you can do for someone else, that's what [learning tarot here](/learn-tarot/) is built around.
