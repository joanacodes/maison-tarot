A first tarot reading at Halloween is much calmer than the decorations suggest. Someone shuffles, a few cards go on the table, the reader says what they see, and you say whether it lands. Nobody summons anything, and nothing is decided for you.

## What happens in a tarot reading, step by step {#step-by-step}

At a party, a fair or an online event, a first reading usually goes like this.

1. The reader asks whether they may read for you. You can say no, or “not here”.
2. Someone shuffles — you or the reader. There’s no wrong way to do it.
3. One card, three cards, sometimes more, go face up on the table.
4. The reader describes what they see — colours, figures, numbers — and what comes to them.
5. You listen. Then you say what landed and what didn’t.
6. It ends. Ten minutes is plenty for a first time.

**A careful reader always asks before reading you, and stops when you ask them to.** That’s the whole of the etiquette on your side: you’re allowed to be curious, sceptical, or both.

At Maison Tarot there’s one more rule, before step one: you keep your question and your situation to yourself. It feels odd for about a minute. It’s also the only way you’ll know, at the end, that I read you and not what you’d told me. [The silence rule, explained](/blog/the-silence-rule/).

## Will the cards be scary? {#scary-cards}

You may well meet one of the three cards people fear most: Death, the Tower or the Devil. At Halloween they look like part of the costume. In a reading, none of them announces a death, an accident or something evil.

Death, in Pamela Colman Smith’s 1909 drawing, rides a white horse while a sun sits on the horizon between two towers. It’s about an ending that makes room: closer to the harvest than to the grave, a season closing so another can start. The Tower is a shake-up — a structure that wasn’t holding, coming down fast. The Devil is attachment: two figures chained so loosely they could lift the chains off themselves.

Every card has a plus and a minus, and these three have as much plus as any. [Why the scariest cards are kinder than they look](/blog/scariest-tarot-cards/) goes through them one by one.

If a reader turns one of them into a threat — “something bad is coming, and I can help, for a fee” — that’s your cue to thank them and leave.

## What a careful reader won't read on {#what-they-wont-read}

Some questions come up more around Halloween, because the season is full of the dead and the unknown: whether someone who is ill will get better, whether there’s a curse on the family, whether you’re pregnant. People ask them out of worry and love, and I understand why.

I don’t read on them, and here’s why. A card can’t diagnose, can’t see inside a body and can’t date a death, and a wrong word on those subjects can stay with someone for years. The same goes for ongoing court cases, money decisions and people who aren’t in the room.

There’s usually a gentler question underneath — “how do I look after myself while I wait for the results?”, “what do I want to say to my grandmother this winter?” — and the cards can hold that one. Here is [the full list of what I don’t read on, and why](/disclaimers/#refusals).

## Party readings, fairs and online events {#party-readings}

Where it happens changes what to expect. At a party, other people are listening, so a careful reader keeps it light, and you can ask to step aside or stop. At a fair or a stall, decide what you’re willing to spend before you sit down, and notice whether the reader gives you room to say “no, that doesn’t fit”.

Online, at a free event, you may be one of several people read in front of the others. [What to know before a free online reading](/blog/before-a-free-online-reading/) explains how that works at Maison Tarot, and what to watch for elsewhere.

Wherever you are, two signs tell you it isn’t a reading but a sale: urgency (“we need to go deeper, today”) and a curse that only this person can lift.

## After your first reading {#after}

Write down what was said, the same evening, before you decide what it meant. Memory edits fast; a week later you’ll remember the reading you wanted, not the one you had.

Then don’t act on it. [Tarot is not an authority](/blog/tarot-is-not-an-authority/), and a ten-minute reading at a party even less so.

And if some of it missed, that’s normal. By my own count, about one reading in ten misses, and a good reader says so rather than bending the miss into a hit.

If one sentence keeps going round in your head that night, say it out loud to a friend, or go back and ask the reader what they meant. A line from a card shouldn’t keep you awake; a conversation usually brings it back down to size.

If you enjoyed it, the next step is to sit on the other side of the table. [Halloween night 2027 is a free online readings evening](/events/halloween-2027/), a good place to watch how it’s done, and the [Samhain spread](/blog/samhain-tarot/) is three cards you can try at home on the night itself.
