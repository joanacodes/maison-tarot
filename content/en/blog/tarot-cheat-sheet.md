You don't need 78 meanings on a cheat sheet. You need three questions you can ask any card: what colour takes up the most room, what number is it, and which element does it belong to. The colour gives the tone, the number says where the situation stands in its cycle, the element names the area of life. Join the three and you can read a card you've never seen.

## Question 1: what colour is everywhere? {#colour}

Start with colour, because it asks for no knowledge at all. Look at the colour that fills the card, then at the small one that stands out. In the 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith, Pamela Colman Smith used her palette consistently, which is what makes this work.

| Colour | What it says |
|---|---|
| Red | Passion, action, strength, impulse |
| Orange | Creativity, dryness, libido |
| Yellow | Joy, optimism, pride, the inner child, will |
| Green | Nature, growth, birth, prosperity |
| Blue | Emotions, water, purifying, vitality |
| White | Purity, innocence, peace |
| Black | Absence, emptiness, dark thoughts, reality |
| Grey | Neutrality, no particular will, stability |
| Purple | The one chosen by a group |

Each colour has [its own page in the Library](/library/tarot-colours/), with the cards where it dominates.

## Question 2: what number is it? {#number}

The numbers on the cards aren't codes. They're the ten steps of a cycle you already know from any project, company or relationship.

| Number | Where you are in the cycle |
|---|---|
| 1 | A beginning, a rough diamond |
| 2 | A bond, a contract, weighing up |
| 3 | The machine running |
| 4 | Stability, structure, a home |
| 5 | Conflict, a test, making do |
| 6 | Success, things flowing fast |
| 7 | Patience, analysis, harvest |
| 8 | Mastery, an empire, control |
| 9 | The home stretch, last efforts |
| 10 | A transition, a page turning |

Major arcana above 10 reduce: add the digits, so the Devil (15) plays a 6 and Justice (11) a 2. The court cards are ranks rather than numbers: the Page learns, the Knight acts, the Queen masters from within, the King decides outwardly. The full cycle is told on [the numerology page](/library/numerology/).

## Question 3: which element? {#element}

| Suit | Element | The area it speaks about |
|---|---|---|
| Wands | Fire | Drive, creation, passion — and what burns too fast |
| Cups | Water | Emotions, relationships, what's under the surface |
| Swords | Air | Thinking, decisions, words that cut or clarify |
| Pentacles | Earth | Matter, money, the body, the result of work |

The majors have elements too: Strength is Fire, Death is Water, the Star is Air, the World is Earth. Each one is noted on its card page. [The four elements](/library/four-elements/) are set out in full, each with its suit.

## Three answers, one sentence {#one-sentence}

Here's how the three questions join up, on two cards.

{{< cards "pe05 sw03" >}}

The Five of Pentacles: two figures in the snow pass a lit stained-glass window. Colour — black night and white snow, with one small window glowing yellow: absence, cold, and a little warmth within reach. Number — five, conflict, making do with what's left. Element — Earth, material things. As a sentence: “A hard time with money or means, getting by on what's left, and help close by that nobody has looked up to see.”

The Three of Swords: a red heart pierced by three swords under a grey, rainy sky. Colour — red passion inside grey neutrality. Number — three, a bond that was working. Element — Air, words and decisions. “Words that cut into something that was going well.” My own notes add a second side: three swords through one heart can also be a blood pact. **Every card has a plus and a minus, and the three questions show you both.**

## Reading a card you've never seen {#unknown-card}

This is the exercise that ends every [workshop](/workshops/): each person reads a card they don't know, in front of the others. It almost always works. Here's how to do it at home.

1. Go through the deck and put aside the cards you already feel you know.
2. Draw one from what's left. Cover its name with your thumb.
3. Ask the three questions out loud, one word each: colour, number, element.
4. Join them in one sentence that starts with “Something…”.
5. Only then look the card up in [the card pages](/library/tarot-cards/), and compare.

You'll often find you said, in your own words, what the page says in its words. When you didn't, note where you parted ways — that's your next thing to look at, not a failure. The exercise also works on a deck you've never held: the numbers and suits survive from one tarot to the next, though each deck has its own palette.

## Two more questions, for later {#two-more}

I teach five lenses. The last two refine the picture once the first three come without effort.

| Lens | The question | The short answer |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | Is it holding or acting? | Feminine waits, imagines, works behind the scenes; masculine acts, shows itself, decides fast. Cups and Pentacles lean feminine, Wands and Swords masculine. |
| Mode | Is it starting, lasting or changing? | Cardinal starts: the Emperor, the Chariot, Justice, the Devil. Fixed holds: the Hierophant, Strength, Death, the Star. Mutable changes: the Lovers, the Hermit, Temperance, the Moon. |

[Feminine and masculine](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) have nothing to do with being a woman or a man: they're two dynamics we all carry, in different proportions. The mode applies to the twelve majors that carry a zodiac sign.

## What the questions don't tell you {#limits}

They give you the tone, the stage and the area. They don't give you the story. That comes from the scene on the card, from its neighbours in the spread, from what rises when you look — and from the person in front of you saying yes or no.

And no cheat sheet can tell you whether you were right. Don't take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself.

## Keep it on one page {#one-page}

Copy the tables onto one card and keep it in your deck box for a month. When you no longer reach for it, you've learned the lenses. If you'd like the whole method in a few printable pages, [the Readers' Guide](/guide/) is free.
