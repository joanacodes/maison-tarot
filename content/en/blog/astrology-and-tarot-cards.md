Twelve major arcana carry a zodiac sign. On most of them the sign repeats what the drawing already says: the rams’ heads on the Emperor’s throne are Aries, Strength’s lion is Leo. On six, the sign changes the reading, often by giving the card a different element from the one the picture suggests.

## What a sign adds to a tarot card {#what-a-sign-adds}

The pairings come from the Golden Dawn, the late-nineteenth-century order that Arthur Edward Waite and Pamela Colman Smith both belonged to. Each sign goes to one major arcanum, and it brings three things at once.

A verb, from my notes: Gemini “I think”, Virgo “I analyse”, Capricorn “I use”. An element: Fire, Water, Air or Earth. And a mode: cardinal signs start things, fixed signs hold on, mutable signs adapt.

The minor cards from Two to Ten carry a planet in a sign as well. That finer layer has its own article, on [the decans](/blog/decans-tarot/). Here I stay with the majors.

## Three mutable signs: the Lovers, the Hermit, Temperance {#mutable}

Mutable signs change, but for a purpose: in my notes, Gemini changes to communicate, Virgo to serve, Sagittarius for the experience.

### The Lovers {#the-lovers}

A man and a woman under an angel: the drawing pulls everyone towards romance. Gemini is an Air sign, and Air is the mind. With the sign, the card moves from the heart to the head: a choice argued out, what is said and by whom. At its best Gemini is the great reporter; at its worst, a game of Chinese whispers. Say someone draws the Lovers about a job offer. With the sign, I’d talk about a decision being discussed, perhaps with someone else, before I’d talk about love.

### The Hermit {#the-hermit}

An old man alone on a snowy peak, holding a lantern. Without the sign, it reads as withdrawal. Virgo is Earth, practical, and in my notes it’s the engineer, the adviser to the throne: solitary by nature, yet wanting to be useful to something bigger. The retreat has a job. You step away to work something out, then you hold the light up for others.

### Temperance {#temperance}

An angel pours water from one cup into another, one foot in a pool. Everything in the picture says Water and patience. Its sign, Sagittarius, is Fire: “I see, I try”, the traveller who gathers knowledge by leaving, then mixes it to see what it gives. **Temperance is full of water and carries a Fire sign, and that gap is where the reading changes.** It becomes an experiment more than a pause: try the mixture, adjust, try again. The [Temperance card page](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/) has the full drawing.

## Two fixed signs: Death and the Star {#fixed}

Fixed signs hold: stable, enduring, and on the other side slow and stubborn. Scorpio holds on to emotional attachments; Aquarius holds on to ideas.

### Death {#death}

A skeleton in black armour on a white horse, a king fallen, the sun rising between two towers. In my notes Death isn’t about dying: it’s the harvest, starting again, making life fertile once more. Scorpio adds Water and “I desire”. What ends is something you were attached to, and the hard part is emotional rather than practical. Because the sign is fixed, the letting go is slow. Say Death comes out for someone who has already moved house: with Scorpio, I’d say the move is done on paper and not yet inside.

### The Star {#the-star}

A woman kneels by a pool and pours water from two jugs, one into the pool, one onto the land, under a large star. Without the sign: hope, calm after the storm. Aquarius is the water-bearer, and it’s an Air sign, not a Water sign; people mix that up all the time. Its verb is “I know”, to be heard as “I think I know”: the new idea, the start-upper. The water isn’t kept, it’s given. Say someone draws the Star about a project they’ve kept to themselves. With Aquarius, I’d say the next step is showing it to someone.

## One cardinal sign: the Devil {#cardinal}

Cardinal signs start things, and Capricorn starts them pragmatically.

The drawing: a horned figure on a pedestal, a man and a woman chained to it, the chains loose enough to lift off. Without the sign, readers go straight to temptation and something wicked. Capricorn is Earth and “I use”: ambition, structure, work. **With Capricorn, the Devil is rarely about evil and often about something useful that took over.** A job that was a tool and became the master. A salary you organise everything around.

One of the Capricorn archetypes in my notes is Miranda Priestly, and the film is called *The Devil Wears Prada*. I couldn’t have planned it better. Say someone draws the Devil about their career: with the sign, I’d ask what they use that now uses them. The [Devil’s card page](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/) goes further.

## Using the sign without letting it take over {#use-it}

Three cautions, said calmly.

The sign of the card isn’t the sign of the person. A Gemini who draws the Lovers draws the same card as anyone else.

The sign comes after the drawing, the number and the colours. If it contradicts what’s in front of you, the drawing wins.

And it’s a lens, not proof. When I [tested my birth chart box by box](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), 30 to 40% held overall: enough to be useful, not enough to decide anything. If you’re new to signs, start with [what a birth chart is](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/).

To try it: take one of these six cards out of your deck. Read it once without the sign and once with it, and write both down. Then look up the sign in [the Library’s table of the twelve signs](/library/astrology/), and its mode among [the three astrological modes](/library/astrological-modes/).
