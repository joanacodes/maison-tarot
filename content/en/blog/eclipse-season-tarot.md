Yes, it's OK to read tarot during an eclipse. An eclipse doesn't damage a deck, doesn't turn a reading upside down and doesn't make the cards dangerous. What it does is bring a lot of people to their cards at the same moment, often worried — and that's the part worth handling with care.

## What an eclipse is, and what eclipse season means {#definitions}

A solar eclipse is when the Moon passes between the Earth and the Sun and hides part or all of it. It can only happen at a new moon. A lunar eclipse is when the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, and it can only happen at a full moon.

Eclipse season is the window when eclipses are possible: a little over a month, about twice a year, when a new or full moon falls close to the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's. Most seasons bring two eclipses, about two weeks apart — one solar, one lunar — and now and then three. In astrology those two crossing points are the [North and South Nodes](/blog/north-node-south-node/).

Astrologers read a solar eclipse as an intensified new moon: a beginning, often one you didn't plan. A lunar eclipse is an intensified full moon: something comes to a head or closes. The dates, signs and times for the coming year are in [Eclipses 2027](/blog/eclipses-2027/).

## Why some readers say “don't read during an eclipse” {#why-some-say-wait}

You'll hear three reasons, and each comes from a real place.

The first is that eclipses are the wildcards of the astrological year: fast, unpredictable, hard to read. So a reading done in the middle of one feels unreliable, as if the picture might change before the ink is dry.

The second is a wish to rest. Some readers treat eclipse weeks as a pause: no new projects, no rituals, no big questions, only watching what happens.

The third is about the reader. Some people feel more tired or more emotional around eclipses and would rather not read in that state.

The third is the one I'd keep. Not because of the sky, but because the state you read in changes a reading far more than the date does. I know that tiredness from the readings themselves: interpreting takes energy, and there are days I stop after the second hour, to be safe.

And if waiting feels right to you, wait. Nothing is lost by leaving the deck in its box for a fortnight; a question you postpone is still there when you come back to it.

## What actually changes during an eclipse {#what-changes}

The noise changes. Eclipse weeks come with videos and posts announcing that something big is on its way, and people who usually draw one card a week start drawing several a day. Each new card is one more chance to frighten yourself.

Say you draw [the Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/) during an eclipse week. Some will tell you it counts double. It doesn't. The Tower during an eclipse is the same Tower as on any other Tuesday: something built on a weak base comes down, suddenly, and leaves room for something sturdier. Its number, its element and its colours haven't moved.

What a reading can hold in eclipse season is what it holds in any week: how you feel about a change that's already under way, what you'd like to let close, where your energy is going. What it can't do any better than usual is tell you the date something will happen.

Nor does your deck soak anything up. There's nothing to [cleanse afterwards](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/): if a reading felt heavy, it's you who needs a rest, not the cards.

My own frame for astrology applies here too. In [my test of my own birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), between 30 and 40% of the descriptions held overall, and eclipses never came into it. A third is enough to treat an eclipse as a theme to think with, and not enough to let it decide when you're allowed to shuffle.

## Three questions before you shuffle in eclipse season {#three-questions}

**The sky doesn't change the cards; your state changes the reading.** So the questions are about you.

1. *Am I calm?* If you're reaching for the deck because a video scared you, put it down and come back in a few days. The advice in [reading when you're upset](/blog/reading-tarot-when-upset/) holds in any week, eclipse or not.
2. *Have I already read on this?* If you have, the new card won't be more true than the first. Reread what you wrote instead.
3. *Is this a decision?* The cards can help you think; they can't decide for you, eclipse or not. Act on what you feel and know.

If all three answers are fine, read. One reading, written down, then the deck goes back in the drawer.

And if you're outside watching a solar eclipse, the cards can wait. Your eyes need proper eclipse glasses, not a spread.

## One reading per season: the lunar eclipse {#one-reading}

If you want one reading in eclipse season, make it on the night of the lunar eclipse. It falls on a full moon, so [the napkin-sized full moon spread](/blog/full-moon-tarot-spread/) works as well then as any month, with its three questions: what the light has shown, what you can set down, what you'll hold on to. Note each card with a line of your own, then reread the page at the next eclipse season, about six months later.
