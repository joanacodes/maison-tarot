Then you're where almost everyone starts, and it rarely means you can't read. Most people are waiting for something much louder than what actually comes. Start with what you can see, keep talking, and check afterwards.

## What “feeling something” actually feels like {#what-it-feels-like}

We all receive impressions: images, sounds, words, smells, sensations, emotions. Some people are sharper in one channel than another. One person sees a picture, another hears a word, a third feels a weight in their chest. What almost nobody gets is a film playing behind their eyes with a narrator.

More often, it feels like your own thought. A word turns up — "kitchen", "brother", "too late" — and it sounds exactly like you. That's why so many beginners decide they felt nothing: what came was quiet and ordinary, and they threw it away while waiting for the real thing.

Say you draw a card for a friend and the word “staircase” crosses your mind. It seems random, so you keep it to yourself and wait for a feeling. That word was the feeling. Say it — “I don’t know why, but a staircase” — and let her tell you afterwards whether it meant anything.

**What you're waiting for is usually louder than what comes.** I've tried to describe [what reading a card feels like from the inside](/blog/what-reading-tarot-feels-like/), and the most honest word I have is "ordinary".

## Why nothing comes {#why-nothing-comes}

My notes give two usual reasons, and neither of them is "you don't have the gift". There isn't one — or rather, everyone has the same one.

The first is your own block: too much pressure. You want it to work, you're watching yourself, and the part of you that receives goes quiet. The second is the other person's: someone who's afraid of hearing something can sit across from you like a closed door. Then the thing to do is talk with them and reassure them before you go on: nothing on the table is a verdict, and they can stop you whenever they like.

I'd add two more. Tiredness, because interpreting takes energy, and an empty tank reads empty. And reading for yourself on a question that matters too much: it's hard to receive anything when you already know which answer you want.

## Start with what you can see {#start-with-what-you-see}

You don't need to feel anything to begin. You need to look.

Say you draw the Nine of Pentacles. A woman stands in a garden heavy with grapes, in a yellow robe, a bird on her gloved hand. Before any feeling: yellow is joy and optimism. Nine is the end of a stage, the last efforts before the finish line. Pentacles are Earth: whatever has value for you, the result of work. My notes on this card say enjoying what comes, and gratitude for everything you have.

That's three things you can say out loud with no feeling at all: something worked, it's nearly complete, take the time to enjoy it. [The five lenses of the method](/method/) exist for exactly this. They give you something true to say while the rest catches up.

Then say the sentence I give every beginner: *I'm about to talk nonsense.* It lowers the pressure enough for the quiet things to come through, and it's at the heart of [how I teach reading from the first card](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/).

## Practise letting go, not feeling {#practise}

You can't train yourself to feel on command. You can train yourself to let go, and in my experience that's the only practice there is.

- Draw one card a day. Describe what's on it, what you see, what you receive. Then three cards, then more.
- Do something meditative: [sitting still for a few minutes](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/), painting, or looking at a painting the way you'd look at a card. You can read in anything.
- Write down the small things — the stray word, the odd image — even when they feel like nothing. Check later whether they meant something. That's also how you learn to tell [reading from making it up](/blog/am-i-making-it-up-tarot/).

Go slowly. My own note on this says: don't go too high too fast, or you might not find your way back home. Start crescendo.

For what it's worth, every person I've helped to read managed it somewhere between the first and the third try. Not on demand, and not always in the way they expected.

## When nothing is the answer {#when-nothing-is-the-answer}

Some days nothing comes, and that's information too. Tired, worried, distracted: put the cards down. After readings you can feel very tired, because interpreting takes energy. Listen to your body and rest. It isn't [your deck that's tired](/blog/do-tarot-cards-get-tired/); it's you, and that's allowed.

I can't promise you'll feel what I feel. Impressions come differently to everyone, and nothing here is proven; it's my experience and what I've seen in the people I've taught. What I can say is that the feeling you're waiting for isn't the entry ticket. Saying what you see is.

You can't be told what it's like. You can only try. That's why a [workshop at Maison Tarot](/workshops/) starts with a card in front of you before any explanation.
