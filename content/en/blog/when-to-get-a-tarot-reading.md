A good time to get a tarot reading is when you’re steady enough to hear something unexpected and free enough to disagree with it. In my notes I keep two good reasons to sit down for one: shadow work, and a check-up of your energy and mindset. Both work best with your feet firmly on the ground.

## As a check-up of your energy and mindset {#check-up}

Think of it like taking your car in for a service when nothing’s broken. You’re not looking for a disaster; you’re looking at where your energy goes, what you’re carrying, and what you’ve stopped noticing.

Say you sit down, say nothing, and draw the Ten of Wands next to the Four of Swords.

{{< cards "wa10 sw04" >}}

The Ten of Wands is someone carrying a bundle of wands so big they can’t see where they’re going. In my notes, it says keep the creative energy, without overloading. The Four of Swords is a figure lying still, at rest. Together: you’re carrying more than you need to, and some rest wouldn’t be a luxury.

**A check-up reads the present, not the future.** It won’t tell you what happens next month. It can show you how you’re walking into it, which is the part you can change. Every few months is plenty; once a week stops being a check-up.

Write down the two or three sentences that struck you, and read them again at the next check-up. Putting two readings side by side, months apart, often tells you more than either one did on the day.

## For shadow work {#shadow-work}

Shadow work means dealing with the things you keep pushing away so you can get through the day — the things that, left alone long enough, end up holding you back. It’s like a cell where you’ve put everything you never wanted to look at, or couldn’t deal with at the time.

A reading can point at the door of that cell. Say the Moon comes up, or the Five of Cups: something isn’t clear, or something lost is still being mourned. The card doesn’t open the door for you; it shows you there’s a door. You have to acknowledge the veil before you can take it off.

It’s hard work, and it’s slow, even if the moment of understanding can be very quick. Can you do it with a therapist? Of course, and you should; a reading can sit beside that work, never replace it. If you want to start gently, [shadow work for beginners](/blog/shadow-work-beginners/) gives you one card to begin with.

And be as gentle and tolerant with yourself as you are with others. That part matters more than any card.

## When to wait {#when-to-wait}

The timing that matters most is yours, and some states make a reading unhelpful.

- *You’re asking the same question for the third time.* At that point the cards aren’t answering anything; the doubt is asking. I wrote about this in [why you should quit tarot reading for a while](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), and it pairs with this page.
- *You want the cards to choose for you.* A reading can lay out a decision, but [the choice should stay yours](/blog/tarot-for-decision-making/).
- *You’re in the middle of a storm.* Some people arrive almost fidgety, with doubt running through them. What helps then is to breathe, make a decision, or go through the thing you’re afraid of — not another reading.
- *The question is about health, a pregnancy, a death, a court case or money.* These questions come from worry or love, and I understand why people bring them. I don’t read on them, and [here’s why](/disclaimers/#refusals); a doctor, a lawyer or an adviser will serve you better, and a reading can still hold how you’re coping.

If your life is fairly stable, you can play with tarot a little more. If it isn’t, it’s the decision-making muscle that needs the work, not the deck.

## Does the calendar matter? {#calendar}

Full moons, Mercury retrograde, birthdays, the end of October: many people wonder whether one date is better than another. Occasions are nice: they give you a reason to stop and look. Plenty of people finally ask for a reading around [Samhain](/blog/samhain-tarot/), and that’s a lovely tradition.

But the date matters much less than the state you arrive in. A calm Tuesday beats an anxious full moon.

## A five-point check before you sit down {#check}

1. You can say what you’d do if the reading told you nothing new.
2. You haven’t had a reading on this question in the last month.
3. You feel more curious than anxious.
4. You’re ready to hear “that’s a miss” and not ask again.
5. You plan to make the decision yourself afterwards.

Four out of five, and it’s a good time. Take a break from your cards now and then; when you come back, you’ll hear them better.
