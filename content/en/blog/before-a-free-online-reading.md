"Free reading" means two different things on the internet. One is a gift. The other is a hook. This is how the House does the first kind — and how to recognise the second.

## How an event at the House works {#how-it-works}

You register. On the evening, a few people are drawn at random — six, usually. Each gets a reading of about ten minutes, in front of everyone, and this is the rule that makes it worth anything: **you tell me nothing.** Not your question, not your situation. I draw, I say what comes, and I expect to talk nonsense, because [that's the method](/method/).

Then you tell me whether it landed. If it did, good. If it didn't, I say "that's a miss", it goes in the notebook, and we move to the next person. Everyone else watches, asks questions, and learns — that's the point of doing it in public. It's a reading, but it's also a lesson.

Nobody is sold anything. There is no "book a private reading to go deeper", because the House doesn't sell readings at all — it sells [lessons](/workshops/), and you'll hear about them exactly once, at the end, from the person who's spent the evening telling strangers where she got it wrong.

## What's refused, even for free {#refused}

Health, death, pregnancy, an ongoing court case, money, and anything to do with a "curse". If your question is one of those, I'll say so kindly and move on. Not because a card can't fall on them — because a wrong word there does damage, and I'm wrong one time in ten. [The full list, and why.](/disclaimers/#refusals)

And if you're in distress that evening: say so, privately, and I'll skip you. A reading is not the right tool for that night. A break is.

## How to get the most from it {#get-the-most}

- **Don't prepare a question.** The best readings happen to people who came for the lesson and got drawn by accident.
- **Be doubtful.** Doubt is the best state to be read in; it keeps both of us honest. The people who write me three days later saying "how did you know" are the ones who frowned during.
- **Write down what was said**, right after, before you decide what it meant. You'll be surprised what you remember differently a week later.
- **Don't act on it.** Act on what you feel. [Tarot is not an authority](/disclaimers/#readings), and a reading in front of twenty strangers even less so.

## The four signs of a hook {#the-hook}

Elsewhere, a free reading is often the opening move. Four things give it away:

1. **It ends with urgency.** "I see something serious, we need to go deeper — privately, today." Fear, followed by a price.
2. **It finds a curse.** Or a block, or an attachment, or negative energy — always something only they can remove, always for money. [This is the definition of a scam.](/disclaimers/#refusals)
3. **It's never wrong.** A reader who cannot say "that one missed" is not reading you. They're keeping you.
4. **It asks for your details first.** Your name, your birth date, your question, your ex's name. Everything a cold reader needs to look accurate. The House asks for nothing, on purpose.
