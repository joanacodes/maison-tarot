A Saturn return is the period when Saturn comes back to the exact place in the zodiac where it was when you were born. It happens first around 29 to 30, again around 58 to 59, and it has a reputation as the great crisis of the late twenties. It doesn't have to be one. And a lot of what gets blamed on it might also be what being 29 does to anyone.

## What a Saturn return is {#what-it-is}

Saturn takes about 29 and a half years to go once around the zodiac. When you're born, it sits in one sign at one degree — say 12° Capricorn. About 29 years later it's back at 12° Capricorn. That's the return.

It isn't a single day. Saturn moves slowly and, seen from Earth, appears to back up for a few months each year, so it can cross your birth degree up to three times. Astrologers usually talk about a season of a year or two around it, and some count the whole time Saturn spends in your Saturn sign: about two and a half years, from 27 or 28 onwards.

To find yours, look up where Saturn sits in your [birth chart](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/), sign and degree, then check when Saturn reaches that degree again. Most chart sites have a transit tool that shows it.

In a chart, Saturn is structure, responsibility, limits, and the work that takes years ([its Library page](/library/astrology/saturn/) has the detail). So its return is read as a review: Saturn comes back to check the foundations.

## What people report around 29 {#what-people-report}

The stories are remarkably alike. Leaving a job taken to reassure someone else. Ending a relationship that was comfortable but going nowhere, or committing to one for good. Moving city. Going back to study, or finally stopping. A sense of being asked, quite suddenly, to be an adult — with the bills, the choices and the consequences.

The astrological reading is that whatever you built on solid ground holds, and whatever you built to please others, or out of habit, starts to shake. Not as a punishment: as a check.

Say someone took a stable office job at 23 because it made their parents breathe easier, and at 29 they hand in their notice to train in the trade they wanted at 18. An astrologer would call it a textbook Saturn return. A friend might call it about time.

**Saturn's lesson isn't that things fall apart; it's that what's left standing is yours.**

## What might just be being 29 {#being-29}

Here's the honest part. Around 29, a lot happens to almost everyone. Studies are long finished; the first career choice has had six or seven years to prove itself. Friends marry, have children or move away. Parents get older. And 30 is a round number that makes people take stock.

Almost everyone reaches their Saturn return between 28 and 30 — the same years that bring all of the above anyway. That makes it very hard to separate the planet from the age. When someone tells me their Saturn return turned their life upside down, I believe them. Whether it was Saturn or being 29, I don't know.

My own count can't settle it. When I [tested my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/), Saturn's position at birth held 75 to 90% of the time, more than almost anything else. But I tested birth placements, not transits like the return, so for this one I have no number.

Neither answer makes the period less real. A hard year is a hard year, whatever you call it.

## Getting through it without the doom {#without-the-doom}

- *It's a review, not a sentence.* Saturn is the planet of things that become solid because they were hard. That's the end of the story, not only the middle.
- *It's not an alibi.* “It's my Saturn return” explains a mood; it doesn't excuse leaving people in the lurch. [Astrology is not an excuse.](/disclaimers/#astrology)
- *The decision stays yours.* If you bring the question to the cards, the rule is the same as always: I read; you decide.
- *If it gets heavier than a hard year, talk to someone.* A friend first, and a therapist if it doesn't lift. That isn't failing your Saturn return. It's handling it well.

## A three-card check-in for your Saturn return {#spread}

Saturn's card is [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/): the wreath, the end of a cycle, what you ended up building. Through Capricorn, the sign Saturn rules, it also carries the Devil: structure that has turned into a chain.

That pair makes a small spread. Three cards, once — not every week of the transit.

1. *What have I built that holds?* The World's question.
2. *What am I carrying that was never mine?* The Devil's.
3. *What do I want to build next?* Yours.

Read each card through its colours, its number and its element before you look anything up.
