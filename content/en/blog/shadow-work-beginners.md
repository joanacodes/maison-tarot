Search the phrase and you'll find guided journals, forty-question prompt lists, and videos that begin "so you've been called to do your shadow work". I want to give you the version that doesn't need to be called.

## Where the idea comes from {#where-from}

Carl Jung. The shadow is his word for the parts of yourself you've pushed out of view because they didn't fit who you decided to be: the anger, the envy, the wanting, the cruelty you're capable of, and — this is the part people forget — the strengths you've disowned because they scared someone. Jung's claim is that what you refuse to look at doesn't leave; it runs things from behind.

That's the whole idea. Everything else — the prompts, the moon phases, the "healing your inner child" language — is packaging.

## What it isn't {#what-it-isnt}

It isn't therapy. It isn't a substitute for therapy. It isn't a practice you do alone at 2 a.m. when you're already falling. [Not everything is about spirits](/blog/tarot-and-mental-health/), and not everything is about shadows either; sometimes what you're looking at is depression, and that needs a person with training, not a prompt list.

It also isn't dramatic. The TikTok version makes it sound like descending into a cave. Mostly it's noticing that you're jealous of a friend and saying so to yourself in plain words. That's it. That's the work.

## Why tarot is good at it {#why-tarot}

Because the cards do the confronting. If I ask you "what do you refuse to see about yourself?", you'll produce something acceptable. If you draw the Five of Swords — a man collecting swords from people who've walked away, smiling — and I ask "who's this?", you might say "me, last year, with my brother" before you've decided whether to admit it. The picture goes first. You follow.

That's also why the card you dislike is the useful one. Everyone has a card they never want to see. It's usually the one that's about them.

## The spread {#the-spread}

Three cards. No candles. Told nothing — draw before you think about the question.

1. **What I show.** The version of me I've agreed to be.
2. **What I hide.** The thing behind it.
3. **What it costs me.** What the hiding takes, every day.

Say each card out loud in plain words before you interpret it: what colour is everywhere, what number, what the figure is doing. Then one sentence per card. Write the three sentences down. Close the notebook. Don't reread it for a week.

If you want one card instead of three: the [Devil](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/). Look at the chains. They're loose. The question is why the figures haven't taken them off.

## Where it stops being yours to do alone {#where-it-stops}

Looking at a thing you'd rather not is uncomfortable, and discomfort is normal here. What isn't normal is the floor giving way. If you draw the cards and what comes up is not "I'm envious" but something that makes it hard to breathe, or something that's been there for years and you've never told anyone — stop. That isn't shadow work; it's the edge of something that needs a professional, and there's no failure in that.

The cards will still be there afterwards. And you'll read them better, because the thing you were avoiding will have a name.
