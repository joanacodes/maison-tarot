The darkest weeks of the year don’t call for a big spread. They call for a slow one: five cards, drawn one evening at a time, each read on its own before you see the next. This one asks what the year has used up, what keeps you warm, what can rest in the dark, and what will be ready when the light comes back.

## What a slow tarot reading is {#slow-reading}

A slow reading is a spread you lay out over several days instead of all at once. You draw one card per evening, in the order of the positions, and you read it alone: what’s on it, what you see, what comes. The next evening you draw the next one and read it beside the first.

Say you start on the winter solstice, on 21 or 22 December in the northern hemisphere, the longest night of the year. By the fifth evening you have a full spread, and every card has had a day to settle before the next one joined it. If you read from the southern hemisphere, the darkest weeks fall in June, and the spread works just as well then.

**A slow reading gives each card a day, which is more time than most of us give ourselves in December.**

It also fits the season. Winter isn’t a time for resolutions; it’s a time for rest, and the cards can follow that pace.

## The five positions {#positions}

One card per evening, laid left to right.

1. *What the year has used up.* What you’re tired from, honestly.
2. *What keeps me warm.* People, places, habits that hold you through the cold.
3. *What can stay in the dark for now.* Something that isn’t ready, and doesn’t need to be.
4. *A small light to tend.* One thing to look after this winter, without forcing it.
5. *What will be ready when the days lengthen.* Not a promise; something to prepare the ground for.

On the fifth evening, read the row as one story.

## A winter spread, card by card {#example}

Say someone draws, over five evenings, the Five of Pentacles, the Four of Wands, the Seven of Pentacles, the Hermit and the Ace of Pentacles.

*What the year used up:* the Five of Pentacles, two figures walking through the snow, one on crutches, past a lit church window. In my notes it’s poverty, snow, and the church where warmth could be found. Tired and cold — and walking past the help that’s right there.

*What keeps me warm:* the Four of Wands, garlands hung between four wands and people raising flowers in front of a castle. In my notes, foundations, marriage, building a family. Home, and the people in it.

*What can stay in the dark:* the Seven of Pentacles, a man leaning on his hoe, looking at the coins growing on a bush. [Seven is patience](/library/numerology/number-7/), reflection before action, and its advice in my notes: everything is temporary. Something is growing, and it doesn’t need checking every day.

*A small light to tend:* [the Hermit](/library/tarot-cards/the-hermit/), alone on a snowy peak with a lantern. Withdraw a little, and keep enough light for the next step, not for the whole road.

*What will be ready:* the Ace of Pentacles, a hand from a cloud holding a coin above a garden, with a path leading out through an arch of hedge. One is a beginning; Pentacles are Earth, something concrete. A real project in spring, once the ground thaws.

Three Pentacles out of five: this winter is about the body and the practical things. The sentence out loud might be: “You’ve been out in the cold too long and walked past the warm window. Go home, rest, let the thing you planted grow without you, and something solid will be there to start in spring.” Then check it, and write it down whatever the answer.

## Darkness isn’t only something to get through {#darkness}

In my colour grid, [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) is absence and void, and also emptying the mind and reality: the bare thing, without varnish. The dark weeks can do that too. Fewer hours of light, fewer plans, a little more truth.

I used to wait for a light to come on before I walked into a dark room; turning on every light at night was living in fear without noticing it. Today I walk through my place with the lights off, and a noise at night doesn’t frighten me. I wrote about that change, and what shadow work has to do with it, in [afraid of the dark](/blog/afraid-of-the-dark/).

The honest limit: a spread doesn’t lift a heavy mood. If the dark weeks weigh on you every year more than rest can fix, mention it to your doctor; that’s a question for a person, not a deck.

## Start on the longest night {#start}

Choose your five evenings, put the deck somewhere you’ll see it, and draw the first card on the solstice. Read it for five minutes, write three lines, and leave the rest for the next evening. You can’t be told what it’s like. You can only try.

Then, when the year turns, the [New Year spread](/blog/new-year-tarot-spread/) is waiting, with a card for each month ahead.
