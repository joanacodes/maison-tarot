Your life path number is your full date of birth added up and reduced to a single digit. Numerology reads it as the main theme of your life. I read it as one step of the 1–10 cycle, which turns it into a good question to ask yourself rather than a verdict.

## What a life path number is {#definition}

In numerology, the life path number is the number you get by adding every digit of your date of birth until only one remains. Modern numerology usually treats it as the central number of a chart: a description of the road you’re on, the lessons that keep coming back, the way you tend to move through things.

For example, someone born on 3 August 1976 has a life path 7.

It’s not the same thing as [angel numbers](/blog/angel-numbers/), the repeated sequences people notice on clocks. A life path is calculated once and stays yours; an angel number turns up on a receipt and is gone.

## How to calculate your life path number {#calculate}

1. Write your date of birth in full: day, month, and the year in four digits.
2. Add every digit together.
3. If the result has two digits, add those two. Repeat until one digit is left.

The worked example, for 3 August 1976 (03/08/1976):

0 + 3 + 0 + 8 + 1 + 9 + 7 + 6 = 34, then 3 + 4 = **7**.

Some numerologists reduce the day, the month and the year separately first: 3; 8; 1976 → 23 → 5. Then 3 + 8 + 5 = 16 → 7. The two methods usually land on the same number. When they don’t, it’s almost always because of a master number.

Master numbers are 11, 22 and 33. If you land on one of them along the way, many numerologists stop there and keep it as it is. I reduce them the way I reduce the major arcana: 11 becomes 2, 22 becomes 4, 33 becomes 6. If master numbers speak to you, keep them — the 1–10 reading still sits underneath.

## What each number says in the 1–10 cycle {#meanings}

The Library tells the cycle of [numerology](/library/numerology/) through the story of Instagram, from the first prototype to a platform nobody recognises. Here is the short version, turned into questions:

| Life path | The step | The question it asks you | The flip side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Initiator, the rough diamond | What are you starting, and are you ready to learn? | Forgetting we’re all students our whole life |
| 2 | Cooperation, the contract | What are you bound to, and have you read it? | Stubbornness, solitude, ego |
| 3 | Cadence, the machine running | Can you finish this before starting the next? | Too many projects at once |
| 4 | Stability, the fruit of work | What have you built, and can you stay flexible? | Carrying on when you should stop |
| 5 | Trial | Can you try, fail, and be disliked? | Denial |
| 6 | Expansion, success | Can you stay calm and humble when it flows? | Losing your footing when it works |
| 7 | Analysis, patience | What needs thinking through before you act? | Too many choices, no decision |
| 8 | Empire, control | What do you hold, and what will you pass on? | Holding on too tight |
| 9 | Home stretch | What are you finishing? | Stopping one effort too early |

There’s no life path 10: it becomes 1 + 0 = 1, the cycle starting again.

**A life path number is a question you carry, not a sentence you serve.** The example above, the 7, is the moment in the Instagram story when the model changes and advertising becomes the core: patience, negotiation, harvest. Its advice is the one I like best in the table — everything is temporary, good or bad. [Number 7](/library/numerology/number-7/) has its own page.

## Your life path in the tarot {#tarot}

Every number also has its cards. A life path 7 points to [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/), major arcanum 7 — a warrior who receives orders and moves when action is called for — and to the Sevens of the minor arcana.

The Seven of Cups is indecision, too many choices. The Seven of Swords leaves things behind and still harvests a lot. The Seven of Pentacles leans on his hoe and looks at what’s growing. Three ways of pausing before the next move.

If you’d rather land on a major than on a digit, [the tarot birth card](/blog/tarot-birth-card/) uses the same addition and stops at 21.

## What a life path number can’t tell you {#limits}

Everyone born on the same day shares it, and with only nine results you share yours with a large slice of the people you know. It points at a theme to look at; it doesn’t describe you.

It isn’t an excuse either. “I’m a 1, I can’t work in a team” is the numerology version of [“because I’m a Scorpio”](/blog/astrology-is-not-an-excuse/).

And I hold it lightly. I counted my tarot readings and tested my birth chart box by box; I haven’t run the same test on life path numbers. Don’t take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself.

## Try it on three people {#try-it}

Calculate your life path, then the life paths of two people you know well. Read the row of the table for each, and tick or cross: does the question fit what you see of their life? Three ticks or three crosses will tell you more than any book. And if you’d like to work through the whole cycle with other people, numbers have their own [workshop](/workshops/).
