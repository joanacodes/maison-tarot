When you pay for a spell online, you’re buying a service with no result you can check: a ritual performed for you somewhere you can’t see, for an outcome nobody can measure. The people who buy them aren’t foolish. They’re usually worried, heartbroken or tired of bad luck, and they want something to change.

## What a paid spell is {#what-it-is}

A paid spell is a ritual, a set of words, objects and gestures, that a seller performs on your behalf for an aim you choose from a list: love, money, protection, luck, “bring back your ex”, “remove blockages”. You pay, give a name and sometimes a photo, and wait.

Say someone buys a love spell for thirty euros. Two days later a message arrives: the spell has been cast, here’s a photo of the altar, results can take a few weeks. That’s the whole product. The photo shows that something was done. It can’t show what that something did.

## What you’re actually buying {#what-youre-buying}

A service whose result you can’t verify. You can’t watch it being performed, you can’t check its effect, and you can’t tell it apart from nothing having been done at all. Then the outcome gets read in a way that always favours the seller. If things improve, the spell worked. If they don’t, it needs more time, or a stronger casting, or a second one to clear whatever is blocking the first.

**With a paid spell, every outcome leads back to the shop: either it worked, or you need another one.**

The listings tend to use the same handful of mechanisms:

- The outcome in the title: a result, sometimes a person, as if it could be ordered.
- Tiers: a basic casting, a powerful one, an extra-powerful one. The price becomes the proof of strength.
- Speed: cast within 24 hours, as though haste were one of the ingredients.
- “Proof”: a photo or a video of the ritual, never of its effect.
- The follow-up: a reason, found later, to buy again.
- The small print: “for entertainment purposes only” under a title that promises a result. [What that phrase really means](/blog/entertainment-purposes-only/) is worth two minutes.

None of this needs anyone to be cynical. A seller can believe sincerely in what they do and still be selling something nobody can check.

## What Etsy’s own rules say {#etsy-rules}

At the time of writing (autumn 2026), [Etsy’s policy on services](https://www.etsy.com/legal/policy/services/242665313101) says metaphysical services can’t be sold on the site. Its examples include spellcasting, rituals, prayers, reiki and distance healing, and it also bars listings that advertise a metaphysical outcome such as love or wealth. Tarot readings are allowed only when they come with something tangible: a photo of the spread, a recording, or the text of the reading.

Searches on the site still turn up listings that call themselves spells. A policy says what a platform allows, not what slips past it. If a listing offers to change your love life for a fee, the platform’s own rules are on your side, and you can report it to them.

## Why people buy them {#why-people-buy}

Because a worry wants a shape. A breakup that won’t settle, money that keeps running out, the sense of being stuck: a spell turns that into something you can do tonight, with a price and a delivery date. It feels like taking back a little control.

That wish deserves to be heard. If someone asks me for a spell, I hear “I’d like this to change, and I don’t know where to start”. I don’t cast spells; I teach tarot. But the question underneath is a good one, and I’ve written about what people are really asking for in [protection spells](/blog/protection-spells/). As for magic, I’d call it [the things we know exist but can’t explain](/blog/what-is-magic/), which is a long way from a checkout page.

## What helps more {#what-helps}

1. Name what you want in one sentence. “I want to stop waiting for his message.” A named wish has edges.
2. Find the action that’s yours: a message to send or not send, a conversation, a budget, a door to close.
3. If a ritual helps you, make it your own. Writing an intention on a piece of paper costs nothing, and nobody needs to sell it to you.
4. If a seller says you need more, stop paying and keep the messages. “It needs another casting” is the same script as [the curse scam](/blog/lift-the-curse-scam/), told more softly.
5. If the worry won’t switch off whatever you do, that’s a door for someone else. Not everything is about spirits; sometimes it’s [a therapist’s door](/disclaimers/#therapy), and that’s a good door.

## A question the cards can hold {#next-step}

A reading won’t change anyone’s heart for you. What it can do is look at where you stand. Instead of “Will the spell work?”, try “What would help me feel steadier this month?” That’s the kind of question we work with at the [free events](/events/), where nothing is sold at the end.
