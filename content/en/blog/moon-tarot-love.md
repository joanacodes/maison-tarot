The Moon in love is uncertainty: you can’t see clearly, and your imagination fills the gaps — with hopes as much as with fears. It’s a card of Water and of Pisces, a calm surface with a great deal moving underneath. Its question is less “what is he hiding?” than “what am I not looking at yet?”

## What the Moon shows, through its lenses {#lenses}

A large moon with a face hangs between two grey towers, drops falling from it. Below, a dog and a wolf howl up at it. In the foreground a crayfish crawls out of a pool, and a path winds from the water, between the towers, towards distant mountains. Nobody walks the path. The light is real, but it’s borrowed light — enough to see shapes, not enough to see faces.

The element is [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/): emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. In my notes on the High Priestess, the Moon is what moves water, and it has a hidden face. Still water shows you a surface and keeps the rest. The crayfish is exactly that: something from the bottom, coming up into the light.

The number: 18 becomes 1 + 8 = [9](/library/numerology/number-9/), the finish line of a stage — introspection, patience, last efforts, but keep moving. It’s the number of the Nine of Swords too, coming out of a long nightmare with protection above you. The Moon is a 9: the fog of the last stretch, not the start of a lost road.

The colours: the blue of emotions, the yellow of the moon, the grey of the towers. The energy is feminine — the intangible, imagination, what stays behind the scenes. And the astrology, if it speaks to you, is Pisces: from the outside the surface is still, and underneath the world teems with life. **The Moon doesn’t say someone is hiding something; it says not everything is visible yet.** Every lens is on [the card’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/).

Put the lenses together and you have a sentence before any book. Say someone draws this card and tells you nothing. Emotion, a last stretch, something rising from the bottom, imagination at work. What you might say out loud: “You’re nearly through something, and the hardest part is that you can’t see the end of it — your mind is filling the dark with things that may not be there.” Then you check it, and listen.

## What it means single, together, after a break-up {#relationship}

When you’re single, the Moon often shows projection: someone you barely know, turned into a whole story by your imagination. It’s a lovely feeling and a fragile one. The card asks you to meet the real person before the moonlit one.

When you’re together, it can be a vague unease you can’t name, things left unsaid on both sides, or a period you can’t read yet — a new job, a move, one of you going quiet for reasons that have nothing to do with the other. Say your partner has been distant for three weeks. The Moon doesn’t tell you why. It tells you that you don’t know yet, and that the story you’ve built in the meantime is yours, not theirs.

After a break-up, it’s the not-understanding. Why it ended, what was true. The path between the towers is there; it’s only dark.

My very first reading for a stranger, on a Discord group I’d found through Tumblr, was about a relationship. She said it didn’t resonate; she wasn’t looking for anyone. Three or four days later she texted me, shocked: everything had happened. And the reading had also shown that she hadn’t been kind to the other person. What’s under the surface is very often our own part. I tell the whole story in [my first reading, and the text three days later](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/).

## Reversed, and next to other cards {#reversed-combinations}

Reversed, the Moon is often the fog lifting: a conversation that clears things, a truth that turns out smaller than the fear. It can also be the confusion in excess — checking phones, rereading messages for hidden meanings, every shadow turned into a wolf.

Next to the High Priestess, it’s knowing more than you say, and needing silence before you speak. Next to the Seven of Cups, it’s too many possible stories at once, each one shining in its cup. Next to the Sun, the card that follows it in the deck, what was unclear comes into full daylight.

## “Is he lying to me?” — and a kinder question to ask {#lying}

The Moon is the card people read as deception, so it often arrives with this question. Usually the person asking has been lying awake with it, and that deserves kindness, not a lecture.

I don’t read on someone who isn’t in the room, and here’s why. They haven’t agreed to be read. And the Moon is the card of projection: ask it to confirm a suspicion and it will look like confirmation, because you’ll see your fear in it. I’ve written more on [reading tarot about someone else](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/).

The gentler question: “What do I actually know, what am I imagining, and what would I need to ask him directly?” Three columns on a sheet of paper. The first is usually shorter than you expect, and the third is where the answer lives.

There’s also the dog and the wolf on the card, howling at the same light. Fear at night is old and very human. Being afraid of the dark can be being afraid of your own shadow — the place where we put what we never wanted to look at. I’ve written about [being afraid of the dark](/blog/afraid-of-the-dark/), and what changed for me when I stopped waiting for a light before walking into a room.

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Draw one card for the crayfish: “What is rising in me right now?” Then sit with it for a day before you act. If the doubt keeps you up at night, [the Nine of Swords in love](/blog/nine-of-swords-tarot-love/) talks about exactly those hours.
