I don’t read on health, death, pregnancy, court cases, money decisions, or what someone who isn’t in the room thinks. People bring these questions because they’re scared, or because they love someone, and that deserves care rather than a closed door. So for each one, here is why the cards are the wrong tool, and the question I’d ask instead.

## Why some questions don’t belong to the cards {#why}

A card reads a moment, in pictures. It doesn’t diagnose, plead a case, read a contract or see into someone else’s head. Those subjects sit on [the list of what I don’t read](/disclaimers/#refusals), and the reason isn’t prudishness.

The risk isn’t that no card falls. One always does. The risk is what a worried person does with the sentence that comes with it. By my own count, about one reading in ten misses. When the question is “what’s on my mind this week?”, a miss is a shrug. When the question is “is the lump serious?”, a miss can keep someone awake for a month, or make them put off an appointment.

**The heavier the question, the more a miss costs — and the cards don’t know which questions are heavy.**

## Health, death and pregnancy {#health-death-pregnancy}

“Am I ill?” “Will the operation go well?” “How long does my grandmother have?” “Will I get pregnant this year?”

Behind every one of these there is fear, love or longing, sometimes all three. I understand why someone would want a picture to hold while they wait. But a card can’t see inside a body. A Tower drawn the night before a scan can frighten someone for nothing; a Sun can reassure them into delaying a test. These are questions for a doctor, a midwife, a fertility specialist — people who can examine, measure and follow up. I go into each in more detail in [why careful readers step back from health, pregnancy and death](/blog/health-pregnancy-death-tarot/).

What the cards can hold meanwhile is the waiting. Kinder questions:

- “What can help me get through the wait for the results?”
- “How can I be present with my grandmother this month?”
- “What do I need for myself while we keep trying?”

Say someone waiting for results asks the first one and draws the Four of Swords.

A knight lies on a tomb in a quiet church, hands joined, three swords on the wall above him and one beneath. The card says nothing about the results, and it shouldn’t. It says: rest. Lie down. Stop fighting a battle that hasn’t been scheduled yet. That’s something a person can do tonight.

## Court cases and money decisions {#court-and-money}

“Will I win custody?” “Should I sign?” “Should I put my savings into this?”

These come with high stakes and, often, a deadline. A reading that says “you’ll win” can make someone prepare less; one that says “you’ll lose” can make them give up before the hearing. And no card has read the contract. A lawyer reads the file; a financial adviser or an accountant reads the numbers. That’s their job, and they can be held to it.

Kinder questions:

- “How can I stay steady through the hearing?”
- “What am I afraid of losing if I say no?”
- “What do I still need to understand before I sign?”

That last one tends to send you back to the paperwork, which is where the answer lives. For reading the Pentacles around money without letting them make the call, see [tarot and money, with your feet on the ground](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/).

## Questions about someone who isn’t in the room {#third-parties}

“Is he cheating?” “What does she think of me?” “Will he come back?”

These usually come from love, hurt, or the particular pain of not knowing. Two reasons I’d rather not read them. You wouldn’t want someone looking through your bedroom while you were out, and reading a person without their consent is a little like that. And someone who doesn’t know they’re being read isn’t open, so you catch half a sentence and build a whole statement on it.

Kinder questions turn back towards you:

- “What do I need in this relationship?”
- “What would I do if I already knew the answer?”
- “How do I want to be treated?”

The question about an ex has [a whole article of its own](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/), because I hear it often, and because it hurts more each time it’s asked again. And if your question only works as a yes or a no, [here’s what I offer instead of a verdict](/blog/yes-or-no-tarot/).

## The kindest question is sometimes none {#no-question}

When I read for someone, I ask them for one thing: tell me nothing. Not the subject, not the question. Whatever is weighing on a person tends to come up on its own, and when it doesn’t, that’s information too.

You can try it yourself. Next time something heavy is pressing on you, shuffle without a question, draw one card, and see what it talks about before you decide what it “should” be about. If you want to know why this works better than it sounds, I explain it in [the silence rule](/blog/the-silence-rule/).
