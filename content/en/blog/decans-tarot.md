A decan is a ten-degree slice of the zodiac. Each sign has three, so there are 36 — and there are exactly 36 minor cards numbered Two to Ten. In the late nineteenth century, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn paired them up, giving each card a sign and a planet: the Five of Cups, for instance, is Mars in Scorpio. Here's how the pairing works, the full table, and how to use it without letting it take over.

## What is a decan? {#what-is-a-decan}

Each zodiac sign covers 30 degrees of the Sun's yearly path. A decan is a third of a sign: 10 degrees, which the Sun crosses in about ten days. Someone born on 5 November, for example, has the Sun in the second decan of Scorpio.

The idea is very old. Egyptian astronomers used 36 groups of stars, rising one after another roughly every ten days, to tell the hours of the night; Greek astrologers later laid them over the zodiac and gave each one a ruling planet ([Britannica's entry on the decans](https://www.britannica.com/topic/decan)).

The planets take turns in what's called the Chaldean order, from the slowest to the fastest as seen from Earth: Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury, the Moon. The sequence starts with Mars on the first decan of Aries and goes round. Seven planets don't divide into 36, so Mars gets one extra: it rules both the first decan of the zodiac and the last.

## How the Golden Dawn mapped them onto the minor arcana {#golden-dawn}

Three rules, and you have the whole system.

1. The suit follows the element of the sign: Fire signs give Wands, Water signs Cups, Air signs Swords, Earth signs Pentacles.
2. The number follows the mode of the sign: cardinal signs take the 2, 3 and 4; fixed signs the 5, 6 and 7; mutable signs the 8, 9 and 10.
3. The planet is the ruler of the decan, in order.

**Once you know those three rules, you can rebuild the table from memory.** The second one is my favourite, because the astrological modes are one of the five lenses I teach: cardinal signs start things, and the 2 to 4 are the start of a cycle; mutable signs adapt, and the 8 to 10 are its end.

Aces and court cards are handled differently in the Golden Dawn system and aren't part of the decans. The Rider-Waite-Smith doesn't print any of this on its cards; the Thoth deck does, which is one of the things that change [when you switch from one to the other](/blog/thoth-vs-rider-waite/). Where the Golden Dawn's correspondences come from is told in [the Golden Dawn and its tarot](/library/tarot-history/golden-dawn-tarot/).

## The table: 36 decans, 36 cards {#table}

Read each row left to right: the first card goes with the first decan and the first planet, and so on. The dates are approximate and shift by a day from year to year.

| Sign | Approximate dates | Cards | Planets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aries | 21 March – 19 April | Two, Three, Four of Wands | Mars, Sun, Venus |
| Taurus | 20 April – 20 May | Five, Six, Seven of Pentacles | Mercury, Moon, Saturn |
| Gemini | 21 May – 20 June | Eight, Nine, Ten of Swords | Jupiter, Mars, Sun |
| Cancer | 21 June – 22 July | Two, Three, Four of Cups | Venus, Mercury, Moon |
| Leo | 23 July – 22 August | Five, Six, Seven of Wands | Saturn, Jupiter, Mars |
| Virgo | 23 August – 22 September | Eight, Nine, Ten of Pentacles | Sun, Venus, Mercury |
| Libra | 23 September – 22 October | Two, Three, Four of Swords | Moon, Saturn, Jupiter |
| Scorpio | 23 October – 21 November | Five, Six, Seven of Cups | Mars, Sun, Venus |
| Sagittarius | 22 November – 21 December | Eight, Nine, Ten of Wands | Mercury, Moon, Saturn |
| Capricorn | 22 December – 19 January | Two, Three, Four of Pentacles | Jupiter, Mars, Sun |
| Aquarius | 20 January – 18 February | Five, Six, Seven of Swords | Venus, Mercury, Moon |
| Pisces | 19 February – 20 March | Eight, Nine, Ten of Cups | Saturn, Jupiter, Mars |

So the Ten of Swords is the Sun in Gemini, roughly 11 to 20 June, and the Four of Pentacles is the Sun in Capricorn, around New Year. These match the Golden Dawn attributions as decan specialists such as [T. Susan Chang](https://www.tsusanchang.com/blog/2019/6/11/reading-the-decans-famous-last-words-gemini-iii) reproduce them.

## Reading a card through its decan {#reading}

Here's what the decan adds to three cards I have my own notes on.

The [Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/) is Mars in Scorpio. My notes put it at the lowest point of the whole deck: something lost, a black mood, and the weight carried alone. Mars adds heat, the anger that sits inside grief; Scorpio adds attachment. It hurts this much because it mattered this much. As I write, on 24 October, the Sun has just entered Scorpio, which by this table puts us in the Five of Cups decan until about 1 November: ten days to look at what spilled, and at the two cups still standing. What else this season is said to bring is in [Scorpio season tarot](/blog/scorpio-season-tarot/).

The Eight of Swords is Jupiter in Gemini. My note: a cell of your own making while every tool to open it is already yours, and nothing but you keeps the blindfold on. Jupiter enlarges; Gemini's verb is “I think”. Together: a mind that has grown too many thoughts to see the way out.

The Six of Cups is the Sun in Scorpio. My note keeps it close to childhood: time kept for yourself, and a second bloom. The Sun lighting up Scorpio's depths reads as warmth found in old, deep places — a memory, a person from long ago.

## How to use the decans lightly {#use-lightly}

1. Read the card first with its number, element and colours. The decan comes last, as a nuance, never as the meaning.
2. If you're curious, look up the card of your birthday decan. It's a curiosity, not an identity, and it isn't the same thing as [the tarot birth card](/blog/tarot-birth-card/) you calculate from numerology.
3. For a ten-day theme, put the current decan's card on your table and note what you notice. Nothing more.
4. If astrology isn't your lens, skip all of this. The cards read perfectly well without it.

The honest limit: this is an elegant system, but it's a nineteenth-century layer on a fifteenth-century card game, not something tarot always had. When I tested my own chart, placements held about 30–40% of the time overall, and the decans were never part of that test, so I can't put a figure on them. Treat them as one more way of looking, and remember that [astrology is not an excuse](/disclaimers/#astrology).
