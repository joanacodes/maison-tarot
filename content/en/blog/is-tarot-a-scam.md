Tarot isn’t a scam. A deck is 78 pictures, and reading them is a skill anyone can learn. What can be a scam is a business built around readings: one where the reading is the advertisement and the real product comes afterwards. The two look different, especially at the end, and that’s what this article is about.

## The cards aren’t the problem {#the-cards}

A tarot deck is a set of printed images; the one I use was drawn in 1909 by Pamela Colman Smith. The cards don’t promise anything. Reading them means looking at a picture and saying what it shows about the person in front of you, and anyone can learn to do it.

What honest readers can’t tell you is how it works. Nobody knows. I count my readings: about nine in ten hold, about one in ten misses, and I say which. That isn’t a guarantee; it’s the opposite of one. It’s also why I’m comfortable with the label “for entertainment”, and I explain [why I like that phrase](/blog/entertainment-purposes-only/).

So asking whether tarot is a scam is a bit like asking whether painting is one because forgeries exist.

**A scam isn’t a belief; it’s a structure, and you can see it from the chair.**

## What a reading looks like {#what-a-reading-is}

A reading is complete in itself. It starts, it says what it sees, and it ends.

The reader can work without your story; I ask people to tell me nothing. They describe what’s on the cards and what it suggests. When something doesn’t land, they say so. A miss is a miss: no “it’ll make sense later”, no extra card to rescue it. At the end, the decision goes back to you, and nothing is left hanging.

Even a heavy card leaves you free. Say someone draws the Tower.

A tower struck by lightning, its crown blown off, two figures falling. An honest reader talks about sudden change and what the person can do with it. They don’t add “and only I can stop it”.

## What a sales hook looks like {#what-a-hook-is}

A sales hook is built backwards from a payment. The reading exists to open a gap, and the gap has a price.

It usually has three parts. An open loop: “there’s something else I’m seeing, but I can’t go into it here.” A fear: a block, a danger, a rival, a curse. And a next step: a longer session, a ritual, a subscription, a number that charges by the minute. Each part looks harmless on its own. Together they turn a reading into a funnel.

Say you comment under a video and receive a free three-card reading by message. It’s warm, and some of it fits. It ends: “There’s a shadow around your relationship that I’d need a full session to look at. I have one slot left tomorrow.” All three parts of the hook are in those two sentences.

Notice that the hook doesn’t need the cards at all. Swap the deck for a pendulum or a horoscope and the script runs the same way. The best-known version, act by act, is [the “lift the curse” scam](/blog/lift-the-curse-scam/), and I’ve listed the warning signs one by one in [how to spot a fake tarot reader](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/). I won’t repeat them here. I’d rather give you one test.

## The test: what happens at the end {#the-test}

When a reading finishes, ask yourself four questions:

1. Is it over, or is something still open that I’m being invited to pay to close?
2. Did the reader say what didn’t land, or did every miss become “you’ll see”?
3. Do I feel freer to decide, or more afraid not to come back?
4. Is the next step something I chose, or something I was told I need?

A reading can be good or poor, accurate or off. But if it ends with a reason to pay again, you’ve met a hook, whatever deck is on the table.

## Charging isn’t the scam {#charging}

To be fair to readers who earn their living this way: being paid isn’t a con. A reader who states a price for a set length of time, with nothing added at the end, is selling time and attention like any other professional. Many do exactly that, and do it well.

The grey zone is pricing that rewards uncertainty: per question, per minute, subscriptions that renew while you’re still waiting for an answer. I explain how those models work in [tarot subscriptions and pay-per-question](/blog/tarot-subscriptions/).

I’ll include myself here. In 2021 I sold sage, candles and “channelled” cards. Nothing in them improved a reading, so I stopped. Today I sell lessons and give readings away, because that’s the arrangement where I gain nothing from you needing me again: [what I charge for, and what I give away](/blog/what-i-charge-for/).

## If you think you’ve been hooked {#if-hooked}

Stop paying, and don’t feel foolish. These scripts work on clever people, because they’re built for the moments when someone is frightened or grieving. Keep the messages, and tell one person you trust.

Then, if you’re still curious about tarot itself, the surest protection is learning to read it yourself: you’ll recognise a hook the moment you see one. The [learn-tarot path](/learn-tarot/) starts with one card and one lens.
