There are five eclipses in 2027: two solar, three lunar. The one people will remember is the total eclipse of 2 August, which crosses southern Spain and North Africa with one of the longest totalities of our lifetimes; from the UK you'll see a partial eclipse that morning. Below are the dates, the signs, where to look, and a small spread to go with them.

## The five eclipses of 2027 {#dates}

A solar eclipse is when the Moon passes between the Earth and the Sun and hides part or all of it; it can only happen at a new moon. A lunar eclipse is when the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon; it can only happen at a full moon. A penumbral lunar eclipse is the faintest kind: the Moon only crosses the pale outer edge of the shadow, and most people notice nothing.

“Greatest eclipse” is the moment the eclipse is deepest somewhere on Earth, not the time you'll see it where you live. On 20 February the Moon only grazes the shadow: from the UK you might catch a faint grey shading on one edge around 23:13. The July one is so shallow that some astrologers call it an almost-eclipse.

The times come from [NASA's eclipse tables for 2027](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/OH/OH2027.html); the signs are the usual astrological positions, which agree across the ephemerides I checked.

## 2 August 2027: the long total eclipse over Spain and North Africa {#august-2}

The Moon's shadow, about 250 km wide, comes in from the Atlantic, crosses the south of Spain (parts of the provinces of Cádiz and Málaga), Gibraltar, then Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Yemen and Somalia. Gibraltar gets a little over four minutes of totality. Near Luxor, in Egypt, it reaches about six minutes twenty: according to [Sky & Telescope](https://skyandtelescope.org/astronomy-news/plan-for-the-2027-eclipse-of-the-century-one-year-from-now/), no total eclipse will last longer until 2114.

From the UK it's a partial eclipse, in the morning. In London it starts at about 09:03 BST, peaks at 10:00 with roughly 42% of the Sun covered, and ends at 10:59 ([times from UK Eclipse](https://ukeclipse.com/how-to-see-the-partial-eclipse-on-2nd-august-2027/)). The further south-west you are, the more you'll see; the north of Scotland sees much less.

**Never look at the Sun without certified eclipse glasses (ISO 12312-2), not even at 42%.** Sunglasses don't protect your eyes. A colander held over a sheet of paper projects dozens of little crescents, and that's a lovely way to watch with children.

## What astrologers read in the 2027 eclipses {#signs}

In astrology, a solar eclipse is read as a new moon with the volume turned up: a beginning, often one you didn't plan. A lunar eclipse is read as a full moon turned up: something comes to a head or closes. Eclipses come in seasons, a few weeks apart, and in 2027 the two seasons fall in February and in late July–August.

The axis astrologers will talk about is [Leo](/library/astrology/leo/) and [Aquarius](/library/astrology/aquarius/): both solar eclipses and the August lunar one sit on it. Leo's verb is “I will”: self-expression, pride, the heart that wants to be seen. Aquarius's is “I know”: the group, ideas, what's coming next. Read together, the theme is the pull between me and us. If you like to give each sign a card, the Golden Dawn tradition puts Strength on Leo and the Star on Aquarius.

## Why I read less in eclipse season, not more {#read-less}

Eclipse weeks are when the questions multiply. The videos say something big is coming, so people pull a card in the morning, three at lunch, a full spread at night — and every new card is another chance to worry.

I'd rather do the opposite: one reading per season, written down, then the cards go back in the drawer until the next one. Take a break from your cards now and then. Whether it's fine to read during an eclipse at all is its own question, and I answer it in [eclipse season tarot](/blog/eclipse-season-tarot/).

## A gentle tarot spread for the 2027 eclipses {#spread}

Three cards, once in February and once in August. Not on every eclipse, and not every day of the season.

1. *What is ready to end on its own?* The lunar card: something that has already done its time.
2. *What is starting quietly?* The solar card: a beginning you might not have noticed yet.
3. *What can I leave alone until the next eclipse?* The kindest card in the spread: permission to not fix everything now.

Read each card through its colours, its number and its element before you look anything up. Write the three cards and one sentence for each in [your reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/), with the date. In August, before you draw again, reread February's page and mark what held, what missed, and what's too early to say.
