A protection spell is a ritual meant to keep harm away from a person, a home or someone they love. When people ask me for one, what they’re usually asking for is to feel safe again. That’s a real need, and the most useful answer starts with the fear rather than the spell.

## What a protection spell is {#what-it-is}

A protection spell is a set of words, gestures or objects used with the intention of keeping something harmful away. You find versions of it in many folk traditions and in modern witchcraft.

A typical example: someone draws a line of salt across the doorstep and says a sentence about what may not cross it. Others carry a small charm, hang something by the front door, write an intention on paper and keep it folded in a drawer, or light a candle before sleep.

People who practise this way often describe it as setting a boundary, a gesture that says *this house is mine*. Whatever you think crosses the line or doesn’t, the gesture itself is real, and it matters to the person making it. If you want the vocabulary around it, I keep a [glossary of the different kinds of witches](/blog/types-of-witches/).

I don’t cast spells. I teach tarot, so when someone asks me for protection, I listen for the question underneath.

## What people are really asking for {#really-asking}

The requests sound different, but they come from the same few places. A run of bad luck that has started to feel personal. A sense of being envied or watched. An ex who won’t let go. A new flat that doesn’t feel like home yet. Nights that have become hard. A worry for a child that won’t switch off.

Underneath, almost every time, there are two wishes: to feel safe, and to get a little control back when things feel out of hand. **When someone asks for protection, I hear: I’m scared, and I’d like this fear to have a shape I can deal with.**

That’s worth taking seriously. It’s also why a spell bought from a stranger often doesn’t settle it: the fear stays vague, so it comes back.

## When the danger and the cure are sold together {#sold-together}

This is the gentle warning in this article. Some readers first tell you that you’re in danger — a dark energy, someone’s jealousy, a curse — and then offer the protection, for a fee. Later it needs renewing. The fear was handed to you along with the price.

Red flags worth knowing:

1. The danger appears near the end of a cheap or free reading.
2. The protection costs more than the reading did.
3. There’s urgency: tonight, before the full moon, before it gets worse.
4. It has to be repeated, and nothing ever counts as finished.

If you recognise this and you’ve already paid, there’s no shame in it. The script is built to work on frightened people. I’ve described [how the curse script works step by step](/blog/lift-the-curse-scam/), including what to do to get out of it.

## What helps you feel safe {#what-helps}

Here is what I’d suggest instead, in this order.

1. Name the fear in one sentence. “I’m afraid my ex will turn up at my door.” “I’m afraid something will happen to my son on his trip.” A vague fear grows in the dark; a named one has edges.
2. Match it with a real action. A new lock, a blocked number, a changed password, a neighbour who knows, a conversation you’ve been putting off. If someone is actually threatening you, the police or a lawyer, not a ritual.
3. Keep the calming habits that cost nothing. If lighting a candle by the door at night helps you sleep, that habit is yours. Nobody needs to sell it to you.
4. Tell one person. Fear kept secret gets louder.
5. Notice if the fear has no object. If it’s there every night, in every room, whatever you do, that’s a door for a therapist or a doctor. Not everything is about spirits, and [the therapist’s door](/disclaimers/#therapy) is a good one.

## What a reading can hold instead {#what-a-reading-can-hold}

A reading won’t put a shield around you. What it can do is look at where you stand, and where your strength already is. Instead of “Am I being attacked?”, try “What would help me feel steady this month?” or “Where am I stronger than I think?” I keep a list of [kinder questions to ask the cards](/blog/questions-to-avoid-asking-tarot/).
