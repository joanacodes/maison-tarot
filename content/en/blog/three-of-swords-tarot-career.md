The Three of Swords in a career reading is the hurt that comes through work: a sentence that cut, a betrayal, a decision taken over your head. It doesn’t say who meant what. It says something hurt, and that the hurt deserves a look before you act on it.

## The Three of Swords at work, through its lenses {#lenses}

A red heart floats in a grey sky, pierced by three swords. Clouds, slanting rain, no person, no ground. Pamela Colman Smith drew the wound and the weather, and nothing else.

The number is [3](/library/numerology/number-3/): the good rhythm, the machine running, the contract that works. At work, that’s the team that clicks. Its minus, in my notes, is betrayal and solitude — and starting new projects before the others are finished, which is how a lot of teams come apart. Next to this card I wrote something else: the Three of Swords is also a blood pact. A bond sealed through pain — colleagues who went through a crisis together and came out tighter.

The element is Air: the mind, decisions, words, including the “viper’s tongue”. At work, the hurt of this card usually arrives in words: an email, an appraisal, a remark in a meeting, gossip by the coffee machine. And Air replays: you hear the sentence again at night.

The colours: the red of a heart still alive, the grey of weather that doesn’t care — and that will change, because weather does. The astrology, if it speaks to you, is Saturn in Libra: a hard lesson about fairness, in the sign of relationships. Optional. Every lens is on [the card’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-swords/).

## What the Three of Swords means at work {#work}

At work, the card often points to a precise moment. Feedback harsher than it needed to be. Being left out of a meeting you should have been in. A colleague repeating something you said in confidence. A manager you liked leaving, and the team falling apart after. Sometimes it’s your own decision: leaving a job you loved because it had stopped loving you back.

The triangle is the classic reading: two people and a third. A promotion between two colleagues, a client taken by someone else, a partnership where one person is quietly pushed out.

And sometimes the blood pact fits better. Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Three of Swords next to the Three of Pentacles — a stonemason at work in a church, two people consulting him with a plan. Lenses first: two threes, a bond twice over; Air then Earth, a hurt and then the work. You might say: “Something hard happened in your team, and you’re still working together — maybe more closely because of it.” Then ask whether it lands, and if it doesn’t, call it a miss.

**The Three of Swords shows a wound at work, not the end of your working life.**

## Reversed, and next to other cards {#reversed-combinations}

Reversed, the hurt is held in: “I’m fine, it’s only work”, said through a tight jaw for months. Or the opposite, the scene replayed again and again, the email reread until you know it by heart. Sometimes it’s the rain stopping, quietly — one morning the sentence doesn’t sting any more.

Next to the Three of Pentacles, it’s the blood pact at its kindest, as above. Next to the Five of Swords — in my notes, an Air conflict, people who don’t share the same point of view — the hurt comes from a disagreement about decisions, and it isn’t always as personal as it feels. Next to the Four of Swords, a knight lying still on a tomb, it’s rest: hang the blades on the wall for a while before you answer anyone.

## “Is my colleague against me?” — and a kinder question {#question}

That’s the question people bring: is my boss against me, did they do it on purpose, are they talking about me? It comes from feeling unsafe at work, which is exhausting. I understand wanting to know.

I don’t read on that, and here’s why. The colleague isn’t in the room and hasn’t agreed to be read; what they feel belongs to them. A card asked “are they against me?” answers with your fear, and you go back on Monday more on guard than before. I’ve written more on [reading tarot about someone else](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/).

A gentler question: “What did that sentence touch in me, and what do I need now — to say it, to rest, or to get help?” Whether you speak up, stay or leave is yours to weigh. I read; you decide.

If the hurt keeps repeating — humiliation, pressure, being singled out — it’s no longer a card’s business. Talk to HR, a staff representative or your occupational health service, and to a lawyer for the legal side; an ongoing case is something I don’t read on at all. If it follows you home every night, a doctor or a therapist is the right door ([more on that here](/disclaimers/#therapy)).

## What helps you recover {#recover}

1. Say it out loud to someone outside work. It’s an Air card: words cut, and words start to close the cut.
2. Don’t decide in the storm. The resignation letter, the reply-all, the angry message can wait a week.
3. Write down exactly what was said, then what you heard. They’re often different.
4. Rest, the way the Four of Swords does.

If the hurt turns out to be rock bottom rather than a cut, [the Ten of Swords at work](/blog/ten-of-swords-tarot-career/) is the next part of the story. And if the same rain is falling on your private life, [the Three of Swords in love](/blog/three-of-swords-tarot-love/) has its own list of what helps.
