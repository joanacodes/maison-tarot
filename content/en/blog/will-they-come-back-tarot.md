If you’re asking “will they come back?”, you’re probably hurting, and I’d rather start there than with a card. I don’t read on that question, and it isn’t because it’s a silly one. It’s because it puts your whole life in the hands of someone who isn’t in the room — and there’s a gentler question that gives it back to you.

## Why ‘will they come back?’ is the question everyone asks {#why-we-ask}

Because a break-up leaves a silence, and silence is hard to sit in. You replay the last conversation. You check your phone. You want someone, anyone, to tell you how it ends.

The question comes in many versions: will he come back, will she text, will they love me again. That last one is in my own notes, on a list of ways people end up asking a reading for something it can’t give. I don’t write that as a reproach. It may be the most human question there is. It comes from love, from worry, and often from the fear of being left alone with what happened.

So if you’ve asked it — to a deck, to a friend, to a video at two in the morning — there’s nothing to be ashamed of. You were looking for somewhere to put your feet.

## What the question does to you while you wait {#what-it-does}

It puts you on hold. Whatever the answer, the question keeps your attention fixed on another person’s next move, and your own life goes into the waiting room.

Think about the three answers a reading could give. A yes keeps you waiting, sometimes for months, sometimes for someone who has already moved on. A no hurts twice: once for the loss, once for the card. And a maybe — which is what most cards honestly say — brings you back to ask again next week.

Some people do come back with the same question, again and again, almost fidgety. I don’t see weakness in that. It’s a high dose of doubt, and what helps it is rarely another reading. It’s a breath, a decision, or going through the thing you’re afraid of. I wrote more about that loop in [why you might put the deck down for a while](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/).

**The question makes you wait; it never helps you live through the waiting.**

## Why I don’t read on someone who isn’t in the room {#not-in-the-room}

Two reasons, and neither is a rule for its own sake.

The first is consent. You wouldn’t want someone wandering into your bedroom to look through your things; reading a person who hasn’t agreed to it feels the same to me. Your ex hasn’t asked to be read, and their choices belong to them. That’s why people who aren’t in the room are on [the short list of things I don’t read on](/disclaimers/#refusals).

The second is honesty. Someone who doesn’t know they’re being read isn’t open to it, so at best you catch half a sentence and build a whole story on top of it. And that story is read through you — your hope, your grief, the ending you’d like. What comes out is mostly your own heart, dressed as theirs. I go into this in [reading tarot about someone else](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/).

Even in good conditions, readings miss. I’ve told the story of the time [information I received was wrong in the part that mattered](/blog/why-a-reading-can-be-wrong/). On a question like this one, a miss can cost you months.

## A kinder question to ask instead {#kinder-question}

Ask about the person who is in the room: you. A few questions that work well:

- What am I holding on to, and what is it giving me?
- What do I need this week, whatever they do?
- What would I do differently if I stopped waiting?
- What did this relationship show me about what I want?

Say someone asks the first one and draws the Five of Cups: a figure in a black cloak, head bowed over three spilled cups, with two cups still standing behind them. In my notes it’s the most negative card of the deck — losing, a dark emotion, being alone with your problems. That’s honest about how it feels. But [the Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/) also keeps two full cups behind the figure, and the figure hasn’t turned round yet.

Next to it, the Six of Cups: two children in a garden, one handing the other a cup full of flowers. Taking time for yourself, going back to what once made you happy, flowering again.

Neither card says whether anyone is coming back. Together they say something you can use tonight: there is grief here, there is still something standing, and there’s a softer place you can return to.

## If you’re in the middle of it right now {#right-now}

1. Put your phone face down for an evening. Not forever; for an evening.
2. If you want to use your cards, draw one, about you. Write down what you see before you decide what it means.
3. Tell one person you trust, out loud, how you feel.
4. If the pain stays heavy for weeks, or it’s reaching your sleep and your work, a therapist is a better door than a deck. That isn’t your cards failing; it’s the right tool for the job.

And whatever a reading says, yours or mine, [a card is not an authority](/blog/tarot-is-not-an-authority/). I read; you decide.
