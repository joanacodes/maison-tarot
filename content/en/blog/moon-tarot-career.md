The Moon in a career reading is uncertainty: you don’t have all the information, and your imagination is filling the gaps. A rumour of reorganisation, an interview with no answer yet, a role nobody has quite defined. The card doesn’t say something is wrong; it says you can’t see far yet, so don’t decide as if you could.

## The Moon through its lenses {#lenses}

A large moon with a face in profile fills the sky, drops falling from it. Below, a dog and a wolf howl at it from either side of a path, and a crayfish crawls out of a pool. The path runs between two grey towers and winds off towards distant hills. There’s no person on the card: only a path you can see, though not clearly.

The number: 18 becomes 1 + 8 = [9](/library/numerology/number-9/), the finish line in sight, the end of a stage, last efforts — and patience while you keep moving. In the Instagram story I use for the numbers, 9 is the redesign around Reels: not another app yet, but everything that defined the old one is ending. At work, the Moon often turns up at that stage. The old way is going; the new one has no name yet.

The element is [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/): emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. In my notes, water seeps into everything with time, like mould in a house; a worry you never look at slowly damages the foundations. The crayfish climbing out of the pool is that worry surfacing. The Moon doesn’t create it; it shows you one that was already in the water.

The astrology, if it helps, is Pisces, “I believe”: still on the surface, teeming underneath. The colours: blue for emotion, a yellow moon for imagination and a will that’s still there in the dark, grey towers that take no side. The energy is feminine — patience, what happens behind the scenes. Every lens is on [the Moon’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/).

## What the Moon says about work {#work}

Uncertainty at work has several faces. Rumours: a merger whispered at the coffee machine, a manager’s door closed more often than usual. Waiting: the interview went well, then two weeks of silence, and every hour you rewrite the story. A blurry role: a new job where nobody has told you what success looks like. Self-doubt: the feeling that everyone else knows what they’re doing.

The Moon’s plus is that it’s honest about the fog. It can also be intuition with no words yet: sometimes you sense a change before it’s announced. Its minus is deciding in the fog as if you could see — resigning over a rumour, or losing a weekend to an email that said nothing.

Say someone is three weeks into a new job, and their manager cancels their weekly meeting twice in a row. The Moon doesn’t say why. A tame animal and a wild one are howling at the same light: it could be a busy fortnight, it could be something else. **The useful sentence isn’t “something’s wrong”; it’s “I don’t know yet, and here’s what I can check.”**

## The Moon reversed at work {#reversed}

Reversed, the fog lifts slowly: the announcement finally comes, the role gets a brief, the answer arrives — not always the one you wanted, but a real one you can work with.

Or the fog thickens, when you stop checking your fears against anything real: the 3 a.m. scenario about your boss, rehearsed so often it starts to feel like a memory.

## The Moon next to other cards {#combinations}

{{< cards "cu07 cu08 ar19" >}}

Next to the Seven of Cups — in my notes, indecision and too many choices — it’s imagination running ahead of the facts: seven possible futures, none of them confirmed.

Next to the Eight of Cups, which has its own moon above a figure walking away towards the mountains, it’s leaving something behind without being able to say yet what you’re walking towards. My note for that card is moving on from what you’re already doing; the Moon adds that the path is there, even if you can’t see its end.

Next to the Sun, the card that follows it, the night is ending. I’ve written about [the Sun in a career reading](/blog/sun-tarot-career/) for the morning after.

## “Will I get the job?” — and a kinder question {#question}

The Moon attracts two questions: “Will I get the job?” and “Is something going on behind my back?” Both come from the same place — waiting without information, one of the hardest things to do well.

I don’t read on what a recruiter or a colleague thinks: they aren’t in the room, and I read the person in front of me ([more on reading about someone else](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/)). As for “when will I hear back?”, timing is where I miss most ([why tarot is weak on timing](/blog/tarot-timing/)). A miss is a miss, and a wrong “yes, next week” would cost you a week of hope.

A kinder question: “What do I actually know, and what am I imagining?” Then: “What’s one thing I can check this week?”

## How to move through it {#move-through}

1. Draw two columns: what I know, what I’m imagining. Most of the weight is usually in the second.
2. Ask one plain question of the person who can answer it: “Is my role changing?” or “When do you expect to decide?”
3. Keep moving. Nine is last efforts: do the work in front of you, update your CV, without deciding the ending in advance.
4. Leave the 3 a.m. thoughts until morning. Water rises at night; no emails, plans or resignations at that hour.
5. For one week, pull a single card each morning and ask only: what do I know today? If you’d like to practise reading in the open, with people who tell you nothing, come to a [free event](/events/).
