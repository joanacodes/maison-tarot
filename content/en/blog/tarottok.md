If you're over thirty-five and you read tarot, there's a good chance your view of TikTok tarot is "I don't watch it, but I've heard". Here is what it actually is, so you can dislike it for the right reasons.

## What a TarotTok video looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

Vertical, under a minute, often under thirty seconds. A reader, usually young, usually in a bedroom, holding a phone in one hand and a deck in the other. Formats:

- **"Pick a pile"** — three or four face-down stacks, choose one, get a short reading for that pile. [I've written about whether these work](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/); the answer is "like a song".
- **"This is for whoever needs to hear it"** — no piles, one message, aimed at the algorithm's guess about who's watching.
- **"Your person / your ex / your next lover"** — the most viewed and the most harmful; [the same reading I refuse one-to-one](/blog/no-contact-tarot-readings/), at scale.
- **Card-a-day** and **card meanings**, which are often decent and the least watched.
- **Live readings**, where viewers pay for each question they ask.

## Why it works {#why-it-works}

Because it's fast, warm, and arrives when you're alone. Tarot's traditional gatekeeping — the books, the vocabulary, the older woman in the shop who won't sell you a deck unless it's a gift — is gone. A sixteen-year-old with a phone can meet the Star card at midnight and feel something. [Surveys keep showing the same thing](/blog/young-people-and-tarot/): belief in at least one esoteric practice is highest among 18-to-24-year-olds, and TikTok is where they meet it.

I don't think that's a problem. I think that's a door.

## Where the critics are right, and where they're wrong {#critics-right}

Some of the dislike is deserved: flattering piles, funnels to paid readings, promises about other people, and readers who never miss. I go through those, and the habits that fix them, in [what the pick-a-card criticism gets right and wrong](/blog/pick-a-card-criticism/). TikTok adds one of its own: the pace. Fifteen seconds is enough to say a card's name and not enough to read anything.

Where the critics go wrong is in blaming the app for the funnels. Readings were a product long before TikTok existed; the app just made the shop window smaller. And they sneer at the audience, who are the people the books never reached. When a reader with twenty years' experience says "these kids don't know what they're doing", what I hear is that the kids are doing it without asking permission, which is exactly how I started. [Nobody gave me a gift](/blog/do-you-need-psychic-abilities/). A video did.

## What I'd want TarotTok to be {#what-id-want}

More of the formats nobody watches: card-a-day and card meanings, which are often decent. Fewer readings about someone else's heart. And more readers willing to say, on camera, that a pile didn't land. The [free events](/events/) are where I try it in public: people drawn at random, nothing said to me beforehand, and the notebook open.
