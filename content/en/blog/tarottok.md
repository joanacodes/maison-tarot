If you're over thirty-five and you read tarot, there's a good chance your view of TikTok tarot is "I don't watch it, but I've heard". Here is what it actually is, so you can dislike it for the right reasons.

## What a TarotTok video looks like {#what-it-looks-like}

Vertical, under a minute, often under thirty seconds. A reader, usually young, usually in a bedroom, holding a phone in one hand and a deck in the other. Formats:

- **"Pick a pile"** — three or four face-down stacks, choose one, get a short reading for that pile. [I've written about whether these work](/blog/do-pick-a-card-readings-work/); the answer is "like a song".
- **"This is for whoever needs to hear it"** — no piles, one message, aimed at the algorithm's guess about who's watching.
- **"Your person / your ex / your next lover"** — the most viewed and the most harmful; [the same reading I refuse one-to-one](/blog/no-contact-tarot-readings/), at scale.
- **Card-a-day** and **card meanings**, which are often decent and the least watched.
- **Live readings**, where viewers pay for a question — in France, one of the country's most-followed young readers charges twelve euros a question, according to a 2025 television investigation.

Hashtags: #tarottok, #witchtok, #pickacard, #tarotreading, and in French #tarotfrançais and #tiragesentimental.

## Why it works {#why-it-works}

Because it's fast, warm, and arrives when you're alone. Tarot's traditional gatekeeping — the books, the vocabulary, the older woman in the shop who won't sell you a deck unless it's a gift — is gone. A sixteen-year-old with a phone can meet the Star card at midnight and feel something. Surveys keep showing the same thing: belief in at least one esoteric practice is highest among 18-to-24-year-olds, and TikTok is where they meet it.

I don't think that's a problem. I think that's a door.

## Where the critics are right {#critics-right}

Experienced readers dislike TarotTok, and some of it is deserved.

The flattery: every pile is special, someone is thinking of you, abundance is coming. The funnels: free video, then "book a personal reading, link in bio". The promises: "he's coming back", said to a hundred thousand strangers at once. The pace: readings compressed to fifteen seconds, which is enough time to say a card's name and not enough to read anything. And the never-missing: [a reader who is never wrong isn't reading](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/).

All of that is real, and all of it is the same list of [red flags](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-tarot-reader/) I'd give for any reader in any medium.

## Where the critics are wrong {#critics-wrong}

They blame the format for the funnels, and they sneer at the audience.

The format is a mirror held up to a crowd. It can be done honestly — commit to something specific per pile, sell nothing, say when a pile is muddy — and some readers do. The funnels exist because readings were a product before TikTok existed; the app just made the shop window smaller.

And the audience is the people the books never reached. When a reader with twenty years' experience says "these kids don't know what they're doing", what I hear is that the kids are doing it without asking permission, which is exactly how I started. [Nobody gave me a gift](/blog/do-you-need-psychic-abilities/). A video did.

## What I'd want TarotTok to be {#what-id-want}

The same thing I want from any reader: told nothing, specific over flattering, misses said out loud, nothing to sell at the end. It's a high bar for a fifteen-second video, and a few people clear it. [The free event format at the House](/events/) is my attempt at the honest version — public, drawn at random, notebook open. It's TarotTok with the funnel removed, which turns out to be most of what needed removing.
