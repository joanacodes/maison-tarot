A free reading night works when it’s small, clear and honest: a set time, a few people drawn at random, a short reading each, and nothing sold at the end. Here’s how I’d set one up, from the format to the questions you’ll gently set aside.

## Pick a format that keeps it fair {#format}

The format I use at Maison Tarot: people register, a few are drawn at random on the evening, each gets one reading in front of the others, and I know nothing about them. Everyone else watches, asks questions and learns. It’s a reading, and it’s also a lesson.

A video call works better than a broadcast for this. You can see who’s there, people can speak instead of typing, and the group stays small enough to feel like a table. If you’d rather broadcast, [reading tarot live](/blog/tarot-live-instagram/) comes with its own problems, and I’ve listed them.

Drawing at random matters. It stops the evening from going to whoever writes first, and it takes the pressure off people who came only to watch.

The person who’s drawn tells you nothing first: no question, no situation. That’s what makes their confirmation afterwards worth something, for them and for everyone watching. I explain [why I ask for silence](/blog/the-silence-rule/) on its own page.

## Timing: the evening, and each person {#timing}

Keep the whole evening short; about ninety minutes is plenty. I sometimes stop after two hours of readings, out of caution, and reading in front of a group asks more of you than reading at home.

Give each person a time limit and say it out loud at the start: around ten minutes each. It sounds strict. It’s actually kind. Nobody wants to watch someone else’s reading drag on for forty minutes, and the person being read isn’t left exposed in front of strangers for too long.

A rough plan for ninety minutes: ten minutes to welcome people and explain the rules, six readings of ten minutes, a short pause halfway, and the rest for questions. **A limit announced at the start protects everyone, you included.** If you end the night worn out anyway, here’s [what helps after a draining evening of readings](/blog/energy-after-tarot-reading/).

## Consent, before every reading {#consent}

Always ask permission before reading someone, even someone who registered. Being drawn isn’t the same as agreeing to be read in front of a group tonight. Give each person the chance to say “not tonight”, with no reason needed.

Agree on these before the first card:

1. Readings happen in front of the others. Anyone who’d rather only watch is welcome to.
2. Nothing is recorded unless everyone has agreed, and you’ve said where a recording would go.
3. Nobody is read about who isn’t in the call: no partner, no ex, no boss.
4. The evening is for adults.
5. Anyone who’s having a hard night can tell you privately and be skipped, without explaining.

## What you don’t read on, said kindly {#refusals}

Say it at the start, before anyone is drawn: health, death, pregnancy, ongoing court cases, money decisions, curses and people who aren’t there are not read on tonight. Say it once, warmly, so nobody feels singled out later.

When one comes up anyway, remember why people ask: usually worry, love or fear. Someone asking whether their mother will get better isn’t breaking a rule; they’re frightened. Acknowledge that first. Then say gently why the cards aren’t the right tool: on a subject like that, a wrong word does damage, and every reader misses sometimes. Then offer something. “I’d rather look at what would help you get through this week. Shall we draw for that?” Or point to the person who can help: a doctor, a lawyer, someone they trust.

I keep a list of [kinder questions to offer instead](/blog/questions-to-avoid-asking-tarot/), and the [subjects I step back from](/disclaimers/#refusals), with the reasons.

## Keep it honest {#honest}

Honesty is what makes a free night worth more than a paid one that hides its misses. Three habits do most of the work:

- Say misses out loud. A miss is a miss: no second card drawn to rescue it, no “you’ll understand later”.
- Sell nothing. No “book a private reading to go deeper” at the end. If you teach, mention it once, at the end, after an evening of saying where you got it wrong.
- Write it down afterwards: what you said, and what was confirmed.

People who’ve been to a free reading elsewhere sometimes arrive braced for a pitch. [What to know before a free online reading](/blog/before-a-free-online-reading/) is the page I’d send them before the night, so they know what to expect.

## Your first night {#next-step}

Pick a date, a ninety-minute slot and a cap of six readings, and put the rules in the invitation. Run the first one with friends of friends if you’re nervous; it’s still a room of people who’ll tell you nothing.
