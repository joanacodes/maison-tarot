A new moon spread is a short reading you do when the moon is new, at the start of its cycle, to decide what you want to begin. Mine has three cards and ends with one sentence you write yourself. You don’t need a candle, a crystal or to stay up until midnight: a deck, a notebook and ten quiet minutes.

## What a new moon spread is {#what-it-is}

The new moon is the point in the lunar cycle when the moon sits between the Earth and the Sun, its lit side turned away from us, so we can’t see it. The whole cycle takes about 29.5 days. Two weeks after the new moon comes the full moon; then the moon thins again.

In astrology, and in a lot of modern practice, the new moon stands for beginnings: the dark before the first crescent, the moment to plant. A new moon spread borrows that idea. You lay out a few cards to look at where you are and what you’d like to start, and you leave with an intention — a decision for the next four weeks.

Whether the moon itself changes what the cards say is another question, and I take it apart in [does the moon change a reading?](/blog/moon-phases-and-tarot/) Here, the new moon is a date that comes back every month. **The moon gives you a date, the cards give you something to look at, and the intention is yours.**

## The three positions {#positions}

Shuffle however you like, then lay three cards from left to right.

1. *The ground: where you stand.* Not where you’d like to be — where you are tonight. This card describes the soil you’re planting in, stones included.
2. *The seed: what’s ready to start.* Something already there, in you or around you, that wants to begin. Not a wish; a beginning that has its conditions.
3. *The first step: what you can do this week.* The concrete gesture. Read it as an action, with a verb.

Before reading the cards one by one, read them as a row. Do the numbers climb or fall? A row that goes 2, 5, 9 is a cycle moving forward; 9, 5, 2 is someone going back to an earlier stage. The [1–10 cycle of numerology](/library/numerology/) does most of the work here. Then the elements: does the seed share the ground’s element, or break with it?

## Then one intention {#intention}

Once the three cards are read, close the deck and write one sentence in your notebook. That’s the intention.

An intention, here, is a sentence about what you’ll do, not about what you’ll receive. “I’ll be more confident” is a wish. “I’ll speak first at Monday’s meeting” is an intention. Three rules make it hold:

- A verb you control. Not “he’ll call”, not “the money will come”.
- Something you can check in two weeks, at the full moon, and again in four, at the next new moon.
- One sentence. If you need three, you have three intentions and none of them will hold.

This is where my spread parts ways with manifestation as it’s often taught online. I’m not asking the cards, or the moon, to send anything. Cards aren’t magic, you are: they help you see, then you choose and act. If you’re curious about the other approach, I looked at [manifestation and the law of attraction](/blog/delulu-manifestation-tarot/) in a separate article.

**An intention is a promise you can check, not a wish you wait on.**

## An example, step by step {#example}

Say you sit down on the evening of a new moon and draw the Five of Pentacles, the Ace of Wands and the Eight of Pentacles.

Lenses first. Numbers: 5, then 1, then 8 — a trial, a start, then mastery through effort. Elements: Earth, Fire, Earth — something material and a little cold, a spark of creative heat, then back to the bench.

Now the cards. The ground is the Five of Pentacles: two figures walking through the snow past a lit church window. Feeling left out in the cold, short of something, with warmth close by that nobody has gone in to ask for. The seed is the Ace of Wands: a hand from a cloud holding a wand that’s sprouting leaves. An idea with heat in it. The first step is the Eight of Pentacles: a craftsman at his bench, working one pentacle after another. Practice, repetition, the unglamorous part.

The sentence you might say to yourself: “I’ve been feeling short — of money or of recognition — and an idea is trying to start. It won’t come from waiting outside the door. It comes from sitting down and doing the work, piece by piece.”

And the intention it could lead to: “Two evenings a week on it until the full moon, and one finished piece shown to one person.” A verb you control, checkable, one sentence. If none of the three cards fits what you’re living, write that down too. It belongs in [your reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/) as much as the hits.

## What you don’t need {#what-you-dont-need}

No candle, no crystal, no bath, no particular hour. In 2021 I sold sage and candles; nothing in them improved a reading, and I stopped. I tell that story in [why I stopped cleansing decks](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/). If lighting a candle helps you sit down and be quiet, that’s your choice. The spread works the same without it.

You don’t need the exact minute of the new moon either. The evening before or after gives you the same three cards to look at; the date is a reminder, not a deadline. And you don’t need to do it every month. If a month goes by without it, nothing is lost.

## Two weeks later {#two-weeks-later}

The spread has a second half. At the full moon, open your notebook to the intention and tick or cross: did you do it? Not “did it work” — did you do it. That check is what turns a ritual into something that teaches you about yourself, and it’s what the [full moon spread](/blog/full-moon-tarot-spread/) is for.
