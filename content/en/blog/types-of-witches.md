Green witch, kitchen witch, hedge witch, sea witch: the words describe what a witch works with, how they practise or what they believe. They aren't ranks, and they aren't species. Here they are, defined plainly, with an example each.

At the end, my own view. It's short.

## Types of witches by what they work with {#by-what}

Most of the words you'll see describe the material of a practice.

- *Green witch.* A green witch works with plants: the garden, herbs, trees, wild places. Picture someone who grows rosemary and mint on a balcony, dries them in bunches, and uses them in teas and small rituals through the year.
- *Kitchen witch.* A kitchen witch makes the kitchen the centre of the practice: cooking, baking and brewing with an intention. For example, stirring a soup while thinking of what you want to bring into the house, or baking bread for a friend who has just moved in.
- *Hearth witch*, sometimes *cottage witch*. Close to the kitchen witch, but centred on the whole home: its rhythms, its care, its protection. Sweeping the doorstep as a way of closing the week is a typical hearth-witch gesture.
- *Hedge witch.* A solitary witch whose practice mixes plant lore with what practitioners call crossing the hedge — the hedge being the old boundary between the village and the wild, and, as an image, between this world and the other. Think of someone who gathers elderflower along a lane in the afternoon and sits in a trance-like meditation in the evening.
- *Sea witch.* A sea witch works with the sea: tides, shells, salt water, driftwood. She might time her practice to high tide and keep a jar of sea water from a beach that matters to her.
- *Cosmic witch*, or *astrological witch*. Works with the planets, the moon's phases and birth charts. Starting a project at the new moon, or checking where Mercury is before signing something, is cosmic-witch thinking.
- *Elemental witch.* Works with the four elements — earth, water, fire, air — and their qualities. If that one speaks to you, it's close to [the four elements](/library/four-elements/) as I use them in tarot.

## Types of witches by how they practise {#by-how}

Other words describe the shape of a practice rather than its material.

- *Solitary witch.* Practises alone, with no group. Many witches are solitary by choice; the hedge witch traditionally is.
- *Coven.* A group of witches who meet and practise together, often with a structure, roles and an initiation.
- *Eclectic witch.* Borrows from several traditions and builds a personal practice — a bit of green, a bit of cosmic, a seasonal calendar from a third source.
- *Hereditary witch.* Someone whose practice was passed down in the family: a grandmother's remedies, a family's way of marking births and deaths.
- *Baby witch.* A beginner, of any kind. I've written a [friendly guide for baby witches](/blog/baby-witch-where-to-start/) if that's where you are.

## Types of witches by belief {#by-belief}

And some words are about belief.

- *Wiccan.* A follower of Wicca, a modern pagan religion that took shape in England in the mid-twentieth century, with its own deities, ethics and eight seasonal festivals — Samhain, on 31 October, is one of them; I've written about [Samhain and tarot](/blog/samhain-tarot/). Wiccans generally practise witchcraft; not every witch is Wiccan.
- *Pagan witch.* Practises within a pagan religion other than Wicca, often a modern revival of an older one, Norse or Celtic for instance.
- *Secular witch.* Practises without religious belief: rituals as a way to focus, plants as plants, the moon as a calendar. For many people this is where witchcraft and everyday life meet most easily.

## Where tarot fits {#where-tarot-fits}

A tarot reader isn't a type of witch. Some witches read cards, and most tarot readers aren't witches; I've written about [the difference between tarot and witchcraft](/blog/is-tarot-witchcraft/).

There is one place where the two meet, though. On many witches' altars sit four tools: a wand, a cup, a blade and a pentacle. They're the four tarot suits — and the four objects on the Magician's table.

In my notes, the Magician is the card of the four elements — wand, cup, sword, pentacle — plus the ether: someone who knows how to use everything on the table. His weak side: he can pretend. A tool on an altar does nothing on its own; the person holding it does.

## Which witch am I? {#which-witch-am-i}

I don't call myself a witch. But I do have a definition, from my own notes: a witch is a human being fully aware of their power, and not afraid to use it and be themselves.

So, which witch am I? **None of these types is a uniform you have to wear; being yourself is plenty.**

The words above are useful for understanding what people mean, the way a glossary is. As a box to fit into, they work about as well as a single star sign does: you're not just one sign, you're a mix of everything. And the same goes for the labels people pick from a video — you don't choose what you are from a quiz, any more than you pick a starseed type ([the words empath, starseed and lightworker](/blog/empath-starseed-lightworker/) are defined separately). Practise for a year, and you'll find you've become your own kind of witch, if you want the word at all.
