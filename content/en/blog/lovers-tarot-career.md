The Lovers in a career reading is a choice: two offers, two paths, a partnership to accept or turn down. It’s a card of Air and Gemini, the mind and the conversation, so it asks you to choose with your eyes open rather than wait for a card to choose for you.

## The Lovers through its lenses {#lenses}

A man and a woman stand apart under an angel with red wings and a large sun. Behind him grows a tree whose leaves are flames; behind her, a tree with fruit and a snake coiled round it. The man looks at the woman, the woman looks up at the angel, and their hands don’t touch. It’s a picture of people deciding, not of people who have decided.

The number is [6](/library/numerology/number-6/): in my notes, “3 exalted” — the machine launched and running fast, harmony regained after the trial of 5. In the Instagram story I use for the numbers, 6 is Facebook deciding to invest: everything speeds up, and nobody reads the conditions any more. That’s the first thing this card says at work: a good offer still deserves a look at its terms. The advice of 6 is to stay calm and humble when things flow.

The element is Air: decisions, analysis, communication. In my notes Air is the best decision-maker of the four, because it sees everything at once — Fire is too impulsive, Water too emotional, Earth too stubborn. The energy is feminine: thinking before acting, reaching delicate subjects by detours. That’s a gift in a negotiation, and a trap when a fast answer is needed.

The astrology, if it helps you, is [Gemini](/library/astrology/gemini/): “I think”, a mutable sign that changes in order to communicate. Well placed, a great reporter; badly placed, a game of Chinese whispers. The colours add drive (the red wings and flames) and a choice made in broad daylight (the yellow sun). Every lens is on [the Lovers’ own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-lovers/).

## What the Lovers says about work: choosing a path {#work}

At work, the Lovers puts a choice on the table. Two job offers. Staying an expert or becoming a manager. Going freelance or staying employed. Joining a business partner, or not. Sometimes it’s quieter: the project you believe in against the one that will get you noticed.

Air reads that choice through values more than feelings. The card asks which option lets you be yourself on an ordinary Tuesday afternoon — not on the day you sign, but on a Tuesday, with the emails and the commute.

It’s also a card of partnerships, because 6 is exchange: two people who work well together, a client who fits, a team where the conversation flows. Gemini adds that a deal is only as good as the conversation behind it — what was said, by whom, and whether everyone heard the same thing.

Say someone is weighing two offers, one better paid, one closer to what they love. The Lovers won’t tell them which. It shows that they’re choosing rather than drifting, and that choosing one means saying no to the other. **People often hope a reading will spare them the “no”; this is the card that shows it can’t.**

## The Lovers reversed at work {#reversed}

Reversed, the Lovers is the choice avoided. You keep both doors open so you don’t have to lose either: two half-finished projects, one foot in each job, a side business that never gets a real decision. Meanwhile, time decides for you.

It can also be values out of line: a job that pays well and asks you to say things you don’t believe, or two partners who want different companies and haven’t said so yet. Gemini reversed is the conversation that doesn’t happen — or happens only in your head, in two versions.

## The Lovers next to other cards {#combinations}

{{< cards "sw02 cu07 ar15" >}}

Next to the Two of Swords — a blindfolded woman holding two crossed swords — the choice is being refused, both options held level so neither has to go. The blindfold comes off before any decision.

Next to the Seven of Cups, which in my notes is indecision, too many choices, the problem isn’t two options but seven, most of them imagined. Find the two that are real.

Next to the Devil, the same two figures come back chained to a block, and both cards come back to 6 (15 = 1 + 5). The Lovers is the 6 that keeps choosing; the Devil is the 6 that stopped. If the job you chose years ago now holds you, I’ve written about [the Devil in a career reading](/blog/devil-tarot-career/).

## “Which job should I take?” — and a more useful question {#question}

It’s the question this card attracts, and it makes sense: a big decision, two decent options, and you’d love someone to settle it.

I read; you decide. I won’t tell anyone to take one job or the other. The people making the offers aren’t in the room, the salary side is a money decision I don’t read on, and a card that chose for you would take away the one part of the decision that’s yours.

A more useful question: “What am I choosing when I choose this one, and what am I saying no to?” Or: “Which of these would I still choose if nobody ever knew about it?” Those have answers inside you, and my piece on [tarot for decision making](/blog/tarot-for-decision-making/) goes further.

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Draw two cards, one for each path, and describe them before you decide which is “better”: the colours, the number, the element. Write one sentence for each. You’ll often find you had already chosen, and the cards gave you the words. If you’d like to learn to read a choice like this with someone beside you, a [one-to-one session](/sessions/) lasts sixty minutes, and you leave reading the cards yourself.
