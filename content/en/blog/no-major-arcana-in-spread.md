No major arcana in your spread means the situation is happening at the scale of everyday life: decisions, feelings, work and people you can act on. It doesn’t mean the reading is small or that nothing important is going on. And in a three-card spread, it’s what happens about one time in three.

## Major and minor arcana, in a few lines {#definitions}

The major arcana are the 22 cards numbered 0 to 21, from the Fool to the World, each with its own name. The minor arcana are the other 56: four suits of ten numbered cards, Ace to Ten, plus four court cards each.

The usual way to read the difference is by time scale. Majors speak of a chapter — months, sometimes years. Minors speak of a situation — days, weeks, what you can do with your hands. A major isn’t more important than a minor; it’s slower. I go through the whole comparison in [major arcana vs minor arcana](/blog/major-vs-minor-arcana/).

## It happens more often than you’d think {#how-often}

There are 22 majors in 78 cards, a little over one in four. Here is what that means for a spread with none at all.

| Spread | Chance of no major |
|---|---|
| 1 card | about 72% |
| 3 cards | about 36% |
| 5 cards | about 18% |
| 10 cards (Celtic Cross) | about 3% |

So in a short spread, no major is ordinary, and you don’t need to read anything into it. In a ten-card Celtic Cross it’s rare enough to mention. **An absence is only worth reading when it’s unusual.**

## What it says when it does stand out {#what-it-says}

When a large spread comes out without a single major, I read it as: this is in your hands. Nothing larger is pressing on the situation. It is being shaped by what the person does, feels, thinks and builds, rather than by a long chapter of their life turning.

For someone who wants to act, that’s good news. It also means the reading won’t hand them a big archetype to lean on — no “this is your Tower moment”. It’s ordinary life, in detail, and detail is where you can change things.

## Read the minors with everything they carry {#read-the-minors}

A spread of minors has more structure than people expect. Each card carries an element, a number and sometimes a rank. Use all three, in this order:

1. Count the suits. Which element leads? Lots of Pentacles is matter and work; lots of Swords, decisions and words; lots of Cups, [feelings and relationships](/blog/lots-of-cups-in-a-reading/); lots of Wands, drive.
2. Line up the numbers. All low (Ace to Three): something is beginning. All middle (Four to Seven): it’s being tested. All high (Eight to Ten): a stage is closing. The [numerology lens](/library/numerology/) gives you the full 1–10 cycle.
3. Look at the court cards. They are people, or ways of behaving: the Page learns, the Knight leaves, the Queen holds, the King decides. [Court cards explained](/blog/court-cards-explained/) covers all sixteen.

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Eight of Pentacles, the Three of Wands and the Page of Swords.

Earth, Fire, Air: work, drive, thought. Eight is effort and mastery; Three is the machine running; the Page is learning, or a message to carry. Add the colours if you like: the yellow sky of the Three of Wands puts some optimism right in the middle of the spread. You might say: “You’re putting in steady work, it’s starting to run on its own, and there’s something to learn or something to say.” No thunderclap. A plan. Then you stop and let them tell you whether it lands.

## What not to do {#what-not-to-do}

Don’t draw extra cards until a major turns up. A card pulled to fill a gap muddies the reading rather than completing it; I explain why in [the clarifier card](/blog/the-clarifier-card/).

Don’t read the absence as “this doesn’t matter”. The person’s question matters exactly as much with three minors as with three majors. The spread describes the scale of the situation, not its weight in someone’s life.

And don’t rank spreads. A reading full of majors isn’t a better reading; it’s a different kind of moment.

## Try it on your next spread {#try-it}

Before you read a single card, count: how many majors, which suits, which numbers. Say those three things out loud first. You’ll find the reading has already started before you’ve looked up one meaning.

If the four suits still feel blurry, the [four elements](/library/four-elements/) are the place to begin. They’re the lens that makes a spread of minors talk.
