When a tarot reading is wrong, say so: “that one’s a miss.” Out loud, to the person, then write it down and move on. No reframing, no extra card, no “it’ll make sense later”.

It feels too small to count as a method. It’s the habit that keeps your hits worth trusting, and it makes you a more precise reader, not a more cautious one.

## Say “that one’s a miss” out loud {#say-it}

You’ve finished speaking, and the person says: “No, none of that fits.” You have about two seconds before you start explaining. Use them to say: “Okay. That one’s a miss.”

It feels exposed the first few times. Then you notice the person relax. They don’t have to pretend, search their memory for something that might fit, or protect your feelings. They can tell you the truth, which is the most useful thing they can give you.

By my own count, about one reading in ten misses. Saying so doesn’t make that number worse; it makes it visible. **A miss said out loud costs you a second; a miss explained away costs you the person’s trust.**

## Three ways readers rescue a reading {#rescue}

Say someone tells you nothing and draws the Four of Wands and the Three of Cups.

A garland held up on four wands, a castle behind; three women raising their cups, fruit at their feet. You say: “Something built and finished, a home, people gathered to celebrate it.” They answer: “Not at all. I’ve been on my own all month.”

Here are the three rescues that tempt every reader at that moment:

1. *Reframing.* “Maybe it’s a celebration you haven’t realised is coming.” Now the reading can’t be wrong, which means it can’t be right either.
2. *The extra card.* You draw one more to “clarify”. It doesn’t clarify; it starts a second story. I explain why I don’t in [the clarifier card](/blog/the-clarifier-card/).
3. *Fishing.* “Does someone in your family have news? A friend?” Ask enough questions and something will fit. That’s no longer tarot; it’s [cold reading](/blog/cold-reading-vs-tarot/), even when it’s done in good faith.

The honest answer is the short one: “Then that one’s a miss.”

## Write it down {#write-it-down}

After the reading, open your notebook. Two columns: what I said, what was confirmed. For this one, the right-hand column reads “no — on her own all month”. Mark it as a miss.

Counting is what turns “I think this works” into “I know when it works”. I kept that notebook for my readings for strangers, and the one-in-ten figure comes from it: a manual tally, not a study. The format is in [how I keep a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/).

If the person writes days later to say something did happen, my suggestion is to add a new line with the date rather than rewrite the first. My very first reading for a stranger went that way. She told me it didn’t resonate: she wasn’t looking for a relationship. Three or four days later she texted, shocked, to say all of it had happened. On the day, it was a miss. Both things are true, and a notebook should be able to show both.

## Why saying it makes you better {#why-it-helps}

A reader who never misses is a reader who never says anything specific. “Changes are coming” can’t miss. “A home, a celebration, people gathered” can. Being willing to say the word is what lets you be precise.

Misses also teach you, once you have a few. Read back over a month of them and they tend to cluster: timing, perhaps, or one kind of question, or readings done late when you were tired. That pattern is worth more than any meaning learned by heart. Sometimes what goes wrong is the timing or the interpretation more than the message itself — I tell the story that taught me that in [why a reading can be wrong](/blog/why-a-reading-can-be-wrong/).

And misses tell you when to stop. Two or three in a row usually means you’re tired, not that the deck has turned against you. I sometimes stop after two hours of readings, out of caution, and I’d suggest the same.

A miss is a miss. It isn’t a failure, and it isn’t a secret.

## If you were the one being read {#if-you-were-read}

If someone read for you and it didn’t fit, you’re allowed to say so, and you’re allowed to leave it there. You don’t owe the reader a match, and you don’t need a second reading to cancel out the first.

A good reader will thank you for it. If a reader insists you’ll understand later, or offers a paid session to “go deeper” into what didn’t land, that’s worth noticing. Either way, [tarot is not an authority](/blog/tarot-is-not-an-authority/): act on what you feel, not on what was said.

## Try it at your next reading {#next-step}

Before you start, decide on the sentence you’ll use: “That one’s a miss.” Say it the first time it’s true, write it down, and notice how the next reading goes. It works with friends too, where it’s harder to say and matters more — there’s more on that in [reading tarot for someone else with care](/blog/read-tarot-for-someone-else/).
