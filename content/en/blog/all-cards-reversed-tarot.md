If every card in your spread came out upside down, look at your shuffle before you look for a meaning. Most of the time the cause is in your hands, not in the cards. And if the shuffle was fair, a fully reversed spread is still readable — it usually says something gentle: the energy is there, but it’s holding back.

## First, check the shuffle {#check-the-shuffle}

Three mechanical causes explain most fully reversed spreads.

1. A block of the deck was turned round. If you once rotated half the deck and then only shuffled overhand, cards that started side by side tend to stay side by side, and a whole run of reversals comes out together.
2. You turn cards over top to bottom. Flip a card from left to right, like the page of a book, and it keeps its direction; flip it from the top edge towards you and it turns round. Mix the two habits and your reversals come from your wrist.
3. Plain chance. If about half your deck is reversed and well shuffled, each card has roughly one chance in two of landing upside down. Three out of three happens about one time in eight; five out of five, one in thirty-two. It feels rare. It isn’t.

If you never turned any cards round on purpose and they’re all reversed, check whether the deck went back into its box the other way up. A deck stored upside down gives you nothing but reversals.

Ten out of ten in a Celtic Cross, on the other hand, is about one chance in a thousand with a fair shuffle. **Before you read a fully reversed spread as a message, rule out the hands that shuffled it.**

The fix takes a minute: turn every card upright, turn half the deck round, then mix the deck your normal way. [Whichever shuffle you prefer](/blog/how-to-shuffle-tarot-cards/) will do, provided your way of laying the cards out never changes.

## If the shuffle was fair: what it can say {#what-it-can-say}

Read each card the way you’d read any reversal: upright first, through its colour, number and element, then ask whether that energy is held back or overflowing. That’s [the shortcut for reversed cards](/blog/reversed-tarot-cards/), and it works the same with three reversals as with one.

Then step back and read the spread as a whole. When everything is reversed, the usual message is that the situation is real but not moving yet: something is waiting, turned inward, or being held in — often by you. Less often it’s the opposite: everything at once, too fast, spilling over. The person you’re reading for usually knows which in a second.

**A fully reversed spread isn’t a bad spread; it’s the same spread, holding its breath.**

## An example, worked through {#example}

Say someone tells you nothing, shuffles fairly, and draws three cards, all reversed: the Ace of Wands, the Eight of Pentacles and the Sun.

Upright first. The Ace of Wands offers a single leafy wand from a cloud: a 1, Fire, the spark of something new, and green for growth. The Eight of Pentacles is a craftsman at his bench carving coins one by one: an 8, Earth, mastery earned by effort. The Sun is a child on a white horse under a huge sun, sunflowers behind a wall, yellow everywhere — joy, self-expression. Its number, 19, adds up to 10, which in [the numbers cycle is the corridor](/library/numerology/number-10/) between two rooms.

Upright, the row would tell a story: a spark, the work, then the joy of a stage completed.

Now all three reversed. The spark is there, but nobody has picked it up. The work goes on with the head down. The joy is waiting for permission. My one sentence for the whole spread would be: “There’s something you’d love to start, you have the skill for it, and you keep working instead of beginning. What would you do if you let yourself enjoy it?”

Then you ask whether it lands. If the answer is no, log a miss, and don’t redraw to get the cards the right way up.

## What it doesn’t mean {#what-it-doesnt-mean}

If a fully reversed spread frightened you, that’s understandable: upside-down pictures look as if something has gone wrong. Nothing has. It isn’t a curse, a warning from somewhere, or the cards refusing to talk to you. Cards don’t punish anyone.

It doesn’t need fixing either. Drawing a fourth card to “clarify” the first three, or reshuffling until they come out upright, only buries the first reading under a second one. [One more card](/blog/the-clarifier-card/) muddies a reading far more often than it clears it.

And if you notice you’re asking again and again, hoping for an upright spread this time, notice it kindly. [Asking the same question twice](/blog/ask-tarot-the-same-question-twice/) tends to tell you more about the asking than about the answer.

## Try it tonight {#try-tonight}

1. Turn every card upright. Then turn half the deck round and shuffle well.
2. Choose how you’ll turn cards over — side to side, like a page — and keep to it.
3. Draw three cards. If they’re all reversed again, read them: upright first, then held back or overflowing.
4. Write one sentence for the whole spread.
5. Put the deck away until tomorrow, and reread the sentence in the evening.
