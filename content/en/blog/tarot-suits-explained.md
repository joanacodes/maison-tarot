The minor arcana come in four suits, and each suit is an element. Wands are Fire, Cups are Water, Swords are Air, Pentacles are Earth. The suit tells you what a card is about — drive, feelings, thoughts or matter — before you know anything else about it.

## What a tarot suit is {#what-a-suit-is}

A suit is a family of fourteen cards that share one object: the Ace, nine numbered cards, Two through Ten, and a royal court of Page, Knight, Queen, King, covered in [the court-card guide](/blog/court-cards-explained/). Tarot shares its suits with playing cards, and the cousins are still recognisable.

| Suit | Element | What it’s about | What’s drawn on the cards | Playing-card cousin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wands | Fire | Drive, projects, desire | Living wood with leaves; salamanders | Clubs |
| Cups | Water | Feelings, relationships | Cups, streams, the sea, fish | Hearts |
| Swords | Air | Thoughts, decisions, words | Bare blades, clouds, wind, birds, butterflies | Spades |
| Pentacles | Earth | Money, body, work, what has value | Star-marked coins, gardens, grapes, bulls | Diamonds |

Other decks rename them — Rods or Staves for Wands, Coins or Disks for Pentacles. Same families. You may also read that Wands are Air and Swords are Fire: a few schools swap the two. I follow the Rider-Waite-Smith pairing, and its drawings back it up, as you’ll see below.

The Aces show the four objects at their plainest: a hand comes out of a cloud holding the suit’s object, and that’s all there is.

## How to recognise each suit, and one card to start with {#the-four-suits}

The element pages in the Library give each element its full portrait: keywords, flip side, archetype, a worked reading. Here I stay with the suits themselves: what to look for on the card, and one card I’d start with.

### Wands: Fire {#wands}

Wands are [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/): drive, projects, desire: what you start, and what you want badly enough to start it. On the cards, the wands are living wood: rough staffs with small green leaves still sprouting from them, whether they’re planted as posts, carried as a load or flying through the air. The court cards add the suit’s own animal: salamanders, which old European lore said could live in fire, on the Page’s and the Knight’s tunics and on the King’s throne, beside a lion.

Example card: the Four of Wands, four wands with a garland strung between them and people raising bouquets behind. In my notes it’s foundations, a marriage, building a family. A 4 is stability; in Fire, it’s a project with something solid under it.

### Cups: Water {#cups}

Cups are [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/): feelings, relationships, the strengths you don’t show. On the cards, water runs well beyond the cups: five streams pouring from the Ace into a pond of water lilies, rivers, the sea behind the Page and under the King’s throne. And fish: one looks out of the Page’s cup, another leaps from the waves beside the King.

Example card: the Six of Cups, two children in a village courtyard, the taller one holding out a flower-filled cup to the smaller. In my notes: taking time for yourself, childhood, flowering again, harmony.

### Swords: Air {#swords}

Swords are [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/): thinking, deciding, saying — the element that cuts. On the cards, look up. Clouds, wind bending the trees behind the Knight, birds in flight, and butterflies on the Queen’s crown and the King’s throne. The blades are bare.

Example card: the Eight of Swords, a woman bound and blindfolded among eight swords planted in the ground. In my notes it’s a prisoner of yourself while every tool is within reach. You can take the blindfold off.

### Pentacles: Earth {#pentacles}

Pentacles are [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): money, the body, work, and whatever has value for you; in my notes, not all riches are gold. On the cards, gold coins marked with a five-pointed star, and around them land that has been worked: gardens, vines, a ploughed field behind the Knight, a craftsman’s bench. The King sits in a robe covered with grapes, on a throne carved with bulls’ heads — the bull of Taurus, an Earth sign.

Example card: the Nine of Pentacles, a woman alone in a vineyard with a bird on her gloved hand. Enjoying what comes, and gratitude for everything you have.

## Reading the suits in a spread {#in-a-spread}

Before you read a single card, count the suits on the table. Say someone tells you nothing and draws five: the Ace, the Three and the Eight of Wands, then the Two and the Six of Swords.

Three Wands, two Swords. Don’t let the water in the Six of Swords fool you: the suit is the object on the card, not the scenery, and here the six swords stand upright in the boat. So this is Fire and Air: a project moving fast, and a decision still being weighed — the blindfolded woman of the Two holds her swords crossed and waits.

Then look at what’s missing. No Cups and no Pentacles: nothing yet about how anyone feels, or about what it will take in time, work and money. A missing suit is often the next thing worth asking about.

To take one number across the four suits — the four Fives, the four Eights — the [minor arcana grid](/blog/minor-arcana/) lays all forty numbered cards side by side.

## Learn them with four cards {#next-step}

Take the four Aces out of your deck and lay them in a row. For each one, say out loud what the hand is holding and what you’d do with it today: light something, pour something, cut something, keep something. Then shuffle the deck and sort it by the object alone, without reading anything: four piles for the suits, and a fifth for the major arcana, which belong to none. That’s the four suits, learned in an evening, and you’ll recognise them in every other card.
