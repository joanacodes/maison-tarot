The best tarot gift for a beginner is a Rider-Waite-Smith deck with the original drawings, and a plain notebook to go with it. Everything else is optional, and a few popular extras are better left on the shelf. Here’s what I’d put in the parcel, and what I’d leave out.

## What to buy a tarot beginner {#what-to-buy}

Five things, in order of usefulness.

1. *A Rider-Waite-Smith deck.* The 1909 deck drawn by Pamela Colman Smith, where every card, the numbered ones included, has a scene. Look for the original drawings on the box or in the description; several editions exist, with different colouring and card sizes, and I compare a few in [my shortlist of decks for beginners](/blog/best-tarot-decks/). Size matters more than people think: pocket and tin editions suit small hands and handbags, larger cards suit tired eyes.
2. *A notebook.* Plain, sturdy, one that opens flat on a table. It becomes the most useful object in the kit: what I said on the left, what the person confirmed on the right. I explain how in [keeping a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/).
3. *One good book, if they like books.* Not five. The ones I recommend are in [what books give you, and what only practice can](/blog/learn-tarot-from-a-book/). If they’re curious about astrology too, *The Only Astrology Book You’ll Ever Need* by Joanna Martine Woolfolk is the one I hand people.
4. *Something to lay the cards on, and something to keep them in.* A plain cotton cloth or a clean tea towel keeps cards off a sticky table; a small box or pouch keeps a deck together in a bag. Practical, not magical.
5. *Time together.* Offer to be their first person to read for. Sit down, tell them nothing, and let them try one card on you. It costs nothing and it’s the gift they’ll remember.

**A deck and a notebook are the whole kit; everything else is wrapping.**

## What to skip {#what-to-skip}

Some extras are sold as if a beginner couldn’t start without them. They can.

- *Cleansing kits and sage bundles.* I sold sage in 2021, and I stopped, because nothing in it improved a single reading. The longer story is in [why I stopped cleansing decks](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/). If your friend loves the smell, that’s another matter; buy it as something to enjoy, not as equipment.
- *Crystals sold as necessary.* Lovely objects, and fine as a present in their own right. A stone doesn’t make a reading more accurate, though, so don’t buy one because a listing says “for tarot”.
- *An oracle deck, if they asked for tarot.* Oracle decks are their own thing: any number of cards, meanings chosen by their author. They’re pleasant to own, and they won’t teach the structure of tarot.
- *A Marseille or a Thoth as a first deck*, unless that’s what they asked for. Both are beautiful, and both have numbered cards without scenes, which makes the first months harder. They make a wonderful second deck.
- *Boxed sets where you can’t tell which deck is inside.* If the description doesn’t say whose drawings are on the cards, keep looking.

## If they already read {#already-reading}

A gift for someone who already reads is a different parcel, because they have the basics and the habits.

A second deck is the obvious choice, and the best moment for one is when they can read a stranger from a single card without a book. The Tarot de Marseille is a good test of whether the lenses have really sunk in: the numbered cards are patterns of cups, swords, coins and batons, so you read them through number and element alone. The Thoth, painted by Frieda Harris for Aleister Crowley, is for someone curious about symbols for their own sake. Ask first which one tempts them; readers often have a list.

Other ideas that work: a fresh notebook, because the old one is always full; a book on the history of the cards rather than on their meanings; or an evening at one of the [free events](/events/), where readings are drawn at random and they can try reading for strangers. Collecting decks is a bit like collecting art, so if they collect, let them choose. A deck they picked themselves will get used.

## Is it all right to give someone their first deck? {#first-deck}

Yes, and some people would love it to happen that way. There’s an old belief that your first deck should be a gift. It’s a belief, not a rule, and [buying your own brings no bad luck either](/blog/do-you-have-to-be-gifted-your-first-tarot-deck/), so your present is welcome without being required.

My own first deck came this way. Friends picked it off my wishlist for Christmas 2020; I was expecting an astrology book. I started reading the day after.

That’s the one thing I’d keep from the old belief: give a deck to someone who has asked for one, or hinted. A deck someone wanted is a beginning. A deck nobody asked for is an object on a shelf.

## A note to put in the parcel {#the-note}

Write one suggestion on the gift tag: “Draw one card tomorrow morning and describe what you see before you look anything up.” That’s a better start than any ritual, and the first week with a deck can follow the plan in [how to get to know a new tarot deck](/blog/new-tarot-deck/).

And if you want a line to finish the card with: cards aren’t magic, you are.
