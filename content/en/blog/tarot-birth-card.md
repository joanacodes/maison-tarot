Your tarot birth card is the major arcana card you reach by adding up your date of birth. Many dates give a pair: a card between 10 and 21, and the single-digit card it reduces to. It’s a lovely way into the major arcana and a good pair of cards to study. It isn’t a portrait of you.

## What a tarot birth card is {#definition}

A birth card is a major arcanum attached to your date of birth by numerology. Like the [life path number](/blog/life-path-number/), it starts from your date. Unlike it, it stops at the major arcana instead of going all the way down to a single digit, so you often end up with two cards.

Some books give the two cards names and separate jobs. Here I’ll keep them as a pair to read together, with the same lenses as any other card.

## How to calculate yours {#calculate}

1. Add your day, month and year of birth as numbers.
2. Add the digits of the total.
3. If the result is above 22, add its digits again (25 becomes 2 + 5 = 7).
4. Between 10 and 21, that’s your first card; add its two digits to get the second.
5. Exactly 22 is the Fool, numbered 0 and sometimes counted as 22, paired with the Emperor (2 + 2 = 4).
6. A single digit gives one card, which counts twice.

The worked example, for someone born on 14 March 1992:

14 + 3 + 1992 = 2009, then 2 + 0 + 0 + 9 = 11, then 1 + 1 = 2.

Eleven is Justice in the Rider-Waite-Smith; two is the High Priestess. The pair is Justice and the High Priestess.

Two details. If you use a Marseille deck, Justice is 8 and Strength is 11 — the Golden Dawn swapped them and Waite followed — so follow the numbers printed on your deck. And 19 goes one step further: the Sun, then 1 + 9 = 10, the Wheel of Fortune, then 1 + 0 = 1, the Magician. Three cards instead of two.

Some people add every digit one by one, as for a life path (1 + 4 + 0 + 3 + 1 + 9 + 9 + 2 = 29, then 11). For this date both methods agree; for many dates they don’t, and the digit-by-digit total often reduces to a single card. Choose one method and keep it.

## Reading the pair through the lenses {#reading-the-pair}

Take the example. [Justice](/library/tarot-cards/justice/) is 11, so 2 once reduced; Air; Libra in the Golden Dawn. A seated figure holds a raised sword and a pair of scales. The [High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/) is 2; Water; the Moon. My notes on her: the moon influences water, her robe falls like a waterfall, and she knows a lot — with a façade over the rest, like the face of the moon we never see.

Both are 2, and both sit between two pillars. In my [numerology table](/library/numerology/), 2 is cooperation, a contract, weighing pros and cons; its minus is stubbornness, solitude, ego. Air and Water together: deciding with the head, feeling under the surface.

A sentence you could test on yourself: “You weigh things before you decide, and you know more than you say. The flip side: decisions kept behind a calm face for too long.”

**Like every card, a birth card cuts both ways: weigh what it offers and what it costs before you decide it describes you.**

## What to do with it {#what-to-do}

- Put the two cards where you’ll see them for a week. Each day, describe what’s on them before you read anything about them.
- Read each card through its number, element and colours, then its plus and its minus.
- Tick or cross, line by line, the way I tested [my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/). Give your own ticks the same scrutiny you’d give a stranger’s.
- Use them as a way into the majors. Two cards known well teach you more than twenty-two skimmed — [the 22 major arcana](/blog/major-arcana/) are all on one page when you’re ready.

What I’d avoid: using it as an explanation. “I’m a Tower, I can’t help it” is the birth-card version of “because I’m a Scorpio”.

## What it can’t tell you {#limits}

Everyone born on the same day shares your pair, and there are only thirteen pairs plus a handful of single cards, so you share yours with plenty of people you know. Nobody drew these cards for you: they come from arithmetic, not from a reading. They point at a theme to look at. They don’t say who you are, or what will happen to you.

Your birthday is a good day to come back to them. The [birthday spread](/blog/birthday-tarot-spread/) gives the year ahead four cards; lay your birth pair beside it and see whether the year talks to them.
