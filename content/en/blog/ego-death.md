Ego death is the experience of the self you usually live inside — the voice that narrates, judges and protects — stepping aside for a while, so there’s the doing and nobody standing apart watching it. My working version: accepting the death of your self while you do something. It isn’t the same as being generous, and confusing the two does more harm than the phrase itself.

## What ego death means {#what-it-means}

You’ll meet the phrase in meditation, in writing about psychedelics and, lately, all over spiritual social media. It describes the moment the sense of being a separate someone — with a name, a reputation and opinions to defend — loosens or drops away.

It comes in two sizes. The large one is what people describe after deep meditation or a psychedelic experience: overwhelming, sometimes frightening, the feeling that “I” wasn’t there. The small one is ordinary. A painter who looks up and finds three hours gone. A cook who forgot to be anxious. A conversation where you stopped planning what to say next.

Reading tarot asks for the small one every time. Before a card, I tell myself I’m about to talk nonsense. That sentence, which I explain in [my method for beginners](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/), lets the person who wants to be right, look clever and be liked step aside for the length of the reading. **Ego death is accepting that your self steps aside while you do something, not that it disappears for good.**

Two cards carry the idea. [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), in my notes, isn’t about dying: it’s the harvest, the field cleared so something can grow again. The Hanged Man has stopped struggling and sees the world from another angle.

## The ego isn’t the villain {#not-the-villain}

The ego isn’t a mean person living in your head. It’s the part of you that tries to make sense of the world, and to protect you from its absurdity and its violence.

It builds a story — I’m the reliable one, I’m the funny one, I’m the one who copes — so the day hangs together. It flinches at criticism because criticism once hurt. It remembers which rooms weren’t safe. Most of the time it’s doing its job.

It oversteps when it protects you from something you need: a lesson, a mistake, an apology. In my numerology notes, the minus of the number 1 is a lack of humility, when we’re all students our whole life. That’s the ego guarding the door a little too well.

So “killing the ego” is the wrong picture. You’d want it back the next time you cross a road or sign a lease. Be as gentle with it as you’d be with a friend who’s over-protective because they care; [shadow work](/blog/shadow-work-beginners/) starts with that kind of gentleness.

## Why ego death isn’t generosity {#not-generosity}

Generosity is giving something of yours — time, money, attention — to someone else. It needs a self: someone who owns the thing and chooses to give it. It usually feels good, and that’s fine.

Ego death has no giver. For a while there’s nobody standing in the middle of the action keeping score, so there’s nothing to give away and nobody to thank.

The two get confused when “killing the ego” starts to mean saying yes to everything, having no needs and giving until you’re empty. That isn’t ego death. It’s often a self that’s afraid of not being liked, which is the ego working overtime.

Picture someone who rearranges their whole week to help a friend move house, and resents it quietly for a month. Generous on paper, and full of self: the need to be the good one, the score being kept. Now picture a musician lost in a song. Not generous, not selfish: absorbed. **Generosity needs a self to give from; ego death is that self stepping aside while you act.**

## When someone says they’ve had one {#claiming-it}

Watch for one thing, kindly: the ego that claims its own death. The person who has “gone beyond the ego” and now feels above everyone who hasn’t. The one who thinks they’re saving the world, or that they belong to a small group with abilities nobody else has.

It’s very human. After an experience that felt huge, we all want it to have made us special. But “I’ve killed my ego”, worn as a badge, is the ego in a new outfit. It’s one of the reasons [I keep a little distance from “spiritual” circles](/blog/spiritual-but-not-in-spiritual-circles/).

## Go slowly {#go-slowly}

Big surrenders can frighten. In late 2020 I dreamed of a gigantic wave every night, gentle, telling me to surrender. I didn’t; I hid in a house with others. I tell it because surrender is a big word, and it’s allowed to scare you.

My rule for anything in this territory: don’t go too high too fast, or you might not find your way back home. Start crescendo.

I don’t read on health, and I won’t advise on substances; if you’re considering an experience like that, it’s a conversation for a doctor. And if the feeling of not being yourself turns up uninvited and stays, it isn’t a milestone to push through. That’s [a door for a professional](/disclaimers/#therapy), and there’s no shame in opening it.

## Where to start {#start}

Start with the small size. Ten minutes of [meditation for people who think they can’t](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/): doing nothing, letting go of control, which is harder than it sounds. A drawing you don’t show anyone. One card read aloud for a friend who’s told you nothing.

You can’t be told what it’s like. You can only try. If you’d like to try with other people in the room, the [workshops](/workshops/) open with a card in the first ten minutes, before any explanation.
