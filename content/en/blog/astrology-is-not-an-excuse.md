Astrology can describe a tendency. It can’t excuse what you do with it. “Because I’m a Scorpio” is usually said with a smile, and that’s fine — until it ends a conversation that needed to happen.

## What it looks like when astrology becomes an excuse {#what-it-looks-like}

Using astrology as an excuse means pointing at a sign or a placement to explain away a behaviour, or to decide in advance about a person, instead of looking at the behaviour or the person themselves.

It sounds like this:

- “I’m a Scorpio, I hold grudges. That’s how I am.”
- “Sorry I snapped. Mercury retrograde.”
- “It’s because I’m a Taurus — I don’t change my mind.”
- “I don’t date Scorpios.”

Most of these are half jokes, and I like that astrology gives people a shared vocabulary for laughing at themselves. I’ve no wish to take the fun out of it. The line I’d draw is this one: a description is welcome; a permission isn’t.

## You’re not one sign; you’re a mix of everything {#a-mix}

When someone says “I’m a Scorpio”, they mean the Sun was in Scorpio when they were born. That’s one placement out of many.

The way I explain it: picture a house. The Sun is the inner house. The Moon is your own room — very important, and often the part people know least. The rising sign is the outside, what others see from the street. Then every planet sits somewhere in that house, in a sign and in a house of the chart. A Scorpio Sun with a Libra Moon and a Sagittarius rising is a very different person from a Scorpio Sun with a Capricorn Moon and a Cancer rising. I go through those three in [Sun, Moon and rising](/blog/sun-moon-rising-big-three/).

**You’re not just a sign; you’re a mix of everything.**

That’s why “I don’t date Scorpios” doesn’t hold up. It judges a whole house by one room, and it rules out a twelfth of the people you might meet on the strength of a single line. If you have a crush, I’d go further: [don’t look up their chart at all](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/). Let the magic happen for the two of you.

## What I found when I tested my own chart {#what-i-found}

I came to astrology to mock it. I put on a video to laugh, read my own chart, and it was too precise to laugh at. So I tested it box by box — a tick or a cross for every placement — then did the same with family, friends and strangers.

Overall, 30 to 40% held. Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held far more, 75 to 90% of the time. The whole story is in [how I tested my birth chart](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/).

What that means here: a sign description is right roughly a third of the time. That’s enough to be interesting. It isn’t enough to excuse yourself, and it’s certainly not enough to rule someone out.

And since Scorpio is in the title: in my notes, its verb is “I desire”. It’s a fixed sign — stable, enduring, and on the other side slow to let go, especially of emotional attachments. Its archetype is the person who says nothing at a party and draws every eye. There’s more on [the Scorpio page of the Library](/library/astrology/scorpio/). In tarot, Scorpio’s card is Death, which in my notes isn’t about dying: it’s the harvest, starting again, making life fertile once more.

So even read generously, the sign describes intensity, attachment and the ability to transform. Nothing in it says “and so the people around you must put up with it”. If anything, its card is about changing.

## If something keeps hurting the people around you, check yourself {#check-yourself}

I mean this kindly. If a pattern keeps coming back — you go silent for days when you’re hurt, you keep score, you leave before anyone can leave you — and the people you love keep getting hurt by it, a chart can help you see the pattern. It can’t ask them to accept it.

Say you’ve always filed your long silences under “Scorpio”. Try another question: what is this silence protecting, and what does it cost the person waiting on the other side? Astrology can help you ask that; in my own test, Saturn was where people’s hardest lessons sat. It can’t answer it for you.

## What astrology is good for, and what it isn’t {#good-for}

Uses I’d keep:

- A vocabulary for tendencies, yours and other people’s.
- A lens for reading cards — that’s how it lives in [my Library](/library/astrology/).
- A starting point for looking at yourself honestly.

Uses I’d gently put down:

- Excusing a behaviour that hurts someone.
- Choosing or ruling out partners by Sun sign.
- Reading someone’s chart without asking them.
- Letting it make your decisions. At a third, it would be wrong two times in three.

It’s also on my [disclaimers page](/disclaimers/#astrology): astrology is not an excuse. Next time you hear yourself say “because I’m a…”, try finishing the sentence differently: “…and what I’m going to do about it is…”.
