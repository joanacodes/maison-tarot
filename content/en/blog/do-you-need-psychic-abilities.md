"I'd love to read cards but I don't have the gift." I hear this every month, and every month I say the same thing: there isn't one. Or rather — everyone has the same one, and it's not the thing you think it is.

## Where the idea comes from {#where-it-comes-from}

Partly from readers, because "gift" is good for business: it makes the reader special and the client dependent. Partly from the stories — the grandmother who saw things, the child who knew. And partly from the experience itself, which does feel like something arriving from outside. I understand why people reach for the word. I used it myself, early on.

What changed my mind was teaching. I sat friends down, people who'd come to make fun of me, and had them read for a stranger who told them nothing. Within a few tries, most of them landed something specific — a job, a name, a decision — and made the face. You know the face: the one that says *how did I do that*. If it were a gift, it wouldn't be that reproducible. It's a state, and states can be entered on purpose.

## What the "gift" actually is {#what-it-is}

Three things, all learnable.

**Lowered expectation.** The single most important one. Before every reading I tell myself I'm about to talk nonsense. It sounds like a joke; it's the mechanism. The moment you try to be right, you start constructing — and constructing is the opposite of reading. People with "the gift" are usually people who accidentally learned to stop trying.

**Attention to the picture.** The cards were drawn to be read. Colour, number, element, posture: [five lenses](/learn-tarot/) that tell you what a card is about before you've learned it. Most of what looks like intuition is fast pattern-reading of pictures, done by someone who's stopped second-guessing.

**Saying it out loud.** The "gift" is largely the willingness to say the sentence that came, without editing it into something safer. Everyone has the sentence. Most people swallow it.

## The test {#the-test}

Ten minutes. Find someone who'll let you read them and who'll tell you nothing — not their question, not their situation. Shuffle, draw one card. Say the sentence to yourself. Look at the card: dominant colour, number, house. Say what it shows, in plain words, to them. Let them tell you whether it landed.

Do it five times with five people. Count. If you land two or three specific things — not "you're going through a change", but a detail you couldn't have known — you have exactly the gift I have. If you land nothing, try five more with the sentence said harder. In my experience, almost nobody lands nothing twice.

## Why the sure ones worry me {#the-sure-ones}

The person who tells me "I've always known I was psychic" reads worse, on average, than the sceptic. Certainty about the gift becomes certainty about the reading, and certainty is where the misses hide. The sceptic says "that's probably wrong, but here's what I see" — and that's precisely the state the work needs.

I'm not saying nobody has unusual perception. I've had experiences I can't explain, and [I've written about some of them](/blog/is-tarot-evil/). I'm saying that none of it was required to read a card, and that treating it as required keeps out the people who'd be best at this: the ones who doubt themselves enough to check.

## No Chosen Ones {#no-chosen-ones}

It's a House principle and it's the reason the House teaches rather than reads. If reading required a gift, teaching it would be fraud. It doesn't, so I teach it, and I count what my students do the same way I count what I do. Their notebooks look like mine.
