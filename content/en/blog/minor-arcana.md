The 56 minor arcana aren’t 56 things to learn. They’re four suits, each with an element, crossed with ten numbers, plus four court ranks — eighteen ideas, and with them you can read any minor card. Below is the whole grid on one screen, every card linked to its own page, then how to read across it, down it, and where to start.

## Four suits, four elements {#suits}

Each suit belongs to one of the four elements, and the element tells you where things happen.

| Suit | Element | What it covers | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wands | Fire | creating, impulse, passion, the drive to act | Masculine |
| Cups | Water | emotions, relationships, hidden strengths | Feminine |
| Swords | Air | thinking, deciding, communicating | Masculine |
| Pentacles | Earth | matter, work, money, whatever has value for you | Feminine |

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) forges tools, warms and lights the night, and spreads fast when nobody masters it. [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) is a current: go with it and it gets you somewhere, though it can also close over your head; a neglected emotion seeps in slowly, like damp eating a wall. [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) has access to everything at once, which makes it the best decision-maker — until it obsesses, or turns into a tyrant. [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is tenacity and endurance, and stubbornness too; my notes add that wealth comes in more forms than gold.

**The suit tells you on which plane something happens; the number tells you at which stage.** If you’d like the suits on their own first, I go through them in [tarot suits explained](/blog/tarot-suits-explained/).

A cell is a handle, not a definition. It gives you somewhere to start, and the card in front of you, with its neighbours and the person you’re reading for, does the rest.

## Reading across a row: one stage, four planes {#across}

Take one number and read it through the four suits. It’s the fastest way I know to understand both the number and the suits, because only one thing changes at a time.

The Fives are the clearest. Five is conflict, trial, making do with what’s left. In my notes, the figures of the Five of Wands seem to fight, without any coordination. In Cups, the Five is the bleakest card I know: something lost, a dark mood, and nobody to share it with. The Five of Swords is an Air conflict, about decisions: not the same point of view. The Five of Pentacles is poverty in the snow, walking past the church where warmth could be found. A fight, a loss, a disagreement, a hard winter. Five’s advice is the same for all four: avoid pointless fights, and be honest with yourself and the situation.

The Eights work the same way. Eight is four raised a level: control, mastery, a doorway joining the material world to the unseen one. The Eight of Wands is the glass half full or half empty: staves flying or falling, and you decide which. The Eight of Cups moves on from what you’re already doing. In the Eight of Swords she is held by nothing but herself, every tool within reach, free to remove the blindfold whenever she chooses. In the Eight of Pentacles, mastery is earned at the workbench. Across the row, 8 asks one question: what are you really in control of?

The Tens close the cycle. Ten is the corridor between two rooms: a transition, freedom, sometimes short-lived. Keep the creative energy without overloading (Wands); happiness and family (Cups); rock bottom with the dawn behind (Swords); complete wealth at home, which will still be there if you leave the cocoon (Pentacles). My note on 10 says it best: people looking on may think you’ve been flattened, when the butterfly has long since left its chrysalis and flown.

Every other row reads the same way, and each number has its own page in the Library with its five cards, the major included.

## Reading down a column: one suit, one story {#down}

Read a suit from Ace to Ten and it tells a single story. Cups: a feeling opens, a bond forms, friends celebrate, the offers stop being noticed, something is lost, you take time for yourself, too many choices appear, you walk away, contentment settles, and a family is happy under the rainbow. A love story, a grief and a recovery in one suit.

Swords tell the mind’s story: a clear idea, a choice put off, a cut, a rest, a clash, a crossing to calmer water, a quiet exit, a trap you could step out of, a nightmare you’re coming out of, and rock bottom with the sky turning yellow behind it.

Wands tell the story of a project: the spark, the plan, the first ships coming back, a place to stand, the scuffle, the public victory, holding your ground, everything speeding up, the bandaged last stretch, and the moment you’re carrying the whole thing on your back. Pentacles tell the story of something you build: a coin to start with, two things juggled, work others recognise, holding on tight, a hard winter, knowing what things are worth, waiting for the harvest, the craft, gratitude, and a home with the whole family in it.

That’s why a spread full of one suit tells you the plane before it tells you anything else; I go through an example in [lots of Cups in a reading](/blog/lots-of-cups-in-a-reading/). Minors also make up most of any spread: they’re 56 of the 78 cards, so a ten-card spread holds about seven of them on average. The minors are where everyday life happens.

## The court cards: Page, Knight, Queen, King {#court-cards}

Each suit has four court cards. The rank says how someone acts; the suit says on what. In the convention I use, each rank also brings an element to its suit — the Page Earth, the Knight Air, the Queen Water, the King Fire — so the Queen of Cups is doubly Water. **A court card can be a person in your life or a state you’re in, and the person in front of you is the one who tells you which.** I go further in [court cards explained](/blog/court-cards-explained/).

| Rank | Wands | Cups | Swords | Pentacles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Page | [curiosity about a project](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-wands/) | [a surprise, an intuition](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-cups/) | [alert, curious, repeats what he heard](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-swords/) | [the student, first steps in a trade](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-pentacles/) |
| Knight | [an impulsive departure](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-wands/) | [the romantic offer](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-cups/) | [quick thinking, sometimes too quick](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-swords/) | [slow, reliable, the job done properly](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-pentacles/) |
| Queen | [warmth and confidence](/library/tarot-cards/queen-of-wands/) | [deep feeling, intuition](/library/tarot-cards/queen-of-cups/) | [clarity and boundaries](/library/tarot-cards/queen-of-swords/) | [practical care](/library/tarot-cards/queen-of-pentacles/) |
| King | [vision, leading](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-wands/) | [calm under the surface](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-cups/) | [the decision nobody else will make](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-swords/) | [making things prosper](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-pentacles/) |

The [Page](/library/numerology/page/) learns, watches, tries and carries the message. The [Knight](/library/numerology/knight/) leaves: he has learned what was needed, or thinks he has, and you can read each one by his horse, from the one that rears to the only one standing still. The [Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) doesn’t leave and doesn’t command: she holds, mastery from the inside. The [King](/library/numerology/king/) decides, then answers for it. The question to ask every King is: for whom?

The double element explains a lot of the keywords. The King of Swords is Fire of Air: a mind that decides, and decides fast, which is why he makes the call nobody else will. The Knight of Wands is Air of Fire, wind on a flame: the departure before the plan. The Page of Pentacles is Earth of Earth, the most concrete of the sixteen: the student who learns by doing. The Queen of Cups, Water of Water, is feeling held inside feeling.

## Reading a minor card in thirty seconds {#how-to-read}

Number first, suit second, drawing third. Say someone draws the Seven of Pentacles. Seven: reflection, patience, the harvest. Pentacles: Earth, work, something concrete. The drawing: a man leaning on his hoe, looking at what’s growing. Out loud, that becomes: “You’ve done the work. Now it needs time, and checking it every morning won’t make it grow faster.” Then you ask whether that lands — and if it doesn’t, you write it down as a miss.

Reversed, a minor stays in its cell: the same stage on the same plane, slowed down, blocked or overdone. The Eight of Wands that can’t take off; the Four of Pentacles gripping even tighter, or finally letting go. You don’t need a second grid.

When the minors sit next to majors, the [22 major arcana](/blog/major-arcana/) give the theme while the minors give the detail.

## Where to start {#where-to-start}

Start with the four Aces: one seed per element, and the easiest way to feel the difference between the suits. Then the Fives, because the contrast is sharpest there. Then one number a week: take its four cards out of the deck, put them side by side and say what changes from suit to suit. Leave the courts for last; they’re the cards people find hardest to place, and they make more sense once the suits feel familiar.

Each day, before you look anything up, describe what’s on the card, what you see and what you receive. Then check its page.
