The North Node and the South Node are two points in a birth chart, always exactly opposite each other, where the Moon’s path crosses the Sun’s. In my notes, they describe your natural strengths and weaknesses.

One thing first: they weren’t part of my chart test. What follows is a definition and a way of reading, not a result.

## What the lunar nodes are {#definition}

The Moon’s orbit is tilted compared with the Sun’s apparent path across the sky. The two circles cross at two points. Where the Moon crosses going north is the North Node; where it crosses going south is the South Node.

They aren’t planets or bodies. They’re calculated points, like [Lilith](/library/astrology/lilith/). And they’re always exactly opposite: same degree, opposite signs, opposite houses. If your North Node is at 12° Aries in the first house, your South Node is at 12° Libra in the seventh.

They have old names. Medieval astrologers in Europe called them the Dragon’s Head and the Dragon’s Tail; Indian astrology calls them Rahu and Ketu. They also matter in astronomy: an eclipse happens when a new or full Moon falls close to the Nodes. That’s why eclipses come on a pair of opposite signs that shifts every year and a half or so; [the eclipses of 2027](/blog/eclipses-2027/) mostly sit on the Leo–Aquarius axis, where the Nodes are that year.

The Nodes move backwards through the zodiac and go all the way round in about 18.6 years, roughly a year and a half per sign. They come back to their birth position around 18 or 19, again around 37, and around 56, which some astrologers watch as turning points.

## Natural strengths and weaknesses {#strengths-weaknesses}

The usual modern reading goes like this. The South Node is what comes naturally: what you already know how to do, your comfort zone. It’s a strength, and when you lean on it too much, it becomes a rut. The North Node is what doesn’t come naturally yet: awkward, unfamiliar, and where the growth is.

**The South Node is what you lean on; the North Node is what you’re learning to stand on.**

Say someone has the South Node in Virgo and the North Node in Pisces. Analysing, organising and fixing details come without effort; overdone, that turns into control and criticism. The Pisces side asks for the opposite muscles: trust, imagination, letting go.

You’ll also meet a karmic reading, common in what’s called evolutionary astrology: the South Node is what you bring from past lives, the North Node the direction of this one. If that framing means something to you, it’s available. The reading in my notes stays closer to the ground: natural strengths and weaknesses, in this life.

## The six axes, in one table {#axes}

Because the Nodes are always opposite, there are only six pairs of signs. Here they are, with the verbs from my notes.

| One Node in… | The other in… | The pull between |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aries, “I am” | Libra, “I balance” | Acting alone, and keeping the peace |
| Taurus, “I have” | Scorpio, “I desire” | Holding on, and letting go to transform |
| Gemini, “I think” | Sagittarius, “I see, I try” | Collecting facts, and finding the meaning |
| Cancer, “I feel” | Capricorn, “I use” | Home and feelings, and work and structure |
| Leo, “I will” | Aquarius, “I know” | Being seen yourself, and serving the group |
| Virgo, “I analyse” | Pisces, “I believe” | Detail and control, and trust |

Whichever side holds your South Node is the one that comes naturally; the North Node’s side is the one to grow into. Houses work the same way. A South Node in the tenth house and a North Node in the fourth: career comes easily, and home and private life are where the work is.

## How to find yours and read them {#find-yours}

Most chart sites list the North Node among the planets, sometimes as “Node”, “True Node” or “Mean Node”. The two calculations differ by a degree or so, which only matters at the very edge of a sign. The South Node isn’t always printed: it’s the same degree in the opposite sign and house. If you don’t have your chart yet, start with [what a birth chart is](/blog/what-is-a-birth-chart/).

1. Note your North Node’s sign and house.
2. Write the opposite sign and house next to it: that’s your South Node.
3. Ask three questions. What do I do without effort? Where does it go too far? What does the other side ask of me that I keep avoiding?
4. Tick or cross each answer, and be no softer on yourself than on anyone else.

## What I can and can’t say about them {#limits}

I have no number for the Nodes. [My own chart test](/blog/i-tested-my-birth-chart/) gave a hit rate between 30 and 40% overall, and far higher where Saturn, Lilith and Chiron were concerned (75 to 90%), but those figures come from other placements. I like the Nodes as a way of looking; I haven’t checked them.

Two more limits, said plainly. Everyone born within the same year and a half shares your Node signs, so the house is the more personal part. And they have no tarot card in the Golden Dawn correspondences this site uses.

Last thing: a South Node describes a habit, it doesn’t excuse one. [Astrology is not an excuse](/disclaimers/#astrology), in either direction.
