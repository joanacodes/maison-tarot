The honest answer to "how does tarot work?" is that I don't know. I've been asked it hundreds of times and I've stopped pretending. What I can do is walk through the four explanations that deserve a hearing, tell you which ones I've closed off at my own table, and say what's left when they're gone.

## Theory one: cold reading {#cold-reading}

Cold reading is the technique stage psychics use: you watch the person, you fish, you say something vague, you see what lands, you follow it. "I'm sensing a loss… a man… older…" and the sitter's face tells you when you're warm. It works, it's a skill, and a lot of what passes for tarot is cold reading with cards as a prop.

I take this one seriously because it's the easiest way to fool yourself. So I removed the material it needs. The person tells me nothing: no question, no context, sometimes no name. Over video I'm often reading a black square. When there is no face to read and nothing to fish from, cold reading has nothing to work with — and the readings still land. That doesn't disprove the theory for other readers. It rules it out for mine.

## Theory two: the Barnum effect {#barnum}

The Barnum effect is the tendency to accept vague, flattering statements as uniquely true of yourself. "You have a great deal of unused potential." "You sometimes doubt whether you've made the right decision." Horoscopes run on it. So do bad readings.

The defence is specificity, and a notebook. A Barnum statement fits everyone; a specific one fits one person. When I write down what I said and the person confirms "the job in logistics, the sister I don't talk to, the offer I turned down last month" — that isn't Barnum. When they nod at "you've been feeling stuck", it is, and I count it as nothing. The counting is what makes the difference visible. Without it, every reader believes they're specific.

## Theory three: synchronicity {#synchronicity}

Jung's idea: meaningful coincidence — the card that falls is the right one because the universe, or the unconscious, or something, arranges it so. Most readers believe some version of this, usually quietly.

I don't argue with it and I don't rely on it. I notice that it explains everything and predicts nothing, which is the mark of an idea you can't test. If it's true, wonderful; my practice doesn't change. If it's false, my practice doesn't change either. That's a theory I can carry without needing it.

## Theory four: the mirror {#mirror}

The cards are pictures. Seventy-eight of them, drawn in 1909 by [Pamela Colman Smith](/library/tarot-cards/) with enough figures, colours and situations to cover most of a human life. When a person looks at one, they see themselves in it — not because the card knows them, but because a good picture is a screen and we project. The reader's job is to say out loud what the person is already seeing and hasn't admitted.

This is the theory I find most useful, because it explains the part that matters most: why the reading is *about the person*. But it doesn't explain the thing that keeps happening — the specific detail I couldn't have known and the person didn't project. That's the part I can't account for.

## What's left {#whats-left}

Remove cold reading with the silence rule. Remove Barnum with the notebook. Hold synchronicity loosely, use the mirror daily. What remains is a residue of specific hits I can't explain, arriving at a rate I can state and misses I can name.

I read from inside that residue. I don't need a mechanism to do the work any more than you need to understand combustion to drive. What I need is to know the conditions under which it happens more often — and those are clear enough to teach: say nothing to me first, expect me to talk nonsense, let me stop when it misses, and count. That's [the method](/method/), and it's the same one the people I teach end up using, with the same result.

## Why "I don't know" is the right place to read from {#why-not-knowing}

A reader who knows how it works has to defend the theory. One who doesn't only has to be honest about the result. The second position is stronger, more comfortable, and — this surprised me — more accurate, because certainty about the mechanism tends to leak into certainty about the reading, and certainty is where the misses come from.

If you want to see the residue for yourself, [read for ten people who tell you nothing](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/), and write it down.
