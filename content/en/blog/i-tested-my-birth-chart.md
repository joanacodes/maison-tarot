During the pandemic I was bored, and I discovered astrology on YouTube. I want to be honest about my initial intention, because it's the funniest part of the story.

## The condescending start {#the-start}

I love understanding people — where they come from, how they get to the train of thought they have. So I put an astrology video on for that reason. But also with a mockery vibe on my face. The *your thing is nonsense but I'll listen, to understand why you are where you are* face. How condescending, right?

It slapped me. I tested it by reading my own birth chart, and it was too specific, too accurate. I wanted to know more, so I needed to test more, and then train more.

## How I tested {#how-i-tested}

I printed my chart and went through it like an invoice: every placement — Sun, Moon, rising, each planet in its sign and house — and next to each one, a tick or a cross. Tick if the description fitted me in a way I couldn't have written about anyone; cross if it was vague, wrong, or would fit my neighbour.

Then I did it for my family, my friends, and anonymous people online, with their permission and without telling me anything first — the same silence rule I'd later use for [three hundred tarot readings](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/). And then for the one friend I admired too much to want him to think I was crazy. I did it anyway. His chart was on point, and very harsh; he'd had a very hard life, and there was one line I said out loud that I still think about, because it was true and it crossed something. That's another story.

## The numbers {#the-numbers}

Overall, across everything I checked: **30 to 40% held.** The Sun sign, the rising sign, Venus, Mercury — the placements everyone reads first — scored about a third. That is not nothing, but it's close to what a well-written horoscope scores by being flattering and vague, so I don't count it as evidence of much.

Three bodies were different. **Saturn, Lilith and Chiron held 75 to 90% of the time** — for me, and then for the people I tested. Where Saturn sits, the person's hardest lesson sits. Where Chiron sits, the wound they keep circling. Lilith, the thing they were told to hide. I didn't expect that, and I checked it more than the rest because I didn't want it to be true.

Same caveat as always: a manual count, by me, on people I mostly knew. Not a study. A notebook.

## Why I keep it — as a lens {#why-i-keep-it}

Because a third is enough to be useful and not enough to be trusted. That's exactly the status of a lens: you look *through* it, you don't obey it.

On this site, astrology lives in the [Library](/library/astrology/) as one of the ways to read a card. Twelve major arcana carry a sign — the Emperor is Aries, the High Priestess is Cancer, Death is Scorpio — and when you draw one, the sign gives you a verb, an element and a tempo for free. The cards from 2 to 10 carry a planet in a sign. That's what I use it for. Nothing more.

## What I refuse to do with it {#what-i-refuse}

- **Let it explain a person.** "You're like that because you're a Scorpio" is not reading; it's an excuse with a vocabulary. [Astrology is not an excuse.](/disclaimers/#astrology)
- **Let it make a decision.** At a third, it would be wrong two times in three. I don't take odds like that for other people's lives.
- **Sell it as a gift.** Anyone can print a chart and check it. That's the whole point of publishing the test: so you don't have to believe me, you can do it.

## Try it {#try-it}

Print your chart. Go placement by placement. Tick or cross, and be as hard on the ticks as you'd be on a stranger's. If your Saturn, Lilith and Chiron score the way mine did, you'll understand why they each got [their own page](/library/astrology/). If they don't, tell me — I'd rather have your crosses than my ticks.
