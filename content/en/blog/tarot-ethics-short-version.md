Tarot associations publish codes of ethics that run to several pages. I've read a few. They're mostly fine, and mostly unread, and I suspect their length is the point: a long code lets you agree with it in general and ignore it in particular. Mine is short enough to fail.

## 1. Only people who asked {#consent}

I read for people who want to be read, in that moment, about themselves. Not about their ex, their boss, their mother, or the woman their husband texts. Reading about a third party who hasn't consented is the most common ethical breach in the trade, and it's done casually because the third party isn't in the room. They're still a person. If a sitter asks "what does he feel about me", I read what the sitter feels, and I say why.

## 2. Ask nothing first {#silence}

The person tells me nothing before I read. No question, no context. This is partly a method — it's how I know the reading isn't their own words fed back to them — but it's also ethics, because a reader who takes context can't help using it, and the sitter can't tell the difference. Silence protects both of us from the reading being a performance.

## 3. Refuse the subjects where wrong does damage {#refusals}

Health. Death. Pregnancy. An ongoing court case. Money decisions. Anything about curses. Not because the cards can't touch these, but because [I'm wrong sometimes](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/), and a wrong word on a diagnosis or a pregnancy isn't a miss, it's harm. A reader who'll read anything hasn't thought about their own error rate. [The list, with reasons, is public](/disclaimers/#refusals).

## 4. Say the misses out loud {#misses}

When a reading doesn't land, I say "that one's a miss" and I write it down. I don't reframe, I don't add a clarifier card, I don't tell the person it'll make sense later. Misses are the price of specificity, and hiding them is the beginning of every dishonest practice I know.

## 5. Nothing to sell afterwards {#no-upsell}

A reading at the House ends when it ends. No "book a deeper session", no clearing, no follow-up needed. This is easy for me because [I don't sell readings at all](/method/) — I teach — but the principle stands for anyone: if your reading has a funnel after it, the funnel is shaping the reading.

## 6. The card is not a reason {#not-authority}

I tell every sitter, at the end: don't act on this. Act on what you feel. A reading can show you something; it cannot decide for you, and a reader who lets it is taking a responsibility that isn't theirs. [Tarot is not an authority](/disclaimers/#readings). Neither am I.

## What isn't on the card {#not-on-card}

Confidentiality — obviously, so obviously it's not a rule but a floor. Not reading for minors. Not reading for someone in acute distress, because a reading is the wrong tool for that night and [a therapist's door is the right one](/blog/tarot-and-mental-health/). Not reading drunk. These are the things everyone agrees on; the six above are the ones readers quietly break.

## Why short {#why-short}

Because a rule you can recite at the table is a rule that stops you at the table. The long codes are for websites. The short one is for the moment the sitter asks "but will he come back?" and you feel the yes forming, because it would be so easy, and it would make them so happy, and it costs nothing tonight.

That moment is the whole of ethics in this work. Six lines, and one of them is for exactly that.
