No. Some of the people I’ve read for were openly doubtful, and in my opinion nobody is better placed to be read than a doubter. What a reading needs is not belief. It’s openness: someone who agrees to sit down, tells me nothing, and answers honestly at the end.

## Does tarot work if you don’t believe in it? {#short-answer}

In the way I read, belief doesn’t enter the process at any point. The person tells me nothing — not the question, not the situation. I draw, I say what comes, and they tell me afterwards whether it landed. Then it goes in my notebook, held or missed.

There’s no step in that loop where the person has to believe anything. There is a step where they have to be honest. That’s a much smaller ask, and a more useful one.

Say you sit down convinced it’s all nonsense. That costs the reading nothing. What would cost it something is telling me your question first, or nodding along to every sentence to be polite. Doubt leaves the test clean; politeness muddies it.

## Why doubtful people are the best people to read {#doubt}

I wrote it in my [guide to reading for beginners](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/) and I still think it: a doubtful sitter is the ideal sitter.

Here’s why. Someone who wants it to work helps too much. They nod, they fill in the gaps, they recognise themselves in anything that sounds warm. That’s the mechanism behind the Barnum effect, and I explain [why readings can feel accurate](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/) even when they aren’t. A doubtful person does the opposite. They wait, they check, and **when a doubtful person says “yes, that’s right”, the yes is worth something.**

The first time I ever read for someone I didn’t know proves the point. She rejected it on the spot, then got back in touch days later, shocked, because it had all happened. Her “no” during the reading didn’t stop anything. It made her message impossible to argue with.

## Belief and openness aren’t the same thing {#openness}

Openness, the way I use the word, means you’ve agreed to be read. You’re in the room, you’ve said yes, and you’re willing to listen to the end.

It matters because a person read without knowing it hasn’t opened up: you catch a scrap of them and inflate it into a portrait. It’s partly why I [leave alone anyone who hasn’t asked to be read](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/). My notes are careful about this: it’s what I’ve noticed and the theory I’ve built on it; nothing has been proven scientifically.

What tends to close a reading, in my experience, isn’t doubt. It’s fear. Sometimes I can’t see anything in the cards, and it’s either my own block — too much pressure to get it right — or the other person’s, because they’re afraid of what they might hear. When that happens I stop and we talk. I reassure them. A person who thinks the whole thing is nonsense can be easier to read than a person who is frightened of it.

## Does the reader have to believe? {#the-reader}

Not in the way people usually mean. I started reading on 26 December 2020, expecting to need training before anything landed. The first reading did. I hadn’t believed it would; it happened anyway.

Before every reading now, I assume that what I’m about to say may be rubbish. Doubt, in my experience, doesn’t get in the way; it helps. A reader who is sure of their gift tends to start rescuing readings instead of saying what comes.

What I have instead of belief is a count, misses included. Readers differ widely on what they believe, and I’ve set out [where I stand, and what to ask any reader instead](/blog/do-tarot-readers-believe/), separately. If you’re a sceptic wondering whether you could learn anyway, I came to astrology to laugh at it, and [how a doubter like me ended up reading](/blog/can-a-sceptic-learn-tarot/) might reassure you.

## When believing too much is the problem {#too-much-belief}

If I worry about one kind of sitter, it isn’t the doubtful one. It’s the person who believes every word: who leaves a reading with an instruction, quits a job because of one card, or comes back every week for the same answer.

The cards can raise something; the choice of what to do with it stays with you. I wrote about [the situations where I’d put the cards down](/blog/is-tarot-dangerous/), and too much belief is one of them.

## Come and test it as a doubter {#test-it}
