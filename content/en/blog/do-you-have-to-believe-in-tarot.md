No. Some of the people I’ve read for were openly doubtful, and in my opinion doubt is the best state a person can be in while they’re read. What a reading needs is not belief. It’s openness: someone who agrees to sit down, tells me nothing, and answers honestly at the end.

## Does tarot work if you don’t believe in it? {#short-answer}

In the way I read, belief doesn’t enter the process at any point. The person tells me nothing — not the question, not the situation. I draw, I say what comes, and they tell me afterwards whether it landed. Then it goes in my notebook, held or missed.

There’s no step in that loop where the person has to believe anything. There is a step where they have to be honest. That’s a much smaller ask, and a more useful one.

Say you sit down convinced it’s all nonsense. That costs the reading nothing. What would cost it something is telling me your question first, or nodding along to every sentence to be polite. Doubt leaves the test clean; politeness muddies it.

## Why doubtful people are the best people to read {#doubt}

I wrote it in my [guide to reading for beginners](/blog/how-to-read-tarot-beginners/) and I still think it: when someone is doubtful during a reading, they’re in the best state a person can be in.

Here’s why. Someone who wants it to work helps too much. They nod, they fill in the gaps, they recognise themselves in anything that sounds warm. That’s the mechanism behind the Barnum effect, and I explain [why readings can feel accurate](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/) even when they aren’t. A doubtful person does the opposite. They wait, they check, and **when a doubtful person says “yes, that’s right”, the yes is worth something.**

My very first reading for a stranger is a good example. At the end she told me it didn’t resonate; she wasn’t looking for a relationship at all. Three or four days later she texted me, shocked: everything had happened. Her “no” during the reading didn’t stop anything. It made her message, days later, impossible to argue with.

## Belief and openness aren’t the same thing {#openness}

Openness, the way I use the word, means you’ve agreed to be read. You’re in the room, you’ve said yes, and you’re willing to listen to the end.

It matters because someone who doesn’t know they’re being read isn’t open, and you only get half a sentence from them, then build a whole statement on it. That’s one of the reasons I don’t [read people who haven’t asked](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/). As I put it in my notes: this is only my experience and the hypothesis I make from it; nothing has been proven scientifically.

What tends to close a reading, in my experience, isn’t doubt. It’s fear. Sometimes I can’t see anything in the cards, and it’s either my own block — too much pressure to get it right — or the other person’s, because they’re afraid of what they might hear. When that happens I stop and we talk. I reassure them. A person who thinks the whole thing is nonsense can be easier to read than a person who is frightened of it.

## Does the reader have to believe? {#the-reader}

Not in the way people usually mean. I started reading on 26 December 2020, expecting to need months of training before anything landed. My first reading was accurate. I hadn’t believed it would be; it happened anyway.

Before every reading now, I tell myself: *I’m about to talk nonsense.* The lower I set the expectation, the more accurate it gets. A reader who is sure of their gift tends to start rescuing readings instead of saying what comes.

I don’t know how tarot works. I’ve laid out [the four explanations people give](/blog/how-does-tarot-work/), and I don’t need any of them to read. What I have instead of belief is a count, misses included. If you’re a sceptic wondering whether you could learn anyway, I came to astrology to laugh at it, and I tell that story in [can a sceptic learn tarot](/blog/can-a-sceptic-learn-tarot/).

## When believing too much is the problem {#too-much-belief}

If I worry about one kind of sitter, it isn’t the doubtful one. It’s the person who believes every word: who leaves a reading with an instruction, quits a job because of one card, or comes back every week for the same answer.

Tarot is not an authority. A reading can show you something; you decide what to do with it. I wrote about [the situations where I’d put the cards down](/blog/is-tarot-dangerous/), and too much belief is one of them.

## Come and test it as a doubter {#test-it}

Don’t take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself. Come to one of the [free events](/events/) with your arms folded if you like. Tell me nothing, listen to the end, and say honestly whether it landed. If it doesn’t, I’ll call it a miss in front of everyone, and you’ll have learned something too.
