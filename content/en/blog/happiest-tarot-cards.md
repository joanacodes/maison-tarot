The happiest cards in the deck are the Sun, the Star and the Ten of Cups, with the Nine of Pentacles close behind. They’re as good as they look. But every card has a plus and a minus — the happy ones too — and knowing their other side is what lets you enjoy them without gripping.

## Which tarot cards are the happiest? {#happiest}

Ask ten readers and you’ll get ten lists, but these four turn up on most of them, and they share out the elements neatly: one kind of happiness each.

| Card | Number | Element | The happiness | Its other side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Sun | 19 → 10 | Fire | joy you can show | joy that has to be shown |
| The Star | 17 → 8 | Air | hope you choose | hope poured out for everyone but you |
| Ten of Cups | 10 | Water | happiness shared at home | the family photo kept up for others |
| Nine of Pentacles | 9 | Earth | enjoying what you have | enjoying it behind a wall |

Look at the numbers. Three of the four sit at the end of a cycle: 9 is the finish line, [10 the transition between two stages](/library/numerology/number-10/). In my notes, 10 is the corridor between two rooms, and its minus is that it can be short-lived. Tarot puts happiness after an effort, not before it, and it doesn’t pretend it stays. **The happiest cards are real, and they are moments.**

## The Sun: joy you can show {#the-sun}

A naked child rides a white horse under a huge sun with a face, arms wide open, a red banner streaming beside them. Behind, a grey wall and a row of sunflowers. No saddle, no reins, no clothes: joy with nothing to hide and nothing to grip. Fire at its kindest — it warms, it lights, it gets you moving.

Its other side is the same light pointed outwards too long. Joy that has to be shown all the time gets tiring: the good mood performed instead of felt, the celebration posted before the work is done. And a 10 closes a chapter; it doesn’t freeze it. I’ve written about the Sun’s side at work in [the Sun in a career reading](/blog/sun-tarot-career/), and every lens is on [the Sun’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/).

## The Star: hope after a hard time {#the-star}

The Star comes straight after the Tower in the deck. A naked woman kneels by a pool under one large star and seven small ones, pouring from two jugs: one into the water, one onto the land. Nothing is falling any more. It’s an Air card, which tells you something surprising: its hope isn’t a mood, it’s chosen. The water is poured, measured, directed.

Its other side lives in the jugs. Sometimes she pours everything onto other people’s land and keeps nothing for her own. Sometimes she holds both jugs full and pours nothing, bracing for the next storm. And Air’s risk is to keep pouring an idea without checking whether anything grows. [The Star’s page](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/) reads each lens in detail.

## The Ten of Cups: happiness at home {#ten-of-cups}

A couple under a rainbow of ten cups, arms raised, while two children dance beside them; a river, green land, a house. My own note for this card is short: happiness, family. The detail I love is that the children aren’t looking at the rainbow. They’re happy without needing a sign.

Its other side: the family photo kept up for others, everyone smiling at the sky and nobody talking. Or waiting for the perfect picture before allowing yourself to enjoy the one you have. A rainbow is the most fleeting thing in the sky, which is a reason to live the moment, not to distrust it. Some people hope this card announces a baby; that’s a question for a doctor rather than a deck, and [the Ten of Cups’ page](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/) explains what the card can hold instead.

The Nine of Pentacles completes the four: a woman alone in her vineyard, a hooded falcon on her glove — in my notes, enjoying what comes, gratitude for everything you have. Its other side is comfort that hardens into “I don’t need anyone”.

## When a happy card comes up on a hard day {#hard-day}

It happens, and it can sting. You’re having a terrible week and the Sun lands on the table, grinning.

A happy card isn’t an order to be happy. That would be toxic positivity, and I’ve written about [the difference between that and a kind affirmation](/blog/affirmations-vs-toxic-positivity/). Often the card points at the small part that is working — a friend, a morning, a skill — or at what comes after the stretch you’re in. Sometimes it doesn’t fit at all, and then it’s a miss.

The other side of the happy cards is also in the hands that shuffle. If you catch yourself drawing again and again until the Sun comes out, the cards aren’t the point any more; reassurance is. Take a break from your cards now and then.

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Next time you draw one of these four, write two lines in your notebook: what the card gives, and what its other side would look like in your week. You’ll enjoy the first more for having named the second.
