The Ten of Swords is the worst drawing in the deck, and in a love reading it can feel like the worst news. Look again, though: behind the man on the ground, the sky is turning yellow. The card is an ending and a dawn in the same frame, and it never tells you what someone else will do.

## What the Ten of Swords shows, through its lenses {#lenses}

A man lies face down by the water, ten swords planted in his back, a red cloth across his body. Above him, a black sky fills half the card. Below the black, along the horizon, a band of yellow light; the water is calm, the hills are quiet.

Ten swords for one man is more than anyone needs. That excess is the first clue. This is an Air card — the mind, thoughts, words — and the mind is very good at stabbing you ten times with the same thought.

The number is [10](/library/numerology/number-10/): the transition between two stages, the corridor between two rooms. Freedom, change, breathing again — and its minus, that it tells you to start something else. My favourite line for the tens: **from the outside you might look knocked down, while in reality the butterfly is already far away, out of its chrysalis.**

The colours: black for reality and for emptying the mind; [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) for joy and optimism, coming back on the horizon; red for passion and action, laid over the body like a blanket. The astrology, if it speaks to you, is the Sun in Gemini: the centre of the stage, and the story told and retold. Optional. The energy is masculine — the visible, the tangible, what happens on stage — which is why a Ten of Swords ending rarely feels private. Everyone seems to know. Every lens is on [the card’s own page](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-swords/).

Lay the lenses over each other and a sentence comes before any book. Say someone draws this card and tells you nothing. An end, the mind going round, darkness and a strip of light. What you might say out loud: “Something is finished, and you’ve already understood it — what’s still hurting is the thinking about it.” Then you ask whether that lands, and let them correct you.

## What it means single, together, after a break-up {#relationship}

When you’re single, the Ten of Swords is often a pattern that has run its full course. The tenth date with the same kind of person, and the evening you say, out loud, “I’m done with this.” That sentence is a 10. It closes the room.

When you’re together, it can be a low point: a row that felt final, a way of relating that is exhausted, a phase that has nothing left to give. The card closes a cycle; it doesn’t say the couple ends with it. Plenty of relationships have a Ten of Swords in the middle, and a different kind of relationship afterwards. It’s also the last card of the Swords: after ten, the suit starts again at the Ace, one clean blade held up in a hand. Air ends a cycle so it can think freshly.

After a break-up, it’s the full stop you already know about. It can also be the overthinking: the ten versions of the last conversation, the ten things you should have said. Each one is a sword you put back in yourself.

## Reversed, and next to other cards {#reversed-combinations}

Reversed, it’s getting up slowly — one sword out, then another. Or refusing to accept the cycle has ended, pulling the swords out and pushing them back in. Sometimes it’s the drama in excess: the end announced ten times, to everyone.

Next to the Nine of Swords, it’s the 3 a.m. version, the worries that grow in the dark; I’ve written about [the Nine of Swords in love](/blog/nine-of-swords-tarot-love/). Next to the Six of Swords — a ferryman poling a boat with a cloaked woman and a child aboard — it’s leaving for calmer water, and accepting help to get there. Next to the Sun, the dawn on the horizon has fully risen.

## “Will they come back?” — and a kinder question to ask {#come-back}

This is the question the Ten of Swords so often comes with. “Will he love me again?” is one I hear in many forms, and I understand it completely. When you’re on the ground, you want to know whether the person who left is coming to help you up.

I don’t read on it, and here’s why. Whether someone comes back is their choice, still to be made, and they aren’t in the room. A card that said yes would keep you lying there, waiting; a card that said no would hurt for nothing, and might be wrong. I’ve written a whole piece on [what “will they come back?” does to you](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/).

The gentler question: “What is ending in me, and what is dawning?” Or, more practically: “What would I do this week if I stopped waiting?” Those you can answer. Some people find that the moment they ask them, they’re already sitting up.

And be as gentle with yourself as you would be with a friend lying on that shore. You wouldn’t tell them to get up and stop being dramatic. You’d sit down beside them and wait for the light with them.

## Where to go from here {#next-step}

Draw one card for the yellow band on the horizon: “What is dawning for me?” Write it down, and put it somewhere you’ll see it in the morning. If the heartbreak is still raw, read [the Three of Swords in love](/blog/three-of-swords-tarot-love/) first: it’s the earlier, rainier part of the same story.
