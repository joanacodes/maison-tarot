Shuffle tarot cards however your hands are comfortable: overhand, riffle, or spread face down on the table and swirled. There’s no wrong way, because the shuffle doesn’t charge the cards with anything. It mixes them, and it gives you a minute to settle.

The one thing your method really changes is whether reversed cards turn up.

## Is there a right way to shuffle tarot cards? {#right-way}

No. A deck is cardboard and ink. Cards aren’t magic, you are — and the shuffle is the small pause where that starts. While your hands are busy, your head gets a moment to step out of the conversation and let your own ideas and assumptions move aside, so that what you say next comes from the card and not from what you expected.

Face to face, the person shuffles, or lets me shuffle; both work. And most of my readings for strangers were online, on Discord servers and group calls, where the person never touched my cards at all. If a reading depended on whose hands shuffled, those readings would have had nothing to hold on to.

So you don’t need a ritual: no knocking on the deck, no cleansing between readings ([I sold sage in 2021, and nothing in it improved a reading](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/)). Whether you let other people shuffle your cards is a preference, not a rule — [letting others touch your deck](/blog/let-others-touch-tarot-cards/) has its own article.

**The shuffle is a pause, not a spell.**

## Three ways to shuffle, step by step {#three-ways}

### The overhand shuffle {#overhand}

1. Hold the deck on its long edge in one hand.
2. With the thumb of your other hand, pull small packets off the top into that hand, one after another, until the whole deck has crossed over.
3. Start again, as many times as you like.

It’s the gentlest on tarot cards, which are taller and stiffer than playing cards, and the easiest with small hands. Its limit: it moves cards in clumps, so neighbours stay neighbours for a while.

### The riffle {#riffle}

1. Split the deck into two halves, face down.
2. Lift the inner corners with your thumbs, bend them slightly, and let the two halves fall into each other.
3. Push them together.

It mixes fastest. It also bends the cards over time, so go gently, or riffle flat on the table, pushing the two halves into each other without lifting them.

### Washing on the table {#wash}

1. Spread the whole deck face down on a table.
2. With both palms flat, move the cards around in slow circles, like washing a window.
3. Gather them back into a pile.

It’s kind to the edges and mixes well. It needs space, it’s noisy on a café table, and it turns cards around.

After any of the three, some readers cut the deck into piles and restack it, or ask the person to cut. It’s a custom; keep it if it helps you start.

## How your shuffle decides whether you get reversed cards {#reversals}

A new deck comes out of the box with every card upright. The overhand shuffle and the riffle keep each card the way it was, so if you only use those, reversed cards never appear.

To get reversals, you need to turn cards around: wash them on the table, or rotate one half of the deck before you riffle. If you don’t read reversals, do the opposite — overhand or riffle, and turn back any card that comes out upside down. Neither choice is more serious than the other; it only has to be the same choice every time, so you know what an upside-down card means when you see one.

If a whole spread comes out reversed, check the shuffle before anything else: the deck has probably been turned over during a cut. [Every card reversed](/blog/all-cards-reversed-tarot/) goes into what it can mean after that. As for whether to read reversals at all, [my answer, and a shortcut](/blog/reversed-tarot-cards/), is the same card slowed down or in excess.

## When to stop, and which card to take {#when-to-stop}

Stop when you feel like stopping. There’s no number of shuffles to reach. Then take the top card, or fan the deck out and pick one, or let the person point.

Sometimes a card jumps out of the deck while you shuffle. Some readers read it as the first card; others put it back. Either is fine. Decide your rule before you shuffle, not after you’ve seen the card, or you’ll end up keeping only the jumpers you like.

And if the same card keeps turning up for days, look at your hands first. An overhand shuffle leaves clumps, and a card that sat near the top yesterday can still be near the top today. [Why the same card keeps coming up](/blog/same-tarot-card-keeps-coming-up/) covers the other explanations.

## Try three shuffles this week {#try-it}

For three days, draw one card each morning, with a different shuffle each day: overhand, riffle, washing. Don’t judge the card; notice which shuffle quiets your head fastest. That’s your shuffle. The [one-card daily pull](/blog/one-card-tarot-pull/) is the practice to build on it.
