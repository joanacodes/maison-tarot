“Baby witch” is the friendly name for someone who's just starting out in witchcraft. If that's you, start with your senses, a notebook and some time — not with a shopping basket.

I'm a tarot reader, not a witch, so I'll answer from my side of the table: what helps you start, and what I'd gently keep you from rushing into.

## What is a baby witch? {#what-is-a-baby-witch}

A baby witch is a beginner in witchcraft: someone who has just started reading about it, practising it, or calling themselves a witch. The term is everywhere on TikTok and Instagram, where people share their first altar, their first spell, their first seasonal ritual. It's affectionate, not mocking. Everyone was new once.

Witchcraft itself is a broad word for practices — spells, rituals, working with plants, seasons, the elements, with or without gods — that aim to change something in the world or in yourself through intention. For some people it's a religion; for others it's a craft without any belief; for others still it's mostly an aesthetic.

And a witch? My own definition, from my notes, is this: **a witch is a human being fully aware of their power, and not afraid to use it and be themselves.** By that definition, plenty of people are witches without a single candle in the house. I don't call myself one; I read cards. But it's a definition I'd happily hand to anyone starting out.

## Start with your senses, not a shopping list {#senses}

We were all born with our senses. To develop them: meditate, read, paint — any meditative art. Read tarot, read coffee cups, read a painting.

What sharpened my own senses wasn't anything I bought, and I say that as someone who once [sold sage and candles](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/). Eating fresher food helped. Living alone in my own flat was the real trigger.

None of this means tools are bad. If a candle helps you sit down and focus, light it. Only know that the noticing is yours, and nothing you can buy will do it for you.

## Three practices to start with {#three-practices}

These cost nothing, and they work whatever path you take next.

1. A few minutes of meditation. Meditation is the art of doing literally nothing: letting go of control, letting things flow through your mind. It's hard, and the people who say they can't meditate are the ones who need it most. What I noticed when I started: sharper decisions, less self-doubt. Here's [meditation for people who think they can't](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/).
2. A working notebook. Many witches keep a book of shadows: a personal notebook of their practice — what they did, when, and what happened. My tarot notebook is the same idea: what I said on one side, what was confirmed on the other. Write down what didn't work too: a miss teaches you more than a hit.
3. One card a day. Tarot isn't witchcraft — I've written about [the difference between the two](/blog/is-tarot-witchcraft/) — but a single card each morning is a good way to train your attention. Give it a full minute before you open any book.

If you'd like a fourth, follow the seasons. Many witches mark the Wheel of the Year, eight festivals spread through the year. One of them is Samhain, on the night of 31 October: the end of the harvest, the start of the dark half of the year, and a time to remember the dead. I've written about [Samhain and tarot](/blog/samhain-tarot/) if you want a quiet way in.

## Go slowly: start crescendo {#go-slowly}

“Don't go too high too fast, or you might not find your way back home. Start crescendo.” That's from my own notes, and it's the advice I'd give any beginner, in any practice.

Think of the Fool: light luggage, a lot of enthusiasm, free to create anywhere. His weakness is being naive — the little dog at his feet is trying to remind him where he's stepping. Be the Fool, and listen to the dog.

A few things I'd let wait:

- Calling spirits. With spirits, you don't know who you're calling.
- Curses and hexes. I don't read on curses, and if someone tells you you're cursed and asks for money to lift it, that's [the oldest con in the trade](/blog/lift-the-curse-scam/), not a diagnosis.
- Reading or working on people without asking them. You wouldn't want someone snooping in your bedroom; it's the same.
- Anyone who tells you they're one of the chosen few. Nobody is.

## Which witch are you? {#which-witch}

Sooner or later a quiz will ask whether you're a green witch, a kitchen witch, a hedge witch or a cosmic one. I've written [a glossary of the types of witches](/blog/types-of-witches/) so you can understand the words. But here's my answer to the question itself: which witch am I? You don't need a label. Just be yourself — that's witchy enough.

## Your first step this week {#next-step}

Pick one practice from the three above and keep it for a month, notebook open. If the cards are the one that pulls you, the [free path to learning tarot](/learn-tarot/) starts with a single card and five lenses — no starter kit required.
