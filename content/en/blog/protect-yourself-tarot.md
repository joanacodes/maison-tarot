You don’t need a ritual to read tarot safely. What you need is to know which emotions are yours and which belong to the person in front of you. If a ritual helps you do that, keep it: it’s a good habit, not a requirement.

## What people mean by protecting yourself {#what-it-means}

Protecting yourself, in tarot, means doing something before or after a reading so that what you pick up from the other person, or from anything else around the reading, doesn’t stay attached to you. The idea behind it: a reader opens up to read, and while they’re open, other people’s emotions or energy can come in.

The practices vary a lot. Some readers picture a bubble or a circle of white light around them before they shuffle. Some say a short intention or a prayer. Others keep a stone on the table, light a candle, draw a line of salt, pass smoke over themselves and the deck, or wash their hands when the reading is over.

Say a reader has a friend coming round to talk about a painful break-up. Before the doorbell rings, she lights a candle, sits for a minute and tells herself she’ll listen, help, and hand back what isn’t hers. That’s protecting yourself, in the sense most readers use the words.

## Do you need it? My answer {#do-you-need-it}

Not in the sense that a reading is dangerous without it. Plenty of readers would disagree with me, and they’re entitled to. I respect anyone whose ritual helps them feel steady. A gesture that tells your body “now I’m reading” and “now I’ve stopped” does something real, and that’s worth keeping.

My own path went the other way: I once sold sage and candles, and I’ve explained [why I stopped](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/) elsewhere.

**The protection I rely on isn’t an object; it’s knowing what’s mine.**

## Empathy: feeling without taking on {#empathy}

My notes split empathy in two: taking in and understanding what the other person feels, and not letting go of yours in the process. Both halves matter. Without the first, you read cards like a dictionary. Without the second, you go home with someone else’s grief.

The more empathic the reader, the likelier they are to feel someone else’s emotions as their own, even to show them: a lump in the throat that isn’t theirs, tears in the middle of someone else’s story. If that happens to you, it doesn’t mean you’re doing it wrong. It means you need a little distance. Listen to the emotion; don’t let it take over.

People sometimes call this being an empath: someone with a higher capacity to feel other people’s feelings. It can be trained, and it has a downside: if you don’t know yourself well, you can absorb an environment that isn’t good for you. ## Grounding, in practice {#grounding}

Grounding means coming back into your body and the room you’re in: your weight on the chair, your feet on the floor, your breathing, the light from the window. It’s the opposite of drifting so far into the reading that you forget where you are.

None of this needs equipment:

1. Before you shuffle, put both feet flat on the floor and take three slow breaths.
2. Name, silently, one feeling you’re bringing in yourself: tired, worried, cheerful.
3. During the reading, if something heavy rises, notice where it sits in your body, and ask whether it was there before the person sat down.
4. At the end, stand up, drink a glass of water, open a window.
5. Before the next person, check again: is what I’m feeling mine?

If sitting still with your breath feels impossible, that’s a skill too, and it can be learned; I wrote about [meditation for people who think they can’t](/blog/meditation-for-beginners/). Go slowly with all of it, as with developing your senses: small steps keep the way back in sight.

## Staying calm: the emotions aren’t yours {#staying-calm}

This is the part that protects both of you. If someone’s fear rises in you while you read, the fear is real, but it’s theirs. You don’t need to fix it, fight it or carry it. Stay calm, slow down, and say what you see.

A calm reader is also a kinder one. When you flinch at a card, the person reads your face before they hear your words. When you stay steady, they can hear a hard card without hearing doom in it.

Protection works in the other direction too. Ask permission before you read anyone: reading someone who hasn’t agreed is like walking into their bedroom and opening the drawers. The basics are in [how to read tarot for someone else](/blog/read-tarot-for-someone-else/).

And if what worries you is spirits rather than emotions: most of what a reader feels during a reading is human, and comes from the person across the table. Reading tarot doesn’t require calling on anyone, and I don’t use the cards that way, partly because nobody calling on spirits can know who will turn up.

## What to try at your next reading {#next-step}

Pick one small gesture to open and one to close: feet on the floor at the start, a glass of water at the end. Then notice, for a week, how you feel after reading.
