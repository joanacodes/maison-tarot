The four cards that make people flinch are Death, the Tower, the Devil and the Ten of Swords. None of them announces a death, a disaster or a curse. Every card has a plus and a minus; these four wear their minus on the outside — and read through their lenses, they’re some of the most useful cards in the deck.

## Why some tarot cards look so frightening {#why-frightening}

Pamela Colman Smith drew scenes, and she drew these four dramatically: a skeleton on horseback, a tower struck by lightning, two people in chains, a man with ten blades in his back. Drama makes a card memorable. It also makes it quick to misread, and films haven’t helped — I go through what [pop culture gets wrong about the Death card](/blog/death-card-pop-culture/) elsewhere.

Then there’s the colour. Black dominates all four, and in my grid [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) is absence, void, the negative thought — but also emptying the mind, and reality. A black card asks you to look at something as it is.

Fear also makes us read too fast. The eye stops at the skeleton and never reaches the sun on the horizon; it stops at the lightning and never notices that the crown went first. Slow down, and describe the whole card before you interpret anything.

**A frightening drawing is not a frightening prediction.** When one of these makes your stomach drop, the fear usually tells you something about what you brought with you. It’s worth asking what you were afraid of before you drew it.

## Death: an ending that clears the ground {#death}

*13 → 1 + 3 = 4 · Water · Scorpio · feminine.* A skeleton in black armour rides a white horse and carries a black banner with a white rose. A king lies fallen, a bishop faces the rider, a child looks up — and on the horizon, a sun shines between two towers.

In my notes, [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/) is not necessarily bad and not about dying. It’s linked to the harvest and to starting again: someone without a goal who wants to make their life fertile again. Thirteen reduces to four, the number of structure, so the card clears one structure to make room for another. Water and Scorpio make it an emotional change, felt deep down, not a paperwork one — and Scorpio’s verb is “I desire”: what you want next starts growing in that clearing.

A card doesn’t announce a death or an illness. If you’re worried about someone’s health, that’s one for a doctor, which is why I don’t read on it. What Death does talk about is the end of a role, a habit, a way of being together — in love, the end of how you were with someone, not necessarily of the relationship ([Death in love](/blog/death-tarot-love/)); at work, a chapter closing ([Death in career](/blog/death-tarot-career/)). Reversed, or surrounded by heavy cards, it’s the ending being held back: clinging to what’s already over. In the Marseille deck the card doesn’t even carry a name, and [why it has none](/blog/death-card-no-name/) is a story of its own.

## The Tower: what was built on a crack comes down {#the-tower}

*16 → 1 + 6 = 7 · Fire · Mars · masculine.* Lightning strikes a tower on a rock, knocks its crown-shaped top off; flames come out of the windows and two people fall.

[The Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/) is sudden change. Look at what the lightning hits first: the crown, the thing placed on top. Often it’s a pretence, a false premise, something that looked solid and wasn’t. Fire and Mars give it speed — it doesn’t ask permission.

Sixteen reduces to seven, and seven’s advice is the one to hold here: everything is temporary, and your situation, good or bad, will change. In the deck the Tower is followed by the Star, and I don’t think that’s an accident. The useful question isn’t “why is this happening to me?” but “what was cracked, and do I want to rebuild the same tower on the same rock?” More on that in [the Tower in love](/blog/tower-tarot-love/) and [the Tower at work](/blog/tower-tarot-career/).

Say someone tells me nothing and draws the Tower next to the Eight of Pentacles. Seven, then eight; Fire, then Earth: after the collapse, rebuilding by hand. I’d say, “Something came down that you’d built on the wrong ground, and you’re already rebuilding — slower, and better.” Then I’d ask whether that lands.

## The Devil: chains you could lift off {#the-devil}

*15 → 1 + 5 = 6 · Earth · Capricorn · masculine.* A horned figure with bat wings crouches on a black block. A naked man and woman are chained to it, wearing small horns and tails of their own.

Fifteen reduces to six, the number of the Lovers, and the drawing echoes theirs: two people, a larger figure above. [The Devil](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/) is a bond that has turned into attachment — to a job, a person, a habit, money, a screen. Earth and Capricorn make it material and stubborn. Capricorn’s verb is “I use” — and here, what you use has started using you.

Now look at the chains. They hang loosely around the necks; the two could lift them off. That’s the card’s plus, and it’s a big one: it shows you the chain. The Devil isn’t evil and isn’t a curse; it’s a picture of what holds you. The cards themselves can hold you too. If the Devil came up for someone who had been drawing every morning for months, I wouldn’t read a curse; I’d ask, gently, whether the deck had become one of the chains. Take a break from your cards now and then. In relationships it’s [attachment, and what to look at](/blog/devil-tarot-love/); at work, [a job that holds you, and how to loosen it](/blog/devil-tarot-career/).

## The Ten of Swords: rock bottom, and the dawn behind it {#ten-of-swords}

*10 · Air · Sun in Gemini · masculine.* A man lies face down under a black sky, ten swords in his back. Behind him, the water is calm, and the horizon is turning yellow.

Ten is the transition between two stages, the corridor between two rooms. [The Ten of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-swords/) is the last numbered card of its suit: the worst is done, it can’t go further. Air is the mind, and the astrology agrees: Sun in Gemini, the sign whose verb is “I think”. The drawing is exactly what the mind does at 3 a.m. — ten swords where one would have been enough. It’s over the top, and I think that’s on purpose.

Then the colours: black above, yellow at the horizon. In my grid, yellow is joy and optimism. The dawn is already in the picture. Reversed, the card is the getting up — slowly, swords still in, but on your feet — or, sometimes, staying down longer than you need to. In love it’s [the worst drawing in the deck, and the dawn behind it](/blog/ten-of-swords-tarot-love/); at work, [rock bottom and what comes after](/blog/ten-of-swords-tarot-career/).

## The other cards people fear {#other-cards}

A few more make people nervous, and each has its other side. The Three of Swords is heartbreak — and, in my notes, also a blood pact: three blades, one heart, a promise sealed, sometimes in pain. The [Nine of Swords](/blog/nine-of-swords-tarot-love/) is someone sitting up in bed at night; I read it as coming out of a nightmare, a long troubled period, with protection above you. The [Five of Cups](/blog/five-of-cups-tarot-love/) is for me the heaviest card in the deck — loss, and being alone with it — yet two cups are still standing behind the figure. The [Eight of Swords](/blog/eight-of-swords-tarot-love/) is a prisoner of herself while she holds every tool: she can take the blindfold off. And the Moon is uncertainty, where the danger is taking a fear for a fact.

## When a hard card comes up for you {#when-it-comes-up}

What I do, and what I’d suggest:

1. Breathe, and read the lenses before the picture: number, element, colour.
2. Ask three questions: what is ending, what is being cleared, what is being shown to me?
3. Look at the neighbours. Death next to the Star is not Death next to the Five of Cups.
4. If it lands too hard, stop and say so — I wrote about [what to say when a reading lands too hard](/blog/reading-lands-too-hard/).
5. Don’t redraw to get a nicer card. The first one was the reading.

If you’re reading for someone else, say the card’s plus first and its minus second, in plain words. I go further in [how to say hard things kindly](/blog/bad-news-tarot-reading/).

And the honest limit, said calmly: a frightening message can be wrong even when it seems to come true. After my closest friend died of cancer, I was so angry that I asked silently “who’s next?” and heard a man’s voice give another friend’s name and “is going to have an accident”. Two weeks later, that friend’s car was hit by a truck. My friend is alive and well today. The information was wrong in what mattered most. I tell it in full in [why a reading can be wrong](/blog/why-a-reading-can-be-wrong/).

Take the four cards out of your deck tonight, lay them in a row, and say each one’s plus out loud. Then, to be fair, do the same with [the happiest cards](/blog/happiest-tarot-cards/) — they have a minus too.
