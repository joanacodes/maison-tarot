I charge for lessons, and I give readings away. A session or a workshop costs money; a reading at one of my events doesn’t, and I have never sold one. Here’s exactly what sits on each side of that line, why I drew it there, and what I think of readers who draw it somewhere else.

## What I charge for {#what-i-charge-for}

Two things, both of them teaching.

A session is one hour by video, one-to-one, in your time zone. You tell me nothing, we draw, you read first, I read after you, and we compare. When it doesn’t fit, we look at why. You leave with the lenses we used, in writing, and with no subscription or follow-up you’re obliged to book.

Each workshop takes one lens — colours, numbers, or elements and energies — and stays with it for three hours, never with more than twelve people. Cards come out in the first minutes, before I’ve explained a thing, and you finish by reading a card you’ve never studied, out loud, for the others. There are no levels and no certificate.

Prices, tax included, are on [the sessions page](/sessions/) and [the workshops page](/workshops/). I’d rather you read them there than in an article that might go out of date.

## What I give away {#what-i-give-away}

Readings. They happen at events: online evenings where a few people are drawn at random and read one after another, having told me nothing; meetups in person; webinars on one lens with questions at the end.

It has been that way from the start. The [blind readings I counted](/blog/does-tarot-work-300-readings/) were all free, because I wanted to know whether reading worked, not to earn from it.

**A reading at Maison Tarot is something you’re given, not something you’re sold.**

## Why I don’t sell readings {#why-not-readings}

Because of what a price does to the reader. To me, in other words.

A practice that lives on readings needs people to come back for readings. That isn’t wickedness; it’s arithmetic, and it shapes small things: how a session ends, whether a refused subject starts to look less dangerous in a slow month, whether a miss gets said out loud. When a reading is free, a miss costs me a line in my notebook. When it’s paid, there’s a pull to rescue it, and I’d rather not find out how strong that pull is.

Then there’s the person in front of me. When someone brings the same question month after month, what they usually need is [a break from readings](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), not another one, and that’s much easier to say when nobody is paying me to do the opposite.

I’ve also tried selling the objects around tarot: sage, candles and “channelled” cards, in 2021. I stopped, for reasons I give in [do you need to cleanse your deck](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/).

A lesson works the other way round. Its job is to make you need me less. If you leave a workshop reading a card you’d never seen, it worked. Getting you to that point is something I’m comfortable charging for.

## Should tarot readers charge money? {#should-readers-charge}

Yes, many can, and honestly. Reading is work: hours of practice behind it, full attention during it, and real tiredness after, because interpreting takes energy. A reader who asks to be paid for that hour is asking for what any other professional asks.

What I’d look at isn’t whether there’s a price, but its shape. A stated price for a stated length of time, and no extras afterwards, is a fair exchange. A “second session” needed because the first ended on a cliffhanger rewards the reader for keeping you uncertain.

So my choice is mine, not a rule for everyone. I sell lessons because that’s the arrangement where my interest and yours point the same way. A reader who charges a fair price, names their misses and steps back from health, death and money questions is doing honest work, and I’d send people to them.

## Should tarot be free? {#should-tarot-be-free}

Much of it already is, here. The Library, with the colours, numbers, elements, energies and every card, costs nothing. So does every article on this blog, my method and my numbers included. You can learn to read without paying me anything.

Free isn’t automatically clean, though. A free reading can be the bait at the top of a funnel, and what matters is what comes after it. At my events, what comes after is nothing: the reading ends, and you go home with it.

What I charge for is my time, live, with you. That seems a fair line to me. The bigger question, what happens to a practice when it becomes a product on platforms, in apps and in subscriptions, deserves more than a paragraph, so I gave it a full piece: [should tarot be free?](/blog/tarot-and-capitalism/)

## Where to start {#where-to-start}

For a reading, the [free events](/events/) are the place: you tell me nothing, and you see how I work before you ever spend anything. If you’d rather learn, pick whichever lens draws you and book a workshop, or a session if you prefer to work one-to-one.
