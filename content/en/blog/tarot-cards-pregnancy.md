I don’t read on pregnancy or fertility — not whether, not when, not a boy or a girl. It isn’t because the question is wrong; it’s one of the most human questions there is. It’s because a card can’t carry it safely, and there’s something else I’d much rather look at with you.

## Why people ask the cards about pregnancy {#why-people-ask}

Mostly, people ask because they’re waiting. Months of trying. A test in two days. A hope nobody else knows about yet. A fear that came after a loss. Sometimes a pregnancy that wasn’t planned, and a decision that feels too big.

Waiting is hard in a particular way: nothing you do seems to change the outcome, and every day looks the same as the one before. When every other door is shut, a deck of cards can look like a door. I understand that pull completely.

**Wanting an answer when nobody can give you one isn’t foolish; it’s what hope and worry do.**

## Why the cards aren’t the right tool for this question {#why-not}

My readings miss sometimes, and I keep count. On most subjects a miss costs an evening of doubt and a line in my notebook. Here, a wrong “yes” can cost months of hope, and a wrong “no” can cost a grief that didn’t need to happen. That’s too much weight for a card to carry.

“When will I be pregnant?” is also a timing question in its purest form, and [tarot is weakest of all on dates](/blog/tarot-timing/). A reading can describe a mood or a movement; it can’t give you a month.

And a card can’t see a body. The cards speak in pictures, and a picture of growth, a garden or a family can describe a project, a home, a new start with yourself. Reading a pregnancy into it is the reader’s projection. You’ll find lists online of cards said to announce one; I don’t keep such a list, for the same reason. A test, a scan and a doctor can see what a deck can’t. I set out the wider reasoning — health, pregnancy and death together — in [why careful readers step back from these questions](/blog/health-pregnancy-death-tarot/).

## What a reading can hold instead {#what-a-reading-can-hold}

A reading can hold the waiting. Not the outcome — you. How you’re carrying this, what’s taking up all the room in your head, what you need from the people around you, what you could set down for a week.

Imagine a woman who keeps her situation to herself and draws the Nine of Swords: someone awake and upright in bed in the dark. If, at the end, she tells me she has been trying for a year, I don’t turn the card into a result. I read the nights — the 3 a.m. thoughts — and we talk about what could make them a little lighter.

Some gentler questions to bring to the cards, or to a notebook:

1. What helps me through this waiting?
2. What do I need from the people close to me right now, and have I said it?
3. What am I carrying alone that I could share?
4. What is mine, outside of this, that I want to keep looking after?

I have more of these in [kinder questions to ask tarot](/blog/questions-to-avoid-asking-tarot/). And if you’ve been drawing cards about it every morning, I’d gently suggest putting the deck in a drawer for a while: the waiting is heavy enough without a daily verdict.

## Who can really help {#who-to-see}

For your body: a pharmacy test or a blood test answers “am I pregnant?” better than any deck. A GP, a midwife, a gynaecologist or a fertility clinic can talk you through what’s happening, what’s normal and what can be done.

For the worry: a counsellor or therapist, ideally one used to working with fertility and pregnancy, and support groups where people are in the same wait as you. There is no shame in needing someone to talk to while your life is on hold.

After a loss, the grief is real even if very few people knew. I wrote about [what the cards can hold in grief, and when you need a person](/blog/tarot-for-grief/); it applies here too.

## If someone asks you, as a reader {#if-you-read}

If you read for friends, this question will come. A sentence that works: “I don’t read on that, because a wrong word there could stay with you for months. What I’d love to do is look at how you’re doing while you wait.” Most people hear the care in it.

Since nobody tells me their question before I start, the subject sometimes surfaces in the middle of a reading instead. When it does, I say so kindly, set that thread aside, and go back to what the cards can hold. I go through how to do that without closing the door on someone in [knowing when to point someone elsewhere](/blog/client-boundaries-tarot/).

This is my practice, not a law for every reader. But it’s the reason it sits on my list of [what I don’t read](/disclaimers/#refusals), next to health, death and court cases.

## One small thing for tonight {#tonight}

If you’re in the waiting right now, pick one of the four questions above and answer it in three lines, no cards needed. It’s a small thing, and it’s yours. And if the worry has started to take up every day, that’s the moment to find someone to talk to — I explain where that door is in [tarot and mental health](/blog/tarot-and-mental-health/).
