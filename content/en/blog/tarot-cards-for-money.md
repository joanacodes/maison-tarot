The Pentacles are the money suit, but they talk about value more than cash — in my notes, not all riches are gold. I read them gladly. What I don’t read on is money decisions: whether to invest, sign, borrow or resign stays yours, and I’ll explain why without wagging a finger.

## Which tarot cards talk about money {#money-cards}

Pentacles belong to [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): matter, prosperity, whatever has value for you. Read through their numbers, they tell a whole story about security.

The Ace of Pentacles is a coin held out over a garden — something real to build on. The Four clutches his coin with both arms: security that won’t let go. The Five walks through the snow past a lit window: hardship, with warmth closer than it looks. The Six holds scales: knowing value, giving and receiving fairly. The Nine stands in her vineyard: enjoying what’s there, gratitude for what you have. The Ten gathers a whole family under an archway: complete wealth at home.

A few majors join them. The Empress feeds — abundance, things growing. The Wheel of Fortune, in my notes, says it’s up to you to turn it: create your own luck. The Devil, an Earth card, shows the chain — and money can be one. The King of Pentacles, among his grapes and bulls, makes things prosper. The rest of the suit is laid out in the [minor arcana grid](/blog/minor-arcana/).

**The Pentacles describe your relationship with value; they don’t predict your bank balance.**

## Not all riches are gold {#not-all-riches}

In my notes, Earth is whatever has value *for you* — money, or something sentimental. It’s tenacity and endurance, and stubbornness too. Its archetype is the artist’s manager working in the shadow, steady and reliable, who sometimes won’t listen because his plan “brings in more”.

So a Pentacle card isn’t always about a payslip. The [Ten of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-pentacles/) can be a grandmother’s house full of people on a Sunday; it may not last, and it will still be there if you leave the cocoon. A Nine can be a garden you finally have time for. Even the Five, the hardest card of the suit, is in my notes poverty and snow *and* the church window where warmth could be found: at its lowest, Earth still points to the help nearby.

Say someone draws the Four of Pentacles. Before I talk about money, I ask myself what this man is holding so tight. It might be savings. It might be a job he’s afraid to leave, or his time, or his privacy. The person in front of me will know which.

## Why your money decisions stay yours {#decisions-stay-yours}

People ask about money because it keeps them up at night: the rent, a debt, a job offer, an investment a friend swears by. That worry is real and I don’t take it lightly.

But a card can’t see a contract, an interest rate or a market. By my own count about one reading in ten misses, which is fine when we’re talking about how you feel and not fine when it’s your savings. A “yes” from the deck can also feel like permission, and that’s a weight a card shouldn’t carry — it’s why money decisions are on my list of [what I don’t read](/disclaimers/#refusals). I read; you decide.

For the decision itself: an accountant, an independent financial adviser, your bank, or a free debt advice service if things are tight. For the worry around it, the cards can help with gentler questions:

1. What does security mean to me right now?
2. What am I afraid of losing?
3. Where does my effort go, and does it come back to me?
4. What would “enough” look like?

I go further in [using tarot for decisions without letting it decide](/blog/tarot-for-decision-making/).

## Reading the Pentacles in a spread {#in-a-spread}

Several Pentacles in one spread say the situation is about matter: work, body, home, money, time. Reversed, a Pentacle is the same card slowed down or in excess — the Four that won’t let go of anything, the Eight that works without ever stopping.

The neighbours change the subject. Next to Cups, value meets feeling: what something is worth to you, not on the market — an inheritance, a gift, a family home. Next to Swords, it’s a decision about something concrete, which is exactly where I hand the decision back. Next to Wands, it’s an idea looking for a budget.

Say the Eight of Pentacles comes up next to the Ten of Wands: skill and effort, then a load carried too far. I’d say, “You’re good at what you do, and you’re carrying more of it than you should.” For questions about work, the [career tarot spread](/blog/career-tarot-spread/) is built to read a situation without making the choice for you.

## Try it this week {#try-it}

Take the fourteen Pentacles out of your deck and draw one each morning with a single question: what has value for me today? Write the answer in a line. By the end of the week you’ll notice how few of the answers were about money.
