Tarot readings seem inconsistent because almost everything around the cards changes from one draw to the next: you, the question, the way you read, and the situation itself. The cards are the only part that stays the same. And sometimes one of the two readings is a miss, which is worth telling apart from the rest.

## You change between two readings {#you-change}

A reading reads the person at the moment it happens: their mood, what they hope for, what they’re afraid of. The Sunday-afternoon you and the Thursday-night you aren’t asking the same question, even with the same words. Cards aren’t magic, you are — the reading comes from you, not from the cardboard — so when you change, the reading changes with you.

Say someone asks about their small business on a Sunday and draws the Six of Wands: a rider crowned with a wreath, a crowd raising staffs around him. Success, recognition. On Thursday, same question, they draw the Five of Pentacles: two figures limping through the snow past a lit church window.

A contradiction? Between Sunday and Thursday, they lost their biggest client. Sunday’s reading read Sunday. Thursday’s read Thursday, including the warmth in the window that the two figures don’t see. And the two cards share a thread if you read the [numbers](/library/numerology/): six is the machine launched and running fast, five is conflict and making do with what’s left. Two stages of the same cycle, a few days apart.

**Two readings that disagree are often two true pictures of two different days.**

## The question changes {#the-question}

A vague question lets each draw answer a different part of it. Ask “What about my love life?” on Monday and the card talks about your ex; ask again on Wednesday and it talks about how you see yourself. Both may be right. They’re answers to two different questions hiding inside one.

The fix is practical. Write the question down before you shuffle, in the present tense, small enough to fit in one sentence: “What do I need to see about how I date right now?” Then, if you ask again, you know you’re asking the same thing — though if you’re asking the same thing three times in a week, read [what repeat readings do to you](/blog/ask-tarot-the-same-question-twice/) first.

## Your reading changes {#your-reading}

The same picture can read differently from one person to another, and from one day to the next. Look at the [Eight of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-wands/): eight staffs crossing the sky. Are they flying, or falling? On a good morning you’ll say flying; on a tired evening, falling. It’s the glass half full or half empty, and the card holds both.

That isn’t inconsistency; it’s interpretation. You can make it steadier. The lenses don’t change with your mood: the number of a card, its element, its dominant colours are the same on Sunday and on Thursday. Start every reading with them, and the part that moves with your mood gets smaller.

## The situation changes {#the-situation}

A reading isn’t a photograph of a fixed future. It reads the present and the direction things are leaning, and people act on what they hear. Someone who hears “you’re not looking at the offer in front of you” may go and look at it — and the next reading will be different because their life is.

Take the business owner from Sunday and Thursday. Say that after Thursday they pick up the phone and call two former clients. A reading the following week would be reading a different situation, because they made it one. Nothing about that is inconsistent; it’s the reading doing its job and the person doing theirs. I explain what I read instead of the future in [can tarot predict the future?](/blog/can-tarot-predict-the-future/) My short answer: the best way to make a future you’ll enjoy is to anchor yourself in the present.

## Sometimes one of them is a miss {#a-miss}

By my own count, about one reading in ten misses. If two readings contradict each other and one of them doesn’t land at all, it may be wrong. Say so, write it down, and don’t stitch both into a single story to save them.

One way to tell a miss from a change: a change has a reason you can name — something happened between the two days. A miss has none. It didn’t fit on the day you drew it, and it still doesn’t. I go through what to do in [when a tarot reading is wrong](/blog/tarot-reading-wrong/).

## How to compare two readings that disagree {#compare}

1. Put them side by side, each with its date and its exact question.
2. Write down what changed in your life between the two.
3. Look for what they share: the same number, the same element, the same colour.
4. Keep what both say. Let the rest go.
5. If neither lands, call it a miss and stop drawing on that question for a while.

Done honestly, this turns a contradiction into information. And if it’s two different readers who disagree rather than two days, that’s a slightly different question, which I answer in [can two readers get different answers from the same cards?](/blog/two-readers-different-answers/)
