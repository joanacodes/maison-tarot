No. When readings start feeling flat, muddled or repetitive, it's almost always the reader who's tired, and the fix is a break rather than a ritual.

## What people mean by a “tired” deck {#what-people-mean}

A tired deck, in the way readers use the phrase, is a deck that seems to have stopped speaking. Readings feel flat, the same cards keep turning up, nothing lands. Many readers believe a deck absorbs something from the readings it's used for — other people's worries, heavy questions — and needs clearing before it reads well again.

Cleansing a deck means doing something to clear it: passing it through smoke, leaving it on a windowsill overnight, resting a stone on top, knocking on it, putting the cards back in their original order one by one. Readers choose the one that fits what they believe, and some do it after every reading.

Say you've read for six friends in one evening, and the last two readings felt like reciting from a book. That's the feeling people describe. It's real, and anyone who has read for long enough knows it. Where I differ is on where it lives.

## What's really happening {#what-is-happening}

What gets tired is your attention. Making sense of a card uses you up, and whatever one reading drains, the next one pays for. Now and then I stop once I've done two hours of readings, as a precaution — not because the deck has had enough, but because I have.

**The cardboard is the same as yesterday. You aren't.**

There are other causes too. Reading too often, so every card starts to blur into the last one. Asking the same question again and again, so the readings answer your anxiety rather than your situation. Carrying a worry so heavy that every card bends towards it. If [the same card keeps coming up](/blog/same-tarot-card-keeps-coming-up/), or [your readings seem to contradict each other](/blog/inconsistent-tarot-readings/), it's worth looking at the reader before looking at the deck.

If you want to check, there's a fair test. Hand the same deck to a friend who reads and who is rested, and let them read for you. If their reading feels alive, the cards were never the problem.

## Why cleansing feels like it works {#cleansing}

I don't think anything was ever stuck in the cards, and I've [sold the sage that's meant to clear them](/blog/cleanse-tarot-deck/).

That doesn't make people who cleanse their decks foolish, and I'm not going to tell them to stop. A ritual does something I'd keep: it makes you pause. Putting seventy-eight cards back in order is ten minutes when you're not reading, and that pause is often exactly what was needed. The ritual works. I think it works on you, not on the cards.

## Signs it's you, not the deck {#signs}

- You reach for the cards several times a day.
- You've asked the same question in three different versions this week.
- Readings for other people feel like reciting meanings.
- You feel [drained after a reading](/blog/energy-after-tarot-reading/), and it doesn't pass with a night's sleep.
- You draw a second card to "clarify" nearly every first one.

If two or more of those sound familiar, the deck is fine. You need a rest.

## How to take a break {#how-to-take-a-break}

Put the deck in a drawer. There's no rule for how long: a few days, a week, until you miss it. In the meantime, do something that has nothing to do with tarot — a walk, a proper night's sleep, a painting, a few minutes of sitting still.

If you find you can't go two days without drawing, that tells you something too, and it's kinder to notice it now. I've written about [taking a few weeks off from the deck](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), and it applies to readers as much as to the people they read for.

When you come back, start small: one card, described out loud, before any meaning. You'll often notice the pictures again — the colour of a sky, a detail in a corner you'd stopped seeing. That's the freshness people hoped the ritual would give the deck.

## The one way a deck does wear out {#worn-out}

Cardboard wears out. Corners bend and soften, cards stick together, a deck stops shuffling cleanly and the worn ones become recognisable from the back. That's real tiredness, and it's physical. A new deck is a perfectly good answer, and [getting to know a new one](/blog/new-tarot-deck/) takes a week of daily cards, not a ceremony.
