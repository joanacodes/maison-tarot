In love, Judgement is a second look: at a relationship that ended, one that’s stalling, or the way you’ve been loving. It isn’t a verdict, and my notes call it neither good nor bad. It’s the moment you hear a call to review something, and then decide what to do with what you see.

{{< cards "ar20" >}}

## A 2 in Fire: re-reading the contract {#reading-the-contract}

Judgement is 20, and 2 + 0 = [2](/library/numerology/number-2/): the number of the contract — two parties, a bond, a weighing. In my notes, 2 asks you to check the terms and set the gains against the costs; its minus is stubbornness and ego. In love, that’s what a second look is: re-reading the agreement you made with someone, out loud this time, and noticing which clauses you’ve been keeping out of pride.

The element is Fire: the drive that launches things and clears the ground. A second look in love doesn’t stay a thought for long; it wants to move — a conversation, a decision, a door opened or closed. The energy is masculine: showing yourself, saying it.

Look at the figures: nobody on this card is being punished. Everyone is answering. Pluto and the colours are on [Judgement’s card page](/library/tarot-cards/judgement/).

## The call unheard, or heard too loud {#reversed}

Judgement holds its plus and its minus in equal measure. Its plus is clarity: you finally see the whole story. Its minus is the verdict that never ends, where you keep judging long after there’s anything left to learn. Reversed, the card leans towards one of two extremes.

Unheard: you keep putting off the review — the same argument, the same doubt, and nobody stops to look at it. Too loud: you replay the past endlessly, judging yourself or the other person without ever closing the file. The first needs a conversation; the second needs a full stop. Either way, the card talks about your part, not the other person’s.

## Second looks, wherever you stand {#second-looks}

If you’re single, Judgement often points to a look back before a step forward: what have your past relationships taught you, and what are you ready to stop repeating? Sometimes it’s an ex who gets in touch again. The card doesn’t say yes or no to them; it asks you to weigh the message with the eyes you have now, not the ones you had then.

If you’re together, it’s a review — a conversation about where you both are, a difficult period that forces you to look, or a renewal: deciding again, consciously, to stay. A relationship can be chosen twice, once on impulse and once with open eyes. To me, the second choice is often the stronger one.

After a break-up, Judgement is the time to understand rather than to blame — including yourself. What was the contract, who kept which part, what did you learn? The answers belong to you whether or not anyone comes back.

Imagine you’re reading for someone who hasn’t told you their question, and Judgement comes out. Read the lenses before any meaning: two, so a bond and a weighing; Fire, so energy, something wanting to move; the open arms of the figures, so an answer rather than a punishment. Your first sentence could be: “There’s a relationship you’re looking at again, and this time you’re looking with your eyes open.” After that, they do the talking: whether it’s true, and which relationship it is. It may not be a romance at all.

## “Should I give them a second chance?” {#second-chance}

With this card, the question is often that one, or “Will they come back?” Both carry a lot: love, hurt, and the fear of making the same mistake twice. I understand why people would like a card to make the decision for them. It would take the weight off.

I don’t read what the other person feels or will do. They’re not in the room, and [reading someone without their consent](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/) is a line I keep for everyone’s sake, theirs and yours. I don’t want a card to make your decision either.

What a reading can do is turn the second look towards you. The [very first stranger I read for](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/) asked about a relationship, and the cards also showed that she had been unkind to the other person. **That’s the second look Judgement asks for: at what they did, and at your own part in it.**

A kinder question: “What would I need to see, this time, to say yes — and what would make me say no?” Write both lists. They’ll tell you more than any spread, and they’ll still be true next month.

## When it lands beside the Hanged Man or Death {#beside}

{{< cards "ar12 cu02 ar13" >}}

Next to the Hanged Man, the review needs time: don’t rush the verdict. [The Hanged Man in love](/blog/hanged-man-tarot-love/) is about what to do while you wait.

Next to Death, the review leads to an ending — calmly, and on purpose. The second look has been taken, and what it showed is that something has done its time.

And next to the Two of Cups, the second look brings two people back to the same table, as equals.

## What was said, what happened {#notebook}

Draw two columns on a sheet of paper, the way I keep my reading notebook: what was said, and what happened. Fill it in for the relationship you’re reviewing, facts only. Then sit with it for a day before you act.
