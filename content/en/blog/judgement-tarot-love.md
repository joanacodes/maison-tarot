In love, Judgement is a second look: at a relationship that ended, one that’s stalling, or the way you’ve been loving. It isn’t a verdict — in my notes, Judgement is neither good nor bad. It’s the moment you hear a call to review something, and then decide what to do with what you see.

## What Judgement means in love {#meaning}

On the card, an angel leans out of the clouds and blows a trumpet hung with a white flag and a red cross. Below, grey figures rise from coffins floating on the water — a man, a woman, and a child between them with their back to us — arms open, faces turned up. More figures rise in the distance, in front of white mountains. Nobody on this card is being punished. Everyone is answering.

The lenses. Judgement is 20, and 2 + 0 = 2: [the number of the contract](/library/numerology/number-2/) — two parties, a bond, a weighing. Its advice, in my notes: read the contract, weigh the pros and cons. Its minus: stubbornness, the ego. In love, that’s what a second look is — re-reading the agreement you made with someone, out loud this time.

The element is Fire: the drive that launches things, and that clears the ground. The colours: a blue sky (emotion), grey bodies (neutral, nothing decided yet), white flag and mountains (peace, clarity), and the red of the cross (passion, action). Pluto, the planet later attached to the card, is about what you bury and what comes back up — which, in love, is often an old feeling. [Judgement’s card page](/library/tarot-cards/judgement/) reads each lens in more detail.

Every card has a plus and a minus, and Judgement holds both evenly. Its plus is clarity: you finally see the whole story. Its minus is the verdict that never ends, where you keep judging long after there’s anything left to learn.

**Judgement doesn’t decide for you; it makes sure you look before you decide.**

## Judgement in love: single, together, after a break-up {#situations}

*If you’re single*, Judgement often points to a look back before a step forward: what have your past relationships taught you, and what are you ready to stop repeating? Sometimes it’s an ex who gets in touch again. The card doesn’t say yes or no to them; it asks you to weigh the message with the eyes you have now, not the ones you had then.

*If you’re together*, it’s a review — a conversation about where you both are, a difficult period that forces you to look, or a renewal: deciding again, consciously, to stay. A relationship can be chosen twice, once on impulse and once with open eyes. To me, the second choice is often the stronger one.

*After a break-up*, Judgement is the time to understand rather than to blame — including yourself. What was the contract, who kept which part, what did you learn? The answers belong to you whether or not anyone comes back.

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws Judgement. Read the lenses before any meaning: two, so a bond and a weighing; Fire, so energy, something wanting to move; the open arms of the figures, so an answer rather than a punishment. The sentence you might say out loud: “There’s a relationship you’re looking at again, and this time you’re looking with your eyes open.” Then stop, and let them tell you whether it lands — and which relationship it is. It may not be a romance at all.

## Judgement reversed in love {#reversed}

Reversed, the call isn’t heard, or it’s heard too loudly. Unheard: you keep putting off the review — the same argument, the same doubt, and nobody stops to look at it. Too loud: you replay the past endlessly, judging yourself or the other person without ever closing the file. The first needs a conversation; the second needs a full stop. Either way, the card talks about your part, not the other person’s.

## Judgement next to other cards {#combinations}

Next to the Hanged Man, the review needs time: don’t rush the verdict. [The Hanged Man in love](/blog/hanged-man-tarot-love/) is about what to do while you wait. Next to the Two of Cups, the second look brings two people back to the same table, as equals. Next to Death, the review leads to an ending — calmly, and on purpose.

## The question people bring with Judgement {#the-question}

With this card, the question is often “Should I give them a second chance?” or “Will they come back?” Both carry a lot: love, hurt, and the fear of making the same mistake twice. I understand why people would like a card to make the decision for them. It would take the weight off.

I don’t read what the other person feels or will do. They’re not in the room, and [reading someone without their consent](/blog/reading-tarot-about-someone-else/) is a line I keep for everyone’s sake, theirs and yours. I don’t want a card to make your decision either. I read; you decide.

A kinder question: “What would I need to see, this time, to say yes — and what would make me say no?” Write both lists. They’ll tell you more than any spread, and they’ll still be true next month. If the question underneath is really “will they come back?”, I’ve written about [a gentler way to hold it](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/).

## Before you decide {#next-step}

Take a sheet of paper and draw two columns, the way I keep my reading notebook: what was said, and what happened. Fill it in for the relationship you’re reviewing, facts only. Then sit with it for a day before you act.
