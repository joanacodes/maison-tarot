Maison Tarot is written so that someone curious can start from nothing, but much of it is for people who already read. There's no forum here and no membership. There's a free Library, a method you can measure yourself against, workshops where you read blind in front of others, and events where readings happen in the open.

## What's here for someone who already reads {#whats-here}

If you've been reading for a while, you probably don't need another list of meanings. What helps at that stage is usually something else: a fresh way into cards you think you know, a way to find out how often you're right, and people in front of whom you can try.

That's what this place is built around. It's also worth saying what it isn't. There are no levels, no certificates and no ranks among readers, and nobody here holds the ultimate truth. If you're after a diploma or a path into selling readings, Maison Tarot won't give you that: I teach reading, not the business of it.

## The Library: a second way into cards you know {#library}

The [Library](/library/) is free and holds everything I teach: colours, the four elements, numbers 1 to 10, feminine and masculine energies, astrological modes, and astrology and symbols for those who want them. Each of the 78 cards has its own page, linked to its lenses.

For someone who learnt meanings by heart, the lenses are a second way in. Take the Eight of Swords. You may know it as "trapped". Through the number, eight is control, and a gate between what you can touch and what you can't. Through the element, Air is the mind. Put together, my note reads: a prisoner of yourself while you hold every tool, and free to take off the blindfold.

## The method: something to measure yourself against {#method}

The [method](/method/) is the part experienced readers tend to argue with, and that's fine. It comes down to three habits. The person tells me nothing first. Before I read, I tell myself I'm about to talk nonsense, which keeps my ego quiet. And I keep a notebook in two columns: what I said, what was confirmed.

By my own count, about nine readings in ten held and one in ten missed. It's a manual tally, not a study, and I publish the misses with the hits. What I'd offer a reader who already reads isn't my number, though; it's the notebook. Keep one for a month and you'll know your own rate, which is worth more to you than mine.

## Workshops and sessions: reading blind, with someone checking {#workshops}

A [workshop](/workshops/) is three hours, twelve people at most, and one lens. You draw a card in the first ten minutes, and at the end you read a card you don't know in front of the others. There's no level to pass first; you start with whichever lens speaks to you.

For a beginner, that last exercise is a first. For a reader, it's practice under a little pressure, with up to eleven other people who can tell you what landed and what didn't. Giving that kind of feedback is a skill of its own, and I've written about [how to give feedback to another reader kindly](/blog/feedback-to-tarot-reader/).

Sessions work the same way, one to one: an hour by video, you read first, I read after, and we compare. I teach because of one moment, when someone says something true and is shocked by what they've just said. You can't be told what it's like. You can only try.

## Events: where readings happen in the open {#events}

The [events](/events/) are free. At online reading nights, a few people are drawn at random and read one by one in front of the others, with me knowing nothing about them. At in-person meetups, in Paris and the cities Maison Tarot visits, we read, compare and talk. Webinars take one hour on one lens, with questions.

For a reader, watching teaches as much as reading. You see someone handle a hard card, a miss or a question they don't read on, in real time. And sitting in the other chair now and then reminds you what it feels like to be read. If you're thinking of reading for a group yourself, [what changes when twenty people are watching](/blog/reading-tarot-in-public/) is the companion piece.

## What I wish I'd had when I started {#when-i-started}

I started reading on 26 December 2020, with a deck my friends had given me. Within a few months I was reading for strangers: in a Discord group I'd found through Tumblr, in Facebook tarot groups, and by letter on a pen-pal app. I learnt by reading for people who told me nothing and writing down what held. [Where to find people to read for](/blog/where-to-find-people-to-read-for/) has the practical side of that.

## Where to start if you already read {#start}

Pick the lens you use least. If you read mostly by intuition, try numbers; if you read mostly from memorised meanings, try colours. Read three unknown cards with that lens alone, and write down what you said. Then come to an event and watch someone else do it, or sit down and be read. Either way, you'll go home with something to check.
