Maison Tarot is written so that someone curious can start from nothing, but much of it is for people who already read. There's no forum here and no membership. There's a free Library, a method you can measure yourself against, workshops where you read blind in front of others, and events where readings happen in the open.

## What's here for someone who already reads {#whats-here}

If you've been reading for a while, you probably don't need another list of meanings. What helps at that stage is usually something else: a fresh way into cards you think you know, a way to find out how often you're right, and people in front of whom you can try.

That's what this place is built around. It's also worth saying what it isn't. There are no levels, no certificates and no ranks among readers, and nobody here has the final word. If you're after a diploma or a path into selling readings, Maison Tarot won't give you that: I teach reading, not the business of it.

## The Library: a second way into cards you know {#library}

The [Library](/library/) is free and holds everything I teach: colours, the four elements, the numbers up to ten, the two energies (feminine and masculine), astrological modes, and astrology and symbols for those who want them. All 78 cards are there, one page apiece, each linked to its lenses.

For someone who learnt meanings by heart, the lenses are a second way in. Take the Eight of Swords. You may know it as "trapped". Through the number, eight is control, and a door between what's material and what isn't. Through the element, Air is the mind. Put together, my note reads: shut in by your own thoughts while holding all the keys, free to slip the blindfold whenever you choose.

## The method: something to measure yourself against {#method}

The [method](/method/) is the part experienced readers tend to argue with, and that's fine. It comes down to three habits. The person tells me nothing first. Before I read, I expect to get it wrong, which keeps my ego quiet. And every reading goes into a notebook, where my words sit opposite whatever the person confirmed.

My notebook says about nine readings in ten held, and I publish the misses with the hits. What I'd offer a reader who already reads isn't my number, though; it's the notebook. Keep one for a month and you'll know your own rate, which is worth more to you than mine.

## Workshops and sessions: reading blind, with someone checking {#workshops}

A [workshop](/workshops/) runs for three hours with no more than a dozen people, built around a single lens. A card is in your hands within minutes, and the session closes with you reading an unfamiliar card while the group watches. There's no level to pass first; you start with whichever lens speaks to you.

For a beginner, that last exercise is a first. For a reader, it's practice under a little pressure, with up to eleven other people who can tell you what landed and what didn't. Giving that kind of feedback is a skill of its own, and I've written about [how to give feedback to another reader kindly](/blog/feedback-to-tarot-reader/).

Sessions work the same way, one to one: an hour by video, you read first, I read after, and we compare. I teach for one moment in particular: someone hits on a truth and can't believe they were the one who said it.

## Events: where readings happen in the open {#events}

The [events](/events/) are free. At online reading nights, names are picked at random and those people are read in turn while the rest watch, without my knowing anything about them. At in-person meetups, in Paris and the cities Maison Tarot visits, we read, compare and talk. Webinars spend an hour on a single lens and end with questions.

For a reader, watching teaches as much as reading. You see someone handle a hard card, a miss or a question they don't read on, in real time. And sitting in the other chair now and then reminds you what it feels like to be read. If you're thinking of reading for a group yourself, [what changes when twenty people are watching](/blog/reading-tarot-in-public/) is the companion piece.

## How I learnt, and why this place looks the way it does {#when-i-started}

I started reading on 26 December 2020, with a deck my friends had given me. To my surprise, that very first reading was accurate, though I'd assumed I would have to study before anything landed. Within a few months I was reading for strangers: in a Discord group I'd found through Tumblr, in Facebook tarot groups, and by letter on a pen-pal app. [Where to find people to read for](/blog/where-to-find-people-to-read-for/) has the practical side of that.

I learnt by reading for people who told me nothing and writing down what held. Most of what Maison Tarot offers readers is that same set-up, gathered in one place: strangers who say nothing first at the events, a notebook to count in, and a room of other readers at a workshop who can tell you what landed.

## Where to start if you already read {#start}

Pick the lens you use least. If you read mostly by intuition, try numbers; if you read mostly from memorised meanings, try colours. Read three unknown cards with that lens alone, and write down what you said. Then come to an event and watch someone else do it, or sit down and be read. Either way, you'll go home with something to check.
