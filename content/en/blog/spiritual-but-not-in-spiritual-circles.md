I'd call myself spiritual. I read tarot, I teach it, and I've had experiences I can't explain. But I keep a little distance from "spiritual" circles, and I'd like to say why without making fun of anyone who loves theirs. My reasons come down to three things that can come with some circles: being ungrounded, being too extreme, and egos nobody needs.

## What I mean by spiritual circles {#what-i-mean}

A spiritual circle is any group that gathers around a shared practice or a shared way of seeing the world: a meditation group, an online tarot community, a weekend retreat, the people who follow one teacher. People come for company, for a vocabulary, and for a place where their experiences don't sound strange.

That last reason matters. If something has happened to you that you can't explain — I've written about [one of mine, involving a very polite grandfather](/blog/is-tarot-evil/) — a circle can be the first place you're believed. That's worth a lot, and plenty of circles give it generously.

**My distance isn't from the people, but from three tendencies that can come with the territory.**

## Ungrounded {#ungrounded}

Being grounded means being able to come back to ordinary life after whatever you've practised: sleep, work, the washing-up, friends who don't share your words. Being ungrounded is the opposite. The practice floats free of the life it was meant to help.

Say every late train becomes a sign, every disagreement a test, every tired evening a message. Nothing is allowed to be only what it is. That's exhausting to live in, and it leaves no room for the plainest explanation, which is often the right one.

When the signs are really a mind under strain, or something heavier, the right door is [a therapist's](/disclaimers/#therapy), not a circle's. The advice I give about developing your senses holds here too: build up slowly, and never lose the road back to ordinary life.

My own version of grounded is modest. When there's a noise in my place at night, I let it be; I wrote about [how I stopped being afraid of the dark](/blog/afraid-of-the-dark/).

## Too extreme {#too-extreme}

A practice becomes extreme when it asks for all of you: everything has to be read through one idea, and a label starts to stand in for the person.

Take the words that travel through these circles: empath, starseed, lightworker. They mean something real to people, and I've [defined them plainly elsewhere](/blog/empath-starseed-lightworker/). What I keep my distance from is the moment a word becomes a rank or a cage. You don't pick your starseed type from a video. Lightwork is one I know little about, and what I've found is that it can be risky if you don't know what you're doing. And you don't need a label to be yourself.

Extreme can also be a matter of how often. A reading before every decision, a ritual for every worry: at that point the practice is making your choices for you. I'd rather people learn to make decisions, make mistakes, and accept being imperfect. A reading is a check-up on where you are, taken with your feet on the ground, never a permission slip. Stepping away from the cards for a while is part of the practice, not a lapse in it.

## Egos nobody needs {#ego}

The third reason is ego: the one who thinks they're saving the world, the one who believes they're among the chosen few, the "awakened" who feel above everyone else. I've written about [those three egos, and how a reader avoids becoming one](/blog/tarot-practice-without-guru/).

What worries me isn't that someone has an ego; everyone does. It's who pays for it. People often arrive in a circle at a fragile moment, and a confident voice that believes it's special can be risky for them. There are no Chosen Ones, in a circle or anywhere else.

## If you love your circle {#if-you-love-yours}

If your circle leaves you calmer, kinder and better able to live your ordinary life, it's doing exactly what it should, and nothing here is aimed at it. A few gentle questions, if you're not sure:

1. Do you come home more grounded than you left?
2. Can you doubt, or disagree, without being told something is wrong with you?
3. Is anyone presented as above the others?
4. Are doctors or therapists ever spoken of as unnecessary?

If one of the last three gives you pause, you don't have to leave anything. Step back a little, the way I do, and see how it feels from there.

And if your circle is where you were first believed, hold on to that. Distance can be partial. You can keep the company and the kindness, and leave the parts that make you smaller at the door.

As for me, I'd rather practise where both feet stay on the floor. Come to one of the [free events](/events/): the readings are drawn at random, the person tells me nothing, and nobody in the room outranks anybody else.
