I'd rather not read about someone who isn't in the room and hasn't said yes, whether it's your ex, your boss or your mother. Not because the question is wrong: it usually comes from love, hurt or worry. Because that person can't consent, and because what you'd get is half a sentence. What you can read instead is your side of the story, and that's the reading that tends to help.

## Why consent matters, even with cards {#consent}

You wouldn't want someone going through your bedroom while you were out, whatever their reasons. Reading someone without their consent feels like that to me: looking into a private room they haven't opened.

There's a practical side too. Someone who doesn't know they're being read isn't open to it. In my experience you catch half a sentence of them, and then you're tempted to build a whole statement on it: "he regrets it", "she's jealous", "they're about to leave". This is only my experience and the hypothesis I draw from it; nothing about it has been proven scientifically. It's enough for me to always ask first, and to step back when the person can't be asked. It's on [the list of what I don't read](/disclaimers/#refusals), and the same goes for [looking up someone's birth chart without asking](/blog/birth-chart-without-permission/).

**A reading about someone who isn't there is mostly a reading about the person asking.**

Here is how that plays out with the three people in the title, with one card for each, drawn as examples.

## Your ex {#your-ex}

"Does he still think about me?" "Is she with someone new?" "Will they come back?" These come from the particular pain of not knowing, and I understand them. But a card can't tell you what another person feels, and a reading that claims to can keep you waiting for months.

Say you ask "what am I still holding on to?" instead, and draw the Eight of Cups: a figure in a red cloak walking away from eight stacked cups, towards the mountains, at night. Eight is the number of moving on from what you're already doing. The card isn't about your ex at all. It's about you, and a door you may already be walking through. I give the hardest version of this question its own article: [“will they come back?”, and a kinder question](/blog/will-they-come-back-tarot/).

## Your boss {#your-boss}

"What does my boss think of me?" "Are they going to let me go?" Work worries seep into everything, and a boss has real power over your days. But your boss's mind isn't on the table, and a fear about losing your job sits right next to money decisions: that's one for a conversation, or for someone who knows employment law, rather than a card.

What you can read is the conflict as you live it. Say you draw the Five of Swords: one man gathering up swords while two others walk away towards the water. Five is conflict, and Swords are Air: decisions, words, two people not seeing things from the same point of view. That gives you something to work with: "Where do we see this differently, and what do I want to say?"

## Your mother {#your-mother}

"Why is she like this?" "Is she hiding something from me?" Sometimes, quietly: "Is she ill?" Questions about a parent carry a whole childhood in them. The health one in particular isn't for the cards; I explain why, gently, in [why careful readers step back from health, pregnancy and death](/blog/health-pregnancy-death-tarot/).

What a reading can hold is the relationship from your side. Say you draw [the Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/): a woman among ripening wheat, the mother who feeds and makes things grow, and who, on her other side, can smother. That card opens a better question than "why is she like this?": "What do I need from her that I've never asked for, and what can I give myself?"

## When someone else turns up anyway {#when-they-show-up}

Other people do appear in readings, even when nobody asked about them. In my very first reading for a stranger, the cards talked about a relationship, and also showed that she hadn't been especially kind to the other person. She told me it didn't resonate; three or four days later she wrote to say it had all happened. I tell it in full in [my first tarot reading story](/blog/first-tarot-reading-story/).

Notice what that reading did: it talked about her, and her part. That's the difference. When someone turns up in your cards, read them as they live in you: your fear of them, your love for them, your share of the story. That's yours to look at. Their inner life isn't.

And if you want to know what the other person thinks, there's a way that needs no cards: ask them. If they're curious, invite them to sit down, say yes, and be read themselves.

## A reading about you, this week {#this-week}

Next time you reach for the deck to ask about someone else, rewrite the question so it starts with "I". Not "what does he feel?" but "what do I feel, and what do I want to do about it?" Draw one card and answer it in three lines. And if that person does say yes one day, here's [how to read tarot for someone else with care](/blog/read-tarot-for-someone-else/).
