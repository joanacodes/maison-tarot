## What an element does to a card {#what-it-does}

The [number](/library/numerology/) says *where* the situation is; the element says *on which plane* it plays out. A Five — conflict, trial — isn't the same conflict in each house: the Five of Cups is grief, the Five of Swords a victory that costs, the Five of Wands an ego fight, the Five of Pentacles material hardship. Same step, four planes.

That's the principle that saves you from learning forty cards: ten numbers, four houses, and the combination does the rest.

## The difference between Water and Fire, to understand the rest {#water-fire}

Fire destroys at once, visibly — a blaze, an argument, an impulsive decision. Water destroys slowly, by seeping in — a neglected emotion that ends up eating the foundations like a mould nobody saw. Neither better nor worse: two timescales. Once you hold those two, Earth (what holds) and Air (what cuts) fall into place by themselves.

## Elements and energies {#energies}

Earth and Water lean towards [feminine energy](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) — what hides, what carries, what waits. Fire and Air lean towards masculine — what shows itself, what acts, what decides. It isn't about sexes; it's about direction.
