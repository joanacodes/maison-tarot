## What Air stands for

Decision-making. The act of thinking. By nature the element of analysis, omnipresence and the mind. The Swords aren’t first about violence: they’re about **what goes on in the head**, and what you do with it.

## Why Air is the one that decides

Compare. [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is too impulsive to decide well. [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) is submerged by its emotions. [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is stubborn and will change its mind later, once faced with reality. Air, being everywhere, has all the possible information at once: it’s **the fittest to decide**. Which doesn’t mean it decides well — only that it decides.

## Keywords

To think · analysis · decision-making · the mind.

## The archetype

The chief, the leader, the authority figure who takes the decisions nobody else can take. And who, by the same token, can become a tyrant. Many Swords in a spread: someone thinks a lot, decides a lot, or has their life dictated by someone who decides for them.

## A reading, as an example

The Two of Swords, for someone who has told you nothing. *Two*: cooperation, binding, weighing pros and cons. *Air*: the mind, the decision. You can already talk about a choice being postponed with eyes closed, two options held at equal distance. Nothing memorised.
