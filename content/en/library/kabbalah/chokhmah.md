## Chokhmah in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Chokhmah (חכמה) means “wisdom”. It is the second of the ten sephirot, right below [Keter](/library/kabbalah/keter/). Jewish sources describe it as the first flash of awareness: an insight that arrives whole, before it has a shape or words. In the Zohar’s language it is Abba, the father, paired with [Binah](/library/kabbalah/binah/), the mother.

**Chokhmah is the idea that arrives all at once, before you know how to explain it.** Think of the moment you see the solution to a problem and then need an hour to write it down: the seeing is Chokhmah.

On the Tree, Chokhmah is number 2, at the top of the right column, the side of expansion and giving that leads down to [Chesed](/library/kabbalah/chesed/). With Keter and Binah it forms the upper three. The [Tree of Life page](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/) shows the whole layout.

## The four Twos {#cards}

The tarot correspondences aren’t Jewish. They come from the Hermetic Qabalah of the Golden Dawn, which borrowed the Tree and rebuilt it as a system for cards, planets and elements. It’s a transformation of the tradition, and I keep the two apart. In that system, each sephirah takes the four minor cards of its number, so Chokhmah takes the Twos.

| Card | Golden Dawn title (decan) | What Chokhmah adds |
|---|---|---|
| [Two of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-wands/) | Lord of Dominion (Mars in Aries) | the plan seen whole, the world in one hand |
| [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/) | Lord of Love (Venus in Cancer) | recognition at first sight |
| [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/) | Lord of Peace Restored (Moon in Libra) | the answer already known, eyes closed |
| [Two of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-pentacles/) | Lord of Harmonious Change (Jupiter in Capricorn) | the knack of keeping two things moving |

The number gives the ground: [two](/library/numerology/number-2/) is cooperation, the contract, the moment you weigh pros and cons. Chokhmah adds the speed of the first insight. Through this lens, a Two is a decision you’ve already half made by instinct — and the advice of the number still holds: read the contract before you sign.

## The court cards: Kings or Knights? {#court}

Book T, the Golden Dawn’s tarot manuscript, also puts the court cards on the Tree. The four ranks follow the four letters of the Name, YHVH. The first letter, Yod, the Fire of each suit, goes to Chokhmah; the Queens go to Binah, the Princes to Tiferet, the Princesses to Malkhut.

The Chokhmah figure is a crowned rider on horseback. Book T titles him Knight and describes him as a king: for Wands, “Lord of the Flame and the Lightning, King of the Spirits of Fire”.

**Which Rider-Waite-Smith card sits here is debated.** Waite’s deck keeps the older names — King, Queen, Knight, Page — and they don’t line up one to one with the Golden Dawn’s. Read by rank, the senior male figure is the King, so the four [Kings](/library/numerology/king/) take Chokhmah. That’s how Benebell Wen teaches it for the Rider-Waite-Smith (she puts the Knights here for the Thoth deck), and she says openly that the point is argued. It’s also the reading the card pages on this site follow: the King of Wands is Fire of Fire. Read by picture, the rider is the [Knight](/library/numerology/knight/), and some authors put the Knights here instead.

## Chokhmah and astrology: the zodiac {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn gives Chokhmah no planet. Its sphere is the zodiac, also called the sphere of the fixed stars: in medieval astronomy, the eighth heaven, beyond the seven planets. All twelve signs at once, before any of them is singled out.

That suits the Twos. They are where the decans begin, the first cards in each suit to receive a planet in a sign. Some modern writers put Uranus or Neptune on Chokhmah; it isn’t the Golden Dawn’s scheme, and the authors don’t agree among themselves.

## Chokhmah in a reading {#reading}

What Chokhmah can add is modest: a question about whether the person already knows. Twos are often read as hesitation. Through this lens, they can also be an intuition that hasn’t been trusted yet.

Say someone draws the Two of Swords next to the Two of Wands. A blindfold on one side, a globe in the hand on the other: someone who can’t decide, and someone who already sees the whole map. Through Chokhmah, they can be the same person at two moments — the answer came first, the fear of it came after.

The Tree stays an optional extra, beside the five lenses I teach. Don’t take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself: lay the Twos out, read them with and without Chokhmah, and keep the lens only if it helps.
