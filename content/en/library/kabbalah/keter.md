## Keter in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Keter (כתר) means “crown”. It is the first of the ten sephirot, the emanations through which, in Jewish Kabbalah, the hidden God — Ein Sof, “without end” — becomes manifest. It is also called Keter Elyon, the supreme crown.

**A crown sits above the head without being part of it, and that is where the tradition puts Keter.** Some Kabbalists leave it out of the ten altogether and count Da’at, knowledge, in its place. Others call it Ayin, “nothing” — not because it is empty, but because nothing can be said about it. Chassidic teaching links it to will: the wanting that comes before any thought.

On the Tree, Keter is number 1, at the top of the middle column. With [Chokhmah](/library/kabbalah/chokhmah/) and [Binah](/library/kabbalah/binah/) below it, it forms the upper three, which later diagrams call the supernal triad. The ten spheres, the twenty-two paths and the four worlds are laid out on the [Tree of Life page](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/).

## The four Aces {#cards}

Jewish Kabbalah has nothing to say about tarot cards. The link was built later: Christian scholars borrowed the Tree, nineteenth-century occultists reworked it, and the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, founded in London in 1888, turned it into a filing system for cards, planets and elements. That Hermetic Qabalah transforms the tradition; it doesn’t speak for it. [The Golden Dawn’s tarot](/library/tarot-history/golden-dawn-tarot/) has its own page.

Its tarot manuscript, Book T, places the numbered minors by number: each sephirah takes the four cards of its number, one per suit. Keter — a sephirah some Jewish sources won’t even count — receives the Aces.

On all four Rider-Waite-Smith Aces, a hand comes out of a cloud holding the emblem of the suit. That is Keter in one picture: a source you can’t see, offering something nobody has used yet.

| Card | Golden Dawn title | What Keter adds |
|---|---|---|
| [Ace of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-wands/) | Root of the Powers of Fire | the urge, before any project |
| [Ace of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-cups/) | Root of the Powers of Water | a feeling, before it has anyone to go to |
| [Ace of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-swords/) | Root of the Powers of Air | the idea, before the decision |
| [Ace of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-pentacles/) | Root of the Powers of Earth | the resource, before it’s spent |

The number already says a lot: [one](/library/numerology/number-1/) is the initiator, the rough diamond, a start where nothing is cut yet. Keter adds the crown’s distance. An Ace is will without a plan — a yes without a how.

## Keter and astrology: the Primum Mobile {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn’s scheme, each sephirah has a sphere of the sky. Keter’s isn’t a planet. It’s the Primum Mobile, the “first moving” sphere: in medieval astronomy, the outermost one, which set all the others turning.

It fits the cards. In the same system the Aces get no planet and no sign; the decans only begin with the Twos. Some modern writers hang an outer planet on Keter — Pluto for some, Uranus for others. They don’t agree, and it isn’t the Golden Dawn’s scheme, so I leave it aside. If [astrology](/library/astrology/) is one of your lenses, remember this much: Keter sits above the chart, not inside it.

## Keter in a reading {#reading}

The Tree isn’t one of the five lenses I teach. It’s an extra, for readers who like to know where the correspondences come from, and Keter adds a question more than a meaning: is this still a wish, or already a direction?

Say someone draws the Ace of Swords next to the Eight of Pentacles. Through Keter, the Ace is the idea at its root, with no skill attached yet; the Eight is the work that will give it a form. The reading becomes: the idea is sound, and now it has to come down the Tree, into someone’s hands.

Several Aces at once are a crowd of beginnings, none of them shaped. That isn’t a bad sign. It says that beginnings need choosing, and the cards around them will often show which one has started to move.

Keter adds no power to the Aces, and it doesn’t make a reading more serious. Cards aren’t magic, you are. If the Tree doesn’t speak to you, nothing is missing: the number and the element have said what matters, and [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) works without it.
