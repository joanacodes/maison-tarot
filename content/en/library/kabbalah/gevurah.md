## Gevurah in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Gevurah (גבורה) means “strength” or “might”. It is the fifth of the ten sephirot, also called Din, “judgement”. Jewish sources explain the name by its role: restraint, contraction, the power to hold back.

**Gevurah isn’t cruelty: it’s the limit that makes kindness possible.** [Chesed](/library/kabbalah/chesed/), on its own, would flood; Gevurah decides how much, for whom, and when. Think of a parent who says no to a third dessert: the no is part of the care.

The tradition attaches it to Isaac, as it attaches Chesed to Abraham. On the [Tree](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/), Gevurah is number 5, on the left column under [Binah](/library/kabbalah/binah/), facing Chesed on the right. The two meet in [Tiferet](/library/kabbalah/tiferet/), in the middle, which balances them; Hermetic diagrams call these three the ethical triangle.

## The four Fives {#cards}

The Golden Dawn turned a teaching about how judgement tempers kindness into a sphere ruled by Mars, and the Fives — conflict, loss, defeat — land on it.

{{< cards "wa05 cu05 sw05 pe05" >}}

| Card | Golden Dawn title (decan) | What Gevurah adds |
|---|---|---|
| [Five of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-wands/) | Lord of Strife (Saturn in Leo) | the fight that tests, not the one that destroys |
| [Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/) | Lord of Loss in Pleasure (Mars in Scorpio) | the loss that shows what mattered |
| [Five of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-swords/) | Lord of Defeat (Venus in Aquarius) | the line someone crossed — maybe you |
| [Five of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-pentacles/) | Lord of Material Trouble (Mercury in Taurus) | the cold of going without, and the lit window nearby |

My notes on these cards are not gentle. On the Five of Wands, five young men seem to fight without coordinating. The Five of Swords is a conflict of decisions, two points of view that don’t meet. The Five of Pentacles is poverty and snow, with the church where warmth could be found. The Five of Cups is, for me, the most negative card in the deck: losing, alone with your problems.

The number frames them: [five](/library/numerology/number-5/) is conflict and trial, trying without fear of failing, making do with what’s left. Its advice is to avoid useless conflicts and be honest with yourself; its minus is denial. Gevurah adds a function to the conflict. Read with Gevurah, a Five marks a boundary — and asks where it is, and who drew it.

## Gevurah and astrology: Mars {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn gives Gevurah [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/): raw energy, anger, desire, the way you fight. Mars is the third planet down, after Saturn and Jupiter, so it lands on the sephirah of judgement, facing Jupiter’s generosity across the Tree.

The Five of Cups holds both at once: Gevurah in the suit of Water, Mars in Scorpio. A cloaked figure looking at three spilled cups, two still standing behind: Mars, here, is the force of grief, not of attack.

## Gevurah in a reading {#reading}

What Gevurah can add is a reframe, said carefully. A Five through this lens is a place where a limit is being set — by you, by someone else, by circumstance. That doesn’t make a loss less of a loss, and I won’t pretend it does.

Say someone draws the Five of Swords next to the Two of Cups: a disagreement inside a bond. Without the Tree, it reads as a fight. Through Gevurah, the question becomes: what boundary is this argument trying to draw, and can it be said more kindly?

When a Five sits next to a Four, Chesed and Gevurah are side by side on the table: what was given, and where it stops. That pairing is often the whole story.

If this lens helps you look at the hard cards with less dread, keep it; if it doesn’t, set it aside.
