## Binah in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Binah (בינה) means “understanding”; older translations say “intelligence”. It is the third of the ten sephirot. Jewish sources call it the power of understanding one thing from another: it receives the flash of [Chokhmah](/library/kabbalah/chokhmah/) and works it out, distinguishes its parts, gives it room to grow.

The tradition calls it Imma, the mother, and the Zohar speaks of a supernal mother from whom the lower sephirot come. **Where Chokhmah sees, Binah understands — and understanding is what lets an idea be born.** A melody you hum in the shower is Chokhmah; the score that lets an orchestra play it is Binah.

On the Tree, Binah is number 3, at the top of the left column, the side of limits and judgement, directly above [Gevurah](/library/kabbalah/gevurah/). With [Keter](/library/kabbalah/keter/) and Chokhmah it closes the upper three; [the whole Tree](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/) is mapped separately.

## The four Threes {#cards}

In the Golden Dawn’s system, the mother of the sephirot becomes a sphere ruled by Saturn, and its number brings it the four Threes.

{{< cards "wa03 cu03 sw03 pe03" >}}

| Card | Golden Dawn title (decan) | What Binah adds |
|---|---|---|
| [Three of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-wands/) | Lord of Established Strength (Sun in Aries) | the plan launched, ships already on the water |
| [Three of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-cups/) | Lord of Abundance (Mercury in Cancer) | a feeling given a circle to live in |
| [Three of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-swords/) | Lord of Sorrow (Saturn in Libra) | understanding that hurts: seeing clearly what happened |
| [Three of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-pentacles/) | Lord of Material Works (Mars in Capricorn) | the plans read together, the craft that gives them a body |

The number already says the machine is running: [three](/library/numerology/number-3/) is the good rhythm, the contract that works, with one piece of advice: don’t open a new project while the last one is still unfinished. Binah adds why it runs: someone took the time to understand it. My notes give the Three of Swords a second face: a bond sealed with blood. Through Binah, that reading makes sense — understanding that binds, even when it costs.

## The Queens {#court}

The Golden Dawn’s Book T places the four court ranks on the Tree after the four letters of the Name, YHVH. The second letter, He, the Water of each suit, goes to Binah. Its figure is a Queen on a throne: for Wands, the Queen of the Thrones of Flame.

How the Rider-Waite-Smith courts map onto the Golden Dawn’s titles is debated — the Kings and the Knights above all, as the Chokhmah page explains — and tarot authors such as Benebell Wen say so openly. **The Queens are the one rank that keeps its name in both systems.** All four sit in Binah, each the Water of her suit, as the card pages here list them — the Cups Queen being Water twice over.

Four women seated on thrones, none of them going anywhere. In my reading, the [Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) holds: she has absorbed her element until it’s part of her. Binah suits her well — understanding that has become your own.

## Binah and astrology: Saturn {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn gives Binah [Saturn](/library/astrology/saturn/): time, limits, structure, the lesson that takes years. It’s the first true planet on the way down the Tree, after Keter’s Primum Mobile and Chokhmah’s zodiac, and the slowest of the seven planets the ancients could see. One card shows the link plainly: the Three of Swords, Binah in the suit of Air, is Saturn in Libra.

When I tested astrology on my own chart and other people’s, Saturn came out among my three most reliable results. That’s my count, not a study — but it’s why the pairing doesn’t seem far-fetched to me: form, like Saturn, is what makes things last, and what makes them hurt.

## Binah in a reading {#reading}

Binah’s question is about form: has this idea found its container yet? Say someone draws the Three of Pentacles after the Two of Wands. Through the Tree, the Two is the plan seen in a flash; the Three is the plan understood well enough to be built with other people.

It works the other way too. A spread full of Twos and no Threes can describe someone who sees clearly and hasn’t yet sat down to work it out. Binah doesn’t tell them what to do; it names the step.

That’s all this lens claims.
