## Malkhut in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Malkhut (מלכות) means “kingship” or “sovereignty”; tarot books usually say “Kingdom”. It is the tenth and last of the sephirot, at the foot of the middle column, just below [Yesod](/library/kabbalah/yesod/).

Malkhut has nothing of its own. The Zohar says so, and Kabbalists compare it to the moon, which shines with light it receives from the sun. **Malkhut is where everything above finally arrives, and is received.** Think of a letter: the writer’s whole intention, and nothing happens until someone opens it.

Kabbalah also calls Malkhut the Shekhinah, a word from the Talmud for God’s presence dwelling in the world. Here that presence is pictured as feminine: the bride whom [Tiferet](/library/kabbalah/tiferet/), the bridegroom, seeks to join. The tradition attaches Malkhut to King David, the figure of kingship. The [Tree of Life page](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/) shows it at the foot of the whole structure.

## The four Tens {#cards}

The tarot correspondences are not Jewish. They come from the Hermetic Qabalah of the Golden Dawn, and Malkhut is where the shift is easiest to see: in Jewish sources, it is God’s presence within the world; in the Golden Dawn’s tables, it is the world itself, the sphere of the elements, and the first grade of initiation, Zelator, 1=10. I keep the two apart. Each sephirah takes the four minor cards of its number, so Malkhut takes the Tens.

| Card | Decan (Golden Dawn) | What Malkhut adds |

| [Ten of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-wands/) | Saturn in Sagittarius | the load carried all the way home |
| [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/) | Mars in Pisces | happiness in a real house, with real people |
| [Ten of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-swords/) | Sun in Gemini | the end touched, and the dawn behind it |
| [Ten of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-pentacles/) | Mercury in Virgo | three generations, the Tree drawn in coins |

The number gives the frame: [ten](/library/numerology/number-10/) is the transition between two stages, the corridor between two rooms; its minus is that it can be short-lived, and it tells you to start something else. In my notes, the Ten of Pentacles is complete wealth at home, the whole family present; the Golden Dawn calls it the Lord of Wealth. Malkhut adds the landing. Through this lens, a Ten is the cycle arrived somewhere you can touch.

Look closely at the Ten of Pentacles: its ten coins are laid out in the shape of the Tree of Life, the whole structure brought down into one family’s courtyard.

## The court cards: the Princesses {#court}

The Golden Dawn puts the court cards on the Tree by the four letters of the Name, YHVH. The final Heh — the Earth of each suit — goes to Malkhut: the Princess, a young woman standing on her own feet.

How the Rider-Waite-Smith courts map onto the Golden Dawn titles is debated, mostly over the Kings and Knights; the [Chokhmah page](/library/kabbalah/chokhmah/) sets out both readings. The Princesses are the clearer case. **By title and by picture, the Rider-Waite-Smith [Pages](/library/numerology/page/) are the Princesses**, and the card pages on this site agree: the Page of Wands is Earth of Fire.

## Malkhut and astrology: the elements {#astrology}

Malkhut gets no planet. The Golden Dawn gives it the sphere of the elements: Earth first, as the ground under everything, and the [four elements](/library/four-elements/) mixed together, as they are in ordinary life. The seven planets stop at Yesod, with the Moon.

That suits the Tens. Each suit is one element, and the four Tens bring all four to the bottom of the Tree at once: Fire carried as a burden, Water as a family, Air as an ending, Earth as an inheritance.

On the Golden Dawn’s Tree, three paths arrive in Malkhut, carrying the Moon, Judgement and the World. Every road down ends here.

## Malkhut in a reading {#reading}

What Malkhut can add is a question about the concrete: where does this land — in which house, which job, which week?

Say someone draws the Ten of Wands. Ten says a stage is complete and asks you to start something else. Malkhut adds that the load is real: carried by one person, towards an actual front door. The question becomes what can be put down at the threshold.

A reading only reaches Malkhut when someone does something with it. I read; you decide. The lens is an optional extra beside the five I teach, and it’s at its best when it brings a reading back down to earth.
