The twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet are paired with the twenty-two major arcana in many tarot books. This site uses the Golden Dawn’s table, set on the Rider-Waite-Smith numbering: the Fool is Aleph, the World is Tav, Strength (8) is Teth and Justice (11) is Lamed. Below: where the letters come from, the table, one paragraph per letter, and the French order that counts differently.

## Where the letters come from {#origin}

The letters belong to the Jewish tradition before they belong to the tarot. Sefer Yetzirah, an early Hebrew text on how the world was made, sorts them into three kinds. Three mother letters (Aleph, Mem, Shin) go with air, water and fire. Seven double letters, called double because each had two pronunciations, go with the seven planets known in antiquity and the days of the week. Twelve simple letters go with the twelve signs of the zodiac and the twelve months, in order, from He for Aries to Qoph for Pisces.

Pairing them with the tarot is not part of that tradition. It is an idea of French occultists, made famous by [Éliphas Lévi](/library/tarot-figures/eliphas-levi/) in the 1850s, then reworked by the [Golden Dawn](/library/tarot-history/golden-dawn-tarot/) in London from 1888.

The Golden Dawn tied each letter to a card with two threads. The first is order: the Fool, numbered zero, comes first and takes Aleph, and the others follow. The second is Sefer Yetzirah’s attributions: a mother letter brings an element, a double a planet, a simple a sign, and the card has to fit. Teth is Leo, the lion, but the eighth card was Justice; so Strength and Justice changed places, a swap already suggested in the Cipher Manuscripts the order was founded on. **The VIII and XI on your Rider-Waite-Smith cards are that swap, printed in 1909.**

One honest limit: the versions of Sefer Yetzirah don’t agree on which planet goes with which double letter. The Golden Dawn used its own order.

The meanings are the usual readings of the names, the ones the Golden Dawn used. For He, Teth, Tsade and Qoph the original meaning is uncertain: window, serpent, fish-hook and back of the head are the Golden Dawn’s readings. In the last column the cards keep their Rider-Waite-Smith names; Marseille decks call some of them differently (the Bateleur for the Magician, the Papesse for the High Priestess, the Maison Dieu for the Tower).

## The three mother letters {#mothers}

Aleph (א), the ox. A mother letter: air, in Sefer Yetzirah. The Golden Dawn gave it to the card numbered zero, the Fool, the trump of [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/). The contrast is worth a look: the plodding ox on the lightest card in the deck.

Mem (מ) means water, and Sefer Yetzirah makes it the water letter. It goes to the Hanged Man, the Water trump: a man upside down, still, waiting. Water at rest, not water rushing.

Shin (ש), the tooth. Its three strokes look like three flames, and Sefer Yetzirah gives it fire. It goes to Judgement, the Fire trump, where the angel’s trumpet gets everyone up.

On the card pages these three also carry Uranus, Neptune and Pluto. Those are later additions, planets discovered from 1781 on; the Golden Dawn gave the three cards elements.

## The seven double letters {#doubles}

Beth (ב), house, the letter that opens the Torah (*Bereshit*, “in the beginning”). Mercury in the Golden Dawn’s order, so the Magician, card I: a man who has set up house, with a table and four tools on it.

Gimel (ג), camel. The Moon, so the High Priestess. A camel carries its water across the desert; she keeps what she knows behind her veil.

Daleth (ד), door. Venus, so the Empress: the mother as the door through which life comes in.

Kaph (כ), the palm of the hand. Jupiter, so the Wheel of Fortune, and the open hand that turns the wheel.

Pe (פ), mouth. Mars, so the Tower: the blow, the word that brings the wall down.

Resh (ר), head. The Sun, so the Sun card, whose great face looks straight at you.

Tav (ת), mark; its oldest written form is a cross. Saturn, so the World, the last card for the last letter. It is the only letter that the Golden Dawn and Lévi give to the same card.

## The twelve simple letters {#simple}

He (ה), read by the Golden Dawn as window. Aries, the first sign, so the Emperor, on his throne, overlooking his land.

Vav (ו), nail or hook; in Hebrew it is also the word for “and”. Taurus, so the Hierophant, who links a tradition to the people in front of him.

Zayin (ז), sword. Gemini, so the Lovers: choosing cuts one road off.

Cheth (ח), fence, an enclosure. Cancer, the crab in its shell, so the Chariot: a man in armour inside a stone box on wheels.

Teth (ט), read as serpent. Leo, so [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), the card with the lion. It is the reason Strength became number 8.

Yod (י), hand, and the smallest letter of the alphabet. Virgo, so the Hermit, his lantern held up in one hand.

Lamed (ל), ox-goad, and the tallest letter. Libra, the scales, so Justice, now number 11.

{{< cards "ar04 ar05 ar06 ar07 ar08 ar09" >}}

Nun (נ), fish (the word is Aramaic). Scorpio, a water sign, so Death: an ending that clears the ground, not death itself.

Samekh (ס), prop, support. Sagittarius, so Temperance: an angel keeping the balance between two cups.

Ayin (ע), eye. Capricorn, so the Devil: what you look at and can’t stop looking at.

Tsade (צ), read as fish-hook. Aquarius, so the Star. Crowley later moved this letter to the Emperor for his Thoth deck, and He to the Star.

Qoph (ק), read as the back of the head. Pisces, so the Moon: what works behind your awareness, at night.

## The French order: Lévi and Wirth {#levi-wirth}

French books often give other letters, and they aren’t wrong: they follow another system. Éliphas Lévi, in *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, lets the letters follow the numbers of the Tarot de Marseille. Aleph goes to card I, the Magician (the Bateleur), Beth to card II, and so on to Resh for Judgement (XX). The Fool, which has no number, takes Shin, the twenty-first letter, so it sits between Judgement and the World, which takes Tav. [Oswald Wirth](/library/tarot-figures/oswald-wirth/) printed Lévi’s letters on the cards he drew in 1889 (see the [Wikipedia article on his deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Wirth_Tarot)).

Marseille decks keep Justice at VIII and Strength at XI, so Lévi gives Cheth to Justice and Kaph to Strength. The result: for most cards, a French book gives the letter just before the one on this site. The Magician is Aleph, not Beth; the Hermit is Teth, not Yod. **Check which table a book uses before you compare its meanings with these.**

## What a letter adds to a reading {#reading}

A letter adds an image, not a meaning. Say the Hermit comes up: Yod, the hand. Look at what his hand is doing, and at what yours would do in his place. If the image helps you see the card, keep it; if it doesn’t, the number, the element and the colours do the work without it.

The letters sit on the paths of [the Tree of Life](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/): that page shows which two spheres each card joins.
