## Hod in the Jewish tradition {#tradition}

Hod (הוד) means “splendour” or “majesty”. It is the eighth of the ten sephirot, at the foot of the left column under [Gevurah](/library/kabbalah/gevurah/), and it completes that column. Jewish sources see it as the lower channel through which judgement reaches the world, and link it with prophecy.

The word shares its root with hoda’ah, acknowledgement or thanks, and Chassidic teaching reads Hod through it: gratitude and humility. **Hod is a splendour that knows where it comes from.** Think of a conductor who takes the applause and turns at once to point at the orchestra: the brilliance and the thanks are one gesture.

On the body, Hod is the left leg, facing [Netzach](/library/kabbalah/netzach/), the right: one pushes forward, the other steadies. Kabbalists pair Netzach with Moses and Hod with Aaron, his brother and the first high priest. With Netzach and [Yesod](/library/kabbalah/yesod/), Hod forms the [third triad](/library/kabbalah/tree-of-life/), often taught as the forces of action.

## The four Eights {#cards}

For the Golden Dawn, a teaching about majesty and acknowledgement became the sphere of Mercury, the mind, and the eighth sphere took the four Eights.

{{< cards "wa08 cu08 sw08 pe08" >}}

| Card | Golden Dawn title (decan) | What Hod adds |
|---|---|---|
| [Eight of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-wands/) | Lord of Swiftness (Mercury in Sagittarius) | the message already in the air |
| [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/) | Lord of Abandoned Success (Saturn in Pisces) | the humility to leave what worked |
| [Eight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-swords/) | Lord of Shortened Force (Jupiter in Gemini) | the mind that ties itself up |
| [Eight of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-pentacles/) | Lord of Prudence (Sun in Virgo) | craft repeated until it shines |

The number gives the frame: [eight](/library/numerology/number-8/) is four exalted — the empire, guaranteed stability, control; the Magician’s lemniscate, an eight lying down; a doorway from what you can touch to what you can’t. In my notes, the Eight of Pentacles is mastery and the effort it takes; the Eight of Cups, leaving behind what you were busy with. Hod adds form. In Hod’s light, an Eight is something given its shape, its method, its words.

## Hod and astrology: Mercury {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn gives Hod [Mercury](/library/astrology/mercury/): thought, language, trade, the messenger. Of the seven planets on the way down the Tree, Mercury is the sixth, just after Venus.

Take the Eight of Wands: Hod in the suit of Fire, and Mercury in Sagittarius. Eight staves flying over open country, nobody holding them: the messenger planet in the sign of long journeys. In my notes, it’s also the glass half full or half empty — wands flying or falling, depending on who looks.

That’s where the Hermetic system moved the tradition. In Jewish sources, Hod is majesty, acknowledgement and prophecy; with Mercury, Hermetic writers made it the sphere of the intellect. Netzach feels, Hod analyses — a neat pair, but a Hermetic one.

## Hod in a reading {#reading}

Hod asks about the mind’s part: is the thinking serving the situation, or running it?

Say someone draws the Eight of Swords alone. A woman bound and blindfolded, the swords planted around her but not touching. My note on this card: you’re your own jailer, holding everything you’d need to get out — and you’re free to untie the blindfold. Through Hod, the bonds are made of thoughts. And the older meaning of the word helps too: the first step out is to acknowledge the blindfold is there.

Next to a Seven, Hod meets Netzach on the table: the drive to continue, and the analysis that checks it. Read them as a conversation.
