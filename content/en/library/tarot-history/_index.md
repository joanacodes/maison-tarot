This section tells the history of tarot period by period, from the first playing cards recorded in Europe in the 1370s to the decks, books and exhibitions of the 20th century. Each page covers one stretch of time: what happened, where, and what the records actually show. The pages run in chronological order, so you can read them like a short book, starting with [how playing cards reached Europe](/library/tarot-history/playing-cards-arrive-in-europe/).

The people have their own pages, in [Historical figures](/library/tarot-figures/): Antoine Court de Gébelin, Etteilla, Éliphas Lévi, [Pamela Colman Smith](/library/tarot-figures/pamela-colman-smith/) and others. A history page mentions them in a sentence or two and links to them; the figure page tells their life, and what they did and didn’t do for tarot. Read the periods for the big picture, and the figures when a name in a book makes you curious.

Two myths come up along the way: that tarot came from ancient Egypt, and that the Romani people brought it to Europe. Both are corrected calmly, with the dates. Every page ends with its sources, so you can check them yourself.
