## What the throne says {#definition}

A throne is a chair with a job. You don’t sit on it to rest; you sit on it because the place is yours and others come to you. **The throne says: this person holds a position, and they aren’t going anywhere.** Picture the head of the table at a family lunch — any chair would do, and everyone still knows whose it is.

{{< zoom "throne" "lead" >}}

It’s the opposite of the horse or the road. On a throne, power has stopped moving and become a place.

## Rams, cushions, butterflies and a seat in the sea {#where}

The Emperor sits on a great grey stone block with four ram’s heads carved into it, armour showing under his red robe. The hardest seat in the deck, and the one built to last.

The Empress leans back on a red-cushioned seat in a field of wheat, a heart-shaped shield with the sign of Venus beside her. Her throne is soft; it feeds rather than commands.

The Hierophant and Justice are both enthroned between two [pillars](/library/tarot-symbols/pillars/): authority that belongs to an institution as much as to the person on the seat.

The High Priestess sits on a plain stone cube. No carving, no cushion: the simplest throne, for the one who says the least.

All eight Queens and Kings are seated too, and each throne tells you the suit: lions for the Queen of Wands, a stone throne standing in the sea for the King of Cups, butterflies for the Swords, bulls’ heads for the King of Pentacles. [The King](/library/numerology/king/) and [the Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) go through them one by one.

## How to read it {#reading}

Ask two questions: what is the throne made of, and does the person look at ease on it? Stone is permanence, and rigidity. Cushions are comfort and care. A throne standing in water is power that rests on emotions.

Then the lenses. Colour: the Emperor’s mature red sits close to [purple](/library/tarot-colours/purple/) — “the one chosen by a group”. A throne is given by others before it’s held. Number: the Emperor is [4](/library/numerology/number-4/), stability and structure, the fruit of work. Element: the Earth thrones (the Empress, the Hierophant) keep and feed; the Fire thrones (the Emperor, the Wands court) rule by drive.

Someone newly promoted who draws the Emperor might hear the throne ask: are you sitting in the place, or is the place sitting on you?

## The other side {#flip-side}

The throne’s flip side is the person who won’t get up. Power that stays put becomes power that can’t move: the manager who decides everything from his chair, the parent who still runs the house of grown-up children. My note on the Emperor: he gives orders, he doesn’t receive them — and, like fire, he can destroy everything.

The Empress’s soft throne has its own shadow: care that smothers, protection that keeps people small.
