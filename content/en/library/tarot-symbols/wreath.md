## What the wreath says {#definition}

A wreath is a ring of leaves or flowers, worn on the head or hung up. Ancient Greek athletes were crowned with leaves — olive at Olympia, laurel at Delphi — and English still says someone is “resting on their laurels”. **The wreath says: something has been won, and others have seen it.** Picture a runner at the finish with a medal round their neck: the race is over, and the medal is the proof for everyone else.

Unlike a metal crown, a wreath wilts. It belongs to one moment, one victory.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The World is framed by the biggest wreath in the deck: a tall oval of green leaves tied top and bottom with red ribbons, the dancer inside. The cycle is complete.

[The Six of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-wands/) has two. The rider on a white horse wears a laurel wreath, and another is tied near the top of the staff he carries. The victory parade.

The Chariot’s driver wears a laurel wreath under his star, and the Empress wears a green wreath under her crown of stars: success worn alongside authority.

On the Two of Cups, the woman wears laurel and the man a ring of red flowers — two people crowned for the same agreement.

The Seven of Cups offers a laurel wreath in one of its seven cups, and a skull is drawn on that cup. Victory as one fantasy among others.

Leaves crown the Fool’s head too, and flowers crown the child on the Sun: lighter wreaths, more joy than triumph.

## How to read it {#reading}

When a wreath comes up, something has been achieved, or is about to be recognised: an exam passed, a project delivered, a reconciliation sealed. Look at where it sits. On a head, it’s a personal success; around a whole figure, as on the World, it’s a cycle closed.

Lenses: green, in [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/green/), is growth and life — the wreath is a victory that’s still alive. Number: the Six of Wands is 6, “3 exalted”: success, triumph, a moment you can rest. [The advice of 6](/library/numerology/number-6/) is to stay calm and humble while the results flow. The World is 21, which comes back to 3: the machine running well.

Say someone draws the Six of Wands after months of effort. The wreath confirms the win. The next card says what they do with it.

## The other side {#flip-side}

Leaves wilt. The wreath’s flip side is resting on your laurels, or needing the parade. The rider on the Six of Wands looks ahead while the crowd walks beside him; turn that around and you get someone who can’t act without applause.

The Seven of Cups shows the other risk: the wreath as a daydream, a victory imagined among six other fantasies, with a skull on the cup.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

Read the wreath after the colour, the number and the element; [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) gives the order. Then look at [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/) and see what closes inside its wreath. For metal rather than leaves, go to the [crown](/library/tarot-symbols/crown/).
