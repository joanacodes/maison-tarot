## What the snake says {#definition}

A snake moves without legs, sheds its skin, and is rarely seen coming. Those three facts make the whole symbol. **The snake is the desire, or the change, that slips in without asking.** Think of an idea that comes back at three in the morning, a little more convincing each time.

It isn’t only a villain. Shedding is renewal: the old skin left on a stone, the animal carrying on without it.

## Coiled on a tree, sliding down a wheel, rising from a cup {#where}

On [the Lovers](/library/tarot-cards/the-lovers/), a green snake coils around the trunk of the fruit tree behind the woman. The scene borrows from the Garden of Eden, and the choice the card is about comes with its temptation built in.

On the Wheel of Fortune, a yellow snake slides down the left side of the wheel while a red jackal-headed figure rises on the right. What goes down, what comes up: the snake is the descent.

On the Seven of Cups, among the visions floating in the clouds, a spotted yellow snake rises from one cup. One option out of seven, and not the most obvious one to refuse.

On the Two of Cups, a caduceus rises between the two cups — the staff of Hermes, with two snakes wound around it — under a winged lion’s head. On the 1909 print the snakes are drawn as two black curling lines. Here they’re joined, not opposed: two forces bound into one agreement.

On the Magician, Waite’s guide describes the belt as a snake swallowing its own tail. On the print it’s a small blue-grey band, so you’ll need to look closely.

## How to read it {#reading}

When a snake comes up, ask whether something is being shed or something is slipping in. Same animal, two directions.

Lenses: the Lovers is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card, and in my notes Air is communication, good or bad — the viper’s tongue. The snake there is the persuasive voice, inside you or outside. The Seven of Cups is [7](/library/numerology/number-7/): indecision, too many choices, and the snake is the option that looks like any other. Colour: the snakes on the Wheel and the Seven of Cups are yellow, will and pride; the Lovers’ snake is green, growth and appetite.

With the Lovers and the Seven of Cups side by side, there are two snakes and two choices, and the useful question isn’t “which one?” but “whose voice is talking me into it?”

## The other side {#flip-side}

The snake’s shadow is the obvious one: deceit, the friend who says what you want to hear, the temptation you call an opportunity. But there’s a second, less obvious one: refusing every change in case it’s a snake. Not everything that sheds a skin is lying to you.

The Magician’s snake biting its tail adds a third: the loop that feeds on itself, close cousin of the [lemniscate](/library/tarot-symbols/lemniscate/) above his head.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

Look again at the [Wheel of Fortune](/library/tarot-cards/wheel-of-fortune/): on which side is the snake, and on which side are you?
