## What the sun says {#definition}

The sun is light, warmth and a day beginning. It’s also visibility: under the sun, there’s nowhere to hide. **When a sun is drawn on a card, something is out in the open — or about to be.**

{{< zoom "sun" "lead" >}}

An everyday example: the morning after a long conversation, when what felt hopelessly tangled at midnight looks obvious over coffee. Nothing changed but the light.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On the Fool, a white sun hangs in the top corner of a yellow sky. The departure happens in full daylight: nothing about it is secret, including the cliff.

On the Lovers, an enormous sun fills the top of the card behind the angel. The choice between the two figures is made in plain view.

On Death, a small sun sits on the horizon between two towers, behind the rider. Rising or setting? The card doesn’t say. In my notes Death is linked to the harvest and to starting again, and that sun holds both.

On Temperance, a rayed light rises between two peaks where the path ends, and the angel wears a small circle with a dot on the forehead — the old sign for the sun.

On the Moon, the moon itself is drawn inside a rayed sun-disc: the light of night is borrowed from the day.

And the Sun, XIX, gives the whole card to it, with a face, straight and wavy rays, and a child riding beneath.

## How to read it {#reading}

Ask what the light is falling on. On the Fool, it’s a beginning; on the Lovers, a choice; on Death, an ending you can see coming. The sun doesn’t judge what it shows. It shows.

The sun is [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) in this deck: joy, optimism, pride, the inner child, will. It belongs with [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/), which warms and lights the night. And it leans towards [masculine energy](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) in my sense of the word: what shows itself, what goes on stage.

## The other side {#flip-side}

Too much sun burns. A light that never goes down becomes exposure: being seen all the time, performing the good mood instead of having it. On my page about the Sun in astrology I call it the shop window, not the shop — a fair warning for the symbol too.

And Death’s sun is a reminder that the sun also sets. Something can be lit up at the very moment it ends.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The sun gives you the light; the card’s [number](/library/numerology/) and its element tell you what the light is falling on.
