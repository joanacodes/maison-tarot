## What the lemniscate says {#definition}

The lemniscate is the sign ∞: a figure eight lying on its side. The name comes from the Latin for a ribbon, and the mathematician John Wallis used the sign for infinity in 1655. Follow the line with a finger and you never reach an end; you cross the middle and come back round.

**The lemniscate says: the energy goes round, and nothing is lost on the way.** Think of the figure of eight a skater traces on the ice: two loops, one line, and the crossing in the middle is where the weight shifts.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The Magician has it floating above his head like a halo. One hand raises a wand, the other points to the ground: what comes in at the top goes out at the bottom, and the loop keeps it moving.

Strength has the same sign above the woman calming the lion. Here the loop is self-control: the energy isn’t stopped, it’s kept circulating.

[The Two of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-pentacles/) brings it down to everyday life. A young man in a tall hat juggles two pentacles held in a long green band that crosses itself into an eight, while ships ride high waves behind him. Keeping two things going at once.

Some readers also see it in the ribbons that tie the World’s wreath. I leave it out: on the 1909 print they cross rather than loop.

## How to read it {#reading}

When the lemniscate is on the card, ask what’s circulating, and whether it’s flowing or going round in circles. It’s a sign of mastery — of a movement, not of a thing.

Count the numbers. The lemniscate is an 8 lying down, and in my notes [8](/library/numerology/number-8/) is the empire: control, lasting stability, a gate between the tangible and the intangible. Strength is card 8, and the Two of Pentacles is a 2 drawing an 8. Then the elements: the Magician is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card, the mind; Strength is Fire, drive; the Two of Pentacles is Earth, daily life. Infinity in thought, in heat, and in the diary.

Say someone draws the Two of Pentacles while holding down a job and a house move at the same time. The eight says it can last — as long as the band keeps moving.

## The other side {#flip-side}

A loop that never ends can also be a loop you can’t get out of. The lemniscate’s flip side is the same worry turning all night, the same argument replayed, the juggler who can’t put either pentacle down.

For the Magician, my note is plain: he knows how to do everything, and he can be a liar if you trust him blindly. Endless skill is also an endless capacity to pretend.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The sign is small; read it after the colour, the number and the element — [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts them in order. Then compare [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/) and [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/): the same sign, one turned outwards, one inwards.
