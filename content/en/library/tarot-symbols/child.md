## What the child says {#definition}

A child is someone at the beginning: small, new, open, and dependent on others. **The child says: something here is young — a beginning, a part of you that hasn’t hardened, or someone who needs looking after.** Think of a five-year-old asking “why?” for the tenth time: tiring, and also the question nobody else in the room dared to ask.

A child on a card is usually beside an adult. Look at which one.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On Death, a small child kneels in front of the white horse, facing the rider, while a young woman beside it turns her face away and a bishop prays. The child looks at what the adults can’t.

On Judgement, a child rises from the open coffin in the middle, back to us, arms open, between a man and a woman. The call reaches the whole family.

On [the Six of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-cups/), a child hands a cup filled with white flowers to a smaller one, in an old village with a stone tower behind them. In my notes the card is childhood, flowering again, taking time for yourself.

On the Ten of Cups, two children dance hand in hand beside their parents, under a rainbow of cups. On the Ten of Pentacles, a child peeks out from behind the woman, near the dogs: the youngest of three generations.

On the Six of Swords, a small figure sits huddled beside a hooded woman in the boat, carried across the water by the ferryman.

And the Sun has the deck’s best-known child, riding a white horse; [its card page](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/) reads it in full.

## How to read it {#reading}

When a child comes up, ask which kind it is. A beginning: a project, a relationship, an idea taking its first steps. A memory: the Six of Cups, where the past comes back with flowers in its hands. Or a responsibility: the child carried across the water on the Six of Swords, who depends on an adult’s choices.

Lenses: [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/), in my grid, is the inner child — joy, optimism, self-expression. Number: the Six of Cups and the Six of Swords are both 6, and in [my numerology table](/library/numerology/number-6/) 6 is when you can finally rest and take a holiday. Their elements add the rest: Water, what you feel about it; Air, the decision you make for it.

Say someone draws the Six of Cups after years without drawing, painting or playing music. The children might say: go back to the thing you loved before anyone marked it.

## The other side {#flip-side}

A child can be a way of not growing up: waiting to be looked after, leaving the decision to someone bigger. The Six of Cups can turn into nostalgia so sweet that you stop living in the present.

And a child is easily read too literally. Not every child in a reading is a real child; before anything else, I ask what the person is beginning.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

A child is a detail; read it after the colour, the number and the element — [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts them in order. Then look at [the Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/): the parents look up at the rainbow, and the children look at each other.
