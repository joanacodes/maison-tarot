## What the tower says {#definition}

A tower is a building that goes up rather than out. It sees far, it’s seen from far, and it keeps people out. **The tower says: here is something built to protect you and to watch the horizon — check whether it still serves you.** Think of a church tower in a village: you find your way by it, and it was also where people once climbed to see who was coming.

This page follows the towers standing in the background of the deck. The one struck by lightning has [its own card page](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/).

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On Death, two grey towers stand on the horizon on the right, a sun between them, beyond the river. On [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/), two towers stand at either side of the card, and the path runs between them into the mountains. Same layout on two cards: a gateway seen from far off. The [pillars](/library/tarot-symbols/pillars/) page calls them cousins.

On the Two of Wands, a man stands high on a stone terrace — the top of a castle wall or a tower; the card doesn’t show which — holding a globe and looking out over the water. Here you’re the one in the tower.

On the Four of Wands, a castle with a round tower and a red pointed roof stands behind the two figures raising flowers under the garland. On the Ten of Pentacles, a tall square tower rises beyond the archway. Towers as home, and as what a family has built.

On the Five of Cups, a ruined keep stands across the river, a bridge further along. On the Eight of Swords, a castle sits on a rock behind the bound woman. On the Seven of Cups, one cup holds a tower on a rock: one fantasy among seven.

Walled towns and castle towers also stand behind the Chariot, the King of Pentacles and the children of the Six of Cups.

## How to read it {#reading}

When a tower comes up, ask whether you’re inside it, living in its shadow, or looking at it from far away. Inside, on the Two of Wands: a safe place to plan from, the world in your hand. Behind you, on the Four of Wands and the Ten of Pentacles: the home you’ve built. In my notes the Four of Wands is foundations, marriage, building a family. Far off, on Death and the Moon: the way out, not reached yet.

Lenses: towers are stone, and stone is [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) — in my grid, neutrality, stability, no particular will. A grey tower holds without saying anything. Number: 4, the structure, is the Four of Wands, and in [my numerology table](/library/numerology/number-4/) its advice is to stay flexible.

Say someone draws the Two of Wands while deciding whether to leave a secure job. The tower might say: you’re safe up here, and you can see a long way. The question is whether you’ll take the stairs down.

## The other side {#flip-side}

A tower protects, and it isolates. On the Eight of Swords the castle stands behind a woman who can’t see it; on the Five of Cups the keep is a ruin across the water, a shelter he can’t reach from where he stands. The Seven of Cups’ tower sits in a cup floating on clouds.

And every standing tower in the deck has one cousin that falls. A structure built only to keep people out ends up cracking from the inside.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

A tower is a detail; colour, number and element come first, and [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) explains the order. Then look again at [the Two of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-wands/): he has the globe in his hand and the wall in front of him.
