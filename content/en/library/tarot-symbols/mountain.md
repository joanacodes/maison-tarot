## What the mountain says {#definition}

A mountain is an obstacle and a viewpoint at once. It’s hard to climb, cold at the top, and it isn’t going anywhere. But from the summit you can see the whole valley you came from. **A mountain on a card is a challenge that doesn’t move: you go round it, over it, or you stay at its foot.**

{{< zoom "mountain" "lead" >}}

An everyday example: an exam you have to resit. It will be there in June whatever you do. The only choice is how you climb.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On the Fool, white peaks rise behind the young man as he steps towards the cliff edge. The hard part of the journey is already on the horizon; he hasn’t noticed.

On the Lovers, a single peak stands between the two figures, under the angel. Whatever they choose, they’ll choose it with something big between them.

On the Emperor, bare, rocky mountains rise on both sides of his throne. Nothing grows there: authority that has been built on rock.

On [the Hermit](/library/tarot-cards/the-hermit/), he stands on a snowy summit with his lantern. The climb is behind him.

On Temperance, the path from the pool winds towards two peaks with a light rising between them. On Judgement, a line of white peaks stands behind the figures rising from their coffins.

On the Eight of Cups, a man walks away from his cups towards dark, rocky mountains under the moon. In my notes, the card is moving on from what you’re already doing.

On the Ace of Swords, a range of mountains lies under the sword, far below the crown.

Mountains also show through the rose arch of the Ace of Pentacles, and the Tower is built on a jagged peak.

## How to read it {#reading}

Ask where the person is in relation to the mountain. In front of it (the Fool, the Eight of Cups), the effort is coming. On top (the Hermit), it’s done and the view is earned. Behind a throne (the Emperor), it’s the hard ground power stands on.

With the lenses: a mountain is [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) at its most solid — tenacity, endurance, and stubbornness. It’s the image of the [fixed mode](/library/astrological-modes/fixed/): stable, enduring, slow to move. And the Hermit’s summit fits his number: in my notes [9](/library/numerology/number-9/) is the finish line in sight, last efforts, patience — but keep moving.

## The other side {#flip-side}

A mountain isolates. The Hermit is alone up there; the Emperor’s mountains are bare. Its shadow is the height that cuts you off — pride, coldness, a viewpoint nobody else can reach.

The other shadow is staring at the mountain instead of taking the first step. An obstacle that doesn’t move can become a reason not to move either.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The mountain tells you the effort is real; the number tells you how far up you are, the colour how it feels — the Emperor’s orange rock and the Hermit’s white snow aren’t the same climb. [The complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts the lenses in order.
