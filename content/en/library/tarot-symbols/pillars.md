## What the pillars say {#definition}

A pillar holds something up; two pillars make an entrance. That’s the whole symbol. **Two pillars mark a threshold, and whoever sits between them is the keeper of the passage.** Think of the two stone posts of a farm gate, or the columns in front of a courthouse: before you’ve read a single word, you know you’re about to go in somewhere with rules.

In tarot, pillars also say “two”. Two forces, two options, two sides that hold the roof up precisely because they stand apart.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The High Priestess sits between a black pillar marked B and a white one marked J, with a veil of palms and pomegranates hung between them. The letters are traditionally read as Boaz and Jachin, the pillars of Solomon’s Temple. She doesn’t get up to let you through.

The Hierophant is enthroned between two grey stone pillars, two tonsured figures at his feet and two crossed keys on the floor. Here the threshold is an institution: you go through by being taught.

Justice sits between two grey pillars as well, with a purple curtain hung behind her. The pillars frame the verdict: what’s weighed here is weighed inside a structure, not on a whim.

The Chariot carries its own: posts hold up the starry canopy above the driver. A doorway that travels.

Two cards have cousins rather than pillars: the two towers on the horizon of [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/) and of Death, a gateway seen from far off. The [tower](/library/tarot-symbols/tower/) has its own page.

## How to read it {#reading}

When pillars come up, ask what’s being kept on either side. The pillars don’t move, and the person between them doesn’t either. The card is about a passage you haven’t made yet, and the conditions for making it.

Then lay the other lenses on top. Colour first: black and white on the High Priestess is two opposites held together; grey on the Hierophant and Justice is, in [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/grey/), neutrality and stability — a frame that doesn’t take sides. Number next: the High Priestess is 2, and Justice, card 11, comes back to 2 when you add its digits. Both sit between two pillars. [The number 2](/library/numerology/number-2/) is binding, signing, weighing: read the contract before you commit.

Say someone draws the Hierophant just as a new job is on the table. The pillars say there’s a structure here, with its own rules, and you’ll go in on its terms or not at all. That isn’t bad news; it’s information.

## The other side {#flip-side}

Pillars hold, and they also hem in. Two fixed positions with someone stuck in the middle: the person who can’t choose between two options and calls it balance. Or the frame becomes a cage — the institution that repeats what it was given without asking why.

When the cards around it are heavy, the question shifts from “how do I get through?” to “who decided there were only two pillars?”

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

A symbol is a detail; it doesn’t replace the card. Read the pillars after the colour, the number and the element, never instead of them — [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts the lenses in order. Then go back to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/) and look at what she’s sitting in front of.
