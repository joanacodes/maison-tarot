## What the stars say {#definition}

A star is a point of light, very far away, that doesn’t move much from night to night. You can’t reach it, but for centuries people found their way by it. **When stars are drawn on a card, there’s a direction, even if the goal is still far off.**

An everyday example: the thing you keep coming back to when you think about the next five years — the job you’d do if money didn’t count, the place you’d live. You’re not there. But you know which way it is.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On [the Star](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/), XVII, one large eight-pointed yellow star shines over seven smaller white ones, above a woman pouring water into a pool and onto the land. It’s the sky after the Tower: nothing is falling any more.

On the Empress, a crown of small white stars sits on her head. The one who feeds and creates is crowned with what guides.

On the Chariot, the canopy above the driver is sewn with stars, and a star sits on his crown. He drives forward under his own sky.

On the Hermit, the light in the lantern is drawn like a star, half hidden behind the bars. He holds his guide up for others, but not all of it shows.

On the Devil, a five-pointed star sits upside down between the horns. Same shape, turned over.

And on every Pentacle card, the suit sign is a five-pointed star inside a coin. The Ace of Pentacles shows it largest.

## How to read it {#reading}

Ask what is guiding the person, and how far away it is. On the Empress or the Chariot, the star is worn: the direction is part of who they are. On the Star, it’s in the sky: hope as something to steer by, not something you already hold.

Then add the lenses. The Star’s small stars are [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/), peace and what hasn’t been soiled; the big one is yellow, joy and optimism. The Star card is [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/): hope as a decision rather than a mood. And the Pentacle’s star belongs to Earth — value, the result of work.

## The other side {#flip-side}

A star is far. You can stare at it so long you forget to walk. That’s the shadow: the ideal that makes the real thing look poor, the goal so high it becomes a reason not to start.

The Devil shows another shadow: the guiding sign turned upside down, a direction that has become a chain. When a star appears in a heavy spread, ask whether the person is following it, or being pulled.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The star gives you the direction; the number and element tell you how far along the road you are. [The complete path](/learn-tarot/) lays the lenses out in order, so you can read the sky and the ground at the same time.
