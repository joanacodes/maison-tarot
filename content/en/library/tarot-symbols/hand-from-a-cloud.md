## What the hand from a cloud says {#definition}

A hand comes out of a cloud, with no arm or body behind it, and holds out the card’s suit. It’s what arrives from outside you: an opportunity, an offer, the start of something you didn’t build yourself. **The hand from a cloud is a beginning held out to you — taking it, and what comes after, is up to you.**

{{< zoom "hand-from-a-cloud" "lead" >}}

An everyday example: a friend forwards you a job advert you would never have found. You didn’t look for it. Now it’s in your inbox, and the rest is your move.

## Four Aces, and one cup nobody takes {#where}

On the Ace of Wands, the hand grips a wand that is still sprouting leaves, a few falling loose. Below, a castle on a hill and a river.

On the Ace of Cups, an open palm holds a cup from which five streams fall into a pond of water lilies, while a dove comes down towards it.

On the Ace of Swords, the hand grips an upright sword whose tip passes through a crown, with branches hanging from it and mountains far below.

On the Ace of Pentacles, an open palm holds out a coin marked with a five-pointed star, above a garden and an arch of roses with mountains beyond.

In all four Aces, the hand is ringed with small rays of light.

And once more on [the Four of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-cups/): a smaller hand, from a small cloud, offers a cup to a man sitting under a tree with his arms crossed, looking at the three cups already in front of him.

## How to read it {#reading}

Look at the grip. On the Wands and the Swords, the hand is closed around the handle: Fire and Air, the two suits that lean towards [masculine energy](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) — action, taking hold. On the Cups and the Pentacles, the palm is open: Water and Earth, which lean feminine — receiving, holding up. Same gesture of offering, two different ways of being given something.

Then the number. Every Ace is [a 1](/library/numerology/number-1/): in my notes, the initiator, the raw diamond, the start of a cycle. The advice that goes with it is humility — get ready to learn. The Four of Cups is a 4: stable, structured, and its advice in my notes is to stay flexible and open to possibilities you didn’t plan for. Which is exactly what the man under the tree isn’t doing.

## The other side {#flip-side}

The hand can make you wait. Its shadow is passivity: sitting under the tree until something comes out of the sky, instead of going to look. Not every beginning arrives by cloud.

The other shadow is the Four of Cups itself: an offer refused because you’re busy counting what you already have. And sometimes that refusal is right — not every offer is for you. The card doesn’t decide which. It asks you to look up.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The hand tells you something is being offered; the suit tells you in which part of life, the colour how it feels. The hand comes out of a [cloud](/library/tarot-symbols/cloud/): read that weather too.
