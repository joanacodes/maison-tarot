## What the crown says {#definition}

A crown sits on the head, and it’s rarely put there by the person who wears it. **The crown says: this authority has been recognised — by a people, a group, or a victory.** Think of the captain’s armband in a football team: it means nothing until the others agree to follow it.

Because it sits on the head, it’s also about the mind. Who’s in charge up there, and on whose behalf?

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The Emperor wears a gold crown set with stones, a sign of office over a soldier’s armour. The Empress wears a crown of stars over a green wreath. The Hierophant’s tiara rises in three tiers, authority stacked on authority. The High Priestess wears a crown of the moon: a full disc between two horns. Justice wears a gold crown with battlements and a single square stone.

The Ace of Swords is the crown on its own: a sword held up from a cloud pierces a gold crown, an olive branch and a palm hanging from it.

Then come the falls. On the Tower, the top of the tower is a crown, and lightning knocks it off; one of the two figures falling head first wears a crown too. On Death, a king lies on the ground and his crown has rolled under the horse’s hoof.

The Four of Pentacles balances a pentacle on his crown and clutches another to his chest: a crown worn to keep things, not to serve.

One to look for: Waite’s guide mentions a crown seen faintly in the light at the end of Temperance’s path. On the 1909 print it’s no more than a dotted line inside the sun. A crown made of leaves is a different symbol — see the [wreath](/library/tarot-symbols/wreath/).

## How to read it {#reading}

When a crown comes up, ask who gave it, and whether it’s still deserved. A crown on a calm head is recognised authority: a role earned, a decision people trust. A crown in the air or on the ground is that recognition in question.

Colour: gold, in [my grid](/library/tarot-colours/gold-and-brown/), is value — what you have. Element: the Ace of Swords puts the crown in [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/), the element that decides, so the crown there is the right decision, the one only you could make. Number: the Emperor and the Four of Pentacles are both [4](/library/numerology/number-4/) — structure on one, structure clung to on the other.

Say someone draws the Tower next to the Emperor. The crown is what’s at stake: a title, a reputation, a position everyone thought was permanent.

## The other side {#flip-side}

The crown’s flip side is forgetting where it came from. Authority recognised by a group can be withdrawn by the group, and the person who treats it as private property is the one most surprised when it falls. In my notes, Air’s archetype is the leader who makes the decisions nobody else can — and can become a tyrant. The crown is where that turns.

There’s a quieter shadow too: the Four of Pentacles, holding on so tightly that the crown becomes a lid.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

A crown is a detail; read it after the colour, the number and the element — [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) sets them out. Then look at [the Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/) again: the crown is the first thing to go.
