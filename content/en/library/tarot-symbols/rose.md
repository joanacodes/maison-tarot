## What the rose says {#definition}

A rose is beauty you can hold, and it pricks you if you hold it carelessly. It’s the flower of declarations: nobody gives a dozen red roses by accident. **The rose says: something here is loved, wanted, cultivated — and its colour tells you how.** Think of a garden someone has tended for twenty years: roses don’t grow like that on their own.

{{< zoom "rose" "lead" >}}

In the Rider-Waite-Smith, roses are red or white, and most of the meaning sits in that difference.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The Fool carries a small white rose in his raised hand as he walks towards the cliff. Innocence, held lightly.

On [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), red roses and white lilies grow in the garden at his feet, and more roses hang over the top of the card. Desire and clear intent, cultivated side by side by the man with all four tools on his table.

Strength wears roses: a chain of flowers with red roses in it winds round the woman’s waist and down her dress, and leaves and flowers crown her hair. What links her to the lion is soft.

On [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), the black banner carries a large white rose with five petals. The heaviest card in the deck flies a flower.

On the Hierophant, the tonsured figure on the left wears a robe patterned with red roses; the one on the right wears white flowers. The Two of Wands has a cross of red and white flowers carved into the stone on the left, usually read as roses and lilies. The Queen of Pentacles sits under a bower of red roses that runs across the top of her card.

## How to read it {#reading}

When roses come up, look at the colour first, then at what’s being done with them. Held, on the Fool: an ideal you carry. Grown, on the Magician and the Queen of Pentacles: something cultivated, which takes work and gives back. Worn, on Strength: a bond made of tenderness. Flown on a flag, on Death: what you keep when everything else goes.

Lenses: in [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/red/), red is passion, action, strength; [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/) is purity, innocence, peace. A red rose wants; a white rose promises.

Say someone draws Death next to the Queen of Pentacles. One white rose on a black flag, a whole bower of red ones over a throne. The reading might be: something is ending, and what you’ve grown with care is still there to come home to. In my notes Death isn’t about dying; it’s the harvest, and starting again.

## The other side {#flip-side}

Roses have thorns, whether or not the drawing shows them. A red rose can be desire that won’t take no for an answer; the Queen’s bower can be a comfort so thick you never leave the garden. A white rose can be an ideal too pure to live with — the Fool admiring his flower one step from the edge.

A rose is also a cliché. If every red flower in a reading means love, you stop seeing the card around it.
