## What the veil says {#definition}

A veil is a thin cloth hung in front of something. It doesn’t lock it away; it lets you guess at the shape. **The veil says: there is something here, and it isn’t shown yet.** Think of a dust sheet over furniture in a closed-up country house — you know it’s a chair, you don’t know which chair.

In a reading, it’s the knowledge that exists but hasn’t been handed over: a secret, an intuition you haven’t put into words, a part of the story the person isn’t ready to look at.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

The High Priestess sits in front of a veil hung between two [pillars](/library/tarot-symbols/pillars/), printed with palm leaves and pomegranates. A long blue mantle also falls from her crown over her head. Two veils, then: one behind her, one on her.

Justice has a purple curtain hung between her two pillars. Nothing is hidden from her; what you don’t see is what stands behind the judgement — the reasons, the institution.

The Seven of Cups puts one inside a cup. Among seven visions floating in the clouds, the middle one is a figure covered by a white cloth, glowing red at the edges. Of all the choices on the card, it’s the one you can’t see.

The Nine of Cups hangs a blue cloth from the shelf of cups behind the seated man, all the way to the floor. What’s under it, nobody knows, and he doesn’t seem to mind.

The Queen of Pentacles wears a green veil falling from her crown down her back: mastery that doesn’t put itself on display.

## How to read it {#reading}

A veil asks for patience, not force. When it comes up, I don’t try to name what’s behind it. I say that something is behind it, and let the person decide whether they want to look.

Its colour tells you what kind of hiding it is. Blue, on the High Priestess and the Nine of Cups, is emotion and water in [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/blue/): a feeling held back. Purple, on Justice, is “the one chosen by a group”: an office, a role. White, on the Seven of Cups, is innocence — a choice that looks pure because you can’t see it. The High Priestess is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card, and still water hides its depth the same way.

Say someone draws the Seven of Cups when they’re torn between offers. The veiled cup is worth a question: which option are you refusing to look at properly?

## The other side {#flip-side}

A veil protects, and it also excuses you from looking. In my notes on shadow work, it takes a long road and a split second: a veil you take off — but first you have to acknowledge the veil. The flip side is knowing it’s there and deciding it isn’t.

It can also be a façade: someone who shows a calm surface and lets you imagine depths that aren’t there. Not every veil hides a treasure. And if what’s behind it feels heavier than a card can carry, that’s a conversation for [a therapist](/disclaimers/#therapy) rather than for the deck.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The veil is a detail; the colour, the number and the element come first, and [the complete path](/learn-tarot/) teaches them in that order. Then sit with [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/) for a minute and ask what you’d want to know before you asked her to lift it.
