## What the river says {#definition}

A river is water with a direction. It comes from a source, goes to the sea, and doesn’t wait for anyone. It also separates two banks. **A river on a card is time and feeling moving on, whether you follow them or not.**

An everyday example: a friendship that slowly drifted. Nobody decided anything; the current did the work. Years later you’re on different banks, and you can’t quite say when it happened.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

On Death, a river runs behind the white horse, with a small boat sailing on it. In my notes Death is the harvest and starting again: the river keeps going past the ending.

On [the Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/), a river runs between the cloaked figure and a ruined keep. A bridge on the right leads across. He is looking at the three spilled cups and doesn’t see it. In my notes this is the most negative card in the deck — alone with your problems — and the river is everything still moving around him.

On the Knight of Cups, a stream winds at the horse’s hooves. He rides slowly, cup held out, following the water.

On the Ten of Cups, a river winds through the green country towards the house, under the rainbow of cups. The same water, at peace.

On the Six of Swords, a ferryman poles a boat across water that is rough on one side and smooth ahead, towards a near bank. A crossing.

On the Ace of Wands, a river runs through the landscape below the hand; on the Eight of Wands, the wands fly over open country with a river below. On the Chariot, water runs behind the sphinxes, between the driver and the city.

## How to read it {#reading}

Ask which way the water is going, and where the person stands in relation to it. Beside the river (Five of Cups), they’re watching life pass. On it (Six of Swords), they’re being carried across. Following it (Knight of Cups), they’re going with their feelings. The river under the Ten of Cups is the same current, arrived home.

With the lenses: a river is [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/), so it’s about feeling. But it’s water that moves, which in my notes means strong emotion — not a still pool, which is the [water close at hand](/library/tarot-symbols/water/), and not the open horizon of [the sea](/library/tarot-symbols/sea/).

## The other side {#flip-side}

A river can carry you where you didn’t choose to go. Its shadow is drifting: letting the current decide, then being surprised by the bank you wash up on.

And a river is a border. The Five of Cups has a bridge, but a bridge only helps if you turn round. When a river appears in a heavy spread, look for the crossing — on this card, it’s already drawn.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

The river gives the movement; the card’s [number](/library/numerology/) gives the step — a 5 is a conflict, a 6 is success and the right to rest, a 10 is the corridor to what comes next. [The complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts those steps in order.
