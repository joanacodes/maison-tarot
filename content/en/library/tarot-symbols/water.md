## What water says {#definition}

Water flows, fills, reflects and takes the shape of its container. In my notes it’s emotions, relationships and hidden strengths: it carries you from A to B when you let it, and it can drown you. Liquid water also seeps into anything with time — a neglected emotion can damage the foundations, like mould slowly eating a house. **Water on a card is a feeling, and the drawing tells you whether it’s moving or still.**

An everyday example: the glass of water someone pours you when you arrive upset. Nobody has solved anything yet. But something is being looked after.

## Where it appears in the deck {#where}

This page follows water close at hand. Rivers have [their own page](/library/tarot-symbols/river/), and so does [the sea](/library/tarot-symbols/sea/).

On [Temperance](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/), the angel pours water from one cup into the other, one foot in a pool and one on the bank. Water handled with patience, neither held nor spilled.

On the Star, the woman pours one jug into the pool and the other onto the land, where it runs off in little streams. She gives back to both.

On the Ace of Cups, five streams fall from the cup into a pond covered with water lilies. The cup overflows; it isn’t meant to hold everything.

On the Empress, a waterfall runs down behind her, on the right. The one who feeds has a source.

On the High Priestess, the robe pours down at her feet like water. In my notes, her robe looks like a waterfall: still water, what we don’t see.

On the Moon, a crayfish climbs out of a pool at the bottom of the card: what lives in the water comes up.

On the Eight of Swords, puddles lie around the bound woman’s feet.

## How to read it {#reading}

Look at what the water is doing. Poured from one vessel to another, it’s an emotion being worked with. Falling and overflowing, it’s more feeling than the moment can hold. Standing in a pool, it’s settled — or stuck. My notes put it plainly: moving water is strong emotion, calm water is emotion that has settled.

Then the lenses. Water belongs with [the Cups and the Water element](/library/four-elements/water-element/). In this deck it’s mostly [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/): emotion, purifying, vitality. And it leans towards feminine energy — what works without showing itself.

## The other side {#flip-side}

Water that isn’t looked after stagnates. The puddles of the Eight of Swords are the image: the feeling left lying where it fell. In my notes that card is being a prisoner of yourself while you have every tool — you can take off the blindfold.

And too much water drowns. Fire destroys at once; neglected water destroys deeper, over longer.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

Water tells you the reading is about feeling; the number and colour tell you how much, and in what state. [The complete path](/learn-tarot/) lays the lenses out one after the other.
