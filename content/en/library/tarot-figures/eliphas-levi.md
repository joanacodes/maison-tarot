Éliphas Lévi is the pen name of Alphonse-Louis Constant, a Paris writer who, in the 1850s, turned the twenty-two trumps into an alphabet. He tied each one to a Hebrew letter, starting Aleph on the Magician. Nearly every occult tarot since has had to answer him — including, in its own way, the deck I read.

## Who he was {#who}

Alphonse-Louis Constant was born in Paris in 1810, the son of a shoemaker. He trained for the priesthood at the seminary of Saint-Sulpice and left in 1836 without becoming a priest. In the 1840s he wrote as a radical, and one book, *La Bible de la liberté* (1841), earned him months in prison.

Around 1854 he took a new name: Éliphas Lévi, his first names put into Hebrew. Under it he published *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*, two volumes that came out between 1854 and 1856, and later *La Clef des grands mystères* (1861) and other books on magic. Read in French and, from 1896, in Waite’s English translation, they shaped a generation of occultists on both sides of the Channel. He died in 1875.

## What he did for tarot {#tarot}

*Dogme et rituel* is built on the tarot. Each of its two volumes has twenty-two chapters, one for each trump and each Hebrew letter, and Lévi called the trumps keys. His table follows the numbers of the Tarot de Marseille: Aleph for card I, the Magician (the Bateleur), Beth for card II, and so on to Tav for the World. The Fool, which has no number, takes Shin and sits between Judgement and the World.

He also drew. His Chariot is pulled by two sphinxes instead of horses, and his Baphomet, a winged figure with a goat’s head, became a model for later Devils. In his guide to the 1909 deck, [Arthur Edward Waite](/library/tarot-figures/arthur-edward-waite/) wrote of the Chariot: “I have accepted the variation of Éliphas Lévi; two sphinxes thus draw his chariot.” [Pamela Colman Smith](/library/tarot-figures/pamela-colman-smith/)’s Devil borrows the pose of Lévi’s Baphomet too.

**Lévi’s lasting idea is that the trumps form an alphabet: a closed set of signs you can read one by one.** The French occultists who came after him built on his table — [Oswald Wirth](/library/tarot-figures/oswald-wirth/) printed his letters on the cards in 1889 — and [the Golden Dawn](/library/tarot-history/golden-dawn-tarot/) reworked it in London from 1888. The whole period is told in [occult tarot in the nineteenth century](/library/tarot-history/occult-tarot-19th-century/).

## What he did not do {#myths}

He didn’t invent the link between tarot and the Hebrew letters. The Comte de Mellet had set the trumps beside the letters in 1781, in the volume that made [Court de Gébelin](/library/tarot-figures/court-de-gebelin/) famous. Lévi turned a passing idea into a system and built two books on it.

He didn’t design a deck. He drew a handful of images for his books; the cards came later, when Wirth drew the trumps with Lévi’s letters on them.

He wasn’t a rabbi or a Jewish kabbalist. He was a former Catholic seminarian who studied Hebrew sources and read them in his own way, Christian and occult at once. The Kabbalah is a Jewish tradition; what Lévi and the occultists after him made of it is something else, and [the Kabbalah section](/library/kabbalah/) keeps the two apart.

And his order isn’t the ancient one, because there isn’t one. Neither is the Golden Dawn’s. Both are nineteenth-century systems, and the Golden Dawn’s moved Aleph to the Fool.

## Why it matters for a reader today {#today}

This is the practical part. The card pages on this site follow the Golden Dawn: the Fool is Aleph, the Magician is Beth. Many French books follow Lévi and Wirth: the Magician is Aleph, and most cards get the letter just before the one you’ll see here. Neither table is a mistake. [The table of the 22 letters](/library/kabbalah/hebrew-letters-major-arcana/) sets them side by side, so you can tell at a glance which one a book is using.

I treat the letters as an extra layer, not a foundation. You can read the whole deck without a word of Hebrew; if a letter adds something to a card for you, keep it. Don’t take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself.

A good place to start is [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), the card Lévi put first.
