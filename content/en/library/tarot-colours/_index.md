## The grid {#the-grid}

| Colour | What it says | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Passion, action, strength, impulse | Masculine |
| Orange | Creativity, dryness, libido | — |
| Yellow | Joy, optimism, pride, the inner child, self-expression, will | — |
| Green | Nature, growth, birth, life, prosperity | — |
| Blue | Emotions, making pure, water, vitality | Feminine |
| White | Purity, innocence, peace | — |
| Black | Absence, energy absorbed, void, negative thoughts, emptying out, reality | — |
| Grey | Neutrality, absence of will, stability | — |
| Purple / fuchsia | The one chosen by a group | — |

Each colour will have its page, with the cards where it dominates and what it does there. Until then, the grid is enough to start.

## How to use this lens {#how}

Take a card and don't read its name. Ask: which colour takes up the most room? That's the card's climate. Then: which colour stands out, small, somewhere? That's the detail that counts.

The Fool walks under a yellow sky with a white dog: joy and innocence, before you even know he's called the Fool. The Five of Cups stands in black before spilled cups and a grey sky: absence, the neutrality of what no longer moves. You memorised nothing.

## Why start here {#why}

Because it's the only lens that needs *no* knowledge at all. The [number](/library/numerology/) assumes you know the cycle; the [element](/library/four-elements/) assumes you recognise the house. Colour, you could see at three years old. That's why the House's first [workshop](/workshops/) is the colours one, and why [the path](/learn-tarot/) starts here.

## What this lens doesn't do {#limits}

It doesn't say *where* the situation is — that's the number's job — nor *what exactly* it's about — that's the element's. It gives the tone. Three lenses laid over each other and the card speaks; one alone, and you have a climate. That's already a lot, and it's often what readers who recite are missing.
