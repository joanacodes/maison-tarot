## What purple says {#definition}

**The one chosen by a group.** That's the [colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/) in one line; the rest of the page says where to look and how to read.

## Where the colour appears in the deck {#where}

The King of Cups' cloak, the Queen of Swords' robe, touches on the Hierophant and on Judgement, the cloth of the Wheel of Fortune. Purple sits on the shoulders of those who carry an office.

## How to read it {#reading}

Purple says **appointed, mandated, chosen by others**. A purple card is about a responsibility received — a post, a role, a place others gave you. It's the colour of institutions and ranks, and it asks: for whom are you carrying this?

## The flip side {#flip-side}

Purple's flip side is the title without the merit — the chosen one who thinks himself special because he was chosen — and the loneliness of office. The House reminds you there are no Chosen Ones; only people chosen for a time.

## With the other lenses {#lenses}

Colour gives the tone; the [element](/library/four-elements/) says the field, the [number](/library/numerology/) the step. Lay all three and the card speaks. [The complete path](/learn-tarot/) puts them in order.
