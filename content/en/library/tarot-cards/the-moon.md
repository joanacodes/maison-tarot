## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A large moon fills the top of the card: a crescent with a face in profile, set inside a full disc with long rays. Drops fall from it towards the ground. Below, a dog and a wolf stand on either side of a path, both howling up at it. In the foreground, a crayfish crawls out of a pool. The path starts at the water, runs between the two animals, passes between two grey towers and winds on towards distant hills.

There is no person on this card — only animals, a path, and a light that isn’t the sun’s. **You can see the way, but not clearly.** The drawing as a whole is about the stretch of road you walk at night, while what lives in the water comes up whether you’re ready or not.

## By number: 18 {#number}

**18 — 1 + 8 = 9 — near completion, almost there.** Eighteen comes back to [9](/library/numerology/number-9/), the number of the Hermit: the finish line in sight, the end of a stage, last efforts, introspection — and patience, while you keep moving. That’s the Moon’s path: long, unclear, and nearly done.

In the Instagram story, 9 is the redesign around Reels: not yet another app, but everything that defined the old one is ending. On this card, that in-between feels like fog. It is still a 9: you’re closer to the end than to the start.

## By element: Water {#element}

The Moon is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card: emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. In my notes, water carries you from A to B when you let it, and can drown you when you fight it. It also seeps into everything with time: a neglected emotion slowly damages the foundations, like mould in a house.

The crayfish climbing out of the pool is that emotion surfacing. The Moon doesn’t create the worry; it shows you one that was already in the water.

## By colours {#colours}

[Blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) dominates, in the sky and the deep pool: emotion, water, feminine energy, what hides and what carries. The moon itself is yellow — the inner child, imagination, a will that’s still there in the dark. The towers are grey, neutral, two doorposts that take no side; the land is green, still growing. Read the colours before the card and you already have: a lot of emotion, a small steady light, and ground that’s alive under your feet.

## By astrology: Pisces {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Moon belongs to [Pisces](/library/astrology/pisces/), the sign whose verb is “I believe”. A fish lives where we can’t breathe: from outside the surface is still, and underneath the water teems. That’s this card — calm on top, a whole world below. Well placed, Pisces is the wisdom that waits for the right moment; badly placed, it’s the one who dived and no longer says when they’ll come up. Keep the lens if it helps; the card reads fine without it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Moon is uncertainty: you don’t have all the facts, your imagination fills the gaps, and some of what it fills them with is fear. It can also be intuition that has no words yet. The card’s plus is that it’s honest about the fog; its minus is deciding in the fog as if you could see. It doesn’t say someone is lying to you. It says you can’t see far yet.

Reversed, the fog is lifting slowly — or thickening, when you stop checking your fears against anything real: the 3 a.m. scenario rehearsed so often it starts to feel like a memory.

Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), who shows a façade and keeps the rest hidden, like the far side of the moon, it doubles the hidden side: there’s more here than you’re being shown. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/) — indecision, too many choices — it’s imagination running ahead of the facts. Next to [the Sun](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/), the card that follows it, the night is ending.

**The Moon doesn’t say something is wrong; it says you can’t see it clearly yet, so don’t decide as if you could.** For the usual questions, I’ve written about [the Moon in love](/blog/moon-tarot-love/) and about [uncertainty at work](/blog/moon-tarot-career/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Moon next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/).

Lenses first. Numbers: 18 comes back to 9, almost at the end; the Eight of Cups is 8, moving on from what you were already doing. Elements: Water and Water, so this is emotional before it’s practical. The pictures agree: the Eight of Cups has its own moon, above a figure walking away towards the mountains.

The sentence you’d say out loud: “You’re leaving something behind without being able to say yet what you’re walking towards. That isn’t a mistake; the path is there, even if you can’t see the end of it.”

Then check it with the person, and this is where the silence rule earns its keep. Tell me nothing, I ask before every reading, so that whatever comes back afterwards is a real answer and not an echo of what I was told. If it doesn’t land, note it as a miss.
