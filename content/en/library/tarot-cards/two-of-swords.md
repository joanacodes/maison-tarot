## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman sits on a grey stone bench in a long white robe, a white band tied over her eyes. Her arms are crossed over her chest, and each hand holds a sword whose blade rises past the opposite shoulder: two long swords spread in a wide V, perfectly balanced, heavy enough that holding them must cost her something. Her feet, in small yellow shoes, sit side by side on pale ground.

Behind her — and she has her back to it — a calm blue sea dotted with rocks and small islands, a far shore on the horizon. In the teal sky, a thin yellow crescent moon. Nobody else, no path, no boat. She can’t see the sea, the rocks or the moon, and she doesn’t move.

The whole drawing says one thing: **a balance kept by refusing to look.**

## By number: two {#number}

**two — to bind, to weigh, to cooperate.** Two is the contract between two parties, and its advice is the most sensible in the cycle: read the contract, weigh the pros and cons. The Two of Swords is that weighing done blindfold — two options held at exactly equal distance, so that neither ever wins. Instagram’s second stage was signing with investors, the first moment you can lose something other than your own time. Here the person is still at the table, pen in hand, not signing. The minus of [number 2](/library/numerology/number-2/) is on the card too: stubbornness, solitude, the mind talking to itself.

## By element: Air {#element}

[Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) is the element that decides, because it sees everything at once. In the Two of Swords, Air is turned against itself: the mind is so busy weighing that it can’t cut. It isn’t a lack of information; often the person knows exactly what the two options are. It’s that every new thought balances out the one before. Air can block or obsess, and this is the block — a decision held in mid-air, arms crossed.

## By colours {#colours}

The [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/) of the robe and the blindfold: peace, innocence — here a peace bought by not seeing. The grey of the stone bench: neutrality, no particular will. Behind her, [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) everywhere, sky and sea: emotions, water, what moves. That’s the key to the card. All the feeling is in the background, behind her back, while she sits in front in white and grey. The yellow of the moon and of her shoes is the only warm note — a little will, still there, at the very edges.

## By astrology: Moon in Libra {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Two of Swords is the Moon in the first decan of Libra, and its title was the Lord of Peace Restored. [Libra](/library/astrology/libra/) balances: it wants each side to leave with the same thing, and it freezes before a dilemma where no scale can be level. The Moon is what you feel when nobody’s watching. Together: a feeling kept under wraps for the sake of balance. The crescent is drawn right there in her sky, which makes this one of the attributions you can actually see. Keep the lens if it helps; the card reads well without it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Two of Swords is a choice postponed. Two options, two people, two versions of a life, held at arm’s length so as not to lose either. It can also be a truce: a conflict put on hold, a peace kept by not mentioning the subject. That calm is real, and sometimes useful — for a while.

Reversed, the blindfold slips. Either the decision comes all at once, the arms uncross and one sword drops: often a relief, sometimes a brutal one. Or it tips into excess — the person drowning in other people’s opinions, everyone’s advice at once, until the balance turns into confusion.

Next to [Justice](/library/tarot-cards/justice/), the difference jumps out: Justice holds the scale with her eyes open, so the decision will be made, and fairly. Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), another 2 under the Moon, it’s someone who knows more than they’re saying. Next to the [Eight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-swords/), the blindfold is back with ties around the body: the waiting has turned into a trap.

**The Two of Swords doesn’t say which side to choose; it says that not choosing has a cost.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Two of Swords next to the [Knight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-swords/). Lenses first. Two, then a Knight: the weighing, then movement. Air and Air: the whole spread is happening in the head. White and grey, then a rider charging under a windy sky.

You might say: “You’ve been putting off a choice for a while, and something or someone is about to make you go faster than you’d like.”

Then check: “Is there a decision you’ve been holding off? Is anyone pushing for an answer?” Let the person answer in their own words, and don’t fill in the blanks for them. I read; you decide — and with this card that’s the whole point, because nobody can take the blindfold off for someone else.
