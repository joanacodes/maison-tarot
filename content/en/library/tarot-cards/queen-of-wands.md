## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A queen sits facing us on a throne set on a grey stone platform. She wears a yellow robe, a pale grey cloak and a crown topped with green leaves. In her right hand she holds a tall sprouting wand; in her left, a sunflower. Her knees are apart and her feet firmly placed: she sits like someone at home.

Lions are everywhere — carved heads on both arms of the throne, two red lions standing on its high back among sunflowers. Behind her, a pale blue sky and, low on the left, the yellow point of a pyramid. And at her feet, right at the front of the card, a black cat sits facing us.

She isn’t looking at the cat, or at us; she looks off to one side. **Sunflowers, lions, a warmth sure of itself — and a small black shadow sitting at the front.**

## By number: the Queen {#number}

The [Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) rules from the inside, seated, with nothing to prove — and this one holds her wand loosely, a sunflower in the other hand. Hold without holding on, says the rank. At her best her warmth draws people in and lets them go again; at her worst she holds on to the room — the attention, the people, the last word — and calls it mastery.

An everyday example: the person whose kitchen everyone ends up in at a party. She hasn’t asked anyone to come. They come because it’s warm there.

## By element: Fire {#element}

Her suit is [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/), the energy that creates and motivates; her own share of it is [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/), the element of feeling and of what’s hidden. Fire with Water in it becomes warmth rather than blaze: the heat that gathers people instead of burning them.

That is also where her shadow lives. Neglected water seeps in slowly; a Fire queen with feelings she never voices can slide from warmth to jealousy without anyone noticing the moment it happened.

## By colours {#colours}

[Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) fills the card — robe, sunflower, pyramid: joy, optimism, self-expression, pride. She is the sunniest figure in the suit. The red of the lions adds strength and passion behind her back, and the grey of the cloak and the stone keeps her steady.

Then the cat, the only [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) in the picture: absence, absorbed energy, reality, the mind emptied. Here black isn’t a threat; it’s what makes the yellow believable. A queen with no shadow would be a poster, not a person.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Queen of Wands is confidence that warms: someone sure of their talent, generous with their energy, at ease being seen. It can be a person around you — or you, the day you stop asking permission to take up space. **Her fire gathers people; it doesn’t need to burn anyone to prove it’s there.**

Reversed, the warmth turns inward or sour: jealousy, a need to be the centre, generosity that comes with an invoice. Or the flame dims, and the person who usually lights up a room stops coming in without saying why. Look at the cat: what she isn’t saying is often where the card is.

Next to [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), the lions of her throne meet the woman who closes a lion’s jaws with her bare hands — mastery through gentleness, twice. Next to [the Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/), two women who feed others, one with wheat, one with warmth; my note on the Empress, that she can smother with care, applies here too. Next to the [King of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-wands/), the two Fire monarchs side by side: one gathers, the other directs.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Here the Queen of Wands comes out first, then the [Two of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-pentacles/) — a spread from imagination, not from memory. The Queen then 2 — inner mastery, then a balance between two sides; Fire then Earth — warmth, then the practical juggle; a seated woman in yellow, then a young man dancing with two coins inside a looped ribbon, ships on high waves behind him.

What the yellow queen and the juggler suggest to me: “Everyone warms themselves at your fire, and you’re juggling more than you show. The queen is sitting down; the hands aren’t.”

Then check: “Who keeps your fire going, while you keep everyone else’s going?” If nobody leans on them and nothing is being juggled, the reading missed: tell them, write it down, and leave the deck alone.
