## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A knight in full armour sits on a heavy black horse. The horse isn’t going anywhere: its legs are straight and planted, a sprig of green leaves tied to its bridle. The knight holds up a pentacle in a red gauntlet and looks at it. His helmet carries a crest of green leaves too; over the armour he wears a red tunic, and the horse’s harness is red.

Behind him, a ploughed field in long red-brown furrows, a green rise under the hooves, low hills, a few trees on the right. The sky is yellow. No road, no other rider, no castle to reach.

Of the four Knights, he’s the only one whose horse stands still. **Here, action starts by looking: the plan before the gallop, the field before the harvest.**

## By number: the Knight {#number}

The [Knight](/library/numerology/knight/) is his suit on the move, yet this one sits on a horse that doesn’t move. The action is real, but it belongs to someone who will set off only once he’s sure.

The rank asks a Knight to know where he’s going before he rides, and this one does little else. Everyone knows someone like him: the friend who gets twelve quotes before having the boiler fixed, and whose boiler then runs for twenty years. Pushed too far, that care becomes the thirteenth quote.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The Knight of Pentacles is Air of Earth: the suit is [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) — matter, value, the result of work — and his own share is [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/): thinking, analysis, decision. Air sees everything at once; inside Earth it becomes the plan, the calculation, the method. He looks at the coin because he’s working out what to do with it.

His energy is masculine, by my definition — acting, building, the tangible — in its most patient version. Earth gives him endurance: he will get there. It also gives him its classic flaw, stubbornness, and Air can add its own: the mind going round in circles. Together, at worst, the plan becomes a reason never to leave.

## By colours {#colours}

The horse is [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) and takes up half the card: absence, absorbed energy, emptying the mind — and reality. Black is the bare thing without varnish; this horse is all weight. The ploughed field is red-brown, [gold and brown](/library/tarot-colours/gold-and-brown/) on the colour pages: work, matter, patience. The red of his tunic and harness is drive, kept in check, and the green leaves on helmet and bridle are growth still to come.

Reality, worked ground, contained drive, and something that will grow if you wait.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: slow, reliable progress. A project advancing step by step, a commitment kept, a routine that works. As a person: someone steady, methodical, loyal — the colleague you hand the file to because you know it’ll be done. **Slow isn’t stuck, as long as the horse eventually takes a step.**

Reversed, his patience either turns into waiting or sets like concrete. The plan is checked so often it never starts, the person waiting for a certainty that doesn’t exist; or method becomes rigidity — the same route, the same way, “because that’s how it’s done” — and the steady friend becomes the one who won’t budge.

Next to the [Knight of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-swords/), two speeds at once: one charges, the other hasn’t left — a team, or two voices in the same head. Next to the [Seven of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-pentacles/), another man looking at his coins: patience before the harvest, twice. Next to [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/), the stillness turns into movement, or meets someone who won’t wait.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Imagine a spread where the Knight of Pentacles comes out first and the Eight of Wands after it, for someone you don’t know. Knight then eight: action held in check, then speed — my note for the Eight of Wands is wands flying or falling, glass half full or half empty. Earth then Fire: a slow plan, then things moving fast. A horse standing still, then eight wands flying across an open sky.

Stillness meeting speed, in words: “You’re the careful one, and around you things are suddenly speeding up. You don’t have to gallop. But you may have to decide a little faster than you’d like.”

The check: “Is something around you moving faster than you are?” If nothing around them is speeding up, admit the miss and record it.
