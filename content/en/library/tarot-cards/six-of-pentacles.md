## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man in a red cloak over a tunic striped blue and white stands between two kneeling people. With his right hand he lets coins fall into the cupped hands of the one on the left, wrapped in a patched, yellowish cloak, who looks up at him. In his left hand he holds a pair of scales, the two pans level. The second person, on the right, in a blue cloak, holds out an open hand and waits.

Six pentacles float in the grey sky above. Behind, small and far, there are green trees and the towers of a town.

The man is standing; the other two are on their knees. **Giving — and who stands and who kneels while it happens.**

## By number: six {#number}

[Number six](/library/numerology/number-6/) is 3 exalted: the machine isn’t only running, it’s fast, and there’s enough. After the trial of 5, things flow again, and it becomes possible to rest — or to give. In my notes, the Six of Pentacles is the scales: knowing value, and the kingdom lasting.

When results flow, 6 wants you calm and unassuming, and the scales are that humility made into an object: give, but weigh. Instagram’s 6 was the investment that came from Facebook, the very company that had looked like a threat.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) makes the exchange concrete: money, help, time, a meal, a loan, a reference for a job. Earth is value, and this card is about knowing the value of things — what you have, what you can give, what something is worth to the person who receives it.

It’s also Earth’s protective side, the one that feeds and looks after. The flip side is the manager in my notes who stops listening because his way “brings in more”: help that comes with conditions, generosity that keeps the other person kneeling.

## By colours {#colours}

The giver wears [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/): action, strength, the one who does. Under it, a tunic striped blue and white — feeling and purity in equal strips, like the two pans of his scales. The coins and the pentacles are gold. The kneeling figures wear a pale yellow-brown and a [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/): one closer to the ground, the other to emotion.

The sky is grey, neutral. One figure in the colour of action, two in quieter colours, and gold passing between them.

## By astrology: Moon in Taurus {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn’s attribution here is the [Moon](/library/astrology/moon/) in the middle decan of Taurus. The Moon is the need for safety, the way you comfort yourself and others. Taurus says *I have*. Classical astrology calls the Moon exalted in Taurus: feeling safe because there’s enough. Both point to the comfort of having enough to share.

The level scales say most of it, Moon or no Moon.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Six of Pentacles is an exchange that works: help given or received, a fair deal, a loan paid back, someone who has enough and shares it. It can be the person giving, or the person who is finally receiving. **The card asks where the person stands in the scene — giving, receiving or waiting — and whether the scales are level.**

Reversed, the scales tip. Help comes with strings; the giver needs to stay above; the one who receives can’t refuse and can’t give back. Or the opposite: someone gives so much — time, money, care — that they end up on their knees themselves.

The card is often drawn around money: lending, borrowing, a debt, a pay rise. Those decisions are yours, with someone who can see the figures; I don’t read on them. What the scales can show is the balance of the relationship ([why the scales don’t settle a loan for you](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)).

Next to [Justice](/library/tarot-cards/justice/), the scales again: fairness that’s weighed, not felt. Next to the [Five of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-pentacles/), the step before — the two in the snow — and here the help arriving. Next to the [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/), the exchange happens standing up, face to face, between equals.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Assume you’re shuffling for a friend, and the Six of Pentacles comes out with the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/) beside it. Six then four: the flow of 6 — in my notes, the number where you can finally rest — then the stability of 4. Earth then Air: something given or received, then the mind going quiet. The drawings: gold passing from one hand to another, then a figure lying still on a tomb under a stained-glass window.

Coins handed out, then a knight resting in a quiet chapel; I’d sum it up as: “You’ve been giving a lot — time, help, maybe money — and now you need to stop and get your breath back.”

Then a question about the recent past: “Does that match the last few weeks?” If it’s yes, I let the person say as much or as little as they like. If the last few weeks looked nothing like that, the miss is mine, and I own it.
