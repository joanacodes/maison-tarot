## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman stands in a long red-orange robe, a pale cloth tied over her eyes. Grey bands wrap her upper body several times and hold her arms against her sides. Around her, eight swords are planted in the ground, hilts up: three on her left, five on her right, like the bars of a fence. Behind her, under a pale grey sky, a castle with red roofs stands on a rocky height.

Now look closer. The bands stop at her waist: her legs are free. The swords stand around her; none of them touches her. The front of the circle, the side she faces, is open. Her feet stand in mud and shallow puddles. Nobody guards her.

**It’s a prison that holds mostly because she can’t see it.**

## By number: eight {#number}

Eight is 4 exalted: the empire, structure become power. It’s also control — lay an 8 on its side and you get the Magician’s infinity sign. In the Eight of Swords, that control has turned inwards. In my notes, this card is someone who is a prisoner of themselves while holding every tool they need, and who can take the blindfold off.

Eight steps in, Instagram was an empire making billions a year, so established that nobody imagined it doing anything else. That’s the trap of this eight: a structure so solid in your head that you forget you built it, and that you could walk out. The advice of [number 8](/library/numerology/number-8/) is to keep working as if nothing were secured; here, the work is noticing which bars are real.

## By element: Air {#element}

Swords are [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/): thinking, analysing, deciding. Air surveys everything in one sweep, which is why my notes trust it with decisions — and it can also block, or obsess. The Eight of Swords is Air blocked. Every option has been thought through, and each one has a sword planted in front of it: “I can’t leave, because…”, “I can’t say it, because…”.

Air’s leader, in my notes, can turn into a tyrant. On this card the tyrant lives inside the head, and the person obeying is the same one.

## By colours {#colours}

The [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) of the robe is the warmest thing on the card: passion, action, strength, drive. All of it is wrapped in [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) — the bands, the sky, the castle — the colour of neutrality, of no particular will. That’s the card in two colours: energy tied up in indecision. The blue puddles at her feet are emotion, pooled and still. Plenty of drive, held still, standing in feelings that have stopped moving.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Gemini {#astrology}

Jupiter in the first decan of [Gemini](/library/astrology/gemini/) is this card’s Golden Dawn attribution. Gemini is “I think”. Jupiter is expansion, confidence, excess. Put them in one head and the thoughts grow bigger than the situation, inflating every risk. On the card, the strength is all there, cut short at the arms. You can drop the planet and keep the picture: the bands already say it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Eight of Swords is feeling stuck: in a job, a relationship, a decision, a story about yourself. Some situations really are hard to leave, and the card doesn’t pretend otherwise. What it asks is which bands are yours to loosen — the fear, the “what will people say”, the belief that you don’t have what it takes. **The Eight of Swords holds every tool it needs to get out; the first one is taking off the blindfold.**

Reversed, the cage starts to give or closes in. It can loosen: one band slips, someone says out loud what you’ve been telling yourself in silence, and it suddenly sounds less true. Or it tightens: you add the swords yourself, finding a reason against every exit, and the waiting turns into a habit.

Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/), the blindfold appears twice: a choice put off has become a cage. Next to [the Devil](/library/tarot-cards/the-devil/), the chains you could take off get louder — look how loosely they hang on his two prisoners. Next to the [Ace of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-swords/), one clear idea cuts the bands. For relationships, there’s [feeling trapped in love, and the way out you already have](/blog/eight-of-swords-tarot-love/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Consider an imaginary reading in which the Eight of Swords comes up beside the [Page of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-pentacles/). Eight then a Page: a structure that holds you, then the student. Air then Earth: a cage made of thoughts, then something concrete to learn. Grey and red, then a young man in a green field holding up a coin.

From the blindfold to the coin held up, my words would be: “You feel stuck where you are, and you tell yourself you don’t know enough to leave. You know more than you think, and what gets you out is learning one practical thing.”

Then ask: “Do you feel stuck somewhere in your life at the moment, unable to move?” A yes opens the next question — what do they think they’re missing? A no is a miss; write it down as one.
