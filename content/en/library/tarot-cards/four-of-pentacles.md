## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A crowned man sits facing us, square and still, on a grey stone seat. He holds one pentacle clasped against his chest with both arms. A second one sits balanced on top of his crown, and two more lie flat under his feet, one under each shoe. He wears a dark red robe edged with blue, and a black cloak wrapped around him.

Behind him, low on the horizon, a city: towers, red roofs, a few trees. He has his back to it. The sky is a pale, empty grey.

Look at what his body can’t do. His arms are busy, his feet are pinned, and his head can’t turn without the top coin falling. **Everything he holds also holds him in place.**

## By number: four {#number}

**four — foundations, stability.** [Number four](/library/numerology/number-4/) is 2 exalted: the contract has held, and now there’s a structure, a home, the fruit of your work. In my notes, 4 is already the most earthly number — material, abundance, Taurus, earth — so on a Pentacles card it’s doubled: stability on top of stability.

The advice of 4 is to stay flexible and open to what you hadn’t planned. Its minus is carrying on when you should stop, and stubbornness. This card shows both: the foundations are real, and the grip is tight. In the Instagram story, 4 is the big success that comes fast — the moment a company starts believing it will never have to change.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is what has value for you, and the will to keep it. Tenacity, perseverance, endurance — and stubbornness. The Four of Pentacles is that last word drawn in full: a man who has built something and has decided nobody will take it from him.

What he clutches isn’t only money. It can be savings, yes, but also a position, a routine, a role in the family, an old way of doing things, a person. Earth protects — it’s mostly feminine energy, the one that keeps things safe. The question is when protecting turns into not letting go.

## By colours {#colours}

His robe is a dark, deep [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/): strength, will, a body that intends to stay put. The [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) cloak wraps around it; in my grid, black absorbs energy — it takes and doesn’t give back. A thin line of blue at the hem is the only cool, emotional note on him. The four coins are gold, and so is the crown.

Behind him, the grey sky: neutrality, matter that doesn’t move. The city has its red roofs and green trees, but it’s small and far away. Read the colours before the card: a dense, dark figure, and the living world behind his back.

## By astrology: Sun in Capricorn {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Four of Pentacles is the third decan of Capricorn, given to the [Sun](/library/astrology/sun/); its title is the Lord of Earthly Power. The Sun is the centre, what a life organises itself around. Capricorn says *I use*: work, responsibility, the wish to depend on no one. Together: a person who has put their security at the centre of everything — power in its earthly sense, having and holding.

Optional, as always. The posture alone says it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Four of Pentacles is security kept: savings, a stable job, a home, a boundary, something built and protected. That’s its plus — every card has one — and it can be the right moment to consolidate. **The card asks whether the grip still protects the person, or now holds them in place.**

Reversed, the hold loosens, or tightens. Either the person starts to open their arms: they share, delegate, spend on something that matters to them. Or the fear of losing grows, and what was protected becomes a cage — control, mistrust, nothing coming in because nothing is allowed out.

People ask this card about saving, spending, investing. I don’t read on those decisions; they stay with you and, for the figures, with an adviser. What the card can show is the grip itself, and what you’re afraid of losing ([the Pentacles and money, read calmly](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)).

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), the major 4, structure meets property: the rule, the frame, the throne — good protection, or a fortress. Next to the [Four of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-cups/), another 4, comfort has gone flat: safe, and bored. Next to the [Six of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-pentacles/), the arms open: the same coins given and received, and the scales level again.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Four of Pentacles next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/). Lenses first. Four then eight: stability, then 4 exalted — and in my notes, the Eight of Cups is moving on from what you’re already doing. Earth then Water: what you hold, then what you feel. The drawings: a man gripping his coins in front of a city, then a figure walking away from eight stacked cups, towards the mountains, under the moon.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’re holding on very tight to something you’ve already started to leave.”

To check, I ask: “Is there something you’re keeping out of habit, or out of fear of losing it, more than because you still want it?” If it’s yes, I don’t ask what. If it’s no, that’s a miss, and I say so plainly.
