## What’s on the card {#the-card}

Inside a stone church, under a pointed arch. High in the arch, three pentacles are carved into the stone in a triangle, linked by tracery. On the left, a young craftsman stands on a low wooden bench. We see him mostly from behind, his face in profile, a tool in his raised hand, an apron at his front: he’s working on the stone.

On the right, by a pillar, two figures. One, in dark clothes with a thick collar, holds a large unrolled sheet — the plans — and looks up towards the craftsman. The other is wrapped in a hooded cloak, orange with red spots; we don’t see his face. Behind all three, the inside of the arch is black.

**Three people around one piece of work — the one who carves, the one who holds the plans, the one who watches — and the work happens where they meet.**

## By number: three {#number}

[Number three](/library/numerology/number-3/) is the cadence found: the machine running, the contract that works, siblings on good terms. Three is the first number where something is produced and can be seen. On a Pentacles card that’s literal — a piece of work, made by hand, that people can come and look at.

The advice of 3, in my notes, is to finish a project before starting another; its minus is the opposite — new projects before the old ones are done, and sometimes solitude or betrayal. On this card the cadence holds because three people agreed on one plan. In the Instagram story, 3 is a launch that works, a first result people can see and use.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is the result of work. Its keywords in my notes — matter, value, finances, the result of work — are all on this card: stone, a building, a craft, a job done properly. Earth is patient and enduring, and a church is about as Earth as it gets: built over years by people who knew they’d be judged on what stays standing.

Earth’s archetype, in my notes, is the artist’s manager — steady, in the shadow — and its flip side is the manager who stops listening because his vision “brings in more”. On this card, the craftsman and the people with the plans are still listening to each other.

## By colours {#colours}

The card is mostly [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) and black: stone, the arch, the dark of the church behind. Grey is stability, matter that doesn’t move; black, in my grid, is reality — and the void the work is filling. Against that, the colour sits on the people only. The hooded cloak is [orange](/library/tarot-colours/orange/) with red spots: creativity, warmth, impulse. The craftsman has a dark tunic, blue hose, a yellow apron.

Read the colours before the card: a solid, sober frame, and all the life on the bodies of the people working inside it.

## By astrology: Mars in Capricorn {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Three of Pentacles is the second decan of Capricorn, given to [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/). Mars is how you act — raw energy, effort, the body moving. Capricorn says *I use*: work, structure, knowing how to use every tool. Classical astrology calls Mars exalted in Capricorn: energy at its best when it has a plan.

That’s the card exactly, and still optional: the number and the stone say the same.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Three of Pentacles is work that’s recognised: a skill being used, a team where each person has a place, a first result people can see. A project at work, a flat renovated with friends, an apprenticeship where someone experienced watches and corrects. **The card says the work is good because several people looked at it — not in spite of it.**

Reversed, the cadence breaks. Either the plans and the hands stop talking — the craftsman carves something nobody asked for, the people with the plans never look up — or one person tries to hold all three roles and does none of them well. In excess, it’s the 3 that starts the next project before this one is done.

If the question behind the card is a pay rise or a quote to accept, that decision stays with you; I don’t read on it. What the card can say is whether the collaboration is sound ([how I read the Pentacles around money](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)).

Next to the [Eight of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-pentacles/), the craftsman is alone at his bench, coin after coin: the skill has become mastery. Next to the [Three of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-cups/), another 3, the team celebrates what it built. Next to the [Five of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-wands/), everyone is there and nobody is coordinated — in my notes, a fight without a common plan, the very thing this card avoids.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Let’s make one up: a colleague who’s curious about tarot draws the Three of Pentacles and the [Four of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-cups/). Three then four: the cadence found, then the stability that follows it. Earth then Water: the work, then the feelings about it. The drawings: three people around a carving in a church, then a young man sitting under a tree, arms folded, not looking at the cup a hand holds out to him from a cloud.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “Your work is going well, people count on you, and you’ve stopped feeling anything about it.”

To check, I ask: “Does work feel like that at the moment — fine, and flat?” If it’s yes, I leave room for whatever the person wants to say about it. If their work still feels alive to them, the sentence missed, and it goes in my notebook as a miss.
