## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man stands on a castle roof, behind a stone parapet, looking out to the left over sea and shore: blue water, green fields, far mountains. In his right hand, close to his face, a small globe, green and red. His left hand grips a wand resting against the parapet; a second wand stands on the right, held to the wall by an iron ring. Red cap, long dark red cloak, brown tunic.

On the left, set into the stone, a small panel: red roses and white lilies crossed in an X. Down to the right, a little house with a red roof.

He’s well dressed, protected, high up — and not going anywhere yet. **He has the world in his hand and a wall in front of him: the card is the moment just before you decide to leave.**

## By number: two {#number}

[Two](/library/numerology/number-2/) is the contract: two parties, a bond, and caution. Here the two parties are the man and the world — what he has inside the walls, and what he could have out there. One wand in his hand, one fixed to the stone. He’s weighing them.

Signing with investors is Instagram’s 2 — the step this man hasn’t taken. The minus of 2 is stubbornness, solitude, the mind going in circles — months on the roof, globe in hand, signing nothing.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is impulse, and here it’s held back on purpose. The raw spark of the Ace has become ambition: the question is no longer “do I want something?” but “how far can I take it?”.

Fire with a plan forges tools instead of burning the barn down. But Fire doesn’t wait well: when the planning drags on, drive turns into frustration, and the teenager who thought they could do anything starts pacing the battlements.

## By colours {#colours}

The deep [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) of cloak and cap dominates: passion, action, strength — wrapped around a man standing still. The action is in his head, ready to come out; the roses on the panel repeat it.

Against the red, the blue of the water and the mountains: what’s far, still to cross. Between the two, green fields, the land that could grow if he went. Under his feet, pale grey stone: the safe position he’d have to leave.

## By astrology: Mars in Aries {#astrology}

Its decan, for the Golden Dawn, is Mars in Aries — the first ten degrees of the sign that opens the zodiac. [Aries](/library/astrology/aries/) says “I am”: the doer, the bricklayer rather than the architect. Mars rules it, so this is drive at home: raw, quick, direct.

It tells you the man on the roof isn’t a dreamer. The energy to go is there; what holds him is a choice. [The decans](/blog/decans-tarot/) are an extra here, never the rule.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Two of Wands is planning with ambition: a project bigger than where you are, a move abroad, a partnership you’re weighing up. The means and the drive are there; the person is looking at the horizon and doing the sums. **The Two of Wands doesn’t ask whether you can go — it asks whether you’ve decided to.**

Reversed, the planning becomes a hiding place: the trip postponed again, the plan revised for the fourth time, the walls winning without anyone saying so. Or the opposite excess, Mars in a hurry: signing before reading because waiting felt unbearable.

Next to the [Three of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-wands/), the decision is made and the ships are out. Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/), two 2s, and nobody signs. Next to [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/), an echo: this man holds the world and two wands, and the World’s dancer holds two wands too. I read the World as action led by the whole — the plan has room to become that.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

An invented pair to practise on: the Two of Wands, with the Eight of Cups laid beside it. Number: two, then eight — weighing a bond, then leaving behind work already in progress. Element: Fire, then Water — ambition, then feeling. Colours: the red man on grey stone, then another figure in red, walking away from eight cups under a night sky. The same red, moving this time.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve been looking at a bigger life from where you are for a while, and part of you has already left. The question isn’t whether it’s possible. It’s whether you’ll let yourself go.”

Then I’d check: is that where they are? If yes: what would the first step be, this month? If no, I write it down as it is — what I said in one column, what was confirmed in the other. That’s what [a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/) is for.
