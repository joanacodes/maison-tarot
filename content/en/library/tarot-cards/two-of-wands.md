## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man stands on the roof of a castle, behind a stone parapet, and looks out to the left over the sea and the shore: blue water, green fields, mountains in the distance. In his right hand he holds a small globe, green and red, close to his face. His left hand grips a wand that rests against the parapet. A second wand stands on the right, held to the wall by an iron ring. He wears a red cap and a long dark red cloak over a brown tunic.

On the left, set into the stone, there’s a small panel: red roses and white lilies crossed in an X. Down to the right, behind the wall, a little house with a red roof.

He’s well dressed, well protected, high up — and he isn’t going anywhere yet. **He has the world in his hand and a wall in front of him: the card is the moment just before you decide to leave.**

## By number: two {#number}

**two — to bind, to weigh, to cooperate.** [Two](/library/numerology/number-2/) is the number of the contract: two parties, a bond, and the caution that comes with it. My advice for a 2 is to read the contract and weigh the pros and cons. On this card, the two parties are the man and the world — what he has here, inside the walls, and what he could have out there. One wand is in his hand; the other is fixed to the stone. He’s weighing them.

In the Instagram story, 2 is the moment the founders sign with investors: the idea exists, and now someone else’s money and conditions come into it. It’s the first moment you can lose something other than your time. The minus of 2 is stubbornness, solitude, the mind going round in circles — standing on the roof for months, globe in hand, signing nothing.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is impulse, the energy that motivates and spreads fast. On the Two of Wands, that impulse is held back on purpose. The raw spark of the Ace has become an ambition: the question is no longer “do I want something?” but “how far can I take it?”.

Fire with a plan is a good thing — it’s what forges tools instead of burning the barn down. But Fire doesn’t wait well. When the planning lasts too long, the drive turns into frustration, and the teenager who thought they could do anything starts pacing the battlements.

## By colours {#colours}

The deep [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) of the cloak and cap dominates: passion, action, strength. It’s wrapped around a man who is standing still, which tells you the action is in his head, ready to come out. The red roses on the panel repeat it.

Against that red, the blue of the water and the far mountains: what’s distant, still to cross. Between the two, the green of the fields — the land that could grow if he went. And under his feet, the pale grey stone of the parapet: stability, the safe position he’s standing on, and the one he would have to leave.

## By astrology: Mars in Aries {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Two of Wands is Mars in Aries — the first ten degrees of the sign that opens the zodiac — and its title is Lord of Dominion. [Aries](/library/astrology/aries/) is the sign of “I am”: the doer, the bricklayer rather than the architect, the first one out. Mars rules Aries, so this is drive at home in its own sign: raw, quick, direct.

It adds something useful here: the man on the roof isn’t a dreamer. The energy to go is fully there; what holds him back is a choice, not a lack. Keep this lens if it speaks to you and leave it if it doesn’t — [the decans](/blog/decans-tarot/) are an extra, never the rule.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Two of Wands is planning with ambition: a project you see bigger than where you are, a move abroad you’re considering, a partnership you’re weighing up. The person has the means and the drive; they’re looking at the horizon and doing the sums. **The Two of Wands doesn’t ask whether you can go — it asks whether you’ve decided to.**

Reversed, the planning becomes a hiding place. The globe stays in the hand: the trip postponed again, the plan revised for the fourth time, the comfort of the walls winning without anyone saying so. Or the opposite excess, Mars in a hurry: leaving before reading the contract, signing in a rush because waiting felt unbearable.

Next to the [Three of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-wands/), the decision is made and the ships are out. Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/), the choice freezes — two 2s side by side, and nobody signs. Next to [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/), a lovely echo: this man holds the world and two wands, and the World’s dancer holds two wands as well. I read the World as action led by the whole; next to it, the plan has room to become that.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Two of Wands next to the Eight of Cups.

Lenses first. Number: two, then eight — weighing a bond, then moving on from what you’re already doing. Element: Fire, then Water — ambition, then feeling. Colours: the red man on his grey stone, then another figure in red, walking away from eight cups under a blue night sky. The same red, and this time it’s moving.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve been looking at a bigger life from where you are for a while now, and part of you has already left. The question isn’t whether it’s possible. It’s whether you’ll let yourself go.”

Then I’d ask whether that lands, and listen. If it does, the next question is concrete: what would the first step look like, this month? If it doesn’t, I write it down as it is. Keeping those two columns honest — what I said, what was confirmed — is what [a reading notebook](/blog/tarot-journal/) is for.
