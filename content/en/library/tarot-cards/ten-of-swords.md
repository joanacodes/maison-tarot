## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man lies face down on the ground at the edge of a stretch of still water. Ten swords stand planted in his back in a row, from his neck down to his waist. A red cloak lies over his legs and spills onto the ground. His face is turned away; one hand rests on the ground in front of him, in a grey sleeve.

The top half of the card is a black sky. Low down, just above a line of blue hills, runs a band of yellow light, and below it the water is calm and blue. Nothing moves. Nobody else is there.

**The drawing is about an ending that is complete — with light already on the horizon.**

## By number: ten {#number}

**ten — the end of the cycle, transition.** Ten is the corridor between two rooms: what was is finished, what comes has no name yet. It brings freedom, change, a breath; its minus is that it tells you to start something else, ready or not.

My notes on 10 hold a sentence made for this card: from the outside you might look knocked down, while in reality the butterfly is already far away, out of its chrysalis. At its tenth stage, Instagram had changed so much that people no longer recognised it. The advice of [number 10](/library/numerology/number-10/) is not to mistake the moult for the fall.

## By element: Air {#element}

Swords are [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/): thoughts, plans, decisions, words. What ends here is usually of that kind: a plan, a belief, a story you’d been telling yourself.

Count the swords. One would have been enough. Ten is the mind at the end of a hard stretch: every argument, every “I should have”, every worst case, planted all at once. Air exaggerates, and the card shows it exaggerating. That doesn’t make the pain any less real. It does mean the picture is louder than the facts.

## By colours {#colours}

[Black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) fills the sky: absence, void, negative thoughts — and reality, the end you can no longer avoid seeing. Under it lies the band of yellow: joy, optimism, will. It’s narrow, but it’s the brightest thing on the card. The blue water and hills are emotion at rest. The red of the cloak is passion and strength, laid down. Read the colours alone and you get: a dark end, calm feelings, a light coming up.

## By astrology: Sun in Gemini {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Ten of Swords is the [Sun](/library/astrology/sun/) in the third decan of Gemini, with the hard-sounding title Lord of Ruin. Gemini is “I think”; the Sun is the centre, what you organise your life around. A ruin of the centre: the way of thinking you’d built everything on, finished. And the Sun is drawn right there on the card, in that yellow band on the horizon. Optional — the number already says the same thing.

## In a reading {#reading}

The Ten of Swords is one of the cards people fear most, and I understand why: it’s the hardest drawing in the deck. It doesn’t announce a death, an illness or an attack, and I don’t read on those. It talks about an ending in your life — a job, a relationship, a role, a plan — that has gone as far as it can go. Rock bottom, and also the floor you push off from. **The Ten of Swords is the end of the fall, not the start of it.**

Reversed, the same ending is slowed down or overdone. Slowed down: getting up slowly, one sword out at a time, once the worst has passed. Overdone: refusing to accept that it’s over and staying on the ground — or treating every setback as rock bottom.

If someone draws this card while things really are at their lowest, the card isn’t the point. Ask how they are. If it sounds heavier than a reading can hold, [a doctor or a therapist is the right door](/disclaimers/#therapy), and saying so gently is part of reading well.

Next to [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), the ending is confirmed — and in my notes Death is also the harvest, the urge to make life fertile again. Next to the [Ace of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-swords/), the cycle closes and a new, clear idea opens the next one. I’ve written about this card [in love](/blog/ten-of-swords-tarot-love/) and [at work, when you hit rock bottom](/blog/ten-of-swords-tarot-career/), and about why [the scariest cards are kinder than they look](/blog/scariest-tarot-cards/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Ten of Swords next to the Ace of Wands. Lenses first. Ten then one: a cycle closes, a new one starts. Air then Fire: a way of thinking is finished, a new urge arrives. A black sky with light along the bottom, then a hand coming out of a cloud, holding a wand that sprouts leaves.

Out loud, you might say: “Something you’d been holding together in your head has come to an end, and it really is over. What’s arriving is a new desire — something you want to make, not something you have to fix.”

Then check: “Has something ended for you recently, something you’d been fighting to keep going?” Let them answer in their own words. If they say no, a miss is a miss: don’t stretch the card to fit.
