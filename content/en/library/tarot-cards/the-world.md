## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman dances in the air inside a large oval wreath of green leaves, tied at the top and bottom with crossed red ribbons. She’s naked but for a long purple scarf wound around her body, and she holds a short wand in each hand. One leg crosses behind the other. In the four corners, heads appear in the clouds: a human face, an eagle, a bull and a lion — the same four as on the Wheel of Fortune.

Nothing on the card is unfinished. No ground under her feet, no road, no task left in her hands: two wands, both held lightly. **A cycle complete, and the ease of moving inside it.** That’s the drawing as a whole.

## By number: 21 {#number}

Twenty-one comes back to [3](/library/numerology/number-3/), the number of the Empress: good rhythm, the machine running, a contract that works. Its advice: bring one project home before you open the next; its minus is starting new things before the old ones are done.

On the last card of the major arcana, 3 says the work has become a result, and the result keeps turning on its own. People signing up, posting and coming back, with nobody pushing them: that was Instagram’s 3, the launch that worked. That’s the dancer in the wreath: no effort showing, everything moving.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The World is an [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) card: matter, what has value for you, the result of work. Earth is tenacity and endurance, what makes things last — fitting for the end of the majors: twenty-one cards of effort, and here is something you can stand on.

In my notes, the World is action led by the whole: the two wands. Not one tool pushing in one direction, like the Magician’s single raised wand, but everything you’ve gathered working together. Earth’s minus is the usual one: holding on to the finished thing so long that you can’t start the next.

## By colours {#colours}

The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of the wreath: growth, life, prosperity. The [purple](/library/tarot-colours/purple/) of the scarf: in my grid, a mature red — a role others give you, a place they recognise. The red of the ribbons: action and strength, tying the circle shut. The blue of the sky behind her: emotions settled. Read the colours before the card and you already have: something grown, a place others recognise, and a calm you’ve earned.

## By astrology: Saturn {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn assigned the World to [Saturn](/library/astrology/saturn/), and to Earth as well. Saturn is the hardest lesson: where you’re held back and kept waiting, until the difficulty itself is what makes you strong. When I tested astrology box by box, Saturn was one of the three placements that held 75 to 90% of the time, and that result is why I kept astrology at all. So I read this card as Saturn’s reward: what you ended up building.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the World is completion: a cycle closes, a project lands, a stage of life is finished and you can feel it. It can also be travel, the wide world, being at ease in your own place. Its plus is wholeness; its minus is that a finished thing can become a place you’re afraid to leave.

Reversed, it’s the same completion slowed down: the last few steps you can’t bring yourself to take, the box you won’t tick. Or in excess: celebrating the ending for so long that the next thing never gets to begin.

Next to [the Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/), who comes after it when the deck starts over, the World says: finished, and ready to set off light. Next to the [Wheel of Fortune](/library/tarot-cards/wheel-of-fortune/), with the same four creatures, it’s the difference between a cycle that turns you and one you’ve completed. Alongside the [Three of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-cups/), which shares its 3, it’s the celebration with the people who helped.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

The cycle closes on this card, so try it next to a beginning: the World beside the [Ace of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-pentacles/).

Numbers: 21 comes back to 3, the first result; the Ace is 1, a raw diamond, the start of a cycle, whose advice is humility — get ready to learn. Elements: Earth and Earth, so this is concrete, something you can hold. The pictures: the World’s green wreath, and on the Ace, a hand from a cloud offering a golden coin above a garden.

The sentence you’d say out loud: “Something you’ve worked on for a long time is complete, and the next thing is already in your hand. It’s smaller, and that’s how it should be: you’re a beginner again, with everything you’ve learned.”

Then check it. Ask what it brings up, and let them say what is finishing and what is starting. If nothing is finishing for them, that’s a miss. If something is, the coin the Ace holds out is theirs to take or leave.
