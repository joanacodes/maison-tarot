## What’s on the card {#the-card}

Four tall wands stand planted in the foreground, and a heavy garland of leaves, red flowers and fruit hangs across them, tied to the outer wands with red ribbons — a gate, or the frame of a canopy. Behind it, two figures in long robes, one in white with a blue drape, the other in dark red, raise bouquets above their heads. A few more people gather further back on the left.

Behind them all, a grey stone castle with red-roofed towers, and a small bridge on the right. The sky is a bright, flat yellow.

Nobody is fighting, nobody is working, and nobody holds the wands: they stand on their own. **The Four of Wands is a structure built to celebrate in — a threshold, a home, an invitation to come through.**

## By number: four {#number}

[Four](/library/numerology/number-4/) is 2 exalted: after the contract, all is well — stable, structured, the fruit of your work. In my own notes, the Four of Wands is the example I give for this number: foundations, marriage, building a family. Four planted wands are the four corners of a house.

In the Instagram story, 4 is the fast success that gives the company a shape, a team, habits. It holds. The advice of 4 — stay flexible, open to what you hadn’t planned — applies to the house too: leave room for whoever turns up. Its minus is carrying on when you should stop, or getting stubborn about how the house must look. The garland is a good reminder: it will wilt and need replacing. The celebration was never the walls.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) warms, cooks and lights the night, and here it’s the hearth, not the blaze. The drive of the first Wands has found a place where it can burn safely: enthusiasm shared, energy spent on a gathering, a home, a party. The warmest card of the suit.

Fire spreads, and here it spreads to people — the figures in the background came because there was light. A party can still go on too long, a house can be built in a hurry. But this is Fire at its most useful: keeping people warm.

## By colours {#colours}

Bright [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) fills the sky and tints the ground: joy, optimism, the inner child — the colour of the world when the world is well.

The garland adds [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) and red, growth and life with a touch of passion. The two figures repeat the palette: white for peace, blue for feeling, red for passion. The only grey is the castle, the steady structure behind the party, the part still there tomorrow morning.

## By astrology: Venus in Aries {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Four of Wands is Venus in Aries, the third decan of the sign. [Venus](/library/astrology/venus/) is how you bond; Aries is the first impulse, “I am”. Together: love that acts, attraction that wants to be celebrated out loud — a fit for garlands and raised bouquets.

An extra lens: it adds warmth to the card’s Fire, and the number and the yellow already say nearly the same. [The decans article](/blog/decans-tarot/) explains how they’re shared out.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Four of Wands is a stable, happy moment: a home that finally feels like one, a move, an engagement, a wedding, a reunion, the end of a building stage that deserves a party. **The Four of Wands celebrates what you’ve built — and reminds you that the people under the garland matter more than the walls.**

People sometimes hope this card announces a wedding or a baby, and it’s a lovely thing to hope for. I don’t read it that way: a card doesn’t give dates, and pregnancy is a question I leave to doctors, because a wrong word there can hurt. What the card does show is the wish for a home and the warmth around it.

Reversed, the celebration doesn’t happen, or happens without joy: the house that doesn’t feel like home, a wedding planned to please others, a family event where everyone is tense. Or the 4 in excess — clinging to the structure, refusing to move or to let someone new in.

Next to the [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/) — happiness, family — the home is the whole subject. Next to the [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/), a bond between two people asks to become official. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the same number in Earth: the house guarded rather than shared.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Suppose the Four of Wands comes out first, with the Six of Cups beside it, and nothing has been said yet about why the person came. Number: four, then six — something built and stable, then harmony and time for yourself. Element: Fire, then Water — celebration, then memories. Colours: the Four’s yellow, then the yellow courtyard of the Six of Cups, two children in red hoods, cups full of white flowers. Yellow twice: a good moment.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “There’s a gathering in your life, recent or coming, that brings you back to people from before — family, childhood friends, the place you grew up. It feels like coming home, and you’re allowed to enjoy it.”

Then I’d ask if there is such a gathering, and which one. If nobody is coming home and nothing is being celebrated, the sentence missed. Practising pairs like this, with other people around the table, is what the [workshops](/workshops/) are for.
