## What’s on the card {#the-card}

Four tall wands stand planted in the foreground, and a heavy garland hangs across them — leaves, red flowers and fruit — tied to the outer wands with red ribbons. Together they make a kind of gate, or the frame of a canopy. Behind it, two figures in long robes, one in white with a blue drape, the other in dark red, raise bouquets above their heads. Further back on the left, a few more people gather.

Behind everyone, a grey stone castle with towers capped in red roofs, and on the right a small bridge. The sky is a bright, flat yellow; the ground is pale and even.

Nobody is fighting, nobody is working. The wands aren’t held by anyone: they’re planted, and they stand on their own. **The Four of Wands is a structure built to celebrate in — a threshold, a home, an invitation to come through.**

## By number: four {#number}

**four — foundations, stability.** [Four](/library/numerology/number-4/) is 2 exalted: after the contract, all is well — stable, structured, the fruit of your work. In my own notes, the Four of Wands is the card I give as the example of this number: foundations, marriage, building a family. Four planted wands are the four corners of a house.

In the Instagram story, 4 is the big success that comes fast: the app has a shape, a team, habits. It holds. My advice with a 4 is to stay flexible, open to what you hadn’t planned. The minus of 4 is carrying on when you should stop, or getting stubborn about how the house must look. The garland is a good reminder: it’s alive, it will wilt, it will need replacing. The celebration was never the walls.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) warms, cooks and lights the night, and on this card it’s the hearth, not the blaze. The drive of the first Wands has found a place where it can burn safely. That’s what makes the Four the warmest card of the suit: enthusiasm shared, energy spent on a gathering, a home, a party.

Fire also spreads fast, and here it spreads to other people — the figures in the background came because there was light. The risk of Fire doesn’t disappear: a party can go on too long, a house can be built in a hurry. But the Four shows Fire at its most useful: keeping people warm.

## By colours {#colours}

Bright [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) fills the sky and tints the ground: joy, optimism, the inner child, self-expression. It’s the colour of the world when the world is well.

The garland adds [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) and red — growth and life, with a touch of passion. The two raised figures repeat the palette: white for peace, blue for feeling, red for passion. The only grey is the castle in the background, the steady structure behind the party, the part that will still be there tomorrow morning.

## By astrology: Venus in Aries {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Four of Wands is Venus in Aries, the third decan of the sign, titled Lord of Perfected Work. [Venus](/library/astrology/venus/) is what you love and how you bond; Aries is the first impulse, “I am”. Put together: love that acts, attraction that wants to be celebrated out loud. It fits a card of garlands and raised bouquets.

It’s an extra lens. If you read astrology, it adds warmth to the card’s Fire; if you don’t, the number and the yellow have already said almost the same. How the decans are shared out across the suits is explained in [the decans article](/blog/decans-tarot/).

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Four of Wands is a stable, happy moment: a home that finally feels like one, a move into a new place, an engagement, a wedding, a reunion, the end of a building stage that deserves a party. Something has been built, and people are invited in. **The Four of Wands celebrates what you’ve built — and reminds you that the people under the garland matter more than the walls.**

People sometimes hope this card announces a wedding or a baby, and it’s a lovely thing to hope for. I don’t read it that way: a card doesn’t give dates, and pregnancy is a question I leave to doctors, because a wrong word there can hurt. What the card does show is the wish for a home and the warmth around it, and that’s worth talking about.

Reversed, the celebration doesn’t take place, or takes place without joy: the house that doesn’t feel like home, a wedding planned to please others, a family event where everyone is tense. Or the 4 in excess — clinging to the structure, refusing to move, to change the date, to let someone new in.

Next to the [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/) — happiness, family — the home is the whole subject. Next to the [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/), a bond between two people asks to become official. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the same number in Earth: the house as property, guarded rather than shared. Look at which of the two the person is living.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Four of Wands next to the Six of Cups.

Lenses first. Number: four, then six — something built and stable, then harmony and time taken for yourself. Element: Fire, then Water — warmth and celebration, then feelings and memories. Colours: the yellow of the Four, then the yellow courtyard of the Six of Cups, two children in red hoods and cups full of white flowers. Yellow twice: a good moment.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “There’s a gathering in your life, recent or coming, that brings you back to people from before — family, childhood friends, a place you grew up. It feels like coming home, and you’re allowed to enjoy it.”

Then I’d ask whether it lands, and which gathering it is. If it doesn’t land, I say so and write it down. Practising pairs like this, with other people around the table, is what the [workshops](/workshops/) are for.
