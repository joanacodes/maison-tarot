## How the deck is built {#the-deck}

Seventy-eight cards. Twenty-two **major arcana**, numbered 0 to 21, with unique figures and scenes: the big steps, what happens to you rather than what you do. Fifty-six **minor arcana**, in four houses of fourteen — the Ace, 2 to 10, then Page, Knight, Queen and King: Wands, Cups, Swords, Pentacles.

Every house is an [element](/library/four-elements/); every number a step in the [cycle](/library/numerology/); every card has [colours](/library/tarot-colours/). That's why you don't learn the 78: you learn the lenses, and the card reads.

## There isn't only one way to read {#not-one-way}

Every card has a definition, and it's only a point to focus on. In reality what a card says changes with what the reader is living through at that moment and with the person asking the question. Every symbol is there for a reason; it doesn't stop you seeing, sometimes, the exact opposite of what the card "indicates". That's why the House counts its readings instead of reciting meanings.

Reversed cards? Some readers read them, others never. The pages below treat them as the same card, held back or in excess.

## The 1909 drawings {#the-drawings}

All the images are Pamela Colman Smith's, published in 1909 by Rider and now in the public domain. They're shown whole and in colour, because a card is a work of art and colour is its first lens.
