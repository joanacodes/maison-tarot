## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man leans on a long staff, both hands folded on top of it, his chin resting on his hands. He looks at a large green bush beside him, thick with leaves, with six pentacles growing in it like fruit. A seventh pentacle lies on the ground at his feet.

He wears an orange tunic over blue sleeves, blue hose and boots. The ground is a dark, purplish brown with patches of blue; the sky is an empty grey. Nothing is moving. He isn’t picking, and he isn’t digging.

**He has worked, it has grown, and now he’s looking at it — the drawing is the pause between the effort and the harvest.**

## By number: seven {#number}

**seven — doubt, assessment, patience.** [Number seven](/library/numerology/number-7/) is analysis: you look at what you have before deciding what comes next. In my notes, 7 is reflection, patience, analysis before action — and harvesting. This card holds all of those at once: the crop is there, and the person isn’t picking it yet.

The advice of 7 is that everything is temporary: the situation you’re in, good or bad, will change. The bush will keep growing or start to wilt, and either way, standing still won’t last. In the Instagram story, 7 is the negotiating table — Facebook choosing to turn the app into an advertising platform: neither triumph nor fall, the moment you decide what the thing will become.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is the result of work, and the patience it takes. Nothing in Earth grows fast: a garden, a career, savings, a skill, a relationship tended for years. The Seven of Pentacles is Earth at its slowest — the effort is done, the result is visible, and it still isn’t ready.

Tenacity, perseverance and endurance are Earth’s strengths, and this card needs all three. Its flip side is stubbornness: staying with a crop that won’t give, because you’ve already put so much into it.

## By colours {#colours}

The bush is a deep [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/): nature, growth, life, prosperity — the biggest, most alive thing on the card. The young man’s tunic is [orange](/library/tarot-colours/orange/): creativity, and also, in my grid, aridity — the dryness of someone who has given a lot. His sleeves and hose are blue: feeling, kept quiet. The coins are gold, the ground a dark brown, the sky grey and neutral.

Read the colours before the card: a lot of life, a tired body, and nothing in the sky pushing either way.

## By astrology: Saturn in Taurus {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Seven of Pentacles is the third decan of Taurus, given to [Saturn](/library/astrology/saturn/); its title is the Lord of Success Unfulfilled. Saturn is time, limits, the work that takes years — and the place where you become solid because it was hard. Taurus says *I have*, patiently, in a fixed sign that doesn’t like being hurried. Together: slow growth, and the discomfort of waiting for it.

Saturn is one of the placements that held best when I tested astrology — 75 to 90%. Here it’s still optional: the number and the bush have said it already.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Seven of Pentacles is the pause to take stock: a project that has grown but hasn’t paid off yet, a career on a plateau, a relationship you’ve tended and now look at with some doubt. It’s a good card for patience and for honest stock-taking. **The card doesn’t say whether to keep going or stop — it says you’re at the moment of looking.**

Reversed, the pause goes wrong in one of two ways. Either it drags into doubt that never decides — leaning on the staff for months, watching the same bush. Or impatience takes over and the crop is pulled before it’s ripe: a project dropped the week before it would have worked.

Questions about investments come with this card — whether to leave money in something or take it out. That’s a decision for you and an adviser who can see the numbers, not for the cards ([reading the Pentacles with your feet on the ground](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)). What the card can describe is the waiting, and what it’s doing to you.

Next to the [Eight of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-pentacles/), the pause ends and the work starts again, one coin at a time, with more skill. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/), another 7, the assessment drifts into daydreaming — in my notes, too many choices, and indecision. Next to [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/), the major 7, the looking is done and it’s time to move.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Seven of Pentacles next to the [Ace of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-wands/). Lenses first. Seven then one: assessment, then a new start. Earth then Fire: slow growth, then a fresh urge. The drawings: a tired young man looking at his bush, then a hand from a cloud holding out a single wand that’s putting out leaves.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve put a lot into something that hasn’t paid you back yet, and a new idea is starting to pull you somewhere else.”

To check, I ask: “Is there something you’ve been tending for a long time — and something new tempting you?” If it’s yes, I don’t say which to choose; that stays with the person. If it’s no, it’s a miss, and I note it as one.
