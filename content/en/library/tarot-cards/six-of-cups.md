## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A courtyard in an old village: a stone tower on the left, a house with a grey slate roof behind, everything in warm yellow stone. In the middle, a boy in a red hood, a blue tunic and red stockings leans towards a smaller girl and hands her a cup full of flowers. She wears a red headscarf, a long speckled dress and a white glove, and she looks up at him. His face is close to the flowers, as if he were smelling them first.

There are six cups, and each holds a white, star-shaped flower among green leaves. Four stand in a row in the foreground, one is in the boy’s hands, one sits on a stone pedestal carved with a shield marked with an X. On the far left, a small figure with a staff walks away up some steps, back turned. **It’s a scene of kindness between children, in a safe place, while the only other person walks off.**

## By number: six {#number}

**six — harmony regained, exchange.** Six is 3 doubled: after the trial of 5, things work again, and faster. In my notes it’s the number of results flowing — and of being allowed to rest, to take holidays. In the Six of Cups that rest has a particular flavour: taking time for yourself, childhood, flowering again, harmony.

That’s why it follows the Five so well: the two cups left standing have become six, full of flowers. The advice of [number six](/library/numerology/number-6/) is to stay calm and humble when things flow; here, it means enjoying the sweetness without trying to live in it for ever. In the Instagram story, 6 is Facebook investing: what threatened the company now carries it.

## By element: Water {#element}

[Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) makes this 6 a harmony of the heart: tenderness, kindness, the memory of feeling safe. Moving water is strong emotion, calm water is settled emotion — and this is calm water, the kind you can drink. Nothing here is passionate. Everything is gentle.

Water also carries you from A to B, and sometimes back to A. The Six of Cups can be the past returning — an old friend, a childhood place, a first love — or a person who brings out the child in you. One limit, said gently: nostalgia is water too, and you can stand in it so long it stops being refreshing.

## By colours {#colours}

[Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) is everywhere: the stone, the ground, the cups. In my grid it’s joy, optimism — and the inner child. It’s hard to find a card where that fits better. The red of the hoods and the boy’s stockings: warmth, drive, action, but on children, so a small and safe kind. The blue of his tunic and of the sky: emotions, vitality. And the [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/) of the six flowers: innocence, peace.

Read the colours before the card: joy, innocence, warmth, and plenty of it. You don’t need the title to know it’s a gentle card.

## By astrology: Sun in Scorpio {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Six of Cups is the second decan of Scorpio, ruled by the Sun; its title is the Lord of Pleasure. The [Sun](/library/astrology/sun/) is the centre, what you organise your life around. [Scorpio](/library/astrology/scorpio/) is *I desire*, a fixed sign whose staying power goes into emotional attachment. Together: a heart that holds on — to people, places, memories — and finds its centre there.

Nice to know, never required. If astrology isn’t one of your lenses, the yellow has already said it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, **the Six of Cups is sweetness — given, received or remembered.** Time for yourself, a visit home, an old friend getting back in touch, a kindness with no second thought behind it, playing again. It can also point to children, or to the child you were.

Reversed, the past takes up too much room. Either someone lives in “before” and finds today grey by comparison, or an old story comes back and no longer fits — you’ve changed and the place hasn’t. In the slowed-down version it’s the opposite: you’ve forgotten how to play, and the card asks where the fun went.

Next to the [Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/), the card just before it, it’s comfort after a loss: what’s left, and who stayed. Next to [the Sun](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/), a child again, this time on a white horse, the joy doubles. Next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/), it’s a choice between going back and moving on from what you’re already doing.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Six of Cups next to the [Nine of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-pentacles/). Lenses first. Six then nine: harmony, then the end of a stage, enjoying what has come. Water then Earth: a feeling, then comfort that has been earned. Colours: yellow in both — the stone of the Six, the bright sky of the Nine, where a woman walks alone in her vineyard.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve worked hard and you’ve earned a pause — something gentle, and for you: a few days somewhere familiar, people you’ve known a long time, time with no goal.”

To check, I ask: “When did you last take time that was only for you?” The answer tells me whether it lands. If the person has just come back from three weeks off, the sentence missed, and I note it as a miss.
