## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A tall grey stone tower stands on a rocky peak against a black sky. A bolt of lightning strikes the top and knocks off its roof, which is shaped like a crown. Flames burst from the windows. Two people fall headfirst, one of them still wearing a crown, and small yellow flames shaped like drops fall all around them.

Look at what the drawing leaves out: no one inside fighting the fire, no rain, and we never see anyone land. **Pamela Colman Smith stopped the picture at the moment of the fall, not after it.** So the card shows a structure that couldn’t hold, and the second it gives way.

## By number: 16 {#number}

Sixteen comes back to [7](/library/numerology/number-7/), the number of the Chariot: reflection, analysis before action, negotiation. On the Tower, 7 is the assessment you didn’t schedule — what doesn’t pass the test falls, and what remains is what you can count on.

The advice of 7 suits this card better than any other: everything is temporary, and your situation, good or bad, will change. In the Instagram story, 7 is the negotiating table where Facebook changes the model; neither triumph nor fall, the moment you choose what the thing becomes. The Tower is that moment arriving without asking first.

## By element: Fire {#element}

The Tower is a [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) card. Fire creates and destroys: it forges tools, warms, lights the night, and spreads fast when nobody masters it. In my notes on the elements there’s a comparison I come back to for this card: fire destroys at once, while neglected water destroys deeper and slower, like mould working through a house from inside.

The Tower is the fast kind. It’s frightening, and it’s also honest: you see what fell, and you can start from there.

## By colours {#colours}

The sky is [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/): absence, void, and reality without varnish. The tower is grey, the colour of a structure that holds without a word — until it doesn’t. Then yellow for the lightning and the falling drops, which in my grid is will and pride (fitting, on a card where a crown falls), and the red-orange of the flames: action, impulse. Black sky, grey stone, a flash of yellow and fire: a plain truth, something that had stopped moving, and a burst of energy that won’t wait.

## By astrology: Mars {#astrology}

In Golden Dawn terms the Tower is a [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/) card: action and combat, raw energy, anger, the body that moves. Here that energy has no plan; it strikes.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Tower is sudden change: a plan that falls through, a truth that comes out, a job, a belief or an arrangement that stops holding. It isn’t a punishment, and it doesn’t announce an accident, an illness or a death — a card can’t tell you that. What it talks about is the moment a pretence ends. Its plus is real: afterwards, you stop holding up something that was already cracked.

Reversed, it’s the same collapse slowed down: you can feel the walls going and you keep propping them up. Sometimes that’s right for a while; sometimes it only makes the fall longer. Or it’s the fall in excess — breaking everything because one thing broke.

Next to [the Star](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/), number 17, the Tower is the storm with the calm already in sight. Next to [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), it’s the fast version of the same change: one harvests slowly, the other strikes. Next to the [Ten of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-wands/), it’s what happens when you carry too much for too long: something drops.

**The Tower doesn’t destroy what was solid; it shows you what wasn’t.** For the questions people bring most, I’ve written about [the Tower in love](/blog/tower-tarot-love/), and the lightning as it strikes at work is just below.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the Tower is a structure meeting the truth. A job: a restructure, a merger, a new director who changes everything in a week. A project you’ve carried for a year, cancelled in a fifteen-minute call. A belief — “this company is my family” — that falls in a single meeting. The announcement is often sudden, but the cracks had been there for months, in the budget that kept shrinking. And sometimes the lightning is yours: the morning you realise you can’t do this job one more year, which can feel like relief as much as fear. **The lightning isn’t a verdict on your career; it’s the start of a review.**

Reversed at work, it’s the collapse in slow motion — the project nobody believes in — or the fall in excess, the resignation email written at midnight and copied to everyone.

The first question is “Will I lose my job?”, and I understand why: a job is rent, routine and a part of who you are. I don’t read that as a prediction. Whether a post survives depends on people who aren’t in the room and decisions not yet made, and a card that said “you’re safe” or “you’re out” would add a false calm, or a second shock to the first. If your contract, your rights or your money are at stake, that’s one for an employment adviser, a union rep or a lawyer ([why those questions stay with professionals](/disclaimers/#refusals)). A gentler question: “What would I do in the first week if it happened?” That one has an answer, and turns fear into a plan.

This week, before deciding anything, make two lists: what fell, and what’s still standing — your skills, the people who answer your calls. The second is usually longer. And step away from the cards for a few days, especially now: pulling a card every hour after a shock is the shock talking.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

A harder pair to practise on: the Tower beside the [Four of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-wands/).

Numbers: 16 comes back to 7, the assessment; the Four of Wands is 4, stability, the fruit of your work — foundations, a marriage, a family being built. Elements: Fire and Fire, so whatever happens, happens quickly. Colours: the Tower’s black sky beside the bright yellow of the Four of Wands.

Lightning beside a garland: “Something you built is being shaken, and what’s still standing afterwards is what you’ll build on next. The celebration isn’t cancelled; it’s moving to firmer ground.”

Then check it, gently: is something they rely on — a home, a plan, a team — being tested right now? Let them answer in their own words. If nothing they lean on is being shaken, don’t stretch the cards to make it fit.
