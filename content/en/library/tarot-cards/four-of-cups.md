## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man sits under a tree on a grassy hill, cross-legged, his arms folded across his chest. He wears a grey-green jerkin over a red shirt, blue hose and red shoes. In front of him, three golden cups stand in a row on the grass. From a small cloud on the left, a hand holds out a fourth cup — the same gesture as on the [Ace of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-cups/).

He doesn’t look at it. His eyes are lowered, towards the three cups or towards nothing in particular. Behind him rise the tree’s trunk and its heavy green leaves; in the distance, a bush and a line of hills under a wide blue sky. **Everything he has is in front of him, something new is being offered from the side, and he’s somewhere else in his head.**

## By number: four {#number}

**four — foundations, stability.** Four is 2 doubled: after the bond and the first result, it holds. It’s the fruit of your work, the structure, the home. In the Four of Cups that stability is emotional — life is set up, the relationships are known, the cups are lined up. And it has gone a little flat.

In my notes, the minus of [number four](/library/numerology/number-4/) is carrying on when you should stop, stubbornness, and having your head in the clouds — here, funnily enough, it’s the cloud that’s trying to reach him. The advice of 4 is the whole lesson of this card: stay flexible, stay open to possibilities you hadn’t planned. In the Instagram story, 4 is the big success that comes fast, and the moment a company starts believing it will never need to change.

## By element: Water {#element}

[Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) in a 4 is calm water, so calm it stops moving. The emotions are settled, sometimes too settled: boredom, weariness, the feeling of “is this it?”. Nothing is wrong, exactly. Nothing is moving either.

And still water is the kind that seeps. A feeling you don’t look at — a quiet dissatisfaction, a small resentment, the wish for something else — doesn’t leave because you’ve folded your arms. It works on the foundations slowly, like damp in a wall. The Four of Cups is often the moment to open a window before that happens.

## By colours {#colours}

A large blue sky: emotions, and a lot of room for them. The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of the grass and the tree: nature, growth, life — he’s sitting in the middle of a fertile place and not using it. The red of his shirt, half hidden under the jerkin and the folded arms: passion and drive, still there, kept in. The yellow-gold of the cups: joy, within reach.

And the [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) of the little cloud the hand comes from: neutrality, no particular will. The offer doesn’t insist. It waits.

## By astrology: Moon in Cancer {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Four of Cups is the third decan of Cancer, ruled by the Moon, and its title is the Lord of Blended Pleasure — pleasure mixed with something less pleasant. The [Moon](/library/astrology/moon/) says what you feel when nobody’s watching. Cancer, the sign the Moon rules, is *I feel*: the protector who absorbs and contains. Together: someone turned inward, feeling a lot and showing little, guarding what they have rather than reaching for more.

Optional: the number has already said the same thing.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, **the Four of Cups is an offer you haven’t looked at yet.** Someone is comfortable, or tired, or sulking a little; things are fine and not exciting; and an invitation, an idea or a person is right there at the edge of their field of vision. The card doesn’t scold. It asks whether you’ve looked.

Reversed, the man lifts his head. Either he finally sees the cup and reaches for it — the end of a sulk, energy coming back — or, in excess, he’s so keen to get out of the boredom that he takes the first cup offered without checking what’s in it.

Next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/), the boredom becomes a departure: moving on from what you’re already doing. Next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/), the stillness is a rest that’s needed, not a sulk. Next to [the Hanged Man](/library/tarot-cards/the-hanged-man/), the pause is chosen, and something is being seen differently from where he sits.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Four of Cups next to the [Ace of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-wands/). Lenses first. Four then one: a settled situation, then a new start. Water then Fire: calm emotions, then a burst of drive. Colours: the blue and green of the Four, then a living wand putting out green leaves — held out by a hand from a cloud as well, the same gesture in Fire.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “Your life is set up and you’re a bit bored in it, and something new has been offered to you — an idea, a proposal, an invitation — that you put aside without really looking.”

To check, I ask: “Has something been offered to you lately that you didn’t take seriously?” If the answer is yes, the next question belongs to the person: do they want it? If it’s no, it’s a miss, and I don’t go hunting for a cup elsewhere to save it.
