## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A bearded king sits on a stone throne set on the open sea, the waves rising around it. He holds a large gold cup in his right hand and a short sceptre in his left. A small fish hangs on a chain at his neck. On one side a fish leaps out of the water — Waite calls it a dolphin; on the other, a ship sails by.

He doesn’t look at his cup. He looks out, at the world he governs with it. The sea moves all around; the throne doesn’t. **Calm in the middle of strong emotion, and a choice about what to do with it.**

## By number: the King {#number}

[The King](/library/numerology/king/) is the rank that decides and answers for it. The question for him is the one I ask of every King — for whom? — and here it means: whose calm is he keeping?

For the King of Cups, what he governs is emotion — his own, and the climate of the room. He decides how much of the sea gets in. He carries masculine energy [as I define it](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/): acting, showing yourself, deciding fast, whoever you are.

## By element: Water {#element}

The suit is [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/), and the King’s rank adds [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/): impulse, drive, the energy that acts. Fire of Water is emotion turned into decision — a feeling that doesn’t stay a feeling but becomes a choice, a gesture, a way of leading.

Fire and Water don’t mix easily, and the drawing shows the effort: a stone throne on a moving sea. Water seeps, too: what he holds down doesn’t disappear. This King’s mastery is real, but the sea is still there. Calm on top can be wisdom — or a lid.

## By colours {#colours}

The [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) of the sea surrounds everything: emotion, vitality, what flows and what carries. The [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) of the stone throne is stability and neutrality — the one fixed point in the picture. The gold of the cup and the crown is value earned. Emotion on every side, and one steady place in the middle of it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: emotional maturity at work. A person — a mentor, a parent, a partner, a manager — who listens, keeps their head when others lose theirs, and decides with their heart without being run by it. Or the moment to be that person yourself. **The King of Cups doesn’t feel less than anyone else; he has decided what to do with what he feels.**

Reversed, the calm goes too far. It can harden into a lid, feelings held under for so long that nobody around him knows what he feels — sometimes not even him. Or it becomes control, a talent for reading others put to work steering them — or the storm finally reaches the throne.

In love, this is the steady partner, the one who doesn’t need to win the argument; [the King of Cups in love](/blog/king-of-cups-tarot-love/) follows him into relationships. Next to the [King of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-swords/), two Kings side by side: heart and head competing for the same throne. Next to [Temperance](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/), the calm is the real thing — feeling mixed with patience. Next to the [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/), he’s the one holding the household steady.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

For the sake of the exercise, the King of Cups comes out next to the Five of Wands. A King then a five: decision, then conflict. Fire of Water, then Fire: emotion turned into action, then five people waving wands who seem to fight without coordinating.

The steady king beside the scuffle of wands leads to: “There’s a lot of noise around you — people pulling in different directions — and you’re the one expected to stay calm and settle it. You can. The question is whether anyone is asking how you feel.” Then ask whether they are the calm one in that noise. If they aren’t, that’s a miss for your notes, and the reading stops there.
