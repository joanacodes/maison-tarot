## What’s on the card {#the-card}

Ten gold cups float in an arc along a rainbow, high in a blue sky. Below, a man and a woman, seen from behind, hold each other by the waist and raise their free arms towards it — the gesture of someone saying “look”. Beside them, two children dance hand in hand. Further off, a river winds through green land, past trees and a house on a rise.

One detail makes the card. The children aren’t looking at the rainbow: Waite, who devised the deck, notes that they are “happy after their own manner”. The adults see a sign in the sky; the children don’t need one. **Happiness is already here, whether or not you look up.**

## By number: ten {#number}

[Ten closes the cycle](/library/numerology/number-10/) and opens onto freedom, onto breathing out. For the Cups, the emotional cycle closes in the best way it can. My own note for this card is short: happiness, family — and the wish to make one.

Every ten has its other side, though: it doesn’t always last, and it nudges you to begin something new. The drawing says so too, since a rainbow is the most fleeting thing in the sky. That isn’t a reason to distrust the moment. It’s a reason to live it while it’s there. Instagram’s 10 is an app its own users barely recognise — the close of one cycle, with a fresh 1 waiting behind it.

## By element: Water {#element}

[Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) here is calm and moving at once: the river runs, unhurried, through the land. That’s settled emotion that still flows — neither stagnant nor stormy, the kind that makes a house feel like home.

Water also seeps, slowly, into foundations. Here it has seeped the right way. What gets tended for years — patience, apologies, meals shared, quarrels settled before bed — ends up holding the house up.

## By colours {#colours}

A [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) sky: emotion, vitality, the feminine, what carries. [Green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) land: growth, life, prosperity — something planted that is giving. The rainbow carries every colour at once, and the cups along it are gold, like a value earned rather than found. Feeling, growth, and everything together.

## By astrology: Mars in Pisces {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn closes the Cups with [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/) in the final decan of Pisces. Mars is action, desire, the way you take what you want. In Pisces, that energy goes into protecting and providing rather than fighting. It’s an odd pairing for such a peaceful card, and it says something useful: this happiness was worked for.

Readers who don’t use the zodiac lose nothing: the rainbow and the dancing children carry the card.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: a home that works. A family — of birth, of choice, of friends — at ease. A relationship that has grown into a household. A peace that arrives after years of effort. **The card asks you to notice the happiness already in the house, not only the one in the sky.**

Reversed, the happiness is either staged or postponed. Staged, it’s the family photo kept up for others, everyone smiling at the rainbow and nobody talking. Postponed, it’s waiting for the perfect picture — the house, the partner, the children, all at once — before allowing yourself to enjoy what is.

People sometimes hope this card announces a pregnancy or a wedding. That hope is tender, and I understand why it’s brought to the cards. Still, [pregnancy is a question I keep away from the cards](/disclaimers/#refusals): a card is the wrong tool to carry that question, and a doctor is the right one. What the Ten of Cups can hold is the wish itself, and the home you’re already making.

Next to the [Ten of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-pentacles/), the happiness has a material base too: a house, generations together. Next to [the Sun](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/), it’s joy without a shadow. Next to the [Five of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-cups/), the happiness exists, but someone is looking at what spilled. ## A reading, as an example {#example}

Give the Ten of Cups and the Six of Swords a dry run together, as if someone had drawn them. Ten then six: a cycle closing well, then a stage where things run and can rest. Water then Air: a home full of feeling, then a decision. A family under a rainbow, then a boat crossing calmer water.

Under the rainbow and across the water, my sentence runs: “There’s a home that works, and you’re about to move it — a crossing, a new place, quieter water ahead.” Then ask whether a move is in the air. If it isn’t, write down the miss, with nothing added to rescue it.
