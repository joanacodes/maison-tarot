## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A figure in a long black cloak stands with head bowed, seen from the side, face hidden. On the ground in front of them, three cups lie knocked over, and what they held runs out — red on one side, green on the other. Behind the figure, two cups are still standing upright. They can’t see them: they’re turned the other way.

Beyond, a river runs across the whole card. On the far bank stand a small stone castle and some trees; on the right, a stone bridge with arches crosses the water. The sky is a flat grey from edge to edge, and the figure is alone under it. **A loss — and, in the same picture, what’s still standing and a way across.**

## By number: five {#number}

Five is the trial in the middle of the cycle, and this card shows what didn’t hold: three spilled cups. In my notes, the Five of Cups is the heaviest card of all: losing, a dark emotion, being alone with your problems. I say it plainly because it’s what the drawing shows, and saying it plainly is kinder than pretending otherwise.

[Number five](/library/numerology/number-5/) also has a minus, and here it’s the key: denial. Not denial of the loss — the figure clearly knows. The other denial: not seeing what’s still there. The advice of 5 is to be honest with yourself and with the situation, both halves of it. Instagram had its own 5 of this kind: IGTV, a bet that flopped and was pulled, while the app itself kept standing.

## By element: Water {#element}

[Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) makes this a loss of the heart: grief, disappointment, a relationship that ended, a friendship that cooled, a hope that didn’t work out. The water here wasn’t neglected for years; it was spilled, and the figure is looking at the stain.

The river matters. Its current is strong feeling, and it’s flowing — past the figure, towards somewhere else. Grief moves too, at its own pace. The bridge is the drawing’s way of saying there’s a crossing, when you’re ready and not before.

## By colours {#colours}

The flat grey sky: neutrality, no particular will — the stillness after something has happened, when you don’t want anything yet. The [black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) cloak: absence, void, energy absorbed. It isn’t wickedness; it’s the colour of mourning, and in my grid black is also emptying out, and reality.

Then the things that aren’t dark: the gold of the five cups, two of them upright; the blue of the river; the green of both banks. A heavy, still moment, worn on the body, and life going on all around it.

## By astrology: Mars in Scorpio {#astrology}

The decan behind this card is the first of Scorpio, which the Golden Dawn gave to Mars, Scorpio’s traditional ruler. [Scorpio](/library/astrology/scorpio/) says *I desire*: intensity, emotional attachment, a tendency to withdraw when it hurts. Mars is the fighter in a chart. Combined, they give a feeling that runs deep, and energy that should go outwards turned inwards — fighting the loss instead of walking away from it.

The black cloak and the spilled cups say this without a single planet.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Five of Cups is grief, regret or disappointment about something that has already happened: a break-up, a falling-out, a plan that failed, a chance missed. **It describes how someone is carrying a loss and where they’re looking — not something that is about to happen.** Nothing on it foretells a death or a diagnosis, and I don’t read it that way; it talks about a feeling, not an event.

Reversed, the figure starts to turn round. Either they notice the two cups behind them — what’s left, the people still there — and the grief begins to move. Or the turn never comes, and they stay facing the spilled cups so long that regret becomes the place they live. Grief that has moved in for good calls for a professional who listens for a living rather than another spread, and I say so ([what tarot doesn’t replace](/disclaimers/#therapy)).

Next to the [Six of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-cups/), the next card in the suit, the two standing cups become six full ones: comfort, old friends, home. Next to [the Star](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/), water is being poured again — hope coming back after the loss. Next to the [Five of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-pentacles/), another 5, the trial is material as well as emotional, and there too a lit church window goes unnoticed. Grief in a relationship gets a fuller treatment on its own page: [the Five of Cups in love](/blog/five-of-cups-tarot-love/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

As a practice case, the Five of Cups lands next to the [Six of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-swords/). Five then six: the trial, then the movement after it. Water then Air: grief, then the decision to move. Colours: the grey sky and the black cloak, then a small boat crossing pale water towards a calmer shore.

I’d keep my voice low for this one: “You’ve lost something that mattered to you, and you’ve started — slowly — to move away from it. You’re not over it, and you don’t have to be yet.”

Then I ask whether that sounds like where they are, and leave room for the answer. A yes doesn’t oblige me to ask what was lost; the person can tell me if they want to. A no means I misread, and I say so plainly.
