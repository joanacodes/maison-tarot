## What’s on the card {#the-card}

No people on this card. In the middle turns a large orange wheel with eight spokes. On its outer rim, the letters T, A, R, O alternate with Hebrew letters; on the inner ring, alchemical signs. A blue sphinx sits on top, holding a sword. Down the left side glides a yellow snake; on the right, a red figure with a jackal’s head — Anubis — rises with the turn. In the four corners, on grey clouds against a blue sky, four winged creatures each read an open book: an angel, an eagle, a bull and a lion.

The missing piece is a hand: nobody is turning the wheel, and there’s no handle. **The drawing shows everything that moves with the wheel and leaves empty the place of whoever turns it** — and that empty place is where I read this card.

## By number: 10 {#number}

[The number 10](/library/numerology/number-10/) is the corridor between two rooms: what was is finishing, what comes doesn’t have a name yet. It brings freedom, change, travel, a breath of air. Its minus: it can be short-lived, and it tells you to start something else.

Changed beyond recognition, Instagram had hit 10: one cycle shut, another opened with its own 1. That’s why the wheel is a circle. Ten brings you back to one.

## By element: Fire {#element}

The Wheel is a [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) card, and that changes the whole reading. Fire is drive, impulse, the energy that motivates. A wheel doesn’t turn on its own; something has to push it.

My note on this card fits in one line: it’s up to you to turn the wheel — create your own luck. Fire is what you put into the push. It’s also the card’s risk: fire spreads when nobody masters it, and a change started in a rush can carry you further than you meant.

## By colours {#colours}

The wheel is orange: creativity, dryness, desire — the colour of a world heating up ([orange](/library/tarot-colours/orange/)). Anubis is red: action, impulse, climbing ([red](/library/tarot-colours/red/)). The snake and the four creatures are yellow: joy, will. The sky is blue — emotions, vitality — and the clouds are grey, neutral.

Orange turning inside blue and grey: something warm and creative in motion, action rising on one side, something slipping away on the other, inside a calm frame.

## By astrology: Jupiter {#astrology}

The Wheel turns under [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/), by Golden Dawn reckoning: expansion, confidence, generosity, excess — luck, or what we take for luck.

Jupiter is useful on this card for one thing: when things go up, the advice is to stay humble.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Wheel is a turning point. Things are moving, a stage is closing, a door is opening. People often read it as luck arriving from outside; I read it as an invitation. The wheel is turning, so this is the moment to put your hand on it, and the useful question is “what am I going to push?” rather than “what’s going to happen to me?”.

Reversed, the wheel feels stuck, or it turns without you. The same problem comes back each time because nothing in it has changed — the same kind of job, the same argument. Sometimes it’s the opposite: change so fast that nobody is steering. Both ask where your hand is.

Next to [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), the hand is found: someone with every tool on the table at the moment things move. Next to the [Ten of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-wands/), another Fire 10, the turn is coming but the person is carrying too much to take it. Next to [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/), which has the same four creatures in its corners, the cycle really is complete. For relationships, I’ve written about [the Wheel of Fortune in love](/blog/wheel-of-fortune-tarot-love/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Here the Wheel of Fortune comes out next to the Magician, in a draw invented for the exercise. 10 then 1: a cycle closing and a new one starting straight away. Fire then Air: energy, then an idea that gives it direction. Orange and blue on the first card; red and white on the second, a man with all four tools on his table.

A wheel in motion beside a table of tools: “Something is ending, and you already have what you need for the next thing — the question is whether you wait for it to happen or start it yourself.”

Does a change feel close? Is there a plan they keep postponing? If their life is standing still, I tell them the reading missed: the person in front of me knows their life better than any card.
