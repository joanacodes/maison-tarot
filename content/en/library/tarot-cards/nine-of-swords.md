## What’s on the card {#the-card}

The background is black. Someone sits up in bed, face buried in both hands, in a long white nightgown. Above and behind them, nine swords hang one above the other in the dark, all level, hilts on the left, blades running to the edge of the card. None of them points at the person. None touches them.

The bed is wooden, with a small scene carved on its side panel. Over the sleeper’s legs lies a quilt of squares: yellow squares with red roses, blue squares marked with little dotted signs. There’s no window, no lamp, nobody else in the room.

**The drawing is about the moment you wake from a bad dream, alone, with the night still all around.**

## By number: nine {#number}

Nine is the finish line: the end of a stage, the last efforts, introspection, patience — and the need to keep moving. My note on this card: coming out of a nightmare, a long troubled period, with protection above you. That’s how I read the swords. They’re lined up above the bed like a rack on a wall, not aimed at anyone, and the person has sat up: the dream is over, even if the fear hasn’t left yet.

The hardest nights often come near the end of a long stretch, when you’re tired from holding on. Instagram’s ninth stage was the redesign that ended everything that had defined it. The advice of [number 9](/library/numerology/number-9/) is not to stop a hundred metres from the finish.

## By element: Air {#element}

Swords are [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/), the element of thinking. Air reaches everything at once. At night, that reach turns against you: every worry is available at the same time, and Air loops. Nine swords, nine thoughts, lined up, each one as sharp as the last.

A thought at 3 a.m. feels like a fact. In daylight, the same thought often weighs less. The card draws the thoughts as swords on a wall: real, heavy, and still outside the person.

## By colours {#colours}

[Black](/library/tarot-colours/black/) takes up most of the card. In my colour grid, black is absence, void, negative thoughts — and also emptying the mind, and reality. Both are here: the night that fills with dark thoughts, and the night that eventually empties. The swords are [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/), the colour of emotions and of what purifies: these are thoughts full of feeling, not cold logic. The nightgown is white, innocence: the person isn’t guilty of the night. And on the quilt, red roses on yellow: passion and joy, still wrapped around their legs.

## By astrology: Mars in Gemini {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Nine of Swords is Mars in the second decan of Gemini. [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/) is how you act and how you fight; Gemini is “I think”, the sign of words and exchanges. Together: a fight that happens in the head — the argument replayed, tomorrow’s conversation rehearsed ten times. Leave this lens aside if it doesn’t help: the swords over the bed already show the fight.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Nine of Swords is worry: a long stretch of stress, bad nights, a fear that grows in the dark. By its number, it’s also the end of that stretch — the moment you sit up. **The Nine of Swords is the waking from the nightmare, not the nightmare itself.**

Reversed, the night is lifting or lingering. Lifting: the hands come down, the worry gets said out loud to someone, the first full night’s sleep. Lingering: the night follows you into the day, and the worry is no longer about anything you can name.

If the bad nights have lasted for weeks, or the worry fills your days too, that isn’t something to read on. A doctor or a therapist is the right door, and [going through it isn’t your cards failing](/disclaimers/#therapy). What a reading can hold meanwhile is smaller and kinder: what would help tonight, and who you could talk to tomorrow.

Next to [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/), the fear is bigger than the facts — check what’s real before you believe it. Next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/), the bad night is followed by real rest. Next to [the Star](/library/tarot-cards/the-star/), calm and hope come back after the dark. For the nights spent awake over a relationship, there’s [the 3 a.m. worries in love, and how to quiet them](/blog/nine-of-swords-tarot-love/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Take a student practising with you who draws the Nine of Swords, with the Six of Cups beside it. Nine then six: the end of a long stretch, then rest and harmony. Air then Water: thoughts that go round, then feelings that settle. Black and steel, then a sunny courtyard and cups full of white flowers.

Out loud, you might say: “You’ve had a long run of bad nights over something you can’t stop thinking about. It’s nearly over, and what comes next is gentler: time for yourself, something from the past that does you good.”

Then check, softly: “Have you been sleeping badly lately?” If they say yes and it sounds heavy, ask whether they have someone to talk to; the reading doesn’t need to go further than that. If they say no, it’s a miss, and that’s fine too.
