## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands on a grassy height, holding one wand across his body with both hands, ready to strike or to parry. Six other wands rise towards him from below the edge of the card. You don’t see who holds them — only their tips, sprouting leaves, coming up from underneath.

He wears a green tunic with yellow sleeves and orange stockings and, if you look at his feet, odd footwear: a low shoe on one, a boot on the other. His face is tense, his eyes on what’s coming. Behind him, a clear light blue sky.

He has the high ground and he’s alone. **The Seven of Wands is the person who got to the top and now has to hold it — dressed in a hurry, outnumbered, still standing.**

## By number: seven {#number}

[Seven](/library/numerology/number-7/) is reflection before action, patience, negotiation. Odd for a man in a fight — until you look at his position. He isn’t charging; he’s measuring where the next wand will come from. The 7 here is the assessment you make under pressure: what’s worth defending, and what isn’t.

At 7, Instagram was being turned by Facebook into an advertising platform — a stage of negotiation, much like this standoff. My advice for any 7 is to remember that nothing here is permanent: whatever the situation, it will turn. Worth telling the man on the hill. The siege won’t last forever, and he doesn’t have to win every round.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is impulse and courage, the energy that makes you stand up when stepping down would be easier. Here it’s Fire in defence: conviction, refusing to give up a place you’ve earned, the nerve to say no to six people at once.

It can also burn itself out. Someone who fights everything — every comment, every rival — ends up exhausted. The young Fire that believes in itself completely makes him brave up there. The question is whether every one of those wands is really an enemy.

## By colours {#colours}

The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of his tunic and of the ground says growth and life: something has grown here, and it’s worth protecting. The yellow sleeves add will and pride. The orange of his legs is heat and drive — and, on its flip side, dryness, the risk of running out.

The pale blue sky is calm: the pressure doesn’t come from above but from below, from the people around him.

## By astrology: Mars in Leo {#astrology}

Mars in Leo, the final third of the sign, is the Golden Dawn’s decan for this card. [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/) is how you act and how you fight; [Leo](/library/astrology/leo/) is the sign of pride. Together: courage with an audience — the fight you won’t back down from because people are watching, and because it’s yours.

It adds one useful question: valour, or pride? Mars in Leo can be both. The man on the hill doesn’t need the planets to show it, though: his stance says most of it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Seven of Wands is holding your ground: defending a position, a project, an opinion or a boundary against competition or criticism. You have the high ground; you also have more people against you than you’d like. **The Seven of Wands isn’t asking you to win every fight — only to choose the ones worth standing up for.**

Reversed, the defence collapses or hardens. Either you give up the hill — you let others decide, you stop defending what matters because you’re worn out — or you defend everything, all the time, until every question feels like a wand.

Next to the [Nine of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-wands/), the siege has gone on: the defender is wounded and tired, still on guard, making the last efforts before the end of a stage. Next to [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), Leo’s own card, the answer is gentleness rather than force — the hand on the lion’s jaw. Next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/), the advice is to put the wand down for a while and rest.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Without anyone in front of you, think through the Seven of Wands followed by the Five of Pentacles. Number: seven, then five — patience under pressure, then the trial, making do with what’s left. Element: Fire, then Earth — conviction, then material reality and the body. Colours: green hill and blue sky, then snow, night, two figures passing a single lit window.

The lone hill and the snowy street meet in one sentence: “You’ve been holding a position on your own for a while, and it’s tiring you more than you let people see. The help you need is closer than you think — you may already be walking past it.”

Then I’d ask whether they feel alone on that hill and, if they do, who that help might be. If they don’t feel alone up there, the hill was my picture, not theirs: a miss, said plainly. To watch this done with strangers and cards drawn at random, come to one of the free [events](/events/).
