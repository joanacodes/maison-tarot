## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands on a grassy height and holds one wand across his body with both hands, ready to strike or to parry. Six other wands rise towards him from below the edge of the card. You don’t see who holds them — only their tips, sprouting leaves, coming up from underneath.

He wears a green tunic with yellow sleeves, orange stockings and, if you look at his feet, odd footwear: a low shoe on one foot, a boot on the other. His face is tense, his eyes on what’s coming from below. The sky behind him is a clear light blue; the ground is green and rocky.

He has the high ground and he’s alone. **The Seven of Wands is the person who got to the top and now has to hold it — dressed in a hurry, outnumbered, still standing.**

## By number: seven {#number}

**seven — doubt, assessment, patience.** [Seven](/library/numerology/number-7/) is reflection and analysis before action, patience, calm, negotiation. That sounds odd for a man in the middle of a fight, until you look at his position. He isn’t charging. He’s on the defensive, measuring where the next wand will come from. The 7 here is the assessment you make under pressure: what’s worth defending, and what isn’t.

In the Instagram story, 7 is Facebook changing the model and turning the app into an advertising platform: neither triumph nor fall, but the negotiating table where you choose what the thing will become. My advice for a 7 is to remember that everything is temporary: your situation, good or bad, will change. That’s worth telling the man on the hill. The siege won’t last forever, and he doesn’t have to win every round.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is impulse and courage — the energy that makes you stand up when stepping down would be easier. Here it’s Fire in defence: conviction, the refusal to give up a place you’ve earned, the nerve to say no to six people at once.

It’s also Fire that can burn itself out. Fire spreads fast when nobody masters it, and someone who fights everything — every comment, every challenge, every rival — ends up exhausted. The teenager who thinks they can do anything is brave on that hill. The question is whether every one of those wands is really an enemy.

## By colours {#colours}

The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of his tunic and of the ground under him says growth and life: something has grown here, and it’s worth protecting. The yellow of his sleeves adds will and pride. The orange of his legs is heat and drive — and, on its flip side, dryness, the risk of running out.

Then the light blue sky, with no storm in it. The pressure doesn’t come from above. It comes from below, from the people around him.

## By astrology: Mars in Leo {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Seven of Wands is Mars in Leo, the third decan of the sign, titled Lord of Valour. [Mars](/library/astrology/mars/) is how you act and how you fight; [Leo](/library/astrology/leo/) is “I will”, pride, the wish to be seen. Together: courage with an audience — the fight you won’t back down from because people are watching, and because it’s yours.

It adds one useful question: is this valour or pride? Mars in Leo can be both. Keep the lens if it speaks to you; the drawing already says most of it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Seven of Wands is holding your ground: defending a position, a project, an opinion or a boundary against competition or criticism. You have the advantage of the high ground; you also have more people against you than you’d like. **The Seven of Wands isn’t asking you to win every fight — only to choose the ones worth standing up for.**

Reversed, the defence collapses or hardens. Either you give up the hill — you let others decide, you stop defending what matters because you’re worn out — or you defend everything, all the time, even when nobody is attacking, and every question starts to feel like a wand.

Next to the [Nine of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-wands/), the siege has gone on: the defender is wounded and tired, still on guard, making the last efforts before the end of a stage. Next to [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), Leo’s own card, the answer is gentleness rather than force — the hand on the lion’s jaw. Next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/), the advice is to put the wand down for a while and rest.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Seven of Wands next to the Five of Pentacles.

Lenses first. Number: seven, then five — patience under pressure, then the trial, making do with what’s left. Element: Fire, then Earth — conviction, then material reality and the body. Colours: the green hill and blue sky, then snow, night, two figures passing a single lit window.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve been holding a position on your own for a while, and it’s tiring you more than you let people see. The help you need is closer than you think — you may already be walking past it.”

Then I’d ask whether it lands, and if it does, who that help might be. If it doesn’t, it’s a miss, and I don’t dress it up. If you’d like to watch this done with strangers and cards drawn at random, that’s what the free [events](/events/) are.
