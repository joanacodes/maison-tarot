## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A well-fed man sits on a plain wooden bench, arms crossed, knees apart, a red cap on his head. He faces us, pleased with himself. Behind him, a long curved counter draped in blue cloth holds nine gold cups in a row, like trophies on a shelf. The background is flat yellow: no landscape, no water, nobody else.

Look where the cups are: behind him, not in his hands. He isn’t drinking from any of them; he’s showing them. And the crossed arms say two things at once — satisfaction, and someone guarding the shelf. **Having what you wanted — and what you do once you have it.**

## By number: nine {#number}

[Nine brings you within sight of the end](/library/numerology/number-9/): a stage nearly done, a final push, time spent looking inward. My note for every nine is “almost there — keep moving”, and this card sits right on that line. The man has his nine cups and he has sat down. A nine that sits too comfortably forgets there’s a ten.

Compare it with the Nine of Pentacles, which I read as delight in what turns up, and thanks for all of it. Same number, different posture: the woman in her garden is busy with what grows; the man on his bench is turned towards whoever is watching. The risk of this nine has a name — resting on your laurels.

## By element: Water {#element}

[Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) is emotion, and here it has been poured into cups and set on a shelf. No river, no sea, no wave: whatever moved has stopped moving. Calm water is settled emotion. That can be peace, a contentment earned after a long stretch. It can also be feeling kept neatly in its place, where it can’t spill on anyone — and can’t reach anyone either.

At his best, this man is the Water archetype, the warm person whose home feels like yours: he has plenty, and the cups are for the table. At his less good, they’re for display, and the bench seats one.

## By colours {#colours}

[Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) fills the whole background: joy, optimism, self-expression — and pride, which sits on the same line of my grid. It’s the colour of someone glad to be exactly where he is. The [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) cloth under the cups is emotion, what carries: feelings are literally holding up the display. The red cap puts a touch of drive on his head. Joy, pride, and emotions arranged with care.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Pisces {#astrology}

Pisces gives this card its middle decan, which the Golden Dawn put under [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/). Jupiter is expansion, generosity, luck — or what you take for luck — and excess. In watery, believing Pisces, it becomes the feeling that things will turn out well, and the plenty that comes with it.

Read the planet here as a reminder rather than a promise: stay humble while things flow. Readers who leave [astrology](/library/astrology/) out get the same warning from the man’s crossed arms.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: contentment. Something wanted has arrived — a relationship that feels right, a home, recognition, a pleasure you finally allowed yourself. Many readers call it the wish card and stop there; I read the posture too. **Getting what you wanted is the start of a question, not the end of one: now what?**

Reversed, the satisfaction sours. Sometimes the wish came true and doesn’t fill you the way you thought — nine cups, and still thirsty. Sometimes contentment turns into smugness, into showing what you have rather than enjoying it, or into sitting so comfortably that the last stretch never gets walked.

Next to the [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/), the shelf opens: what you had for yourself becomes shared. Next to the [Four of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-cups/) — another man with crossed arms — you have everything and see none of it. Next to [the Sun](/library/tarot-cards/the-sun/), the pride relaxes into plain joy, the kind you don’t need to show. And if a spread fills up with this suit, here’s [what a lot of Cups is telling you](/blog/lots-of-cups-in-a-reading/).

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Here the example is hypothetical, and so is the person: the Nine of Cups comes out next to the Eight of Pentacles. Nine then eight: almost at the end of one thing, while another is well under way. Water then Earth: emotional contentment, then hands busy with a craft. A man sitting still, then a man at his workbench.

The seated man beside the busy craftsman makes a two-part sentence: “You have something you wanted, and you’re right to be pleased with it. But you haven’t sat down for good — part of you is working on something else, patiently, piece by piece.” Then ask whether they recognise themselves in it. If they don’t, tell them, and count the miss; the two cards are enough.
