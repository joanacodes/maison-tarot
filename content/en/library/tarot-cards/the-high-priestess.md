## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman sits very still between two pillars and looks straight at you: on your left a black one marked B, on your right a white one marked J. Between them hangs a veil printed with pomegranates, hiding whatever lies behind her.

She wears a blue mantle over a white robe, a white cross on her chest, and a crown shaped like the moon: a full disc between two crescents. A scroll rests in her lap, half covered by the mantle, the letters TORA showing. A crescent moon lies at her feet, and the hem of her mantle flows down like a waterfall.

What’s missing: action, landscape, anyone else. She isn’t showing you what she knows. **The drawing is about knowledge kept behind a veil — present, real, and not handed over.**

## By number: 2 {#number}

The [number 2](/library/numerology/number-2/) is the alliance: two parties, a bond, a contract. Here it’s drawn on the card itself — one black pillar, one white — and she sits between them, weighing. The advice of 2 is to read the contract before you commit, and the scroll in her lap is half hidden: a contract nobody has read to the end. In the Instagram story on my numerology pages, it’s the founders signing with investors, the best moment to read slowly. The minus of 2 shows too: solitude, or a mind so busy weighing that it stops sharing.

## By element: Water {#element}

The High Priestess is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card: emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. The moon on her head and at her feet is what moves water.

But this is calm water. Moving water is strong emotion; calm water is emotion that has settled, and it hides what lies under its surface. In my notes, water seeps into anything with time: a feeling nobody looks at can damage the foundations, slowly, like mould eating a house. That is this card’s minus. Still waters run deep, and sometimes they stagnate.

Her energy is [feminine](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) as I teach it — patience, the intangible, what works behind the scenes — which has nothing to do with being a woman.

## By colours {#colours}

Blue dominates: the mantle, and the water it turns into. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/blue/), blue is emotion, water, vitality, feminine energy. White on the robe, the cross and the J pillar: peace, a clear intention. Black on the B pillar: absence, the void, and reality without the varnish. Two sides, and she isn’t choosing in front of you; between them, an answer that will be felt before it’s explained.

## By astrology: Moon {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the High Priestess goes with the [Moon](/library/astrology/moon/): what you feel when nobody’s watching. When I explain a birth chart, I call the Moon “your own room”, the most private place in the house — and that’s this card, a room you aren’t shown. The Moon also has a face we never see from Earth; the High Priestess works the same way, a façade with a great deal behind it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the High Priestess says that something is known and not yet said. Sometimes the person knows it; sometimes they feel it and haven’t found the words. It’s the card of intuition, the impression that arrives before the reasoning ([how to read intuitively and still check yourself](/blog/read-tarot-intuitively/)).

Reversed, the still water stagnates. The secret is kept so long it becomes a wall, or the façade is all anyone is allowed to see. Concretely: the feeling you’ve decided not to look at, the conversation postponed for the fifth time, the intuition overruled because it wasn’t “logical”.

Next to [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), it’s 1 and 2: one speaks, the other keeps quiet, and the reading sits in the gap. Next to [the Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/), what was held behind the veil starts to grow into something you can see. Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/) — a blindfolded woman holding two crossed swords — the silence is no longer wisdom; it’s a refusal to look. **The High Priestess doesn’t hide things from you; she asks whether it’s time to look at them.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Picture the High Priestess coming out beside the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/): someone in red turning their back on eight stacked cups and heading for the mountains by moonlight.

2 then 8: a bond being weighed, then what you leave behind — my note for the Eight of Cups is moving on from what you’re already doing. Water and Water: feelings, not logistics. Her blue and white, then a red cloak walking away; and a moon on both cards.

What I’d say out loud: “You’ve already decided to leave something, and you haven’t told anyone yet.”

Then I’d check: does that land, and if so, is it a place, a person or a habit? If none of it fits, I call it a miss on the spot.
