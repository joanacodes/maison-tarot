## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman sits between two pillars, very still, and looks straight at you. The pillar on your left is black and marked B; the one on your right is white and marked J. Between them hangs a veil printed with pomegranates, and it hides whatever lies behind her.

She wears a blue mantle over a white robe, with a white cross on her chest. On her head sits a crown shaped like the moon: a full disc held between two crescents. A scroll rests in her lap, half covered by the mantle, with the letters TORA showing. A crescent moon lies at her feet, and the bottom of her mantle falls and flows like a waterfall.

What’s missing: action, landscape, anyone else. She isn’t doing anything, and she isn’t showing you what she knows. **The drawing is about knowledge kept behind a veil — present, real, and not handed over.**

## By number: 2 {#number}

**2 — two — to bind, to weigh, to cooperate.** The [number 2](/library/numerology/number-2/) is the alliance: two parties, a bond, a contract. Here the 2 is drawn on the card itself — two pillars, one black, one white — and she sits between them, weighing. Its advice is to read the contract properly and weigh the pros and cons before you commit.

Look at the scroll in her lap, half hidden by her mantle. That’s the High Priestess’s version of 2: a contract nobody has read to the end yet. In the story of Instagram on my numerology pages, it’s the moment the founders sign with investors — the first time you can lose more than your own time, and the best time to read slowly. The minus of 2 shows here as well: solitude, or a mind so busy weighing that it stops sharing.

## By element: Water {#element}

The High Priestess is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card: emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. Her mantle runs down like a waterfall, and the moon on her head and at her feet is what moves water.

But this is calm water. Moving water is strong emotion; calm water is emotion that has settled — and still water also hides what lies under its surface. In my notes, water seeps into anything with time: a feeling nobody looks at can damage the foundations, slowly, like mould eating a house. That is this card’s minus. Still waters run deep, and sometimes they stagnate.

Her energy is [feminine](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) in the sense I teach it: patience, the intangible, what works behind the scenes. It isn’t about being a woman; it’s a way of acting — by waiting, by listening, by not saying everything at once.

## By colours {#colours}

Blue dominates: the mantle, and the water it turns into. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/blue/), blue is emotion, water, vitality, feminine energy — what flows and what carries. White on the robe, the cross and the J pillar: peace, a clear intention. Black on the B pillar: absence, the void, and also reality without the varnish.

Put together, the two pillars say there are two sides to this, and she isn’t choosing one in front of you. The blue between them says the answer will be felt before it’s explained.

## By astrology: Moon {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the High Priestess goes with the [Moon](/library/astrology/moon/): what you feel when nobody’s watching, your reflexes, how you comfort yourself. When I explain a birth chart, I call the Moon “your own room” — the most private place in the house. That’s this card exactly: a room you aren’t shown. The Moon also has a face we never see from Earth, and the High Priestess works the same way: a façade, and a great deal behind it. It’s an optional lens; the drawing already puts the moon on her head and at her feet, so you lose little without it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the High Priestess says that something is known and not yet said. Sometimes the person in front of you knows it; sometimes they feel it and haven’t put words on it. It’s the card of intuition, of the impression that arrives before the reasoning ([how to read intuitively and still check yourself](/blog/read-tarot-intuitively/)). She knows a lot, and she takes her time.

Reversed, the still water stagnates. The secret is kept so long it becomes a wall, or the façade is all anyone is allowed to see — the person included. Concretely: the feeling you’ve decided not to look at, the conversation postponed for the fifth time, the intuition overruled because it wasn’t “logical”.

Next to [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), it’s 1 and 2: one speaks, the other keeps quiet, and the reading sits in the gap between them. Next to [the Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/), what was held behind the veil starts to grow into something you can see. Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/) — a blindfolded woman, two swords crossed over her chest, the sea and a crescent moon behind her — the silence is no longer wisdom; it’s a refusal to look. **The High Priestess doesn’t hide things from you; she asks whether it’s time to look at them.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the High Priestess next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/): a figure in a red cloak walking away from eight stacked cups, towards the mountains, under a moon.

Lenses first. Numbers: 2 then 8, a bond that is being weighed, then what you leave behind to move on — my note for the Eight of Cups is moving on from what you’re already doing. Elements: Water and Water, so this is about feelings, not logistics. Colours: her blue and white, then the red of a cloak walking away. And one detail: there’s a moon on both cards.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve already decided to leave something, and you haven’t told anyone yet.”

Then I’d check: does that land, and if it does, is it a place, a person or a habit? If they hesitate, I leave the silence alone; this is a card where people sometimes need a minute. If the answer is no, it was a miss, and I say so.
