## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A queen sits outdoors on a carved stone throne, holding a large pentacle in her lap with both hands. Her head is bent towards it, the way you look at something you’re taking care of. She wears a gold crown, a red dress with white sleeves, and a long green veil that falls from the crown down her back to the ground.

An arch of roses and leaves frames the top of the card. Around her: flowering plants, brown earth and grass, blue mountains and water in the distance, a few trees. In the bottom right corner, a rabbit in the grass. The sky is yellow.

She’s on her own land, and she isn’t going anywhere. **Care you can touch: holding what has value close, and letting things grow around you.**

## By number: the Queen {#number}

The [Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) is seated: she knows, and has nothing to prove. This one holds her coin like a loaf of bread, or a child. The rank’s advice is to hold without holding on. At her best she welcomes and lets things grow; at her worst she keeps — people, things, worries — and mistakes care for control.

An example everyone knows: the person in a family whose kitchen is always open, who knows who’s coming to Sunday lunch and what they don’t eat. Nobody asked; it’s how she masters her world.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The Queen of Pentacles is Water of Earth: [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) from her suit — matter, prosperity, what has value for you — and [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) from her rank: emotions, relationships, hidden strengths. Water in Earth is what makes soil fertile. It turns feeling into something concrete: a meal, a garden, a warm room. Water’s archetype, in my notes, is the warm person you feel at home with. This Queen is that person, with a garden.

The flip side comes from both elements. Earth is stubborn; neglected Water seeps in slowly, like damp in a wall. A Queen who gives everything and never says what she feels can end up exhausted — or holding everyone a little too tight.

## By colours {#colours}

[Red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) is her dress: passion, action, strength. This Queen isn’t passive; she works. [Green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) is her veil and the garden: nature, growth, life, prosperity. Red and green together are also the colours of the roses above her — of anything that grows because someone tends it. The yellow sky adds joy, the white sleeves a touch of peace.

Active warmth, growth, and a quiet kind of contentment.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: care made practical. Someone who makes a place liveable — a home, a team, a family — and looks after people with real things. She can stand for somebody you know, or for a state you’re in: grounded, generous, at ease in your body and your home. It isn’t about sex: the open kitchen above can just as well be a man’s. **The best care leaves people stronger, not more dependent.**

Reversed, her care either runs dry or holds too tight. The one who feeds everyone forgets to eat, and the garden goes untended because there’s nothing left to give. Or protection smothers — my note on the Empress, who can smother with her maternal presence, applies here too — or comfort becomes the only goal.

Next to [the Empress](/library/tarot-cards/the-empress/), the earth mother twice, and the same question: feeding, or keeping? Next to the [King of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-pentacles/), the household complete: one tends, the other manages. Next to the [Five of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/five-of-pentacles/) — two figures in the snow passing a lit church window — her warmth is what’s missing, or what someone needs to ask for.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Practise on this pair: the person you’re reading for draws the Queen of Pentacles, then the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/). Queen then four: inner mastery, then stability — a pause, a structure. Earth then Air: care with the hands, then a mind that needs to stop. A queen holding her coin, then a knight stretched out on a tomb beneath a wall of hung swords.

The garden queen beside the resting knight puts it like this: “You look after a lot of people, and you do it well. These cards are asking who looks after you — and suggesting a rest that isn’t a reward. Only a rest.”

Then the check: does that sound like them, or like someone close to them? If it sounds like nobody they know, the miss gets written down, and that’s the end of it.
