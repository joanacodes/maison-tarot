## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands alone in a green field, holding a pentacle up in front of his face with both hands. His fingertips barely touch it. He looks at it the way you look at something you’ve just been handed and don’t yet know what to do with.

He wears a green tunic with a brown belt, soft brown boots, and a red cap whose long scarf falls over his shoulder and down his back. Around him, a meadow dotted with small flowers, a clump of trees on the left, a blue mountain on the right and, below it, a field already ploughed in red-brown furrows. The sky is plain yellow. No one else, no house, no road.

He stands on fresh grass, with worked earth further off. **A value you can see but haven’t made yours yet: the student’s first long look at their material.**

## By number: the Page {#number}

The [Page](/library/numerology/page/) is young without being a child, and this one holds his coin at eye level, still working out what it’s for. In the tradition they’re also the messenger — the letter, the offer, the news.

In Pentacles, the message is concrete: a job offer, a place on a course, a first pay slip, a plot of land to look after. Pages are meant to remain students. My note for number 1 fits him too: humility, get ready to learn — a student for life, like all of us. A Page of Pentacles who thinks he’s already a King drops the coin.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The Page of Pentacles is Earth of Earth: suit and rank are both [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/), so this is the element at its purest. Matter, value, the result of work — at the very beginning. It’s mostly feminine energy, protective and stable, the soil things grow in. This Page won’t rush; he’ll turn the coin over until he understands it.

That’s the card’s strength — tenacity, perseverance — and its flip side: stubbornness, and a slowness that can look like standing still. The ploughed furrows behind him are a gentle reminder that, at some point, the ground has to be worked.

## By colours {#colours}

[Green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) dresses him and the field: nature, growth, birth, life. It’s the colour of something that has just started to grow. [Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) fills the whole sky: joy, optimism, the inner child, will. The red of the cap puts a little drive at his head — the wish to do something with what he holds. Brown boots and ploughed earth add worked ground and patience.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: the start of an apprenticeship. Someone learning a trade, beginning a course, taking a first job, or receiving a concrete offer and studying it before saying yes. The figure might be someone young, someone studying, a newcomer — or you, at the start of something you want to learn properly. It’s a card I’d hand anyone who wants to [learn tarot](/learn-tarot/) one card at a time. **The Page doesn’t need to know yet; he needs to keep looking, and then to start.**

Reversed, the student either never starts or can’t stop preparing: the offer admired but never used, the course planned and never begun, the coin left in the air — or studying turned into a way of not doing, one more training, one more book.

Next to the [Ace of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-pentacles/), the coin itself is offered from a hand out of a cloud, and the Page is the one who receives it. Next to the [Eight of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-pentacles/), the student reaches the bench: a look becomes a gesture, repeated. Next to the [Knight of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/knight-of-pentacles/), the same coin on horseback: the student becomes the one who carries the plan.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

As an illustration, a neighbour asks you to try, and draws the Page of Pentacles beside the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/). Page then two: a student, then a choice between two sides — in my notes, a contract to read carefully, pros and cons to weigh. Earth then Air: something concrete in the hands, then a mind weighing it up. A young man gazing at a coin, then a blindfolded woman balancing a sword in each hand, the sea behind her.

A student’s coin, then a decision on hold, put into words: “Someone has offered you something real — a place, a job, a course — and you’re looking at it from every angle. You haven’t said yes or no. Maybe you’re waiting to feel sure, and that feeling may only come once you’ve started.”

Then ask whether an offer like that is around them at the moment. If not, the reading missed; note it, and don’t go fishing for another meaning.
