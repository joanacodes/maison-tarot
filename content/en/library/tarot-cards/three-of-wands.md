## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man stands on a ridge with his back to us, at the edge of a cliff. Below him stretches a wide sea, drawn in gold like the sky, with a few small ships sailing across it and a low line of mountains beyond. Three wands are planted around him, all sprouting leaves; he holds the one on the right lightly, leaning on it.

He wears a long red robe, a green cloak over one shoulder, a black band patterned with yellow across his back, a blue sleeve, and a white band in his hair.

We never see his face. We see what he sees. **The Three of Wands is someone watching their ships go out — the work is launched, and now it travels without them.**

## By number: three {#number}

**three — to create, to celebrate, the first result.** [Three](/library/numerology/number-3/) is cadence: the machine running, the contract that works. After the hesitation of the Two, something has been sent out, and it holds. In the Instagram story, it’s the launch: people sign up, post, come back, and the thing turns without being pushed.

My advice with a 3 is to finish one project before starting another — and on this card it has a real target. A man who has seen his first ships leave is already picturing the next fleet. The minus of 3 is exactly that: launching new things before the others are home, and a certain solitude at the top of the cliff.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) here is no longer a spark or a plan: it’s drive released into the world, working at a distance. That’s the part Fire finds hardest. Once the ships are out, you can’t make them sail faster by staring.

The Three shows Fire mastered — calm, planted, leaning on its own work — and its limit: what it lit is travelling now, on winds it doesn’t control.

## By colours {#colours}

[Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) floods sky and sea alike: joy, optimism, pride, will — the sun on a project that’s going well. Against it, the man’s [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) robe: action, strength, the one who made it happen.

The green cloak adds growth: what he sent out should come back bigger. The touch of blue on his sleeve, the only cool colour on him, is feeling kept in check. Yellow everywhere, red at the centre: a confident card, maybe a little proud.

## By astrology: Sun in Aries {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Three of Wands is the Sun in Aries, the second decan of the sign, titled Lord of Established Strength. [The Sun](/library/astrology/sun/) is the centre of a chart, what a person wants to project; Aries says “I am” and goes first. Together: initiative that has become visible, and is happy to be seen.

The lens matches the drawing — a man in full light, at the front of his own story. It stays optional: the yellow has already told you the same.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Three of Wands says: it’s launched, and it’s working. A project sent out, an application posted, a business selling beyond its first circle — often with a sense of distance: abroad, online, far from where you stand. The person is waiting for returns and has reasons to be confident. **The Three of Wands is the patience of someone who has done their part: the ships are out, let them sail.**

Reversed, the waiting goes sour. The ships are late, or never really left: the project announced but not sent, the email still in drafts. Or the excess of 3 — launching the fourth idea while the first three haven’t come home, and calling that ambition.

Next to the [Eight of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-wands/), the returns come fast — news, answers, all at once; glass half full or half empty, depending on how you look. Next to the [Three of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-pentacles/), the other face of 3: one looks far, the other works on site with a team, and the project needs both. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/), too many ships for one harbour: options piling up, none chosen.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Three of Wands next to the Four of Swords. Lenses first. Number: three, then four — it’s running, then it settles. Element: Fire, then Air — drive, then the mind. Colours: the sunny yellow of the Three, then the pale stone of the Four of Swords, a figure lying still with joined hands under a stained-glass window.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve sent something out recently — a project, an application, a proposal — and now you’re waiting. You don’t need to do more right now. You need to rest while it travels.”

Then I’d ask whether it lands. If it does, I’d ask what they’re tempted to do instead of resting, because that’s where the next mistake usually hides. If it doesn’t, it goes in the notebook as a miss. Number, element, colours, one sentence, then the check: that order is [the method](/method/), and it works on any pair of cards.
