## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A rider on a white horse moves through a crowd, a laurel wreath on his head. He carries a wand with a second wreath tied near the top with a red ribbon. Around him, people on foot raise five more wands; you see only their heads and shoulders, packed together on the left and behind the horse.

He wears a red cloak over an orange tunic and tall brown boots. A long green cloth with a gold border covers the horse almost to the ground, so you see little of it but its head.

The sky is a clear light blue, and everything moves one way, the rider higher than everyone. **The Six of Wands is a victory seen from outside — the moment everyone around you knows you’ve won.**

## By number: six {#number}

[Six](/library/numerology/number-6/) is 3 exalted: the machine isn’t only running, it’s fast. Success, triumph, and at last the chance to rest. Harmony regained: after the jumble of the Five, the group has agreed on someone. Exchange: they give him recognition, he gives them a victory to celebrate.

In the Instagram story, 6 is Facebook deciding to invest: the company is carried forward, like this rider. He wears one wreath and carries another — a lot of laurel for one man. That’s why the advice of 6 weighs so much on this card: stay calm and humble while the results flow, because praise this heavy can turn a head before the next fight.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) lights the night and draws people to it. Here the drive has paid off and it shows: enthusiasm, confidence, the energy that makes others follow — a torch at the head of a procession.

It’s also Fire that needs fuel. Recognition feeds it — applause, a promotion, likes, thanks — and the risk is needing that fuel to keep going. The teenager who thinks they can do anything has been proved right, in public. Lovely, and worth watching.

## By colours {#colours}

The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of the horse’s cloth and the laurel says growth and prosperity: the victory is real, and bearing fruit. The red of cloak and ribbon is action and strength, the person who did it.

The white horse adds peace — a clean win, no blood. The orange tunic is the heat that got him here. And the light blue sky has no cloud in it. Nothing threatens, for now.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Leo {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Six of Wands is Jupiter in Leo, the second decan of the sign. [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/) is expansion, confidence, generosity — and excess, like that second wreath. Add [Leo](/library/astrology/leo/), the “I will” of the zodiac, and you have the drawing: a big win, in public.

Keep the lens if it speaks to you. It adds one warning: Jupiter enlarges whatever it touches, and Leo loves an audience. If astrology isn’t your thing, the two wreaths have said it already.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Six of Wands is recognition: a project that succeeds and is seen to, a promotion, good results, an exam passed, a win the people around you celebrate. Confidence comes back. **The Six of Wands is success with witnesses — enjoy the procession, and remember who is walking beside the horse.**

Reversed, the procession doesn’t come. The work is done and nobody notices, the credit goes to someone else, or you wait for applause before letting yourself feel you’ve won. The other excess: the wreath gets heavy — pride, needing the crowd to keep going at all.

Next to the [Seven of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-wands/), the next step is drawn: once you’re on top, you have to hold the position. Next to the [Six of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-pentacles/), another 6 — the scales, knowing your value, a kingdom that lasts — the success is shared fairly. Next to [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/), the victory came from will: the person drove this.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

What would you make of the Six of Wands next to the Four of Cups? Treat it as a practice case. Number: six, then four — success, then a stability that can turn to boredom. Element: Fire, then Water — public recognition, then private feeling. Colours: the green, red and blue of the procession, then a young man under a tree, arms crossed, ignoring the cup a hand holds out to him from a cloud.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve had a success recently, and people noticed. But inside it doesn’t feel the way you imagined — something about it leaves you flat, and you might be missing a new offer because of it.”

Then I’d ask whether it lands. If it does, the useful question is what they hoped the success would give them. If not, I note the miss. Working through pairs like this at your own pace is what a one-to-one [session](/sessions/) is for.
