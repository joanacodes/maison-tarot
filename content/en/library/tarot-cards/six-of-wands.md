## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A rider on a white horse moves through a crowd, a laurel wreath on his head. He carries a wand with a second laurel wreath tied near the top with a red ribbon. Around him, people on foot raise five more wands; you see only their heads and shoulders, packed together on the left and behind the horse.

He wears a red cloak over an orange tunic and tall brown boots. The horse is covered by a long green cloth with a gold border that falls almost to the ground, so you see little of it but its head, turned forward.

The sky is a clear light blue. Everything moves one way: the procession goes forward, and the rider sits higher than everyone else. **The Six of Wands is a victory seen from outside — the moment everyone around you knows you’ve won.**

## By number: six {#number}

**six — harmony regained, exchange.** [Six](/library/numerology/number-6/) is 3 exalted: the machine isn’t only running, it’s fast. Success, triumph and, at last, the chance to rest. Harmony regained: after the jumble of the Five, the group has agreed on someone. Exchange: they give him their recognition, he gives them a victory to celebrate.

In the Instagram story, 6 is Facebook deciding to invest. What threatened the company at 5 becomes what propels it, and everything goes faster and better. My advice for a 6 is to stay calm and humble while the results flow. The rider wears one wreath and carries another, which is a lot of laurel for one man. A 6 goes to the head, and a swollen head makes the mistakes of the 7.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) lights the night and draws people towards it. On the Six of Wands, the drive has paid off and it shows: enthusiasm, confidence, the energy that makes others follow. It’s Fire as the torch at the head of a procession.

It’s also Fire that needs fuel. Recognition feeds it — applause, a promotion, likes, a word of thanks — and the risk is starting to need that fuel to keep going. The teenager who thinks they can do anything has just been proved right, in public. Lovely, and worth watching.

## By colours {#colours}

The [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) of the horse’s cloth and of the laurel says growth and prosperity: the victory is real, and it’s bearing fruit. The red of the cloak and the ribbon says action and strength — the person who did it.

The white horse adds peace: a clean win, without blood. The orange tunic adds heat and creativity, the Fire that got him here. And the light blue sky above has no cloud in it. Nothing threatens, for now.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Leo {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Six of Wands is Jupiter in Leo, the second decan of the sign, titled Lord of Victory. [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/) is expansion, confidence, generosity — and excess. [Leo](/library/astrology/leo/) is “I will”: play, challenge, pride, wanting to be seen. Together they’re the drawing: a big win, in public.

Keep the lens if it speaks to you. It adds one warning the picture only hints at: Jupiter is the planet of “it’ll be fine”, and Leo loves an audience. If astrology isn’t your thing, the two wreaths have said it already.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Six of Wands is recognition: a project that succeeds and is seen to succeed, a promotion, good results, an exam passed, a win the people around you celebrate. Confidence comes back. **The Six of Wands is success with witnesses — enjoy the procession, and remember who is walking beside the horse.**

Reversed, the procession doesn’t come. The work is done and nobody notices, the credit goes to someone else, or you’re waiting for applause before you allow yourself to feel you’ve won. The other excess: the wreath gets heavy — pride, arrogance, needing the crowd in order to keep going at all.

Next to the [Seven of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-wands/), the next step is already drawn: once you’re on top, you have to hold the position. Next to the [Six of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-pentacles/), another 6 — the scales, knowing your value, a kingdom that lasts — the success is shared fairly. Next to [the Chariot](/library/tarot-cards/the-chariot/), the victory came from will: the person drove this, they didn’t wait for it.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Six of Wands next to the Four of Cups.

Lenses first. Number: six, then four — success, then a stability that can turn to boredom. Element: Fire, then Water — public recognition, then private feeling. Colours: the green, red and clear blue of the procession, then a young man sitting under a tree with his arms crossed, ignoring the cup a hand holds out to him from a cloud.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve had a success recently, and people noticed. But on the inside it doesn’t feel the way you imagined. Something about it leaves you flat — and you might be missing a new offer because of it.”

Then I’d ask whether that lands. If it does, the useful question is what they hoped the success would give them. If it doesn’t, I note the miss and move on. Working through pairs like this at your own pace, with your own cards, is what a one-to-one [session](/sessions/) is for.
