## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A naked woman kneels at the edge of a pool, her left knee on the ground and her right foot on the surface of the water — on it, not in it. She holds a jug in each hand. One pours into the pool; the other pours onto the land, where the water runs off in small streams. Above her shines one large eight-pointed star, surrounded by seven smaller ones. On the right, a bird sits in a tree on a rise of ground.

After the Tower, the sky is clear again: nothing falls. No clothes, no crown, no building, no one watching. **She pours what she has into the world, calmly, with nothing left to protect.** That’s the drawing as a whole: giving back after a hard time.

## By number: 17 {#number}

**17 — 1 + 7 = 8 — movement, mastery, the fruit of work.** Seventeen comes back to [8](/library/numerology/number-8/), the number of Strength: “4 exalted”, lasting stability, what is no longer a bet. Eight is also a gate between the tangible and the intangible — the lemniscate, infinity lying down. The Star shows that gate: one jug for the land, one for the water, what you can touch and what you feel, fed at the same time.

After the Tower’s 7, the assessment, 8 is what you rebuild on. In the Instagram story, 8 is the empire nobody questions any more because it works; the Star is the quieter version, something that lasts because you keep tending it.

## By element: Air {#element}

The Star is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card, though it’s full of water, and that’s worth stopping on. Air is the mind, decision, communication; it has access to everything at once, which makes it the best decision-maker of the four. The water here isn’t a flood. It’s poured: deliberate, measured, directed.

So the Star is hope as a decision rather than a mood — you decide where your attention and your care go. Air’s risk is the usual one: an idea you keep pouring without checking whether anything grows.

## By colours {#colours}

[Blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) for the sky and the pool: emotion, purifying, vitality, what flows. [Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) for the great star: joy, optimism, the inner child. White for the small stars: peace, what hasn’t been soiled. Green for the land and the tree: growth, life starting again. Read the colours before the card and you already have: emotions washed clean, a joy that’s coming back, and ground where something can grow.

## By astrology: Aquarius {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Star belongs to [Aquarius](/library/astrology/aquarius/), the water-bearer — and the woman with her two jugs is exactly that. Aquarius is an Air sign whose verb is “I know”, to be heard as “I think I know”: the new idea, the bridge between old and new, the urge to share what you’ve found. It adds a social touch: the water isn’t kept, it’s given. If astrology isn’t your lens, leave it; the drawing says the same.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Star is hope after a hard time — not the loud kind, the steady kind. Things are calmer, you’re beginning to trust again, and you’re giving something back: time, care, ideas. It’s also a card of being seen as you are, with nothing to hide.

Reversed, the same hope is blocked or overdone. Blocked: you’re past the worst and can’t believe it yet, so you keep both jugs full and pour nothing. Overdone: you pour everything out for others and keep nothing for your own ground.

Next to [the Tower](/library/tarot-cards/the-tower/), it’s the calm right after the storm. Next to [Temperance](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/), the other card that pours water, it says the same thing at two speeds: Temperance mixes with care, the Star gives freely. Next to [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/), the card that follows it, hope has a darker stretch to cross before it’s confirmed.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Star next to the [Six of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-swords/).

Lenses first. Numbers: 17 comes back to 8, what lasts; the Six of Swords is 6, the machine running, possible to rest. Elements: Air and Air, so this is about decisions and the mind, not only feelings. The pictures help too: the Star’s open sky, and on the Six of Swords, a boat crossing water towards a calmer shore.

The sentence you’d say out loud: “You’re past the hardest part and you know it, even if you’re still crossing. What’s helping now is something you decided, not something that happened to you.”

Then check it. Ask what it brings up, and let them answer before you add anything. If it lands, notice that you got there with three lenses and no memorised meaning: cards aren’t magic, you are. If it doesn’t, write it down as a miss.
