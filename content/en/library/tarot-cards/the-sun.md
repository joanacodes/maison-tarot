## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A huge sun with a human face fills the top of the card, its rays alternating straight and wavy. Below, a naked child with flowers in its hair rides a white horse, arms wide open, while a long red banner streams out beside them. Behind them runs a grey stone wall, and above the wall, a row of sunflowers.

Look at what’s missing: no saddle, no reins, no adult, no clothes. The child isn’t holding on to anything. **Joy with nothing to hide and nothing to grip.** That’s the drawing as a whole: being out in the open, and safe there.

## By number: 19 {#number}

**19 — 1 + 9 = 10 — the end of the cycle, transition.** Nineteen comes back to [10](/library/numerology/number-10/), the number of the Wheel of Fortune: the corridor between two rooms, freedom, change, room to breathe. Ten can be short-lived, and it folds back to 1: the Sun is a finish line that’s also a beginning.

In the Instagram story, 10 is the point where people no longer recognise the platform, because it has become something else entirely: the cycle closes, and a new story starts with its own 1. The Sun is that moment seen from the bright side. You came through the night of the Moon, 18, and for now there’s nothing left to cross.

## By element: Fire {#element}

The Sun is a [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) card, and here Fire is at its kindest. Fire forges tools, warms and lights the night; it’s passion and the energy that gets you moving. The archetype in my notes is a teenager full of life who thinks they can do anything. The child on the horse is that energy before anything destructive has crept in: warmth, vitality, the pleasure of being alive and seen. Fire’s risk stays in the background — it spreads fast when nobody watches it — but on this card it only warms.

## By colours {#colours}

[Yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) everywhere — the sun and the sunflowers: joy, optimism, pride, self-expression, the inner child, here almost literally. The red of the banner: action, strength, impulse. The white of the horse: innocence, peace. The grey wall: stability, something that holds without a word. Read the colours before the card and you already have: joy, energy, nothing to hide, and solid ground behind you.

## By astrology: Sun {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, this card belongs to [the Sun](/library/astrology/sun/) itself — the placement everyone knows, the answer to “what sign are you?”. It says the centre: what someone organises their life around, the light they want to show. In my own test it was one of the least reliable placements, about a third accurate: the shop window rather than the shop. That’s a fair way to read the card too — what shows itself willingly. If astrology isn’t your lens, leave it aside.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Sun is success, clarity, warmth, being seen: something works, people notice, and you can enjoy it without apologising. It’s one of the most open cards in the deck. Its minus is small but real: the shop window can be mistaken for the whole shop, and a joy that has to be shown all the time gets tiring.

Reversed, it’s the same light held back — a success you won’t let yourself enjoy, a good moment you keep for later. Or in excess: being seen becomes the point, and you perform the good mood instead of having it.

Some people see the child and ask whether it means a pregnancy; the question usually comes from hope or worry. I don’t read on pregnancy, for [the same reasons as health](/disclaimers/#refusals): a doctor can answer, and the Sun can hold the joy around what you hope for.

Next to [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/), the Sun is morning after a long night. Next to the [Six of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-wands/), another rider on a white horse, it’s success that people see. Next to the [Wheel of Fortune](/library/tarot-cards/wheel-of-fortune/), another 10, it reminds you that it’s up to you to turn the wheel: create your own luck while the light is good.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Sun next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/).

Lenses first. Numbers: 19 comes back to 10, the end of a cycle; the Four of Swords is 4, stability, rest after the work. Elements: Fire and Air — energy, and a mind that has been resting. The pictures: the Sun’s open yellow beside the grey stillness of the Four of Swords, a knight lying at rest in a quiet chapel.

The sentence you’d say out loud: “Something has finished, and it went well. You’ve rested; you’re allowed to get up and enjoy it now.”

Then check it. Ask what it brings up, and let them say it before you do. If it doesn’t land, it’s a miss, and you note it. When it does, you’ll understand something no page can give you: you can’t be told what it’s like, you can only try.
