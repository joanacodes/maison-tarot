## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man walks away from us, bent forward, carrying ten wands in his arms. He clutches them against his chest in one tight bundle; their tops fan out above his head and in front of his face, so he can’t see where he’s going. We never see his face either: his head is pressed into the wood.

He wears a short red-orange tunic, pale leggings and brown boots. Ahead of him, on the right, a house with a red roof stands among green trees — not far now. The ground is flat and yellow, the sky pale blue. Even here, the wands carry green shoots.

Nobody helps him. No cart, no second pair of arms, no path visible past the bundle. **He’s almost home, and he’s carrying everything alone, in a way that stops him from seeing the door.**

## By number: ten {#number}

**ten — the end of the cycle, transition.** [Ten](/library/numerology/number-10/) is the corridor between two rooms: what was is finishing, what comes has no name yet. Its gifts are freedom, change, breathing; its minus is that it can be short-lived, and that it tells you to start something else. The Ten of Wands is the Fire cycle at its close: everything you lit since the Ace is now in your arms, all at once.

My note on this card: keep the creative energy, without overloading. Every wand against his chest was once an idea worth having. The 10 doesn’t ask you to drop them; it asks you not to carry all ten through the next door. In the Instagram story, 10 is the platform turning into something else entirely — the old form doesn’t come through the corridor intact, and isn’t meant to.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) creates fast and spreads fast. That speed is what makes the Wands thrilling at the start — the spark, the first plans, the race — and what makes the Ten heavy: everything you lit kept burning, and now all of it needs tending. Fire forges tools; on this card, the tools have become the load.

The Fire archetype is the teenager who believes they can do anything. The Ten is what happens when they try to do everything, at the same time, by themselves.

## By colours {#colours}

The man is dressed in [red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) — action, strength, impulse — and the roof of the house ahead is red too. The place he’s walking towards is the colour of his effort: what he carries is meant for that house.

The ground is a flat [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/): will, pride, the inner child. Will is what keeps him walking; pride may be what keeps him from asking for a hand. Pale blue sky, green trees by the house: there is air and life on the other side of the effort.

## By astrology: Saturn in Sagittarius {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Ten of Wands is Saturn in Sagittarius, and the Order called it the Lord of Oppression. [Saturn](/library/astrology/saturn/) is the hardest lesson: responsibility, structure, the work of years. Sagittarius wants the open road. Together, the journey turns into a duty — you’re still travelling, with all the luggage on your own back.

Saturn was one of the three placements that held best when I tested my chart and others’, at 75 to 90% (with Lilith and Chiron). That’s why I keep this attribution when the Ten comes out. It stays optional all the same: the drawing is enough without it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Ten of Wands is the load: too many responsibilities, a project grown bigger than the person carrying it, someone who says yes to everything and then does it all. It’s also, plainly, a success — those wands are his, and he’s nearly there. **You don’t have to put the fire out; you have to stop carrying it alone.**

Reversed, two roads. Either the bundle wins, and everything is dropped in a heap a few metres from the door. Or some of it is finally put down — delegated, cancelled, refused — and the reversal is a relief rather than a fall.

If the weight someone describes sounds like it lives in the body — sleep gone, exhaustion that doesn’t lift — that’s for a doctor rather than a card. Health is one of [the subjects I don’t read on](/disclaimers/#refusals), because a wrong word there costs too much; what the card can hold meanwhile is the question of what to set down first.

Next to the [Three of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-pentacles/), the answer is in the picture: a craftsman at work while two others hold the plan — share it. Next to the [Four of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-wands/), which I read as foundations and a family being built, the house becomes a celebration worth arriving at. Next to [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/), Saturn’s major card, the cycle truly closes: he gets through the door.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Picture someone who sits down without a word and draws the Ten of Wands followed by the [Ace of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-wands/). Lenses first: 10 then 1 — the end of a cycle, then the start of another; Fire and Fire — the same energy, spent, then renewed; a man bent under ten branches, then a single branch held out by a hand from a cloud.

What you might say: “You’re finishing something heavy, and a new idea is already asking for your arms. Put the bundle down before you take it.”

Then check: “Is there something you’re carrying that you could hand over this month?” Their answer tells you whether you read the right load. If none of it fits, call it a miss, and don’t go looking in the next card for a rescue.
