## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A man stands in front of a row of eight wands planted upright in the ground, like a fence at his back. He grips a ninth with both hands at chest height, its foot set on the ground in front of him. A white bandage is wrapped round his head. He doesn’t look at us: he glances to the side, wary, as if he has heard something.

He wears a rust-red tunic with a belt, white sleeves, pale leggings and boots. He stands on flat grey ground, like a stone terrace; beyond the wands rise green hills under a pale blue sky. The wands still have their green shoots.

What’s missing is the enemy. No one attacks, nothing comes over the hill. **He has already been hurt, he’s still standing, and he’s guarding against a blow that isn’t in the picture.**

## By number: nine {#number}

[Nine](/library/numerology/number-9/) is the finish line in sight: last efforts, patience, a look inward before the cycle closes — and you keep moving. That’s the drawing exactly. Eight wands are already planted behind him, the ninth is in his hands; one more and the series is complete. He isn’t starting anything, he’s holding on near the end.

The risk of 9 is stopping a hundred metres from the line — or, here, standing guard so long that you forget to walk the last stretch. In the Instagram story, 9 is the redesign built around Reels: what defined the app is ending, and the next form isn’t there yet. The Nine of Wands is that in-between, lived with your back to a fence.

## By element: Fire {#element}

[Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) is the energy that motivates, the urge to begin. By the ninth wand, that urge has become endurance: nothing is being launched any more, something is being kept alight. Fire spreads fast when no one masters it; it also dies when no one tends it. This man tends it — perhaps too anxiously.

The teenager I use as the Fire archetype has grown up a little on this card. They know now that things can burn, and they have the bandage to prove it.

## By colours {#colours}

The ground is [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/): neutrality, stability, no particular will. He isn’t on a battlefield but on ordinary, solid ground, which suggests the danger may live more in his head than in the landscape. The [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/) of the bandage is peace and care: the wound has been dressed, someone has looked after it.

The rust red of his tunic keeps the strength and action of the suit, and the green on every wand says the project is still alive. Grey, white, red, green: solid ground, a wound tended, strength left, life in the wood.

## By astrology: Moon in Sagittarius {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, this card is the Moon in Sagittarius. The [Moon](/library/astrology/moon/) speaks for the private self and its need to feel safe. Sagittarius is the traveller who wants to try everything and go and see. Put a need for safety inside someone built to roam and you get this man: a free spirit who was hurt once and now checks the horizon before taking a step.

Keep it if astrology is one of your lenses, because it adds the intimate side, the fear under the guard. Leave it if not; the drawing already says it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Nine of Wands is resilience: you’ve been through a lot, you’re still here, and you’re close to the end. It’s the person who keeps going on a project after three setbacks, who won’t give up the last stretch. It also says you’re on guard. **You can lower your guard without losing your ground.**

Reversed, the vigilance curdles into suspicion: every message read as a threat, every hand refused, the fence of wands turned into a wall no one can cross. Or the strength gives out, and he sits down a few steps from the end, too tired to plant the last wand. Ask which of the two it is.

Next to the [Seven of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-wands/), you see the fight that came before: the Seven defends a height against six wands rising from below, the Nine has survived it and waits. Next to [the Hermit](/library/tarot-cards/the-hermit/), the other 9, the watch turns inward — less scanning the hills, more looking at what the battles changed. Next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/), the advice is rest: the bandage needs time more than another guard shift.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

This one is for practice: the Nine of Wands, then the [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/). Nine to two is the end of a long effort, then an exchange between two people; Fire to Water, endurance, then feeling; and in colour, grey ground and a white bandage, then two figures facing each other, each holding out a cup.

The sentence you might try: “You’ve been holding something on your own for a long time, and an exchange is being offered. The question is whether you can put one wand down to take the cup.”

Then check without pushing: “Is there a place in your life where you’re still on guard?” Leave the wording of the answer to them. If nothing in it fits, say so and write it down as a miss.
