## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man with curly hair sits on a low wooden bench, bent over his work. Hammer raised in one hand, chisel in the other, he is cutting a pentacle into a disc propped in front of him. Blue tunic, black apron, red hose. His eyes don’t leave the coin.

On the right, a tall wooden post carries the finished pieces: five pentacles hung one above the other, a sixth at its foot. One more lies on the ground near his feet. Six done, one under the chisel, one to come: eight.

Behind him, almost nothing: a pale grey sky and, small in the lower left corner, a town with white walls and a tower on a green hill — behind his back. **Repetition turning into mastery — and whatever gets left aside while you repeat it.**

## By number: eight {#number}

[Eight is the empire](/library/numerology/number-8/): four exalted, structure turned into lasting strength. My note for this card is short — prosperous business, mastery, necessary effort — and the third part matters as much as the first two. Tip the 8 over and it becomes the infinity sign floating above the Magician: control, a gesture that loops back on itself.

Instagram as an empire is the loud version of 8; this card is its quiet side, the hours at the bench that nobody films.

## By element: Earth {#element}

Pentacles belong to [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): matter, value, and on this card above all the result of work. Here Earth isn’t a field or a treasure; it’s a material being shaped, with tenacity, perseverance, endurance.

Earth’s flip side is stubbornness, and it lives here too: the same coin, cut the same way, because that’s the habit. The skill in his hands is worth more than the six coins on the post.

## By colours {#colours}

The sky is [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) and fills half the card: neutrality, stability, no particular will. Nothing happens up there, which is the point — nothing distracts him. The black apron says it another way: absorbed energy, the mind emptied, reality.

Then the body: a blue tunic for calm emotions and vitality, red hose for drive, down in the legs where the work is braced, and the yellow-gold of coins appearing one by one.

## By astrology: Sun in Virgo {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn set the Sun here, at the start of [Virgo](/library/astrology/virgo/): its first decan. Virgo’s verb is “I analyse” — the engineer, the excellent executor — and the Sun is what a life is organised around. The two make someone whose centre is doing a thing properly.

Worth keeping as a small confirmation: Virgo’s major card is the Hermit, and this man is a hermit with a hammer. Without [astrology](/library/astrology/), the bench and the post of finished coins say the same.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: work in progress, and going well. Training, an apprenticeship, a craft, a project improved piece by piece; the person who stays late because they want it right. **Mastery is the same gesture, made well, many times over — the card doesn’t promise a shortcut, and doesn’t need one.**

Reversed, the work at the bench either loses its care or never stops. The gesture goes on autopilot and the apprenticeship is dropped at the sixth coin — or perfectionism takes over, and so many hours go into the bench that the town in the corner, and the people in it, fade into the horizon.

Next to the [Three of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-pentacles/), the craftsman’s work is seen by others. Next to [the Hermit](/library/tarot-cards/the-hermit/), Virgo twice: withdrawal for the work, good as long as it ends. Next to [the Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/), something left without a plan, and a skill learned by doing.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Put yourself across the table from someone who has just turned over the Eight of Pentacles, with the Seven of Cups beside it. Eight then seven: mastery being built, then reflection — and in my notes, too many choices. Earth then Water: hands on the material, then a head full of wishes. A man who sees only his coin, then a figure facing seven cups floating in a cloud.

The bench beside the floating cups comes out as: “You know how to do something well, and you’re doing it. But part of you keeps looking at other lives you could be living, and isn’t sure the bench is enough.”

Then find out whether it means anything to them. If it does, ask which cup tempts them most. If it doesn’t, admit you missed, jot it down, and put the deck away.
