## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A king sits in profile on his throne, turned towards the left of the card. A tall sprouting wand stands in his right hand, its foot on the ground. He sits upright, his other hand resting in his lap.

His crown is gold, its points shaped like flames, over a red cap. He wears an orange-red robe, a green cape with a scalloped edge over his shoulders, and a long cloak patterned with salamanders curled into rings. The high back of the throne, yellow and orange, is decorated with black lions and salamanders. At his feet, on the grey stone platform, a small live salamander walks past — the fire lizard of the old stories.

He doesn’t look at us, at his wand, or at the salamander. He looks past the edge of the card, at something we can’t see. **This is Fire that governs: it has a vision, and it’s already looking at the next horizon.**

## By number: the King {#number}

The [King](/library/numerology/king/) of this suit holds a living wand and looks past it, towards what he leads; the salamanders on his throne name his house, Fire. Decide, then answer for it, the rank says — and with Fire, answering means saying whom all that heat is for.

Follow the wand through the court. The Page discovered it, the Knight charged with it, the Queen held it warmly; the King uses it to lead. Picture a founder who still carries the first idea and now has thirty people following it. Whether they follow because the vision is good, or because no one else gets to speak, is exactly what this card asks.

## By element: Fire {#element}

The King is Fire of Fire: the suit’s element twice, unmixed. [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) creates, forges tools, lights the night, and runs wild when nobody holds it. Doubled, it gives vision and charisma — the person who walks into a room and sets a whole team moving.

It also carries the risk I note for the Emperor, the major card of Fire and of the father: like fire, he can destroy everything. A pure Fire leader with no Water or Earth around him burns through people as fast as he inspires them. The salamander at his feet is the reminder: only a creature made for fire lives comfortably in it.

## By colours {#colours}

[Orange](/library/tarot-colours/orange/) and red dominate — the robe, the throne: creativity, drive, passion, action. Gold flames sit on the crown. Then comes a band of [green](/library/tarot-colours/green/) at the shoulders: growth, life, prosperity. It’s the sign that his fire is meant to make something grow, not only to shine.

The grey stone under the throne gives him stable ground. Heat everywhere, a collar of growth, stone underfoot: a ruler whose fire has somewhere to stand.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the King of Wands is leadership by vision: someone who sees where to go, decides quickly and carries others with them. It can be a boss, a mentor, an entrepreneur — or you, the day you stop waiting for permission to lead your own project. **A good King of Wands lights the way; he doesn’t need to be the only light.**

Reversed, the fire turns domineering: impatience, temper, the boss who decides everything and hears no one. Or it goes out — the visionary who no longer believes in his own idea, still on the throne, looking at nothing. Ask the King’s question: who is this power serving now?

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), two authorities with one temperament: he gives orders and doesn’t receive them, so check there is room left for anyone else. Next to the [Three of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/three-of-wands/), the vision gets a horizon — a man on a height, watching his ships go out. Next to the [King of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/king-of-pentacles/), two Kings side by side: vision and resources, and possibly a struggle over who leads.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

A case invented for teaching: the King of Wands comes out, then the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/). The King then 2 — decision, then a choice between two that isn’t being made; Fire then Air — speed, then a mind locked in place; orange flames, then a blindfolded woman holding two crossed swords by the sea, under a crescent moon.

A sentence to try: “You decide fast everywhere in your life except on one subject, and on that one you’ve tied the blindfold yourself.”

Then check: “Is there one decision you’ve been leaving open?” If they nod, let them name it or not; you don’t need to know. If no decision is hanging open, the blindfold was my invention: that’s a miss, and it counts as one.
