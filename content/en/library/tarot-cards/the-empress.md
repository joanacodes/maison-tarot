## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman sits at ease on red cushions in the open air, leaning back, looking at you. Her crown is made of twelve stars and her hair is fair. Her white robe is covered in pomegranates, and she wears a necklace of pearls. She holds a sceptre topped with a globe.

Beside her, a heart-shaped shield carries the sign of Venus. Ripe wheat grows in front of her; behind her rise a forest of cypress trees and a waterfall pouring into a stream. The sky is yellow.

What’s missing: walls, armour, work in progress — and people. No child, even though everyone calls this the mother card. **It shows abundance that has already grown, and someone at rest inside it.**

## By number: 3 {#number}

The [number 3](/library/numerology/number-3/) is the machine running: the contract works, it shows, it gives. After the 1 who has the tools and the 2 who weighs, the Empress is the first card where something has come out: the wheat is ripe.

When Instagram launched and it worked, that was its 3: the thing turned without being pushed. The advice of 3 is to finish a project before starting the next; its minus is starting new ones before the others are done. Abundance invites exactly that — when everything grows, you want to plant everywhere.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The Empress belongs to [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): matter and prosperity, anything you value, in money or in feeling. In my notes, Earth is protective and stable, the mother of all growth.

That’s the Empress I teach: the mother, the earth, the one who feeds — the wheat at her feet — who gives and creates life in the widest sense: a project, a home, a garden, a team. Her minus comes from the same place. She can smother with her care, protect so much that nobody grows, or one day stop feeding and leave behind people who depended on her. Not all care is light.

## By colours {#colours}

Green and gold dominate: the trees, the wheat. [Green](/library/tarot-colours/green/), as I read it, means nature, growth, birth, life, prosperity — something planted that is giving. Yellow in the sky and her hair: joy, optimism. Red on the cushions: desire, a body at rest but very much alive. White on the robe: peace.

It’s one of the warmest drawings in the deck.

## By astrology: Venus {#astrology}

No need to guess the Golden Dawn’s planet for the Empress: the sign of [Venus](/library/astrology/venus/) is drawn on her shield. Venus says what you love and how: taste, attraction, what you find beautiful, what you’re willing to tend. It adds a nuance worth keeping — the Empress doesn’t only feed others; she enjoys.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Empress says something is growing and being looked after. It’s often the card of someone who takes care of others, at work as much as at home, and in love it asks whether there’s room for you in the care you give ([the Empress in love](/blog/empress-tarot-love/)).

Many people hope or fear that this card means a pregnancy, and I understand why: it’s the mother card, and the question comes with a lot of hope or worry. But a card can’t tell you that, and a wrong word there can hurt; that one is for a doctor and a test. What the Empress can hold is everything around it: what you’re growing, and how you’re looking after yourself ([pregnancy questions, and what I read instead](/blog/tarot-cards-pregnancy/)).

Reversed, the care turns heavy or runs dry. Either the Empress holds too tightly — the parent who can’t let go, the manager who redoes everyone’s work — or she has stopped feeding, often herself first.

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), 3 beside 4: what grows next to what structures. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the care turns into holding on. Next to the [Nine of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-pentacles/), where a woman strolls through her own vines, the abundance has come back to the person who grew it. **The Empress gives; the question is always whether she keeps something for herself.**

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the Empress is growth that needs care: a creative project you’ve fed for months, a small business that grows season by season rather than in a sprint, a team where you’re the one people come to. She’s often the person who looks after others — the manager who notices when someone is struggling, the colleague who trains every new hire. That’s her plus at work: real, and too rarely thanked.

Say someone runs a small ceramics studio and draws the Empress. The card doesn’t forecast sales; it describes someone feeding something alive. **The useful question isn’t whether it will grow, but whether the way you’re feeding it can last.** Next to the Eight of Pentacles — in my notes, mastery and the necessary effort — the abundance comes from the craft, one piece at a time.

Reversed at work, she smothers or runs dry: the team so well protected that nobody can decide without you, or the person who has fed everyone and has nothing left. If that exhaustion has reached your body, it’s one for a doctor before it’s one for the cards.

“Will my project succeed?” comes in many forms: will the business hold, will the book sell? It makes sense; you’ve put yourself into it. But I don’t read on money or sales ([here’s that list, and my reasons](/disclaimers/#refusals)). A forecast belongs to real numbers and someone who knows how to read them, such as an accountant. A card that promised success could push you to invest more than you can afford; one that predicted failure could make you drop something that only needed another season. A kinder question: “What does this project need from me this month, and what can it do without?”

This week, hand one task to someone else and keep one evening for yourself.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Suppose the Empress lands next to the Ten of Wands — a man hunched over the ten heavy wands he hugs to his chest, on his way to a town.

3 then 10: the first result, then the end of a cycle — my note for the Ten of Wands asks you to keep your creative fire burning without taking on too much. Earth then Fire: what grows next to what burns. The Empress’s open green and gold, next to a man whose face is hidden behind his load.

Abundance beside a load, put plainly: “You’re the one feeding everyone at the moment, and it has started to feel like a load instead of a pleasure.”

Then I’d check: who are you looking after right now — and who’s looking after you? If no one depends on them, or they’re well looked after themselves, I write the reading down as a miss.
