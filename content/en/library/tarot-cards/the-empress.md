## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A woman sits at ease on a pile of red cushions in the open air, leaning back and looking at you. Her crown is made of twelve stars and her hair is fair. Her white robe is covered in pomegranates, and she wears a necklace of pearls. She holds a sceptre topped with a globe.

Beside her, a heart-shaped shield carries the sign of Venus. Ripe wheat grows in front of her; behind her rise a forest of cypress trees and a waterfall pouring into a stream. The sky is yellow.

What’s missing: walls, armour, any sign of work in progress. There are no other people either — no child, even though this is the card everyone calls the mother. **The drawing is about abundance that has already grown, and someone at rest inside it.**

## By number: 3 {#number}

**3 — three — to create, to celebrate, the first result.** The [number 3](/library/numerology/number-3/) is the machine running: the contract works, it shows, it gives. After the 1 who has the tools and the 2 who weighs in silence, the Empress is the first card where something has come out the other end. The wheat is ripe.

In the story of Instagram on my numerology pages, 3 is the launch that works: people sign up, post, come back, and for the first time the thing turns without being pushed. The advice of 3 is to finish a project before starting the next; its minus is starting new ones before the others are done. Abundance invites exactly that — when everything grows, you want to plant everywhere.

## By element: Earth {#element}

The Empress is an [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) card: matter, prosperity, whatever has value for you, money or not. In my notes, Earth is protective, stable, the mother of all growth, with mostly feminine energy. It’s also tenacity, and its flip side, stubbornness.

That’s the Empress I teach: the mother, the earth, the one who feeds — the wheat at her feet — who gives and creates life in the widest sense: a project, a home, a garden, a team. Her minus comes from the same place. She can smother with her care, protect so much that nobody grows, or one day stop feeding and leave behind people who depended on her. Not all riches are gold, and not all care is light.

## By colours {#colours}

Green and gold dominate: the trees, the wheat. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/green/), green is nature, growth, birth, life, prosperity — something planted that is giving. Yellow in the sky and her hair: joy, optimism. Red on the cushions: desire, a body at rest but very much alive. White on the robe: peace.

Read those before the card and you already have: growth, comfort, pleasure, ease. It’s one of the warmest drawings in the deck.

## By astrology: Venus {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Empress goes with [Venus](/library/astrology/venus/), and the card doesn’t hide it: the sign is drawn on her shield. Venus says what you love and how: taste, attraction, what you find beautiful, what you’re willing to tend. It adds a nuance worth keeping — the Empress doesn’t only feed others; she enjoys. If astrology isn’t your lens, the shield and the cushions say it anyway.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Empress says that something is growing and being looked after: a project, a relationship, a home, a piece of creative work. It’s often the card of someone who takes care of others ([the Empress in career](/blog/empress-tarot-career/)), and in love it asks whether there’s room for you in the care you give ([the Empress in love](/blog/empress-tarot-love/)).

Many people hope or fear that this card means a pregnancy. I understand why: it’s the mother card, and that question usually comes with a lot of hope or worry. But a card can’t tell you that, and a wrong word there can hurt; it’s one for a doctor and a test. What the Empress can hold is everything around it — what you’re growing in your life, and how you’re looking after yourself while you do. I’ve written more on [pregnancy questions and what I read instead](/blog/tarot-cards-pregnancy/).

Reversed, the care turns heavy or runs dry. Either the Empress holds too tightly — the parent who can’t let go, the manager who redoes everyone’s work — or she has stopped feeding, often herself first.

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), she’s 3 beside 4: what grows next to what structures, a pair that works together. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the care turns into holding on. Next to the [Nine of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/nine-of-pentacles/), a woman alone in her vineyard, the abundance has come back to the person who grew it. **The Empress gives; the question is always whether she keeps something for herself.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Empress next to the Ten of Wands — a man bent forward, carrying ten heavy wands in his arms towards a town.

Lenses first. Numbers: 3 then 10, the first result, then the end of a cycle; my note for the Ten of Wands is to keep the creative energy without overloading. Elements: Earth then Fire, what grows next to what burns. Colours: the Empress’s green and gold, open and warm, next to a man whose face you can’t even see behind his load.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’re the one feeding everyone at the moment, and it has started to feel like a load instead of a pleasure.”

Then I’d check: who are you looking after right now — and who’s looking after you? If it doesn’t land, it’s a miss, and it goes in the notebook as written.
