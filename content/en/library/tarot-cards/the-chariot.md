## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young warrior stands upright in a chariot made of stone, facing you. He wears armour with crescent moons on the shoulders and a crown marked with a star, and he holds a wand. Above him, a blue canopy scattered with stars rests on four posts.

Two sphinxes lie in front of the chariot, one black, one white. Behind him stands a walled city with towers; above, a yellow sky. On the front of the chariot, a winged disc.

What’s missing: reins. He holds nothing that steers the sphinxes, and they aren’t harnessed. **The drawing is about moving forward by will alone — keeping two opposite forces going the same way without tying them down.**

## By number: 7 {#number}

A card in motion filed under the patient 7 looks like a contradiction. It isn’t: the [number 7](/library/numerology/number-7/) is analysis before action, the pause where you look at what you have and decide what comes next. The Chariot is the 7 that has done its thinking and moves because it knows where it’s going.

Instagram’s 7 is the advertising turn under Facebook: neither triumph nor fall, a negotiation over what the app will become. The advice of 7 suits a driver: everything is temporary. The position you’re in, good or bad, will move — which is a reason to steer rather than to grip.

## By element: Water {#element}

The Chariot is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card, which surprises people who see a warrior. Water is emotions and relationships, and the hidden strengths underneath them. A chariot of Water is emotion held steady: strong feelings channelled instead of spilled.

In my notes this card is a warrior with both a feminine and a masculine spirit — the black and white sphinxes say the same — and a warrior who receives orders: the action is called from above, from the starry canopy. Where the Emperor gives orders and takes none, the Chariot answers a call. Its [feminine and masculine energies](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) pull together only as long as he keeps them balanced.

## By colours {#colours}

Yellow fills the sky, and in [my grid](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) it stands for will, pride, self-expression — what you want, wanted in daylight. Blue on the canopy: emotion, Water, held overhead like a roof. Black and white on the sphinxes: absence and presence, the void and the clean start, two forces that would pull apart if left alone. Grey on the stone of the chariot: stability, a vehicle built to last.

## By astrology: Cancer {#astrology}

The Chariot’s Golden Dawn sign is [Cancer](/library/astrology/cancer/): “I feel”, ruled by the Moon, a cardinal sign that leads humanely. The crab is a good key to this card — hard shell, soft inside, like a warrior in armour with moons on his shoulders. In my notes Cancer is whiny but tough: it carries 78 kilos while others lift 4, and keeps going.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Chariot says: go, with a direction. A move, a project pushed through, a goal reached by holding opposite pulls together. It’s will, not brute force: there are no reins, so it works only as long as the person is clear inside.

Reversed, the sphinxes pull apart. The person pushes ahead without knowing where to, or stops dead because two parts of them want opposite things — the job and the family, the head and the heart. It can also be the warrior who never takes the armour off, still driving long after the race is over.

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), the one who gives orders lies next to the one who receives them: who is driving whom? Next to [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), it’s 7 then 8, the will that drives and then the gentleness that tames. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/), seven cups of illusions floating in the clouds, it’s the same number in two moods: one has picked a direction, the other can’t. **The Chariot moves because the driver is clear, not because the road is.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

For contrast, pair the Chariot with the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/): the effigy of a knight lying on a tomb in a church, hands joined, one sword beneath him and three on the wall.

7 then 4: a will in motion next to a stillness. Water then Air: feelings held steady, then a mind that needs quiet. The Chariot’s bright yellow sky next to the grey of a closed room.

From the rolling chariot to the still effigy: “You’ve been driving everything forward with no reins for a while, and the next move is to stop on purpose, before something stops you.”

Then I’d check: when did you last have a day where nothing moved? If their days are already still, I’ve misjudged the speed, and that goes down as a miss.
