## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young warrior stands upright in a chariot made of stone, facing you. He wears armour with crescent moons on the shoulders and a crown marked with a star, and he holds a wand. Above him, a blue canopy scattered with stars rests on four posts.

Two sphinxes lie in front of the chariot, one black, one white. Behind him stands a walled city with towers; above, a yellow sky. On the front of the chariot, a winged disc.

What’s missing: reins. He holds nothing that steers the sphinxes, and they aren’t harnessed. **The drawing is about moving forward by will alone — keeping two opposite forces going the same way without tying them down.**

## By number: 7 {#number}

**7 — seven — doubt, assessment, patience.** A card in motion filed under the patient 7 looks like a contradiction. It isn’t: the [number 7](/library/numerology/number-7/) is analysis before action, the pause where you look at what you have and decide what comes next. The Chariot is the 7 that has done its thinking and moves because it knows where it’s going.

In the story of Instagram on my numerology pages, 7 is Facebook deciding to turn the app into an advertising platform: neither triumph nor fall, but a negotiating table where you choose what the thing will become. The advice of 7 suits a driver: everything is temporary. The position you’re in, good or bad, will move — which is a reason to steer rather than to grip.

## By element: Water {#element}

The Chariot is a [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/) card, which surprises people who see a warrior. Water is emotions and relationships, and the hidden strengths underneath them. A chariot of Water is emotion held steady: strong feelings channelled instead of spilled.

In my notes this card is a warrior with both a feminine and a masculine spirit — the black and white sphinxes say the same — and a warrior who receives orders: the action is called from above, from the starry canopy. Where the Emperor gives orders and takes none, the Chariot answers a call. Its [feminine and masculine energies](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) pull together only as long as he keeps them balanced.

## By colours {#colours}

Yellow fills the sky. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/), yellow is will, pride, self-expression — what you want, wanted in daylight. Blue on the canopy: emotion, Water, held overhead like a roof. Black and white on the sphinxes: absence and presence, the void and the clean start, two forces that would pull apart if left alone. Grey on the stone of the chariot: stability, a vehicle built to last.

Read before the card: will, emotion under control, two opposites, and something solid carrying them.

## By astrology: Cancer {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Chariot goes with [Cancer](/library/astrology/cancer/): “I feel”, ruled by the Moon, a cardinal sign that leads humanely. The crab is a good key to this card — hard shell, soft inside, like a warrior in armour with moons on his shoulders. In my notes Cancer is whiny but tough: it carries 78 kilos while others lift 4, and keeps going. On the Cancer page you’ll find the High Priestess, through the Moon; the Golden Dawn gives the sign itself to the Chariot. Optional, as always.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Chariot says: go, with a direction. A move, a project pushed through, a goal reached by holding opposite pulls together. It’s will, not brute force: there are no reins, so it works only as long as the person is clear inside.

Reversed, the sphinxes pull apart. The person pushes ahead without knowing where to, or stops dead because two parts of them want opposite things — the job and the family, the head and the heart. It can also be the warrior who never takes the armour off, still driving long after the race is over.

Next to [the Emperor](/library/tarot-cards/the-emperor/), the one who gives orders sits beside the one who receives them: who is driving whom? Next to [Strength](/library/tarot-cards/strength/), it’s 7 then 8, the will that drives and then the gentleness that tames. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/), seven cups of illusions floating in the clouds, it’s the same number in two moods: one has chosen a direction, the other can’t. **The Chariot moves because the driver is clear, not because the road is.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Chariot next to the [Four of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-swords/): the effigy of a knight lying on a tomb in a church, hands joined, one sword beneath him and three on the wall.

Lenses first. Numbers: 7 then 4, a will in motion next to a stillness. Elements: Water then Air, feelings held steady, then a mind that needs quiet. Colours: the Chariot’s bright yellow sky next to the grey stillness of a closed room.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’ve been driving everything forward with no reins for a while, and the next move is to stop on purpose, before something stops you.”

Then I’d check: when did you last have a day where nothing moved? If that doesn’t speak to the person, it’s a miss; I write it down and move on.
