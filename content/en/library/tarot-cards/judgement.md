## What’s on the card {#the-card}

An angel leans out of the clouds at the top of the card, blowing a long golden trumpet. A white banner with a red cross hangs from it. Below, open coffins float on water and people rise out of them: in front, a man, a woman and a child; further back, others doing the same. Their bodies are grey. They stand with their arms open or raised, faces turned up towards the sound. Behind them, tall white mountains.

What’s missing is a judge. No scales, no sword, no one being sent away — compare it with Justice. **Everyone on the card is answering the same call.** The drawing as a whole is about hearing something you can’t ignore any more, and getting up.

## By number: 20 {#number}

Twenty comes back to [2](/library/numerology/number-2/), the number of the High Priestess: two parties, a contract, a bond. Its advice is to read the contract and weigh the pros and cons; its minus is stubbornness, solitude, the mind going round on its own.

On Judgement, the two parties are the call and the answer: the trumpet sounds, and someone has to decide whether to stand up. In the Instagram story, 2 is the moment the founders sign with investors — the idea now involves someone else, and you can lose more than your own time. Judgement carries that weight: answering commits you.

## By element: Fire {#element}

Judgement is a [Fire](/library/four-elements/fire-element/) card. Fire is impulse, creation, the energy that motivates; it forges, warms, lights the night. Here it’s the spark that gets people out of the box they were lying in.

Fire is the only attribution the Golden Dawn gave this card, and that says something about it: Judgement isn’t about thinking it over, it’s about the moment you move.

## By colours {#colours}

[Grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) for the bodies and the coffins: neutrality, no will of its own yet — people who have been waiting. White for the mountains and the banner: purity, peace, a clean slate. The red of the cross: action, strength. The gold of the trumpet: will, the voice that carries. Read the colours before the card and you already have: a long wait, a fresh start, and one clear signal to act.

## By astrology: Pluto {#astrology}

The Golden Dawn gave Judgement to the element Fire, through the Hebrew letter Shin, and not to a planet. Modern readers later linked it to [Pluto](/library/astrology/pluto/), discovered in 1930: the planet of power and transformation, where you die and are reborn while alive, what you bury and what comes back up. Through Scorpio, Pluto also ties Judgement to Death: two cards that frighten in the picture and say the same thing, that something ends so something else can begin. And Pluto is a generational planet: it stays in one sign for years on end, so it speaks to a generation more than to one person.

## In a reading {#reading}

Judgement is neither good nor bad. Every card has a plus and a minus, and this one shows both plainly. Upright, it’s a call: a second look, a review, a second chance, the moment you can’t pretend you didn’t hear. The plus is the getting up. The minus is the verdict we bring in ourselves — hearing the call as a sentence rather than an invitation.

Reversed, it’s the same call left unanswered: you hear it and stay lying down, or you keep reviewing the past without deciding anything. In excess, it’s judging yourself, or others, without end.

Because the drawing shows the dead rising, some people fear it’s about someone they’ve lost. I understand why. A card doesn’t carry news of the dead, and I don’t read on death. What rises here is usually something of yours that was put aside — a project, a part of you, a relationship worth a second look.

Next to [Death](/library/tarot-cards/death/), Judgement is what comes back after the harvest. Next to [the World](/library/tarot-cards/the-world/), the card that follows it, it’s the last call before a cycle closes. Next to the [Six of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/six-of-cups/) — childhood, flowering again — it’s something from the past returning in a form you can use now.

**Judgement isn’t a verdict on you; it’s a call, and you choose whether to answer.** I’ve written more about [Judgement in love](/blog/judgement-tarot-love/); a calling at work has its own section below.

## At work {#at-work}

At work, Judgement comes in three forms. A calling: something you wanted to do long before you had a job keeps coming back — the training you didn’t take, the trade you admired. A review: an annual appraisal, a skills assessment, the birthday when you look at your CV and wonder how you got here; the card describes the looking, not the grade. A second chance: going back to studies at forty, applying again where you were turned down, picking up a project you shelved. **The people rising on the card aren’t new people; they’re the same people, standing up.** Next to the Eight of Pentacles — in my notes, mastery and the necessary effort — the calling becomes an apprenticeship.

Reversed at work, the call goes unanswered (“when the children are older”, every January) or gets answered so often that you review your career endlessly and never decide. The first needs a small step; the second, a full stop.

“Is this my true calling? Is it too late?” Those questions carry hope, regret and the fear of losing more years, and I understand wanting a card to settle them. A card can’t tell you what you’re meant to do, and I’d be wary of any reading that tried. A calling isn’t a verdict handed down; it’s something you test, because nobody can tell you from outside what it feels like. Whether to resign or pay for a course is a money decision ([what I don’t read, and why](/disclaimers/#refusals)), and a careers adviser will help you more than a card. A kinder question: “What keeps calling me back, and what would a small test of it look like?”

This week, book one small test: a weekend course, a coffee with someone who does the job, an evening trying it on the side.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

For this exercise, Judgement comes out with the [Ace of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-wands/) beside it.

Numbers: 20 comes back to 2, a bond, a commitment; the Ace is 1, the raw start of a cycle. Elements: Fire and Fire, so this moves fast once it moves. The pictures: a trumpet calling, and on the Ace, a hand coming out of a cloud holding a branch that’s still putting out leaves.

The sentence you’d say out loud: “Something you put down a while ago is calling you back, and this time it comes with the energy to start it properly.”

Then check it: is there a project, a person or a plan they set aside that keeps coming back to mind? Let them answer before you say more. If it doesn’t fit, it’s a miss; if it does, what they do with it is their decision, not yours and not the cards’.
