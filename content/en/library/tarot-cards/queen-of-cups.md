## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A queen sits on a throne at the very edge of the sea, the water lapping at the pebbles by her feet. She holds a large, ornate cup and gazes into it intently. It’s the only closed cup in the suit: it has a lid, two angel-like figures for handles, and a small cross on top. Her throne is carved with little sea-children and crowned with a shell.

Every other cup in the Cups is open. This one is closed: what she sees, she sees alone, and she doesn’t pour it out. Waite describes her as one “who sees visions in a cup”, and adds that she acts on what she sees. **Deep feeling held with care — looking inside without spilling.**

## By number: the Queen {#number}

[The Queen](/library/numerology/queen/) masters her suit from within, and the Queen of Cups does it by looking: she knows what’s in her cup without having to show it. For a Queen of Water, holding without holding on means feeling with people without keeping their feelings. At her best, what others pour into her comes back as understanding; at her less good, she keeps it all, and mistakes holding for helping.

Her energy is feminine [in the way I mean it](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/): patience, imagination, what happens behind the scenes. Gender doesn’t decide it; the best listener you know may well be a man.

## By element: Water {#element}

Cups are [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/), and the Queen’s rank adds Water again: the element twice. Emotion at its deepest, and the archetype at its warmest — someone who understands before you’ve finished your sentence.

Twice the water is also twice the risk: the current that carries you can drag you down. My definition of empathy is feeling and understanding someone else’s emotions while keeping your own. This Queen is the picture of that skill — and, when it tips over, of the person who soaks up everyone’s moods and carries them home. I write about [feeling without taking on, when you read for someone](/blog/protect-yourself-tarot/).

## By colours {#colours}

[Blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) and [white](/library/tarot-colours/white/) run through the card — the sea, the folds of her robe: emotion and purity, a feeling with no hidden motive. The grey stone of the throne gives it a base, the one thing in the picture that doesn’t move while the water does. The gold of the cup is the value of what she holds. Deep feeling, clean intention, a steady place to sit.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: emotional intelligence. Someone who listens deeply, reads a room, takes care — a friend, a parent, a nurse, a reader. Or it’s you, at the moment you finally listen to yourself. **The Queen of Cups feels everything and still knows which feelings are hers.**

Reversed, the depth either closes up or spills over. Closed up, the lid stays on — feelings so guarded no one can reach them, intuition heard and then ignored. Spilling over, the water floods — absorbing other people’s emotions, carrying their moods home, losing track of your own until you can’t say what you want.

Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), intuition is doubled: what isn’t said gets heard. Next to the [Queen of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/queen-of-swords/), the two Queens share the work: one takes in what’s felt, the other names it. Next to [the Moon](/library/tarot-cards/the-moon/), the feeling risks turning into illusion, and it’s worth checking what’s yours and what’s fear.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Try the method on a pairing that exists only on paper: the Queen of Cups next to the Nine of Wands. A Queen then a nine: inner mastery, then the last stretch. Water twice, then Fire: deep feeling, then a fighter worn out by the effort. A woman seated by the sea, then a man with a bandaged head still guarding his wands.

The queen by the sea next to the bandaged guard says something like: “You’re the one people come to with their feelings, and you’ve been holding the line for a long time. You’re tired, and some of what you carry isn’t yours.” Then ask whether they recognise the tiredness. If they do, let them say how, in their own words. If they don’t, the reading has missed, and the notebook says so.
