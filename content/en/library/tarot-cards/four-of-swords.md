## What’s on the card {#the-card}

Inside a church or a chapel, a knight lies on his back on a stone tomb, eyes closed, hands joined in prayer on his chest. He’s in armour, and the drawing leaves it open whether he’s a carved effigy or a man so still he could be one. Figure and tomb are both a soft, pale yellow.

Three swords hang on the wall above him, points down, and his joined hands point up towards them. A fourth sword lies along the side of the tomb, flat, as if set down. In the top corner, a stained-glass window — the only bright patch on the card, red, blue, yellow and green — shows a standing figure bending towards a smaller one who kneels. The rest is pale wall. No movement, no landscape, no open door.

The drawing is about **a stop chosen and protected: the swords are put away, for now.**

## By number: four {#number}

**four — foundations, stability.** Four is “two exalted”: the contract has been signed and now it holds — structure, the fruit of your work. In my notes, its advice is to stay flexible, and its minus is carrying on when you should stop. The Four of Swords is the four that has stopped. After the cut of the Three, the structure becomes a frame for rest: four walls, a tomb, a pause that holds you. At this stage Instagram had a shape, a team and habits. [Number 4](/library/numerology/number-4/) can protect or confine; this card is the protective version, as long as the stop stays a stop and doesn’t become a hiding place.

## By element: Air {#element}

[Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) is the mind, analysis, decision — the element that never stops thinking. That’s what makes this card precious: it’s the mind at rest. The swords aren’t gone; they hang on the wall, within reach. Nothing has been decided, nothing abandoned; the thinking is set aside so it can be done better later. If Air’s risk is to obsess, the Four of Swords is its answer: silence, sleep, a break from your own head.

## By colours {#colours}

Mostly pale: off-white and [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/) walls, the grey of stability and no particular will — nothing here wants anything. The knight and his tomb are a muted [yellow](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/), which in my grid is joy and will; here it’s softened, as if will itself were asleep. Then the window: the only place where red, blue and green meet, full colour, high up and far away. Life is out there, behind the glass, and it will still be there when he gets up.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Libra {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Four of Swords is [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/) in the third decan of Libra, with the title Lord of Rest from Strife. Jupiter is expansion and generosity; Libra is balance. Together: balance granted generously — time, space, a truce long enough to recover. Jupiter’s own warning is excess: a rest that keeps expanding until it becomes avoidance. An optional lens; the number and the element already carry the card.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Four of Swords is rest: a break, a retreat, sleep, a time-out after a hard stretch. Sometimes it’s literally a holiday; more often it’s permission to stop thinking about something for a while.

Some people fear the card because of the tomb, and I understand why. It doesn’t announce a death or an illness: the hands are joined, the swords are hung up, and the subject is rest. If a health worry is behind the question, that one belongs with a doctor.

Reversed, the pause goes wrong. Slowed down: you can’t rest, the swords keep coming off the wall, sleep won’t come. In excess: the retreat that has lasted too long, the person who stopped and can’t remember how to start again.

Next to [the Hanged Man](/library/tarot-cards/the-hanged-man/), the pause is long and deliberate, and a new angle will come out of the stillness. Next to the [Four of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-cups/), rest has tipped into boredom: something is being offered and not seen. Next to the [Ten of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-wands/), the reason for the rest is obvious — too much carried for too long.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Four of Swords next to the [Eight of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-wands/). Lenses first. Four then eight: a structure at rest, then a structure in full motion. Air then Fire: the thought set aside, then speed and impulse. Pale yellow and grey, then eight wands flying through an open sky.

You might say: “You’re in a pause that won’t last much longer. Things are about to move fast, so this is the rest to take now.”

Then check: “Are you on a break at the moment, or about to go back to something busy?” If the answer is no on both counts, it’s a miss; don’t bend the reading to save it. To practise this kind of pairing, one card a day and then two, the [learning path](/learn-tarot/) takes it step by step.
