## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young page stands on the shore, the sea rolling behind him in small waves. He holds a gold cup in one hand, the other on his hip, and looks into it: a fish has put its head out of the cup and looks back at him. He wears a blue tunic patterned with flowers and a soft hat whose long scarf falls over his shoulder.

No one else, no boat, no letter in his hand. The sea is at his back; what holds his attention is the little water in the cup, and what came out of it. Waite calls this “the pictures of the mind taking form”. **A feeling that surprises you, and the curiosity to look at it rather than look away.**

## By number: the Page {#number}

[The Page](/library/numerology/page/) is the student and the messenger of the suit, and the Page of Cups gets the strangest message of the four: a fish, where you expected a drink.

The Page’s advice is to stay a student, and this one does it well. He isn’t frightened by the fish, and he isn’t pretending he understands it. He’s looking. His energy is [feminine by my definition](/library/feminine-masculine-energies/) — imagination, softness, what happens inside — and a girl or a boy can carry it just the same.

## By element: Water {#element}

To the suit’s [Water](/library/four-elements/water-element/), the rank adds a second element, and for every Page it’s [Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/): the concrete, the first form a thing takes. Earth of Water is a feeling becoming tangible — a crush you can finally name, a sensitivity that turns into a first drawing, a sudden tenderness for someone you’d never noticed.

Water on the move stands for strong feeling in my notes. Here the big water stays behind him, in the sea, and the feeling he faces is small enough to hold in one hand. That’s the Page’s scale: not the ocean, the first fish.

## By colours {#colours}

[Blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) dominates — the tunic, the hat, the sea: emotion, the feminine, what flows. The card is dressed in its own element. The flowers scattered over the tunic add lightness: this is someone who dresses like a daydream. The gold of the cup says the feeling has value, however small. [The full colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/) confirms it: emotion everywhere, something precious in the hand.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: a surprise of the heart. News about feelings, an invitation, a crush, a creative idea that arrives sideways. Sometimes a young person; more often, the start of learning something about your own emotions. **The fish is allowed to be strange; the Page’s job is to look at it before deciding what it means.**

Reversed, the surprise either stays bottled up or takes over. The feeling stays in the cup — the message never sent, the tenderness never said, the poem kept in a drawer. Or every fish becomes a sign, and you live in the cup rather than on the shore, reading meaning into each small coincidence.

Next to the [Ace of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ace-of-cups/), a feeling begins and gets its first messenger. Next to the [Two of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-cups/), the message is meant for someone: an exchange, a first date. Next to the [Seven of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-cups/), imagination runs ahead — too many cups, too many visions — and it’s worth checking what’s real. Telling a person from a state is the hard part of every court card; [court cards explained](/blog/court-cards-explained/) goes through it.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Let’s practise on the Page of Cups, fish and all, with the Three of Pentacles at its side. A Page then a three: learning, then something that works with others. Earth of Water, then Earth: a feeling taking shape, then concrete work. A boy alone on the shore, then a craftsman showing his work to two people.

The fish in the cup and the church workshop come together as: “Something you feel is turning into something you make — maybe a creative side you didn’t take seriously, and now other people are looking at it.” Then ask whether that means anything to the person. If it doesn’t, write the miss down as it is, without a rescue card.
