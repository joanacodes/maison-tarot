## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands behind a table and faces you. His right hand lifts a white wand towards the sky; his left points at the ground. Above his head floats an infinity sign, a figure eight lying on its side. He wears a white robe under a red cloak, a white band in his dark hair and, as a belt, a snake biting its own tail.

On the table lie the four suits of the deck: wand, cup, sword, pentacle. Red roses hang above him and grow at his feet among white lilies, against a flat yellow background with no horizon.

What’s missing: an audience, a road, anything finished. **The drawing is about the second before the first move, when everything you need is within reach.**

## By number: 1 {#number}

**1 — one — the beginning, the rough diamond.** The [number 1](/library/numerology/number-1/) opens the cycle: precious, and not yet usable, like a stone nobody has cut. On the Magician that’s literal — four tools, no result. He can do everything; he hasn’t done anything yet. In the Instagram story on my numerology pages, it’s Burbn becoming Instagram: two founders keep the one feature people used and start again from it. The advice of 1 is humility: a Magician who forgets he’s on page one starts selling the ending.

The infinity sign over his head is an 8 lying down, and 8, in my notes, is mastery. Above his 1, the card draws what the beginning could become.

## By element: Air {#element}

The Magician is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card: the mind, the decision, the word. Air reaches everything at once, which is why I call it the best decision-maker of the four — Fire is too impulsive, Water too emotional, Earth too stubborn.

It’s also why he holds all four tools, one per element, plus the ether that links them. In my notes he knows how to do everything and stays anchored. He relays: the raised wand receives, the hand pointing down passes it on. His minus belongs to Air too, its “viper’s tongue”: someone who speaks well enough to pretend.

## By colours {#colours}

Yellow fills the background: joy, self-expression, will. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) it’s the colour of the Magician and the Fool, of wanting something in broad daylight. Red on the cloak and the roses: action, impulse, masculine drive. White on the robe and the lilies: an intention that’s still clean. Before you read the card, you already have will, action and a clean start.

## By astrology: Mercury {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Magician goes with [Mercury](/library/astrology/mercury/), the messenger: how you think, speak and learn. It suits a figure who relays between sky and ground. Mercury rules Gemini, who talks, and Virgo, who checks; the Magician needs both. Keep this lens if astrology speaks to you. If not, the number, the element and the colours have said it already.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Magician says you have what you need, and the next move is yours. Not “wait for a sign”: the tools are on the table. At work it’s a skill ready to be used ([the Magician in career](/blog/magician-tarot-career/)); in love it’s the first move, and whether it’s meant ([the Magician in love](/blog/magician-tarot-love/)). It’s often called the manifestation card; I read it more plainly — things appear because someone picked up the tools ([what I think about manifestation](/blog/delulu-manifestation-tarot/)).

Reversed, the tools stay on the table. Either nobody uses them — the project polished in someone’s head for two years — or they’re used for show, and the pitch is better than the work. That’s the minus I note for this card: trusted blindly, the Magician can lie, or pretend.

Next to [the Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/), it’s 0 then 1: an urge becoming a tool. Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), the one who speaks stands beside the one who keeps quiet; listen to both. Next to the [Seven of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-swords/), talent comes with a little sleight of hand, so check twice. **The Magician isn’t a promise; it’s a set of tools, and the reading is what someone does with them.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Magician next to the Four of Cups: a young man under a tree, arms crossed, three cups on the grass, ignoring a fourth that a hand offers him from a cloud.

Lenses first. 1 then 4: a beginning, then a comfort that has set. Air then Water: a decision, then a mood. The Magician’s loud yellow, then a quieter, greener card. The means are there; the energy has gone flat.

What I’d say out loud: “You have what you need to start something, and you keep waiting for a better offer before you use it.”

Then I’d check: is there something you’ve been ready to begin for a while? If not, I don’t pull another card to rescue the sentence. A miss is a miss.
