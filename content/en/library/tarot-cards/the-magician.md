## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands behind a table and faces you. His right hand lifts a white wand towards the sky; his left hand points at the ground. Above his head floats an infinity sign, a figure eight lying on its side. He wears a white robe under a red cloak, a white band around his dark hair and, as a belt, a snake biting its own tail.

On the table lie the four suits of the deck: a wand, a cup, a sword and a pentacle. A garland of red roses hangs above him; in front of the table, more roses grow among white lilies. The background is a flat yellow, with no horizon.

Notice what’s missing: no audience, no road, nothing finished. The four tools are laid out and none of them is in use yet. **The drawing is about the second before the first move, when everything you need is already within reach.**

## By number: 1 {#number}

**1 — one — the beginning, the rough diamond.** The [number 1](/library/numerology/number-1/) opens the cycle: precious, and not yet usable, like a stone nobody has cut. On the Magician that’s literal. Four tools on the table, no result. He can do everything; he hasn’t done anything yet.

In the story of Instagram that runs through my numerology pages, this is the moment two founders take a location app called Burbn, keep the one feature people used — sharing a photo — and start again from there. The advice of 1 holds here too: humility. A Magician who forgets he’s on page one starts selling the ending.

One more number hides in the drawing. The infinity sign over his head is an 8 lying down, and 8, in my notes, is mastery and control. The card carries, above its 1, a picture of what the beginning could become. A picture, not a result.

## By element: Air {#element}

The Magician is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card: the mind, the decision, the word. Air reaches everything at once, which is why I call it the best decision-maker of the four — Fire is too impulsive, Water too emotional, Earth too stubborn.

That’s also why this card holds all four tools: the wand for Fire, the cup for Water, the sword for Air, the pentacle for Earth, plus a fifth thing that links them, the ether. In my notes the Magician is the one who knows how to do everything and stays anchored. He relays: the raised wand receives, the hand pointing down passes it on to the ground. His minus belongs to Air too, to its “viper’s tongue”: someone who speaks well enough to pretend.

## By colours {#colours}

Yellow fills the whole background. In [my colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) it’s joy, self-expression and will — wanting something in broad daylight — and it’s the colour I give to the Magician and the Fool. Red on the cloak and the roses: action, impulse, masculine drive. White on the robe, the headband and the lilies: an intention that’s still clean.

Read those three before you read the card and you already have it: will, action, a clean start.

## By astrology: Mercury {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Magician goes with [Mercury](/library/astrology/mercury/), the messenger: how you think, how you speak, how you learn. It suits the drawing, a figure who relays between sky and ground and between the four suits. Mercury rules Gemini, who talks, and Virgo, who checks — and the Magician needs both. Keep this lens if astrology speaks to you; if it doesn’t, the number, the element and the colours have already said the same thing.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Magician says you have what you need, and the next move is yours. Not “wait for a sign”: the tools are on the table. At work it’s a skill ready to be used ([the Magician in career](/blog/magician-tarot-career/)); in love it’s the first move, and whether it’s meant ([the Magician in love](/blog/magician-tarot-love/)). People often call it the manifestation card. I read it more plainly: things appear because someone picked up the tools, which is also [what I think about manifestation](/blog/delulu-manifestation-tarot/) in general.

Reversed, the tools stay on the table. Either nobody uses them — the project polished in someone’s head for two years — or they’re used for show, and the pitch is better than the work. That’s the minus I note for this card: trusted blindly, the Magician can lie, or pretend.

Next to [the Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/), it’s 0 then 1: an urge turning into a tool. Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), the one who speaks stands beside the one who keeps quiet; listen to both. Next to the [Seven of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-swords/), talent comes with a little sleight of hand, so check twice. **The Magician isn’t a promise; it’s a set of tools, and the reading is what someone does with them.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Magician next to the Four of Cups — a young man under a tree, arms crossed, three cups on the grass in front of him, and a hand coming out of a cloud to offer a fourth that he doesn’t look at.

Lenses first. Numbers: 1 then 4, a beginning followed by a comfort that has set. Elements: Air then Water, a decision followed by a mood. Colours: the Magician’s loud yellow, then a quieter, greener card. Before any meaning, that’s already a story: the means are there, and the energy has gone flat.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You have what you need to start something, and you keep waiting for a better offer before you use it.”

Then I’d check, plainly: does that land? Is there something you’ve been ready to begin for a while? If the person says no, I don’t pull another card to rescue the sentence. A miss is a miss, and it goes in the notebook as one.
