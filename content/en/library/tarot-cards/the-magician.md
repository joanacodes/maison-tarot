## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man stands behind a table and faces you. His right hand lifts a white wand towards the sky; his left points at the ground. Above his head floats an infinity sign, an 8 tipped over. He wears a white robe under a red cloak, a white band in his dark hair and, as a belt, a snake biting its own tail.

On the table lie the four suits of the deck: wand, cup, sword, pentacle. Red roses hang above him and grow at his feet among white lilies, against a flat yellow background with no horizon.

What’s missing: an audience, a road, anything finished. **The drawing is about the second before the first move, when everything you need is within reach.**

## By number: 1 {#number}

The [number 1](/library/numerology/number-1/) opens the cycle: precious, and not yet usable, like a stone nobody has cut. On the Magician that’s literal — four tools, no result. He can do everything; he hasn’t done anything yet. In the Instagram story on my numerology pages, it’s Burbn becoming Instagram: two founders keep the one feature people used and start again from it. The advice of 1 is humility: a Magician who forgets he’s on page one starts selling the ending.

The infinity sign over his head is an 8 lying down, and 8, in my notes, is mastery. Above his 1, the card draws what the beginning could become.

## By element: Air {#element}

The Magician is an [Air](/library/four-elements/air-element/) card: the mind, the decision, the word. Air reaches everything at once, so of the four it’s the one I’d let decide: Fire rushes, Water is swayed by feeling, Earth digs its heels in.

It’s also why he holds all four tools, one per element, plus the ether that links them. In my notes he knows how to do everything and stays anchored. He relays: the raised wand receives, the hand pointing down passes it on. His minus belongs to Air too, its “viper’s tongue”: someone who speaks well enough to pretend.

## By colours {#colours}

Yellow fills the background: joy, self-expression, will. My [colour grid](/library/tarot-colours/yellow/) gives yellow to the Magician and the Fool: wanting something in broad daylight. Red on the cloak and the roses: action, impulse, masculine drive. White on the robe and the lilies: an intention that’s still clean. That’s will, action and a clean start, all before the first move.

## By astrology: Mercury {#astrology}

[Mercury](/library/astrology/mercury/), the messenger, rules the Magician in the Golden Dawn: how you think, speak and learn. It suits a figure who relays between sky and ground. Mercury rules Gemini, who talks, and Virgo, who checks; the Magician needs both.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Magician says you have what you need, and the next move is yours. Not “wait for a sign”: the tools are on the table. At work it’s a skill ready to be used (more on that below); in love it’s the first move, and whether it’s meant ([the Magician in love](/blog/magician-tarot-love/)). It’s often called the manifestation card; I read it more plainly — things appear because someone picked up the tools ([what I think about manifestation](/blog/delulu-manifestation-tarot/)).

Reversed, the tools stay on the table. Either nobody uses them — the project polished in someone’s head for two years — or they’re used for show, and the pitch is better than the work. That’s the minus I note for this card: trusted blindly, the Magician can lie, or pretend.

Next to [the Fool](/library/tarot-cards/the-fool/), it’s 0 then 1: an urge becoming a tool. Next to [the High Priestess](/library/tarot-cards/the-high-priestess/), the one who speaks stands beside the one who keeps quiet; listen to both. Next to the [Seven of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/seven-of-swords/), talent comes with a little sleight of hand, so check twice. **The Magician isn’t a promise; it’s a set of tools, and the reading is what someone does with them.**

## At work {#at-work}

At work, the Magician is the moment your skills meet a starting point: a new job where you’ll use everything you know, a project you’ve been asked to lead, a pitch, a first client, or the freelance plan you keep sketching in a notebook. He’s someone who can catch an idea and land it on the ground. In a career change, he’s also the beginner again: we are all students our whole life, and the first months in a new trade belong in the toolkit.

The four tools make a good check-up. The wand: do you still want this? The cup: do you care about the people it’s for? The sword: have you thought it through? The pentacle: does it hold up in practice — time, materials, the plain logistics? Most people are strong on two and quietly avoid the other two.

Reversed at work, it’s the pitch better than the product — or the opposite worry, real skill you don’t dare show because you’re sure someone will find you out. A raw diamond is still a diamond.

The question this card brings is often “Will my business work if I go for it?” I understand: starting costs money, security, sometimes pride, and you’d like a card to carry part of the risk. Resigning, investing or borrowing are money decisions, and [those stay out of my readings](/disclaimers/#refusals); whether a business works depends on clients and markets no card can see. For the figures, an accountant or a business adviser will help more than any spread. Look at the table instead and ask: “Which of my four tools am I not using yet?”

This week, draw four columns on a sheet of paper: wand, cup, sword, pentacle. Under each, write what you already have for the thing you want to start. **The emptiest column is your first task.**

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Try this pairing as an exercise: the Magician, then the Four of Cups, where a young man with folded arms sits beneath a tree, three cups lined up on the grass, not taking the fourth that a hand holds out from a cloud.

1 then 4: a beginning, then a comfort that has set. Air then Water: a decision, then a mood. The Magician’s loud yellow, then a quieter, greener card. The means are there; the energy has gone flat.

What I’d say out loud: “You have what you need to start something, and you keep waiting for a better offer before you use it.”

Then I’d want to know: is there something you’ve been ready to begin for a while? If nothing is waiting on the table for them, I don’t pull another card to rescue the sentence.
