## What’s on the card {#the-card}

On the left of the card, a white hand appears from a grey cloud, turned palm up, and holds out a large gold pentacle: a coin with a five-pointed star drawn inside its circle. The hand doesn’t grip it. The coin rests on the open palm, and pale rays shine around the hand against a flat grey sky.

Below, a garden. Green grass, white lilies along the bottom, a dark hedge dotted with red flowers. An arch has been cut into the hedge, and a yellow path runs through it. On the far side, a long way off, you can see the blue of mountains.

There’s nobody in the garden: no one to take the coin, no one on the path. **Everything here is offered — something of value held out, and a way through the hedge, for whoever decides to take them.**

## By number: one {#number}

[Number one](/library/numerology/number-1/) is the initiator, the start of a cycle: something precious that can’t be used yet. On a Pentacles card the rough diamond is almost literal. A coin, a resource, a concrete opening whose real value nobody knows yet — a job offer, a first client, a plot to dig.

In my notes, what 1 needs most is humility, being ready to learn. Its minus is the lack of it — treating the coin as a fortune already made, when nobody ever stops learning. Instagram started out the same way: Burbn’s two founders kept the single feature that worked and began again with something small enough to fit in one hand.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) covers matter and prosperity: whatever you value, in money or in feeling. Not all riches are gold. The coin on this card can be a salary, a home, a body you’ve started looking after, a relationship that finally has ground under it. That’s what Earth brings here: the beginning isn’t an idea any more, it’s something you can hold.

Earth also brings staying power, and the stubbornness that goes with it. A Pentacles beginning is slow by nature; it asks to be planted and tended, not admired. The garden under the hand is the reminder: what the coin becomes depends on what you grow with it.

## By colours {#colours}

The pentacle is [gold](/library/tarot-colours/gold-and-brown/): value earned, matter, what you can keep. The hand is white — something clean, not handled yet. The grey sky is neutral: the card doesn’t push you either way. At the bottom, green grass and a green hedge: nature, growth, prosperity. The red flowers add a touch of desire; the blue mountains, far off, are where the path leads.

Something valuable, offered cleanly, above ground where things can grow.

## By astrology: Root of Earth {#astrology}

No planet or sign sits on this card. The Golden Dawn made each Ace its suit’s root rather than a decan: here, Earth whole, before it’s shared out among Taurus, Virgo and Capricorn, whose decans are spread from the Two up to the Ten.

What it adds is one idea: the Ace holds the whole suit in seed, and every Pentacle after it grows from this coin. That picture is a bonus for anyone who reads with [astrology](/library/astrology/); the coin on the open palm makes the same point without it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Ace of Pentacles is a concrete start: an offer, an opening, a resource arriving, a project that finally has a base. **It shows something real being held out — not whether the person will take it, nor what it will be worth.** The hand on the card is open; the person’s hand still has to close.

Reversed, the coin is left lying in the open hand: hesitation, a gate seen and not walked through, an opportunity ignored until it’s gone. Or it’s grabbed too fast — a 1 without humility, the fortune spent before anything has grown.

People often bring money questions to this card, and I understand why: it looks like money arriving. I don’t read on money decisions. Whether to accept, sign or invest stays with you, and with an adviser who can see your figures. The card can show how you’re meeting the offer — often the more useful half ([a coin card facing a money question](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)). In love, it’s something solid to build on, which I unpack in [the Ace of Pentacles in love](/blog/ace-of-pentacles-tarot-love/).

Next to [the Magician](/library/tarot-cards/the-magician/), another 1, the skill meets the material: the tools and the base arrive together. Next to the [Page of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/page-of-pentacles/), the coin is being studied — the opening is a training, an apprenticeship. Next to the [Ten of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-pentacles/), the first coin meets the full estate: what starts now is meant to last.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Suppose a friend you’re practising with draws the Ace of Pentacles, then the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/). One then two: a beginning, then a choice between two sides. Earth then Air: something concrete, then a decision held in the head. The drawings: a gold coin on an open hand, then a blindfolded figure with two swords crossed over her chest.

With the coin on one side and the blindfold on the other, I would say: “Something concrete is being offered to you, and you’re keeping your eyes closed so you don’t have to choose yet.”

Then I hand the question back: “Is there something on the table at the moment that you haven’t answered?” A yes doesn’t mean I tell the person whether to take it. If nothing is waiting for an answer, the sentence missed, and I write that down.
