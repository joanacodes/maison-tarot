## What’s on the card {#the-card}

An old man with white hair and beard sits in the foreground, on the left, in a cloak covered in patterns: checks, flowers, little bunches of grapes. Two white dogs come up to him; one lifts its head towards his hand.

Behind him, under a stone archway, a man with his back to us holds a long staff and faces a woman in red, who turns her head towards him. A small child in blue stands close behind her, a hand on one of the dogs. Through the arch: a red house, a tall slim tower, trees, a blue sky. On the stone, on the left, a coat of arms with a castle.

Over the whole scene, ten pentacles are laid out in a pattern that ignores the people: the Tree of Life of the Kabbalah, the diagram the Golden Dawn used to order the whole deck. **The card is a household complete for a moment — three generations, the animals, the house — with the old man at the edge who made it possible.**

## By number: ten {#number}

[Ten is transition](/library/numerology/number-10/): on this card, the archway between one room of life and the next. My note for this card is one of my longest: complete wealth at home, celebration, the whole family present — may be short-lived; if you leave the cocoon, it will still be there.

By its 10, Instagram had become a different platform altogether. Here the cycle of building closes: the house exists, and can be handed on. A ten also tells you to start something else — and the young couple already stand under the arch, turned towards what lies beyond it.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) is matter, value, the result of work — worth measured in cash or in affection. Here both meet: a house, a coat of arms, and also the dogs, the child, the people.

The flip side is Earth’s stubbornness on a family scale: the house that must stay as it is, the trade you’re expected to take over. Not every inheritance is welcome; this card can also describe a cocoon grown a little tight.

## By colours {#colours}

It’s the busiest card of the suit. The archway is [grey](/library/tarot-colours/grey/): stability, a structure that holds without a word. The woman’s dress and the house are red: passion, action, life going on. The sky is blue, and the old man’s cloak is every colour at once, as if a whole life were sewn into it. The ten gold coins float over everything, in nobody’s hands. A solid frame, people alive inside it, value laid over the whole scene.

## By astrology: Mercury in Virgo {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn system, this card is [Mercury](/library/astrology/mercury/) in the third decan of [Virgo](/library/astrology/virgo/). Mercury rules Virgo, so here it’s at home: the mind that counts, sorts and passes things on. It’s also the planet of exchange, fitting for a card about what passes between generations. Worth keeping, as a reminder that an inheritance is also a conversation; if astrology isn’t your lens, the number already said transmission.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright: a home that holds, a family gathered, something built and now handed on. It can be literal — a house, a family business, a celebration with everyone present — or a feeling: belonging to something older and larger than you. **A full house is a moment, not a fortress: enjoy it, and know you can leave and find it still there.**

Reversed, the household either goes quiet or shuts its doors. Gone quiet, everyone is present and nobody talks, and the inheritance becomes a weight or a quarrel. Shut in, the cocoon becomes the whole world, and leaving it feels like betrayal.

Many people hope this card means an inheritance or a windfall. I understand: money worries are real, and heavy. But I don’t read on money predictions or decisions. The card can show who is around you and what’s being passed down; more in [inheritance hopes and what the coins can say](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/).

Next to the [Ten of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-cups/), the same family seen from the heart — in my notes, happiness, family, children. Next to the [Eight of Cups](/library/tarot-cards/eight-of-cups/), someone walks out of the full house: the cocoon left, and still there. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the house kept rather than handed on.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

An exercise: someone draws the Ten of Pentacles, then the Page of Wands, and the reading is yours to make. Ten then Page: a cycle closing, then a student starting something. Earth then Fire: a house that holds, then a new urge. Three generations under an archway, then a young man alone in a desert, looking up at a wand that sprouts leaves.

A full house beside a lone young traveller gives this: “Something at home is complete, and it’s good. And someone young, maybe you, wants to try something of their own. It doesn’t have to be a break.”

Then check: “Does that speak to you?” If nothing fits, it’s a miss — say so, note it, and don’t draw another card to rescue it.
