## What’s on the card {#the-card}

A young man in a very tall red hat holds a pentacle in each hand, and a green band loops around both coins in a figure of eight — an infinity sign lying on its side. The left coin is up at his shoulder, the right one down at his hip. His knees are bent and his feet point different ways, as if he’s been caught mid-step in a dance. His head tilts; his face is calm, almost absent.

Behind him, the sea rises in high blue waves, and two ships ride them — a small one in the distance, a bigger one on the crest of a wave. He stands on flat grey ground. They’re on the swell.

Notice what isn’t there: no table to put a coin down, no one to hand one to. **He keeps two things moving, and he does it well — while the sea behind him rises and falls the whole time.**

## By number: two {#number}

**two — to bind, to weigh, to cooperate.** [Number two](/library/numerology/number-2/) is two parties and the bond between them: a contract, an alliance, a choice being weighed. Here the two parties are two coins — two jobs, two budgets, work and home — and the green band is the bond. They’re tied together: move one and the other moves too.

The advice of 2, in my notes, is to read the contract and weigh the pros and cons. The minus of 2 is the mind that insists, stubbornness, solitude — and he is alone, sure he can keep it all going. In the Instagram story, 2 is signing with investors: the first moment there are two things to keep in balance, your idea and someone else’s money.

## By element: Earth {#element}

[Earth](/library/four-elements/earth-element/) brings this down to the practical: time, money, energy, the things you can count. It’s the element of results, so the Two of Pentacles talks about everyday logistics more than big feelings. Who picks up the children, which bill goes first, how many hours are left in the week.

Earth is also endurance, and the juggler can keep going a long time — that’s his strength. The flip side is the stubbornness of someone who keeps juggling when they could put one coin down.

## By colours {#colours}

[Red](/library/tarot-colours/red/) dominates the figure: the tall hat, the sleeves, the tights. Passion, action, impulse — a body busy doing something. His tunic is a warmer orange-brown; his shoes and the band are green, the colour of what grows and stays alive. The coins are gold. Behind him, [blue](/library/tarot-colours/blue/) everywhere, in the pale sky and the waves: emotions, water, vitality.

Read the colours before the card: an active body in front of a moving sea. The action is up front. The feeling is behind, and it’s rough.

## By astrology: Jupiter in Capricorn {#astrology}

In the Golden Dawn tradition, the Two of Pentacles is the first decan of Capricorn, given to [Jupiter](/library/astrology/jupiter/); its title is the Lord of Harmonious Change. Jupiter is expansion, confidence, luck — and excess. Capricorn says *I use*: work, structure, pragmatism. Classical astrology places Jupiter in its fall in Capricorn: the planet that wants more, in the sign that counts. That tension is the card — growth kept inside a budget.

Optional: the number and the waves have already said most of it.

## In a reading {#reading}

Upright, the Two of Pentacles is juggling that works: two jobs, two projects, a budget stretched between two needs — and the person keeping it all in the air. Change is moving through, and they adapt. **The card asks how long the juggling can last, not whether the person is good at it — they are.**

Reversed, the band slackens. One coin drops and the other wobbles: a deadline missed, the end of the month arriving early, a day when everything lands at once. Or, in excess, the juggling has become the whole life, and the person keeps both coins moving so they never have to choose one.

This card often comes with money worries — which bill first, whether to take on a second job. I don’t read on those decisions; they stay with you, and the figures belong with someone who can see them. What the card can show is how the juggling feels and what it costs you ([reading the Pentacles with your feet on the ground](/blog/tarot-cards-for-money/)).

Next to [Temperance](/library/tarot-cards/temperance/), the juggling calms into a steady pour from one cup to another: the same balance, without the dance. Next to the [Two of Swords](/library/tarot-cards/two-of-swords/), the other face of 2: one figure keeps both options moving, the other blindfolds herself so as not to choose. Next to the [Four of Pentacles](/library/tarot-cards/four-of-pentacles/), the juggler has stopped and clutches everything: the movement has turned into a grip.

## A reading, as an example {#example}

Say someone sits down, tells you nothing, and draws the Two of Pentacles next to the [Ten of Wands](/library/tarot-cards/ten-of-wands/). Lenses first. Two then ten: a balance being kept, then the end of a cycle, the corridor between two rooms. Earth then Fire: practical life, then energy and drive. The drawings: a body dancing in front of the sea, then a man bent under a bundle of ten wands. In my notes, the Ten of Wands says keep the creative energy — without overloading.

The sentence I’d say out loud: “You’re keeping a lot going and you’re doing it well, but you’re carrying more than you need to, and something will have to be put down soon.”

To check, I ask: “Is there something in your week you could put down, and haven’t?” If an answer comes straight away, it landed. If the person looks blank, it’s a miss, and I say so.
